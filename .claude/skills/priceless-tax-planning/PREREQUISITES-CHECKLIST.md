# Mississippi (MS) — State Tax File

Mississippi enacted the **Build-Up Mississippi Act (HB 1, 2025)** signed March 27, 2025, continuing the state's path to income tax elimination. From 5% pre-reform to 4.4% for 2025, **4.0% for 2026**, with annual 0.25% reductions through 2030 (reaching 3.0%), then trigger-based reductions toward full elimination targeted ~2040. Would be first US state to fully eliminate an existing income tax.

Corporate franchise tax (capital stock tax) phasing out by 2028. Grocery sales tax dropping to 5% (July 2026) then 4.5% (2027+) per trigger mechanism.

Very low Priceless concentration.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: **First $10,000 exempt**; income over $10,000 taxed at flat rate
  - 2025: **4.4%**
  - 2026: **4.0%**
  - 2027-2030: 3.75%, 3.5%, 3.25%, 3.0% (scheduled)
  - Post-2030: trigger-based further reductions; full elimination targeted ~2040
- **Corporate income tax**: 4% on first $10K exempt; 5% on income > $10K (unchanged for now)
- **Corporate franchise tax**: $0.50 per $1,000 on capital > $100,000 for 2026 (reduced from $0.75 in 2025); scheduled full repeal for tax years beginning on or after 1/1/2028
- **PTET**: Yes; refundable credit to owners
- **Sales tax**: 7% state (one of highest in nation); groceries currently 7% reducing to 5% (July 2026) → 4.5% (2027-2036 per trigger) → 2.5% (2036)
- **Estate tax**: NONE (repealed); no inheritance tax
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Retirement income**: Fully exempt (qualified retirement plans, pensions)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal); automatic extension with 80% payment

### "Exempt + flat" structure mechanics

Mississippi is unique in its "exempt first $10,000 then flat" structure (not pure graduated brackets):
- First $10,000 of taxable income: 0%
- All income above $10,000: flat rate (4.0% for 2026)
- Each taxpayer gets the $10,000 exemption independently

**MFJ with both spouses having income**: Each spouse computes separately. Filing jointly, a couple with each having $50,000 income ($100,000 combined):
- Each spouse: $10K × 0% = $0; $40K × 4% = $1,600
- Combined: $3,200 total tax (2026)
- This is more favorable than treating $100K combined: $10K × 0% = $0; $90K × 4% = $3,600
- **Savings: $400** by separate-calculation approach (always applies; not an election)

### Standard deduction and exemptions

- Standard deduction 2025: $2,300 single; $4,600 MFJ; $3,400 HoH [VERIFY 2026 figures — minor inflation adjustments]
- Personal exemption 2025: $6,000 single; $12,000 MFJ; $8,000 HoH [VERIFY 2026]
- Dependent exemption: $1,500 each

These deductions apply BEFORE the $10,000 exemption → effectively, first $10,000 + standard deduction + personal exemption is untaxed.

### Retirement income

- **Qualified retirement income**: Fully exempt (pensions, 401(k), IRA distributions)
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Military retirement**: Fully exempt

Mississippi is highly retirement-friendly. Zero state tax on all retirement income.

### Capital gains

Taxed as ordinary income at 4% rate (2026). No preferential treatment.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

Enacted as § 27-7-22.43 (effective TY 2022 and forward):
- **Rate**: Same as individual (4.4% 2025 / 4.0% 2026; tracks annual reductions)
- **Election**: Annual
- **Refundable credit**: To owners
- **Form**: Form 84-PTE [VERIFY exact form number]
- **Deadline**: Election made by original due date of entity return

### Worked example

S corp with $500K MS-source income; one owner:
- Entity PTET 2026: $500K × 4% = $20,000 paid at entity level
- Federal SALT deduction: $20,000 × 37% = $7,400 federal savings
- Owner gets $20,000 refundable credit
- Net MS cost: $0

Given low 4% rate, PTET benefit is modest but still positive vs. federal SALT cap limitations for MAGI > $600K.

## Corporate income tax

- **Rate structure**: Same as individual "exempt first $10K" — $10K × 0%, then 5% on income > $10K
- **Rate is 5% flat** (above $10K)
- **Base**: Federal taxable income with MS modifications
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor (since TY 2018)
- **Combined filing**: Not required
- **Corporation franchise tax (capital stock tax)**: Phasing out — see below

### Corporate franchise tax phase-out

Mississippi is one of few states with a corporate franchise tax on capital:

| Year | Rate per $1,000 Capital | Exemption |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | $1.25 | $100,000 |
| 2025 | $0.75 | $100,000 |
| **2026** | **$0.50** | **$100,000** |
| 2027 | $0.25 | $100,000 |
| **2028** | **REPEALED** | |

This affects C corporations only (not pass-through entities). Priceless clients with LA/AL/MS corporate structures benefit from this phase-out.

## Sales and use tax

- **State rate: 7%** (one of highest in US)
- **Local adds**: Generally 0%; Jackson has a 1% local; few others
- **Groceries**: Currently **7%** (unusual — most states exempt groceries); Build-Up MS Act phase-down:
  - **July 1, 2026**: Reduces to **5%**
  - **2027-2036**: Reduces by 0.2% each July 1, reaching **4.5%** in 2027
  - **2036**: Final rate **2.5%** (per trigger reductions)
- **Prescription drugs**: Exempt
- **Clothing**: Taxable at 7%

### New local option grocery tax

Build-Up MS Act allows counties and municipalities to impose a **1.5% local sales tax on groceries and non-groceries** starting July 1, 2026. This offsets the state grocery rate reduction; local can replace lost revenue.

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic 6-month to October 15; must pay 80% by April 15 to avoid penalty
- **Extension form**: Form 80-106 (voucher)
- **Forms**: 80-105 (resident); 80-205 (nonresident/part-year)
- **Estimated payments**: Required if tax liability > $200; quarterly (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15)
- **Estimated safe harbor**: 80% of current year or 100% of prior year
- **Interest rate**: Varies; penalty for underpayment similar pattern

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Gulf Coast business owner
**Setup**: MS resident; S corp with $400K income.

**2025**:
- Tax: $10K × 0% + $390K × 4.4% = $17,160
- With PTET: $17,160 paid at entity; federal SALT deduction $17,160 × 37% = $6,349 savings

**2026**:
- Tax: $10K × 0% + $390K × 4.0% = $15,600
- Savings from rate drop: $1,560
- With PTET: $15,600 × 37% = $5,772 federal savings

**2030 projected**: $10K × 0% + $390K × 3.0% = $11,700 (saving $5,460 vs. 2025).

### Scenario 2: Mississippi retiree
**Setup**: MS resident; 68; MFJ with spouse 66; $70K combined pension + $40K combined Social Security.

**Analysis**:
- Social Security: Fully exempt
- Pension: Fully exempt
- **Total MS tax: $0**

Mississippi is the most retirement-friendly state after those with no income tax at all. Perfect for retirees seeking low tax burden.

### Scenario 3: Multi-state client with MS operations
**Setup**: TX-based S corp with 30% of sales in MS; $2M total income.

**Analysis**:
- MS-sourced: $600K via single sales factor
- MS PTET 2026: $600K × 4% = $24,000 at entity level
- MS franchise tax phase-out: If TX S corp has MS capital over $100K, applies $0.50/$1,000 for 2026 (down from $0.75 in 2025); repealed 2028
- TX owners: No MS credit needed (TX has no income tax); election still advisable for federal SALT benefit

### Scenario 4: Income tax elimination planning
**Setup**: MS-based HNW client considering timing of income recognition.

**Analysis**:
- MS rate trajectory: 4% (2026) → 3% (2030) → possibly 0% (~2040)
- **Defer ordinary income recognition** to later years (lower rates)
- **Accelerate deductions** into current year (higher rate = bigger tax benefit)
- Capital gains treated as ordinary; same timing logic
- **Roth conversions**: Unfavorable if deferring to lower rates (pay conversion tax now at 4%; pay 0% on future qualified distributions at later point)

**Priceless action**: For MS clients, model rate trajectory into long-term tax planning. Relative value of deferral increases given known reducing rates.
