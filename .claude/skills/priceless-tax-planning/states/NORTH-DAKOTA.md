# North Dakota (ND) — State Tax File

North Dakota has a remarkably low top individual income tax rate of **2.5%** (tied with Arizona for the lowest top rate in the US among states with an income tax). 2-bracket structure established via **HB 1158 (2023)**, which dramatically reduced rates from prior 5-bracket 2.9% top structure.

ND is also a **gross income tax state** — starts from federal AGI. Oil/gas severance tax revenue has enabled the state to aggressively cut income tax.

Very low Priceless concentration. Occasional oil & gas industry clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 2 brackets — 1.95% / **2.5% top** (tied with AZ for lowest top state income tax rate)
- **Corporate income tax**: 1.41% to 4.31% (graduated)
- **PTET**: Yes
- **Sales tax**: 5% state + local up to 3.5% (combined max 8.5%); food exempt
- **Estate tax**: None
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Military retirement**: Fully exempt
- **Standard deduction**: Starts from federal taxable income (conforms federal standard deduction)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### Starting point: Federal taxable income

ND is unusual in using **federal taxable income** (not federal AGI) as starting point. This means federal standard deduction flows through automatically. OBBBA post-2025 standard deduction ($16,100 single / $32,200 MFJ for 2026) applies on ND return by default.

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: 100% exempt
- **Military retirement**: 100% exempt
- **Other retirement**: Taxed at ordinary rates (1.95% / 2.5%)

At 2.5% top rate, ND is effectively retirement-friendly even without pension exemptions — any retirement income is taxed at modest rates.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

- **Rate**: 2.5% (top individual rate)
- **Base**: ND-source income
- **Election**: Annual
- **Credit**: Refundable to owners

### Worked example

S corp with $500K ND-source income:
- Entity PTET: $500K × 2.5% = $12,500 at entity level
- Federal SALT deduction: $12,500 × 37% = $4,625 federal savings

Modest PTET benefit given low 2.5% rate, but still positive for federal SALT cap workaround.

## Corporate income tax

- **Rates**: 1.41% / 4.31% top (graduated)
- **Base**: Federal taxable income with ND modifications
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor
- **Combined filing**: Optional water's-edge combined

## Oil and gas severance

ND's oil/gas severance taxes are highly material to state revenue. Oil production tax + oil extraction tax = effectively ~11% combined on Bakken shale production. This revenue funds the "Legacy Fund" and enables income tax reductions. For Priceless clients with ND mineral interests, severance tax is a significant consideration separate from income tax.

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic 6 months via federal
- **Forms**: Form ND-1 (resident); Form ND-1NR (nonresident)
- **Estimated payments**: Quarterly if liability > $1,000

## Priceless client scenarios

### Bakken oil and gas royalty owner
**Setup**: ND mineral rights owner; $500K annual royalty income; 200 producing wells.

**Analysis**:
- ND income tax: $500K × 2.5% effective ≈ $12,500
- Oil and gas severance: Imposed on producer (operator), not royalty owner; reduces royalty payment
- Depletion allowance: Federal 15% percentage depletion on royalties; reduces both federal and ND taxable income
- Net ND tax after depletion: ~$10,600

Extremely low effective state tax; ND is very favorable for mineral owners.

### ND farmer/rancher
**Setup**: ND resident; family farm; $200K Schedule F income; sold cattle $100K LTCG.

**Analysis**:
- Ordinary income $200K × blended 1.95-2.5% ≈ $4,800 ND tax
- LTCG $100K × 2.5% (ordinary at top rate) = $2,500 — no preferential capital gains rate in ND
- Total ND tax ~$7,300

At 2.5% top, capital gains taxed same as ordinary but rate low enough it barely matters.
