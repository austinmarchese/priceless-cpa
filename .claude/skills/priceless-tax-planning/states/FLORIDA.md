# Florida (FL) — State Tax File

**Priceless headquarters state.** This is the file most often consulted because the majority of Priceless clients live, work, or operate businesses in Florida. Also the destination state for most Priceless clients executing tax-motivated relocations from CA, NY, NJ, IL, and elsewhere.

## Quick reference

- **No personal income tax** — constitutionally protected (Fla. Const. Art. VII, §5)
- **No estate tax, no inheritance tax, no gift tax** — at state level
- **Corporate income tax**: 5.5% flat rate on Florida-source income (C corps and entities taxed as corporations only; pass-throughs exempt)
- **Sales tax**: 6% state base + 0–2.5% county surtax (Miami-Dade 1%; max 8.5%)
- **Documentary stamp tax**: 0.7% on deeds + 0.35% on notes/mortgages (significant closing cost on real estate)
- **Property tax**: ~0.83% effective average; Homestead Exemption + Save Our Homes 3% cap for primary residents
- **Reemployment assistance** (unemployment): 2.7% new employer rate; varies by experience
- **No PTET** (not applicable; no individual income tax to offset)

For Priceless purposes: Florida is the state where most strategy mechanics are simplest because state-level tax overlay is minimal. The focus shifts to federal optimization, with state considerations mainly around real estate transactions, sales tax compliance, and protecting domicile when clients have multi-state ties.

## Personal income tax

**Florida has no personal income tax. Period.**

The prohibition is constitutional, not merely statutory. Fla. Const. Art. VII, §5 explicitly forbids the imposition of a personal income tax. Amendment requires a 60% voter supermajority — an extraordinarily high bar that has never been seriously approached politically.

Practical implications:
- No state Form 1040 equivalent
- No state withholding from wages
- No state quarterly estimated payments for individuals
- No state itemized deduction interaction
- No state capital gains tax
- No state tax on interest, dividends, retirement distributions
- No state tax on pass-through K-1 income

This is the headline reason high earners relocate to Florida from CA, NY, NJ, IL, MA, and other high-tax states.

### What Florida residents DO pay at the federal level

Federal obligations are unaffected by Florida residency:
- Federal income tax at standard brackets
- Federal FICA (Social Security 6.2% / Medicare 1.45%; SE tax 15.3% for self-employed)
- NIIT 3.8% on investment income above thresholds
- Additional Medicare 0.9% above thresholds
- Federal capital gains tax (0/15/20% + NIIT)
- Federal estate tax above $15M (post-OBBBA permanent)

### The "Florida advantage" math

For Priceless Full-Wealth clients moving from high-tax states, the annual savings calculation is straightforward:

| State of Origin | Annual Savings on $750K AGI |
|---|---|
| California (top 13.3%) | ~$70,000+ (actual depends on bracket buildup) |
| New York City (NY 10.9% + NYC 3.876%) | ~$80,000+ |
| New Jersey (top 10.75%) | ~$60,000+ |
| Illinois (4.95% flat) | ~$33,000 |

These are the savings BEFORE any other strategy. Add Operators 1-7 (and Operator 8 for Full-Wealth) and the multi-year tax delta from a high-tax state move can compound into seven figures over a working career.

## Corporate income tax (Fla. Stat. Ch. 220)

Florida imposes a flat 5.5% corporate income tax on C corporations and entities taxed as corporations.

### Who pays

- C corporations (federal Form 1120 filers)
- LLCs and other entities that have elected federal C corp treatment
- Out-of-state corporations with Florida-source income meeting nexus

### Who is exempt

- Sole proprietorships
- Partnerships and multi-member LLCs (federal Form 1065)
- S corporations (federal Form 1120-S) — Florida explicitly does NOT impose corporate tax on S corps
- Single-member LLCs treated as disregarded entities

### Filing mechanics

- **Form**: Florida Corporate Income/Franchise Tax Return (Form F-1120; short form F-1120A for eligible small corporations)
- **Due date**: First day of the fourth month following close of fiscal year (April 1 for calendar-year)
- **Estimated payments**: Required if annual tax exceeds $2,500 (Form F-1120ES)
- **Extension**: 6-month automatic extension via Form F-7004; extension to file is NOT extension to pay
- **Late filing penalty**: 10% per month, max 50% of tax due
- **Interest**: Floating rate published quarterly (11% for Jan-Jun 2026 per TIP #25ADM-03)

### Federal conformity for corporate tax

Florida adopts federal taxable income as the starting point with specific addback/subtraction adjustments. Florida uses a rolling federal conformity for the most part — federal IRC changes flow through automatically unless Florida specifically decouples.

Annual conformity update bill — Florida legislature passes legislation each session reaffirming conformity to the IRC as of a specific date. Watch for:
- Annual conformity bill (typically passed in spring session)
- Specific decoupling provisions (rare in FL but possible for unusual federal changes)

### Florida-specific corporate adjustments

Florida departs from federal income at the corporate level for:
- Federal NOL carryback/carryforward (Florida has its own NOL regime; carryforward 20 years generally)
- Section 179 expensing — typically follows federal
- Bonus depreciation — Florida historically conforms but has had decoupling periods; verify current bill
- Subtraction for foreign-source income exempt from US tax
- Specific Florida credits (see Credits section below)

## OBBBA conformity analysis

Because Florida has no individual income tax, OBBBA's individual provisions have no direct state impact. Conformity analysis applies only to corporate income tax for C corp and corporate-electing entities.

| OBBBA Provision | Florida Treatment | Practical Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 100% bonus depreciation permanent (post-1/19/2025) | Florida currently conforms (verify with annual conformity bill) | C corps: full federal benefit; pass-throughs: no state impact (no individual tax) |
| §199A QBI permanent | N/A — Florida has no individual income tax | No state QBI dimension |
| §163(j) interest limitation back to EBITDA | Florida conforms | C corps benefit from increased deductibility |
| §174 R&D domestic immediate expensing restored | Florida conforms | C corps: standard federal treatment |
| §1202 QSBS expanded ($15M cap, tiered) | N/A — exclusion is from federal capital gains; no state cap gain tax in FL | Florida residents get full federal QSBS benefit without state tax loss; major advantage for tech-sector exits |
| QOZ permanent + Dec 31 2031 trigger | N/A at individual level; corporate QOZ-investing C corp would have FL impact (rare) | Florida residents get full federal QOZ benefit |
| §250 GILTI deduction | Florida conforms for corporate GILTI inclusion | C corps with CFC ownership: benefit; individual CFC owners (no state tax) |
| Estate exemption $15M permanent | N/A — Florida has no estate tax | Florida residents pay no state estate tax regardless of federal exemption |
| SALT cap $40,400 (2026) phase-down | N/A directly; affects federal itemized deduction calculation only | Florida residents have no state tax to count toward SALT cap, freeing the cap for property tax (which CAN be high in FL) |

### The SALT cap angle for Florida residents

Florida residents itemizing on federal Schedule A still face SALT cap ($40,400 in 2026 with phase-down for high income). But because they have NO state income tax, the entire SALT cap is available for property tax + sales tax (option) deduction.

For Florida property owners with significant property tax bills (e.g., Miami-Dade single-family $20K+ annual), this preserves more federal deduction capacity vs. high-tax state residents whose state income tax already consumes the cap.

Practical example:
- Miami client with $2M home, ~$24K Florida property tax + ~$2K sales tax
- Total SALT = $26K (under the $40,400 cap)
- Full $26K federally deductible
- Compare: NY client with same $26K in NY property + $30K in NY income tax = $56K, capped at $40,400 = $15,600 lost

## Pass-through entity treatment (no PTET)

Florida does not have and does not need a Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) because there is no individual income tax for the PTET to offset.

PTET is the SALT-cap workaround mechanism for pass-through owners in income-tax states. In CA, NY, NJ, etc., PTET shifts state tax from owner-level (capped by federal SALT) to entity-level (federally deductible).

For Florida pass-through owners:
- No state-level entity tax to elect
- No SALT cap workaround needed for state tax (there isn't any)
- Owners pay only federal tax on K-1 income

This does NOT mean Florida pass-through owners with multi-state operations don't need to evaluate PTET — they may need PTET elections in the OTHER states where they have nexus. See `tax-strategy/strategies/PTET-ELECTION-BY-STATE.md` for multi-state PTET coordination.

## Sales and use tax (Fla. Stat. Ch. 212)

### Base rate and surtaxes

- **State base rate**: 6%
- **County discretionary surtax**: 0% to 2.5%, varies by county
- **Common county surtaxes**: Miami-Dade 1% (= 7% combined); Broward 0% (= 6%); Palm Beach 1% (= 7%); Orange 0.5% (= 6.5%); Hillsborough 1% (= 7%)
- **Maximum combined**: 8.5% in some counties

Average combined state and local sales tax rate: ~7.02% (Tax Foundation 2026 data).

### What's taxed

- Tangible personal property (most goods)
- Commercial rent (Florida is unusual in taxing commercial leases — rate phasing down; verify current)
- Many services (admissions, certain personal services, security services)
- Communications services (separate Communications Services Tax)
- Transient rentals (hotels, short-term rentals — typically with additional tourist development tax)

### Common exemptions

- Groceries (unprepared food)
- Prescription drugs and most OTC drugs
- Most medical equipment
- Manufacturing equipment (with proper certificate)
- Agricultural inputs
- Sales for resale (with resale certificate)

### Wayfair / nexus thresholds

Post-Wayfair, Florida economic nexus threshold for remote sellers:
- $100,000 in retail sales of taxable goods/services delivered into Florida in prior calendar year
- Marketplace facilitators required to collect on behalf of marketplace sellers

Nexus also created by:
- Physical presence (employee, office, inventory in FL)
- Affiliate nexus
- Click-through nexus

### Commercial rent tax — REPEALED effective October 1, 2025 (HB 7031)

**Major 2025 change**: Florida has fully repealed its commercial rent sales tax via HB 7031, signed by Governor DeSantis on June 30, 2025. This ended a tax that had been in place since 1969 (Florida was the only state with such a tax).

**Repeal details**:
- Prior rate: 2% state + local discretionary surtax (most counties 2.5% to 3.5% combined)
- Effective October 1, 2025: 0% — both state tax and local surtax eliminated
- Florida Department of Revenue TIP 25A01-04 announced the change
- Rent for periods BEFORE October 1, 2025 remains taxable (even if paid after); rent for periods ON OR AFTER October 1, 2025 is fully untaxed
- Statutory anti-avoidance: tax rate cannot be avoided by delaying/accelerating payment dates — accrual determined by occupancy period, not payment date

**What remains taxable** (these were never under §212.031 and are unaffected):
- Short-term residential rentals (< 6 months)
- Parking facilities and garages
- Boat slips and dock rentals
- Aircraft hangar rentals
- Commercial equipment leases (still taxable under separate rental statute)
- Self-storage units [VERIFY: some sources say taxable, others say exempt; confirm via FL DOR]

**Priceless implications**:
- Commercial landlords: should have deregistered sales tax accounts by October 1, 2025 if commercial rent was their only taxable activity; confirm deregistration to avoid late-filing penalties for non-occurring filings
- Commercial tenants: verify landlord has removed sales tax from invoicing as of October 1, 2025 (landlords sometimes miss the change)
- Prepaid rent issue: tenant paying for occupancy period before October 1 after the repeal date still owes tax on that prepayment
- Annual Florida commercial rent tax revenue was approximately $900M-$1B — this is a significant positive change for Florida commercial real estate economics and improves cap rate math for investors

## Property tax (significant for Priceless clients)

State-level property tax framework:
- Tax assessed and collected at county level
- State sets framework; counties administer
- Assessed value × millage rate = tax bill
- Most counties: 1.5%-2.0% effective (gross of homestead exemption)
- Effective average across state: 0.83% (per Tax Foundation 2026; this includes homestead-protected primary residences)

### Homestead Exemption (Fla. Const. Art. VII, §6)

For primary residence:
- First $25,000 reduction in assessed value applies to all property taxes
- Second $25,000 reduction applies to non-school portion only
- Total exemption value: $50,000 reduction in assessed value

For a $500K assessed primary residence: assessed value reduced to $450K-$475K (depending on millage breakdown).

### Save Our Homes (SOH) cap (Fla. Const. Art. VII, §4(d))

Annual increase in assessed value of homesteaded property capped at lesser of:
- 3%, or
- Annual change in CPI

This is one of Florida's most significant taxpayer protections. For long-held primary residences in appreciating markets, the SOH cap creates dramatic disparity between assessed value and market value over time.

Example: $300K home purchased in 2010, market value 2026 = $750K. Assessed value capped at 3% annual growth from 2010 base. After 15 years of 3% caps: assessed value approximately $467K. Tax savings vs. fully-assessed: significant.

### Portability of SOH benefit

When homesteaded owner sells and buys new primary residence:
- SOH-protected differential transfers to new property (up to $500K)
- Must apply within 3 years of selling original homestead
- Significant benefit for long-term FL residents upgrading homes

### Non-homesteaded property

Investment, vacation, and commercial property:
- No SOH cap
- 10% annual cap on assessed value increases (per 2008 Amendment 1) — less generous than 3% homestead
- No homestead exemption
- Property taxes can be substantial; factor into investment analysis

## Documentary stamp tax (Fla. Stat. Ch. 201)

Significant closing cost on Florida real estate transactions.

### On deeds (Fla. Stat. §201.02)

- **State rate**: $0.70 per $100 of consideration ($7 per $1,000)
- **Miami-Dade County exception**: $0.60 per $100 base + $0.45 per $100 surtax on documents transferring anything OTHER than single-family residence
- **Customarily paid**: by seller (negotiable in contract)
- **Example**: $1M sale outside Miami-Dade = $7,000 documentary stamps on deed
- **Miami-Dade $1M single-family**: $6,000 (uses $0.60/$100)
- **Miami-Dade $1M commercial**: $6,000 + $4,500 surtax = $10,500

### On notes and mortgages (Fla. Stat. §201.08)

- **Rate**: $0.35 per $100 of indebtedness ($3.50 per $1,000)
- **Maximum tax on UNSECURED notes**: $2,450 (cap applies to unsecured only)
- **Recorded mortgages**: NO cap on documentary stamps
- **Customarily paid**: by buyer/borrower (negotiable)

### Nonrecurring intangible tax (Fla. Stat. §199.133)

Imposed on mortgages secured by Florida real property:
- **Rate**: 2 mills per dollar of indebtedness (0.2%)
- **Example**: $400,000 mortgage = $800 intangible tax
- **One-time**: nonrecurring (no annual obligation)
- **Paid at recording**

### Combined real estate transfer tax burden

For a typical $500,000 financed Miami-Dade single-family purchase:
- Documentary stamps on deed (single-family rate): $3,000
- Documentary stamps on $400K mortgage note: $1,400
- Intangible tax on $400K mortgage: $800
- **Total**: $5,200 in transfer-related taxes

For a $5M investment commercial property in Miami-Dade with $3M financing:
- Documentary stamps on deed (commercial rate, $0.60 + $0.45 = $1.05 per $100): $52,500
- Documentary stamps on $3M mortgage: $10,500
- Intangible tax on $3M mortgage: $6,000
- **Total**: $69,000

This is material for Priceless real estate clients and must be modeled in transaction planning.

### Avoidance strategies (limited)

- Loans documented and delivered outside Florida may avoid documentary stamps on the note (but mortgage recording in FL still triggers tax)
- Spousal transfers: exempt
- Divorce-related transfers: exempt
- Government transfers: exempt
- Personal property allocation in mixed sales: legitimate way to reduce taxable consideration

## Estate, inheritance, and gift tax

**Florida has none.**

- No state estate tax (last vestige repealed 2004)
- No inheritance tax
- No gift tax

For Priceless Full-Wealth clients with estates approaching the $15M federal exemption: Florida residency means only the federal estate tax matters.

For clients moving to FL from estate-tax states (NY, NJ, MA, CT, MN, OR, IL, MD, etc.): residency change eliminates state estate tax exposure (subject to ancillary state estate tax on real property in former state).

## Reemployment assistance (unemployment) tax (Fla. Stat. Ch. 443)

State unemployment tax framework:
- **Wage base**: $7,000 (one of the lowest in the country — most states $15K-$70K)
- **New employer rate**: 2.7% for first 10 quarters
- **Subsequent rate**: experience-rated; range 0.10% to 5.4%
- **Maximum tax per employee**: ~$378/year (5.4% × $7,000)

Low wage base means total state unemployment burden is relatively modest in Florida vs. high-base states like Washington ($72,800 base) or New Jersey ($43,300).

Federal unemployment (FUTA) applies on top: 6.0% on first $7,000 of wages, with 5.4% credit for state unemployment tax = effective 0.6% federal.

## Residency rules (CRITICAL for clients with multi-state ties)

Florida residency rules are the OPPOSITE of high-tax states. Florida WANTS you to claim residency. The challenge is satisfying the EXIT requirements of the high-tax state you're leaving.

### Florida's domicile standard

Per Fla. Stat. §222.17 and case law (Bloomfield, Keveloh):
- Domicile = place where person has fixed an abode WITH PRESENT INTENTION of making it permanent home
- Once established, continues until superseded by new one
- No minimum day requirement to establish or maintain
- Established by overt acts demonstrating intent + actual physical presence

### Establishing Florida domicile (the Priceless checklist)

1. **Acquire Florida residence** (own, rent, or long-term lease)
2. **File Declaration of Domicile** at county clerk's office (Fla. Stat. §222.17) — sworn statement of intent
3. **Florida driver's license** — surrender prior state license
4. **Voter registration** in Florida — cancel prior state registration
5. **Vehicle registration** in Florida
6. **Apply for homestead exemption** (if owned primary residence)
7. **Federal tax returns** — file using Florida address (IRS Form 8822 change of address)
8. **Update Social Security records**
9. **Move banking** — primary checking, safety deposit box in FL
10. **Update will, trusts, healthcare directives** under Florida law
11. **Establish primary medical care providers in FL**
12. **Move household goods** (overt act per Bloomfield)
13. **Notify insurance carriers, financial institutions, etc.**

### Florida's day-counting irrelevance

Florida has no minimum-day requirement. The 183-day rule is a high-tax state concept (NY, NJ, CA, etc. use it to claim STATUTORY residency).

Critical: Florida domicile alone is not enough if former state can still claim you as statutory resident. Both must be addressed:
- **Florida domicile**: established by overt acts + intent
- **Statutory residency in former state**: must keep days in former state below threshold (typically 183 days; CA's 9-month rule different)

### Residency audit defense

When clients move from CA, NY, NJ, IL, MA — assume the former state will audit. Build the file from day one of move:

**Documentary evidence to maintain**:
- Closing documents on Florida residence
- Declaration of Domicile filed
- Florida driver's license issuance date
- Voter registration confirmation
- Vehicle registration
- Homestead exemption application
- Cell phone bills showing Florida usage patterns
- Credit card statements showing geographic usage
- Travel records (planes, trains, mileage)
- Medical care location
- Country club, religious institution, charitable affiliations in FL
- Bank account statements showing FL primary
- Estate planning documents (will, trust) revised under FL law

**Day-counting**:
- Maintain calendar showing days in each location
- Apps like Monaeo specifically built for state residency tracking
- Aim for clear majority of days in FL (60%+ ideal)
- Stay under 183 days in former state (or that state's specific threshold)

## Audit posture (state-level)

Florida Department of Revenue audit profile:
- **Less aggressive than CA, NY** — partly because less to audit (no individual income tax)
- **Sales tax**: highest audit area; common for retailers, restaurants, contractors
- **Corporate income tax**: routine for large filers; less frequent for small C corps
- **Documentary stamp tax**: audited periodically; understatement of consideration draws scrutiny
- **Property tax**: county-level; appeals to Value Adjustment Board

Common audit triggers:
- Cash-intensive businesses (retail, restaurants, service businesses with cash)
- Sales tax filings inconsistent with income reporting
- Documentary stamp underpayment
- Out-of-state remote seller compliance gaps
- Corporate apportionment issues for multi-state filers

Statute of limitations:
- General: 3 years from return due date
- Failure to file: no statute
- Fraud: no statute
- Substantial understatement: 6 years
