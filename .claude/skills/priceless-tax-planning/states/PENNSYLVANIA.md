# Pennsylvania — State Tax Reference

## The essentials

Pennsylvania is one of only FIVE states with a personal income tax but NO pass-through entity tax (PTET) — the others being Delaware, Maine, North Dakota, and Vermont. This is a critical planning factor: PA residents who own pass-through entities with multi-state activity CANNOT access a PA-level SALT workaround.

HB 1709 has been introduced to create a PA PTET but remains pending in the House Finance Committee as of April 2026. The state's most recent budget (S.B. 654) did NOT include a PTET election despite lobbying efforts.

PA has the lowest flat income tax rate among all flat-tax states at **3.07%** (per PA Constitution Article VIII §1, which prohibits graduated income taxation without constitutional amendment). Combined with moderate property taxes (outside Philadelphia/Pittsburgh and high-priced suburbs), PA's overall personal tax burden is among the lowest in the Northeast.

Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT) is reducing significantly: 7.99% in 2025 → **7.49% in 2026** → scheduled path to 4.99% by 2031.

**Critical "out-of-state PTET credit" gap**: PA allows residents a credit for PTET paid to other states IF the owner holds an S corporation interest (per 72 P.S. §7314). But PA DOR's position is that partnership owners do NOT get the credit — creating double-taxation risk for PA residents with partnership PTET exposure in other states. SB 660 has been introduced to fix this but is not yet enacted.

For Priceless, PA clients typically need: (1) recognition that no PA PTET exists — the SALT workaround is federal-level only (via entity's home state if not PA), (2) careful modeling of out-of-state PTET credit eligibility for partnership vs. S corp owners, (3) CNIT planning for C corporation clients given the phase-down schedule, and (4) Philadelphia-specific local tax compliance (BIRT, NPT, wage tax).

## Personal income tax

### Rate structure (2026)

**Flat rate**: 3.07% of PA taxable income

Verified via:
- Tax Foundation (February 2026)
- PA Department of Revenue (pa.gov/revenue)
- PA Constitution Article VIII §1 (prohibition on graduated rates — requires constitutional amendment to change)

### Key characteristics of PA's flat tax

**No standard deduction**: PA does not have a standard deduction
**No personal exemption**: PA does not have personal exemptions
**No indexing**: Rate has been 3.07% since 2004; no inflation adjustments

**Categorical income taxation**: PA has one of the most unusual income tax structures — income is divided into 8 categories, losses in one category CANNOT offset gains in another:
1. Compensation
2. Interest
3. Dividends
4. Net gains from dispositions of property
5. Net income from rents, royalties, patents, copyrights
6. Income from estates and trusts
7. Gambling and lottery winnings
8. Net profits from a business, profession, or farm

Critical planning implication: A rental property loss cannot offset wage income or business income for PA purposes (unlike federal). This is a trap for multi-stream income clients.

### Retirement income exemption

PA offers **significant retirement income exemptions**:
- Social Security: FULLY exempt
- Traditional IRA distributions (after age 59½): exempt
- 401(k)/403(b) distributions (after age 59½ or separation): exempt
- Roth IRA distributions: exempt
- Pension income: exempt (from qualified retirement plans)
- **Military retirement**: fully exempt
- **Railroad retirement**: exempt

**Practical impact**: PA is exceptionally favorable for retirees — essentially zero state income tax on most retirement income streams. This is why many PA residents choose to remain in PA rather than relocate to FL.

### Residency rules

PA uses domicile + statutory residency per 72 P.S. §7301:

**Resident**: Domiciled in PA, OR maintained a permanent place of abode in PA AND spent more than 183 days in PA during tax year

**Statutory resident test** similar to NY:
- Permanent place of abode in PA
- More than 183 days of physical presence

**Domicile factors**:
- Physical presence
- PA driver's license
- Voter registration
- Vehicle registration
- Location of family
- Professional licenses
- Religious/social affiliations

**Audit posture**: Moderate. PA DOR audits residency changes when significant tax liability reduction, but less aggressively than NY or CA.

**Philadelphia Net Profits Tax (NPT)**:
- 3.4481% on net profits from business, profession, or farm for residents
- 3.4481% on Philadelphia-source net profits for non-residents
- [VERIFY 2026 rates via Philadelphia Dept of Revenue]

**Philadelphia Business Income and Receipts Tax (BIRT)**:
- Applies to businesses with Philadelphia activity
- Gross receipts component: 0.1415%

- Complex apportionment rules; multi-factor test

**Pittsburgh local tax**:
- 1% resident wage tax (plus school district tax varies by school district)

**Other PA municipalities**:
- Most PA municipalities impose local Earned Income Tax (typically 1% combined municipality + school district)

- Effective combined rate for most working residents: 3.07% state + 1% local = ~4.07%

**Planning implication**: Philadelphia's 3.7483% wage tax + PA's 3.07% = **6.82% effective top marginal rate for Philadelphia residents** — higher than many "high-tax states" like Colorado or Massachusetts on wage income.

## Business taxes

### Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT)

**Authority**: 72 P.S. §7401 et seq.

**Rate schedule (phasing down under Act 53 of 2022)**:
- 2023: 8.99%
- 2024: 8.49%
- 2025: 7.99%
- **2026: 7.49%** (confirmed)
- 2027: 6.99%
- 2028: 6.49%
- 2029: 5.99%
- 2030: 5.49%
- 2031: 4.99% (final scheduled rate)

This phase-down is statutorily fixed (not trigger-based).

**Apportionment**: Single sales factor (since 2013)

**NOL deduction** (significant change enacted in S.B. 654 2024 budget):
- Prior: limited to 40% of taxable income per year
- **2026-2029 phase-in**: increasing 10% per year
  - 2026: 50%
  - 2027: 60%
  - 2028: 70%
  - 2029: 80% (final; federal-equivalent cap)
- Pre-2025 NOLs: remain subject to 40% cap
- Complex blending formula for pre-2025 vs. post-2024 NOLs

**Filing**:
- Form RCT-101 (annual)
- Due: April 15 for calendar year (one month after federal); extensions via Form REV-853
- Estimated payments quarterly for liability > $500

### Capital Stock / Foreign Franchise Tax

**Repealed effective 2016.** No ongoing capital stock tax.

### S Corporations and Partnerships

**S corps**: PA recognizes federal S elections; income flows through to PA personal income tax. S corps file Form PA-20S/PA-65.
**Partnerships**: File Form PA-20S/PA-65.
**LLCs**: Follow federal classification for state purposes.
**No state-level S corp entity tax** (unlike CA's 1.5%).

### Planning implications for PA pass-through owners

Without a PA PTET, PA residents who own pass-through entities have limited options:

**PA resident with PA-only pass-through income**:
- No SALT workaround available at PA level
- State tax of 3.07% + local 1% (or 3.7483% Phila) paid individually
- Subject to federal OBBBA SALT cap on all PA + local tax paid
- At $500K+ MAGI, federal SALT cap phases down; lose federal deductibility

**PA resident with multi-state pass-through income (can use other states' PTETs)**:
- PTET available in states where entity has operations and that offer PTET
- PA residency credit (72 P.S. §7314) available for taxes paid to other states — **BUT CRITICALLY**:
  - S corporation owners: PA DOR position — credit available
  - Partnership owners: PA DOR position — credit NOT available (disparate treatment; SB 660 would fix)

**Partnership PTET double-taxation risk (urgent planning issue)**:
- PA resident owns interest in partnership
- Partnership elects PTET in operating state (e.g., NY, NJ)
- Entity pays PTET (e.g., NY 6.85-10.9%, NJ 5.675-10.9%)
- PA resident receives federal K-1 reflecting reduced income (PTET deducted at entity)
- But PA DOR requires the partner to add back the PTET deduction on PA return
- PA DOR denies credit for PTET paid by partnership (distinction from S corp)
- Result: partner pays BOTH other state's PTET AND PA's 3.07%

**PA S corp treatment (favorable)**:
- S corp PTET paid in other state
- PA resident shareholder entitled to credit per 72 P.S. §7314
- No double taxation

**Priceless planning action**: For PA residents considering multi-state pass-through structures:
- Prefer S corp structure over partnership IF multi-state PTET exposure likely
- Model PTET benefit against PA double-tax risk for partnership ownership
- Document residency carefully (domicile change to PTET state may avoid issue)
- Monitor SB 660 or similar legislation

### Philadelphia Business Income and Receipts Tax (BIRT)

For Priceless clients with Philadelphia business activity:

**Two components of BIRT**:
1. **Gross Receipts component**: 0.1415% of Philadelphia gross receipts (no reduction for expenses)
2. **Net Income component**: 5.99% of Philadelphia net income

**Nexus**:
- Any business activity within Philadelphia
- Includes real estate in Philadelphia
- Includes services performed in Philadelphia
- Remote workers: if employee has permanent work location in Philadelphia, employer has BIRT nexus

**Apportionment** (for multi-location businesses):
- Three-factor: sales, property, payroll (equal weighting for pre-2016; double-weighted sales thereafter)

**Filing**:
- Philadelphia Business Income and Receipts Tax Return
- Due: April 15 (annual); extensions available
- Estimated payments quarterly

**Exemption**: $100,000 of gross receipts exempted for first-time filers and small businesses (applies to net income component only)

## Real estate considerations

### PA Realty Transfer Tax
**State rate**: 1% of consideration
**Local/Municipal**: typically additional 1% (combined 2% typical)
**Philadelphia**: 4.278% combined (state 1% + city 3.278%) — one of highest in nation

### Property tax
**Statewide average effective rate**: ~1.5% of market value
**Philadelphia**: 0.95% effective (lower than suburbs due to valuation practices)
**Main Line (Montgomery, Chester, Delaware counties)**: 1.5-2.2% effective
**Allegheny County (Pittsburgh)**: 2.0-2.3% effective

**Homestead exemption**: PA has limited statewide homestead exemption ($45,000 off assessed value reduction for primary residence; varies by school district)
**Senior citizen rebate program**: PA Property Tax/Rent Rebate (income-limited)

### §1031 Exchanges
PA does NOT fully conform to federal §1031 at PIT level:
- Pre-2023: PA did NOT recognize §1031 for PIT purposes; required immediate recognition
- **Act 53 of 2022**: PA now allows §1031 for PIT tax years beginning after 2022 for qualifying like-kind exchanges of real property
- Major recent reform; PA now conforms

## Estate, inheritance, and gift taxes

### PA Inheritance Tax (critical — PA has NO estate tax but DOES have inheritance tax)

**Authority**: 72 P.S. §9101 et seq.

**Rates depend on beneficiary relationship**:
- **0%** — Spouse
- **0%** — Child age 21 or younger
- **4.5%** — Lineal descendants (children 22+, grandchildren, parents, grandparents)
- **12%** — Siblings
- **15%** — Other beneficiaries (nieces, nephews, friends, unrelated persons)
- **0%** — Charities and exempt organizations

**Tax on transfers at death** (not inter vivos gifts generally):
- 3-year look-back for gifts (gifts within 3 years of death added back)

**Filing**: Form REV-1500
**Due**: 9 months from date of death

### Federal estate tax
$15M exemption (post-OBBBA permanent). PA residents face federal-only estate tax; PA has no estate tax.

### Gift tax
PA has no gift tax. But 3-year look-back for inheritance tax purposes (§9107).

### Planning implications for PA clients

PA inheritance tax hits non-direct heirs significantly:
- Sibling inheriting $1M: $120,000 PA inheritance tax
- Friend inheriting $1M: $150,000 PA inheritance tax
- Spouse inheriting $1M: $0 PA inheritance tax
- Child inheriting $1M: $45,000 PA inheritance tax

**Priceless planning for PA residents**:
- Direct bequest strategies (skip non-direct heirs via trusts if possible)
- Lifetime gifting (PA has no gift tax — major advantage for wealth transfer)
- Irrevocable trusts for collateral beneficiaries
- Charitable bequests (0% rate) for portion of estate
- Pre-death transfers beyond 3-year window

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 6%
**Local additions**: 
- Philadelphia: 2% additional (8% combined)
- Allegheny County (Pittsburgh): 1% additional (7% combined)
- Most other counties: no additional (6% only)

**Economic nexus (Wayfair)**:
- $100,000 in PA sales in prior 12 months

- Clothing: GENERALLY exempt (except sports apparel, ski gear, etc.) — unusual PA rule
- Groceries: exempt

## Multi-state mechanics

### PA resident with out-of-state income
- PA taxes worldwide income at 3.07%
- Resident credit for taxes paid to other states (per 72 P.S. §7314)
- **CRITICAL**: Credit available for S corp PTET paid in other state; credit NOT available for partnership PTET paid in other state (per PA DOR position; SB 660 would fix)

### Non-resident with PA-source income
- Form PA-40 (non-resident)
- PA-source: services performed in PA, real estate in PA, PA business allocation
- Philadelphia BIRT if Philadelphia activity

### Part-year residents
- Form PA-40 with allocations
- Pro-ration based on residency period

### Philadelphia wage tax (for non-residents working in Philadelphia)
- 3.44% for non-residents (vs. 3.7483% for residents)
- Applies regardless of residence state
- PA residents get credit; NJ residents get NJ credit; NY residents get NY credit
- Wayne v. Philadelphia Revenue Dept. — constitutional challenges historically rejected
