# New York (NY) — State Tax File

**Among the most complex state tax environments in the US.** New York imposes state income tax PLUS NYC residents pay city income tax, creating a 14.776% combined top marginal rate. NY is the second-largest origin state for Priceless clients relocating to Florida, and is highly aggressive on residency audits.

## Quick reference

- **NY State top rate**: 10.9% (income > $25M; high-income surcharge brackets extended through 2032 via FY 2026 budget)
- **NYC additional**: 3.078% to 3.876% on top of state (Yonkers also adds a percentage)
- **Combined NYC top**: 14.776% (10.9% + 3.876%)
- **9 NY State brackets**; rates 4% / 4.5% / 5.25% / 5.5% / 6% / 6.85% / 9.65% / 10.3% / 10.9%
- **NYC brackets**: 4 brackets, 3.078% to 3.876%
- **PTET available**: NY State (Article 24-A) AND NYC (Article 24-B); March 15 election deadline (HARD deadline; no extension)
- **Standard deduction**: $8,000 single / $16,050 MFJ (2026; verified via Tax Foundation Feb 2026 snapshot; check NYS DTF for subsequent updates)
- **Sales tax**: 4% state + local up to ~4.875% (NYC: 8.875% combined)
- **NY State estate tax**: separate from federal; $7.16M exemption (2025); cliff at 105%
- **Capital gains**: taxed as ordinary income (no preferential rate)
- **Convenience of the employer rule** (CRITICAL for remote workers)
- **Tax benefit recapture** for high-income (effectively flat-rates the higher brackets)
- **OBBBA non-conformity for bonus depreciation** (NY decouples from §168(k))
- **OBBBA conformity for QSBS** (NY follows §1202)

For Priceless purposes: NY requires careful state planning. NYC residents face the highest combined US state-and-local rate. PTET is critical for pass-through owners. Residency planning for departures is high-stakes and high-audit.

| 2 | $8,501 - $11,700 | 4.5% |
| 3 | $11,701 - $13,900 | 5.25% |
| 4 | $13,901 - $80,650 | 5.5% |
| 5 | $80,651 - $215,400 | 6.0% |
| 6 | $215,401 - $1,077,550 | 6.85% |
| 7 | $1,077,551 - $5,000,000 | 9.65% |
| 8 | $5,000,001 - $25,000,000 | 10.3% |

For MFJ, brackets are roughly 2x single thresholds.

### NYC brackets (residents only)

| Bracket | Income Range | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0 - $12,000 | 3.078% |
| 2 | $12,001 - $25,000 | 3.762% |
| 3 | $25,001 - $50,000 | 3.819% |
| 4 | $50,001+ | 3.876% |

NYC tax is on top of state tax. Calculated together on NY State return (Form IT-201) for residents.

### Yonkers tax

Yonkers residents pay an additional ~16.75% of NY State tax liability as Yonkers tax. Significant additional burden for Yonkers-resident high earners.

### Tax benefit recapture (the "hidden flat tax")

For high-income filers (AGI above $107,650 single / $161,500 MFJ), NY recaptures the tax benefit of LOWER bracket rates. Effectively, high earners pay the top marginal rate on ALL income, not just the top portion.

This makes NY's top brackets effectively flat rates for high-income filers. A NYC client at $5M income effectively pays approximately 14.776% on the full $5M, not just on the portion above $5M.

This is a critical point that simple bracket calculations miss. Priceless must use NY's "tax tables" or full IT-201 instructions for any client above the recapture threshold.

### Standard deduction (2026)

- Single: $8,000
- Single (claimed as dependent): $3,100
- MFJ: $16,050
- HoH: $11,200
- MFS: $8,000

NY's standard deduction is much lower than federal ($16,100 single / $32,200 MFJ for 2026 post-OBBBA). High-AGI clients typically itemize on NY.

### Personal exemption

NY allows a $1,000 dependent exemption per qualifying dependent. No taxpayer/spouse personal exemption (similar to federal).

### Itemized deductions

NY itemized deduction structure differs from federal:
- **Property taxes**: fully deductible at state level (no SALT cap at state)
- **Income tax paid to other states**: claimable if not deducted at federal
- **Mortgage interest**: conforms generally
- **Charitable contributions**: generally conforms
- **Miscellaneous deductions**: NY-specific rules

For NY itemizers above the SALT cap impact at federal: significant federal-state difference in deductible amounts.

## Capital gains taxation

**NY taxes capital gains as ORDINARY INCOME.** No preferential rate.

For NYC top-bracket: 14.776% on long-term capital gains
Combined with federal: 23.8% federal + 14.776% NY/NYC = 38.576% combined on long-term gains for NYC top earner

This is one of the highest combined rates in the country and a primary driver of pre-exit residency planning for NYC clients.

## OBBBA conformity (NY-specific)

NY's federal conformity is **selective and complex**. Some OBBBA provisions flow through to NY; others do not.

| OBBBA Provision | Federal Treatment | NY Treatment | Practical Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% bonus depreciation (post-1/19/2025) | Permanent | DOES NOT CONFORM (long-standing decoupling) | Federal/state depreciation differences; separate schedules required |
| §199A QBI permanent | 20% deduction permanent | DOES NOT CONFORM | NY has no QBI; pass-through K-1 fully NY-taxable |
| §163(j) interest limitation back to EBITDA | EBITDA basis | NY conforms (per CalCPA / multistate analyses) | C corps benefit from increased deductibility |
| §174 R&D domestic immediate expensing | Restored permanent | NY generally conforms (verify per industry analyses) | NY follows federal R&D treatment generally |
| §1202 QSBS expanded ($15M cap) | $15M cap, tiered | NY CONFORMS to §1202 | NY residents get full federal QSBS exclusion at state level — major advantage vs. CA |
| QOZ permanent + Dec 31 2031 trigger | Permanent | NY generally conforms but with state-level differences on appreciation | Verify case by case |
| §250 GILTI deduction | Continued | NY corporate framework follows federal generally | C corps with CFC income |
| §179 expensing $2.5M / $4M phase-out | $2.5M / $4M | NY conforms to federal §179 | Higher §179 limit at state level |

### Critical NY non-conformities

- **Bonus depreciation**: NY does not allow §168(k) bonus depreciation. NY requires straight-line MACRS depreciation for state purposes.
- **NY S corp election**: separate from federal S election; state S corp election required (Form CT-6)
- **Federal NOL changes**: NY has its own NOL regime

## NY State and NYC PTET (Article 24-A and Article 24-B)

NY's PTET is one of the most-utilized SALT cap workarounds because of the high marginal rates. Critical for Priceless NY pass-through clients.

### NY State PTET (Article 24-A) — basics

- **Effective**: tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021
- **Eligible entities**: partnerships, NY S corporations, LLCs treated as partnerships (NOT single-member LLCs)
- **Rate structure**: graduated based on PTE taxable income
  - $0 - $2,000,000: 6.85%
  - $2M - $5M: 9.65%
  - $5M - $25M: 10.3%
  - $25M+: 10.9%
- **Owner credit**: receives state tax credit equal to PTET paid on owner's share

### NYC PTET (Article 24-B) — basics

- **Effective**: tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2022
- **Eligible entities**: NY State PTET-eligible entities with NYC-resident owners
- **Rate**: 3.876% (NYC top resident rate)
- **Coordinated with NY State PTET**: separate election but typically made together

### Election deadlines (CRITICAL)

**NY State PTET election deadline**: March 15 of the tax year (HARD deadline; no extension)
- For 2026 tax year: must elect by March 16, 2026 (March 15 was Sunday)
- NY does NOT allow extension of election deadline (proposed multiple times; not enacted)
- Once made, election is irrevocable after first estimated payment due date

**NYC PTET election deadline**: same as state (March 15)

This March 15 deadline is one of the strictest in the country. Most states allow PTET election through tax filing deadline (or extended). NY's stricter deadline requires proactive Q1 planning.

### Estimated payments

PTET requires QUARTERLY estimated payments:
- March 15 (Q1)
- June 15 (Q2)
- September 15 (Q3)
- December 15 (Q4)

Underpayment penalties apply.

### Election process

- Must be made through entity's NY State Online Services account
- Must be made by AUTHORIZED PERSON of entity (not tax advisor)
- Cannot be revoked after first estimated payment due date

### Math example (NYC client benefit)

NYC S Corp owner with $1.5M K-1 income:

WITHOUT PTET:
- NY State + NYC personal tax: $1.5M × ~14.776% effective = ~$222K
- Federal SALT cap limits deduction (capped at $40,400 in 2026)
- Federal benefit on state tax: only $40,400 × 37% = $14,948

WITH PTET (state + city):
- NY State PTET on $1.5M: ~$144K (tiered rate calc)
- NYC PTET on $1.5M: ~$58K (3.876%)
- Total entity-level PTET: ~$202K
- Federal deduction for PTET: $202K
- Federal tax saved: $202K × 37% = $74,740
- Owner gets $202K credit on personal NY return → no additional state/city personal tax owed

Net incremental federal benefit from PTET vs. no PTET: ~$60K annually for this client.

### Multi-state PTET coordination

For clients with NY + other states pass-through income:
- Each state's PTET applies to its sourced income
- Coordinate elections across jurisdictions
- Apportionment determines PTET base
- See `tax-strategy/strategies/PTET-ELECTION-BY-STATE.md`

## NY State estate tax (significant Priceless concern)

NY State has its own estate tax with significant divergence from federal:

### Exemption amount
- 2025: $7,160,000 (verify 2026 amount; indexed annually)
- Federal: $15M post-OBBBA
- Difference: ~$7.84M of estate not subject to federal but IS subject to NY

### The "cliff" provision (CRITICAL)

NY estate tax has a CLIFF: if estate exceeds 105% of exemption, the entire estate becomes taxable from $0 (not just the excess).

For 2025 with $7.16M exemption:
- Estate $7.16M: NY estate tax $0
- Estate $7.518M (105% of exemption): NY estate tax STILL $0 by statute
- Estate $7.519M: full estate ($7.519M) subject to NY estate tax (no exemption sheltering anything)
- Effective marginal rate within cliff zone: extreme

### Rates

- Graduated: 3.06% to 16.0%
- Top rate at $10M+ estate

### Planning implications

For NY residents with estates approaching $7M:
- Aggressive use of $19K annual gift exclusion
- Lifetime gifts (federal exemption available but NY has no state gift tax)
- Charitable planning (CRT, foundation, DAF)
- Life insurance trusts
- Estate freezes

The NY estate tax cliff makes estate size management for NY residents far more important than for FL residents (where $15M federal-only applies).

For Priceless NY estate planning clients: this is consistently one of the highest-value planning conversations. See `capital-deployment/CRT-CRUT.md`, `capital-deployment/CLT.md`, `capital-deployment/PRIVATE-FOUNDATION.md`.

## Residency rules (NY's aggressive framework)

NY uses a TWO-PRONG test for residency:

### Prong 1: Statutory Residency

Individual is statutory resident if:
- Maintains a "permanent place of abode" in NY for substantially all of the tax year, AND
- Spends more than 183 days in NY during the tax year

Note: this is the 183-day rule. Different from CA's 9-month rule.

### Prong 2: Domicile

Individual is domiciliary resident if NY is their domicile (true permanent home).

Either prong creates NY residency.

### "Permanent place of abode" definition

Per Matter of Gaied v. NY State Tax Appeals Tribunal (2014):
- Must be a residence the taxpayer maintains and uses
- Includes second homes, vacation homes
- Apartment lease, ownership, or maintenance
- Even if rarely used, can constitute "permanent place of abode"

### Day-counting

NY counts ANY presence in NY as a "day":
- Even brief presence (e.g., catching a flight at JFK) typically counts
- Must be a "physical presence" but threshold is low
- Travel days through NY (not stopping) generally don't count
- Day-counting accuracy is critical for borderline cases

### NYC residency

Separate analysis for NYC residency. Same statutory framework but applied to "in NYC" rather than "in NY State."

For statutory residency in NYC: maintain permanent place of abode in NYC + 183+ days in NYC.

This means individuals can be NY State resident but not NYC resident (e.g., live in Westchester, work in NYC). NYC residency creates additional 3-3.876% tax obligation.

### The "Convenience of the Employer" Rule (CRITICAL for remote workers)

20 NYCRR §132.18 — NY's most controversial source rule:

If individual works for NY employer but performs services REMOTELY from outside NY:
- Income is NY-source UNLESS the work outside NY is for the employer's CONVENIENCE (not employee's)
- "Bona fide employer office" outside NY requires substantial business reasons
- NY interprets aggressively — most remote work for NY employers remains NY-source

Practical implication: employee living in CT/NJ/FL/anywhere working remotely for NY-based employer typically owes NY tax on those wages.

Multiple states impose similar rules (Connecticut, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Nebraska, Arkansas, New Jersey for some). For NY workers who relocated during COVID: NY continued asserting NY-source status on most wages.

For Priceless clients in this situation:
- Document employer's reason for permitting remote work
- Establish "bona fide" employer office outside NY if possible (requires substantial business presence)
- Negotiate explicit employer requirement (rather than employee preference)
- Consider whether the federal credit for taxes paid to other states fully offsets

### NY residency audit posture

NY's residency audit team is among the most aggressive in the country:
- Specific high-net-worth audit unit
- Detailed record requests (cell phone records, credit card statements, EZ-Pass records)
- Physical day-counting verification
- Look-back periods of 6+ years common
- Pursues collection across state lines

For Priceless clients leaving NY:
- Document the move EXTENSIVELY
- Sell or convert NY residence (residence retained = significant audit risk)
- Day-counting discipline (Monaeo or similar app)
- Engage NY residency specialist for the transition year
- Expect audit; build the file from day one

### Common Priceless residency scenarios for NY

**Scenario A: NYC tech executive moving to FL**

- Year 1 (move year): part-year NYC + NY State resident
- File IT-201 for NY part-year + IT-203 for NY non-resident period
- Allocate income carefully
- Establish FL domicile per FL playbook
- Maintain audit defense file
- Year 2+: NY non-resident return only if continuing NY-source income (e.g., NYC-based S Corp)

**Scenario B: NYC business owner with continued NY operations**

Owner moves to FL but business operations remain in NY:
- Personal residency: FL (no NY State personal income tax on non-NY-source income)
- Business S Corp K-1: NY-source (business operates in NY)
- File NY non-resident return on NY-source K-1 income
- May elect NY State PTET at entity level
- Net: FL eliminates personal-level NY tax on non-NY-source income; PTET captures federal benefit on NY business tax

**Scenario C: NY snowbird splitting time**

Individual splits time NYC and FL:
- Risk: maintains NYC apartment + spends 183+ days in NY = statutory residency
- Risk: Convenience of employer rule on remote NY work
- Solution: stay under 183 days in NY (track carefully); reduce NY presence

## Sales tax

### Rates

- **State rate**: 4%
- **Local rates**: vary by county/city, up to ~4.875%
- **NYC combined**: 8.875% (4% state + 4.5% NYC + 0.375% MCTD)
- **Statewide range**: 7% to 8.875%

### MTA Mobility Tax

- Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax (MCTMT)
- Applies to employers in NYC and surrounding counties
- Self-employed and pass-through business owners may also be subject
- 2025 changes: rate increases for NYC employers; self-employed under $150K eliminated as of January 1, 2026

### Sales tax base

NY taxes:
- Tangible personal property
- Some services (limited)
- NOT food for home consumption
- NOT prescription drugs

### SaaS treatment

NY generally taxes SaaS as a sale of tangible personal property (electronically delivered software). This is OPPOSITE of CA. For Priceless software/SaaS clients with NY customers: sales tax obligation likely.

### Wayfair / nexus

NY economic nexus threshold:
- $500,000 in sales of tangible personal property delivered into NY in prior 4 quarters AND
- More than 100 transactions

Marketplace facilitator rules apply.

## Corporate tax

NY State Article 9-A franchise tax:
- 6.5% rate for most corporations
- Higher rate (7.25%) for corporations with > $5M business income
- Capital base alternative tax
- NYC adds NYC General Corporation Tax (8.85% on net income, with various adjustments)

NY S corporations:
- Federal S election + separate NY State S election (Form CT-6) required
- Without state election: NY treats as C corporation regardless of federal
- Common Priceless trap: client makes federal S election but not NY S election; NY tax double on entity + personal

## Industry-specific notes

### Real estate

- NY State and NYC transfer taxes (NY State 0.4% + NYC 1% + 1.425% mansion tax over $1M)
- NYC real property transfer tax: 1% on residential under $500K; 1.425% over $500K
- Mansion tax: additional 1% on $1M+ residential; tiered up to 3.9% on $25M+
- §1031 exchange: NY conforms; both federal and NY defer

### Software / AI

- §1202 QSBS exclusion at state level (favorable vs. CA which doesn't conform)
- R&D credit available
- Stock-based comp: NY-source for services performed in NY
- Convenience of employer rule key for remote tech workers

### Doctors / medical

- All income taxed at state/city brackets up to 14.776%
- NY State + NYC PTET valuable for practice S corps
- Solo 401(k) and DB plan contributions deductible at state level

### E-commerce

- NY sales tax compliance complex
- Wayfair-triggered nexus for >$500K + 100 transactions
- SaaS taxation may apply

## State and local credits

NY offers numerous credits but most narrowly targeted:

- **NY State R&D Credit**: limited; corporate income tax credit
- **Excelsior Jobs Program**: discretionary; for qualifying businesses
- **Low-Income Housing Credit**: federal coordination
- **Brownfield Redevelopment Credit**
- **Film Production Credit**: significant for entertainment clients
- **NYC R&D Credit**: separate from state credit; NYC-specific

For Priceless clients: R&D credit (state and NYC) is most commonly relevant. Coordinate with `industries/SOFTWARE-AI.md`.

## Common Priceless engagement scenarios

### Scenario A: NYC Full-Wealth client business sale

Client: NYC founder selling tech business for $20M (basis $200K).

Federal: §1202 QSBS exclusion $15M + $5M residual gain × 23.8% (LTCG + NIIT) = $1,190,000 federal
NY/NYC: $20M × 14.776% effective for top bracket = $2,955,200 (NY conforms to §1202 — verify; some practitioners debate exact treatment)

Wait — NY conforms to §1202: $15M excluded at NY level too; remaining $5M × 14.776% = $738,800

Combined tax: $1,190,000 federal + $738,800 NY/NYC = $1,928,800

Compared to FL resident: $1,190,000 federal + $0 state = $1,190,000

NYC cost: $738,800 differential

Pre-exit FL residency planning saves ~$738,800. Substantial; justifies relocation planning effort.

### Scenario B: NY S Corp owner $1M K-1 considering PTET

Client: NY S Corp $1M K-1, NYC resident, top brackets.

WITHOUT PTET:
- NY State + NYC personal tax: ~$148K (effective ~14.776%)
- Federal SALT cap: $40,400 max deduction
- Federal benefit on state/city tax paid: ~$15K

WITH NY State + NYC PTET:
- Entity-level state PTET on $1M: ~$80K (graduated)
- Entity-level city PTET on $1M: $38,760 (3.876%)
- Total entity-level: ~$119K
- Federal deduction: $119K → federal tax saved $119K × 37% = $44,030
- Owner gets $119K credit on NY personal return; no additional NY/NYC personal tax

Net additional federal benefit from PTET: $44,030 - $15,000 = ~$29,000

Annual benefit. Implement annually; March 15 deadline crucial.

### Scenario C: NY estate planning client at $9M estate

Client: 65-year-old NYC professional, estate $9M (residence $3M, investments $5M, retirement $1M).

Federal estate tax exposure: $0 ($9M < $15M exemption)
NY estate tax exposure: SIGNIFICANT
- Exemption $7.16M
- Cliff at 105% = $7.518M
- Estate $9M: above cliff → entire $9M subject to NY estate tax
- NY estate tax on $9M: approximately $1.04M (per NY rate schedule)

Strategies:
- Reduce estate to under $7.16M: $1.84M reduction needed
- Annual gifting: $19K × beneficiaries
- Lifetime gifting (federal exemption $15M; NY no gift tax)
- Charitable strategies (CRT moves assets out of estate)
- Life insurance trust (ILIT) to provide liquidity outside estate
- Estate-freezing techniques

Move to FL: eliminates NY estate tax on personal estate; only federal estate tax applies (which is $0 for $9M estate). Single biggest savings strategy.

### Scenario D: Remote NY tech worker living in CT

Client: NY-based startup employee, $500K compensation, lives in CT (no NY apartment).

Convenience of Employer analysis:
- Employer is NY-based
- Employee works from CT
- Employer's "bona fide office" in CT? Generally no (small startup; no CT office)
- Default: NY treats as NY-source income

Tax exposure:
- NY non-resident tax on $500K wages (despite living in CT)
- CT also taxes (resident)
- Federal credit for taxes paid to other state mitigates double tax (CT typically gets credit for NY tax)

Solutions:
- Negotiate employer office in CT (substantive presence)
- Move to a state with no income tax (FL eliminates CT layer too)
- Document business reason for remote work (employer's convenience, not employee's preference)

Common Priceless engagement: this analysis for clients across the NY metro area.

## Audit posture (NY — among most aggressive)

NY State Department of Taxation and Finance (DTF) is one of the most aggressive state tax authorities:
- Specific residency audit team
- Cross-references federal data extensively
- Subpoenas cell phone records and credit card statements
- Look-back periods of 6+ years standard
- Pursues collection aggressively

Common audit triggers:
- High-income exit from NY
- Statutory residency challenges (permanent place of abode + 183 days)
- Convenience of employer rule disputes
- PTET election timing/payment errors
- NY S corp election failures
- Estate tax cliff issues
- Sales tax nexus and compliance for remote sellers

Statute of limitations:
- Standard: 3 years from return due date
- 6 years if substantial omission
- Indefinite if no return filed
- Separate analysis for residency audits (often longer effective look-back)
