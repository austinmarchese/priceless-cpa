# Minnesota (MN) — State Tax File

Minnesota has **graduated income tax with top rate 9.85%** — among highest in the US. Net Investment Income Tax (NII Tax) of 1% on investment income > $1M (effective 2024). **PTET regime EXPIRED December 31, 2025** — this is a critical planning issue; re-enactment legislation pending in 2025-2026 legislative session. Corporate rate 9.8% (also high). Retail delivery fee of $0.50 per transaction (unusual).

Low Priceless concentration. Some Twin Cities clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 4 brackets; top rate **9.85%** (on MFJ income > $304,970 approximate for 2025; inflation-adjusted 2.369% for 2026)
- **Corporate income tax**: **9.8% flat** (one of highest in US)
- **Net Investment Income Tax (NII)**: **1% on investment income > $1M** (individuals, estates, trusts); effective 2024 (H.F. 1938)
- **PTET**: **EXPIRED 12/31/2025**; re-enactment legislation pending (HF 3127/SF 3405)
- **Sales tax**: 6.875% state; local up to 1.5% (combined up to 8.375%); **Metro regional surcharge 0.75%** in 7-county metro (Minneapolis/St. Paul); **Retail delivery fee $0.50** per transaction
- **Estate tax**: YES — $3M exemption (unchanged for years); rates 13-16%
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Partial exemption (phased by income); NOT fully exempt
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### Net Investment Income Tax (NII) — 1%

Minnesota enacted (H.F. 1938, effective 2024) a **1% surtax on investment income above $1 million**:
- Applies to: Individuals, estates, and trusts
- Threshold: $1,000,000 of investment income (not AGI — investment income specifically)
- Includes: Interest, dividends, capital gains, rental income, royalties, pass-through investment income
- **Does NOT apply**: Active business income

**For Priceless HNW clients**: MN NII tax adds 1% on top of 9.85% top income rate for investment income over $1M. Combined rate on investment income > $1M: **10.85%** (state only, before federal).

### Social Security

Unlike many states, Minnesota **partially taxes** Social Security benefits:
- Full exemption if MFJ AGI < $105,380 (2025 threshold)
- Partial phase-out for MFJ AGI $105,380 - $142,150
- **Taxed at full rate** for MFJ AGI > $142,150
- Similar phase-out for single filers at lower thresholds

MN is less retirement-friendly than many states due to Social Security taxation above modest income thresholds.

### Capital gains

Capital gains taxed as ordinary income at 5.35% - 9.85% + potential 1% NII tax. **Top effective rate on investment gains > $1M: 10.85%**.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) — EXPIRED DECEMBER 31, 2025

### Critical status alert

Minnesota's PTET regime **EXPIRED for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025**. This is confirmed by MDOR notice published November 21, 2025.

**Implications**:
- No PTET election can be made for 2026 or later calendar-year tax returns
- Late elections for 2025 (fiscal year filers) still accepted if made by extended due date
- **Credit for PTET paid to other states ALSO expired** — MN residents cannot claim credit against MN tax for PTET paid to NY, CA, etc., for TY 2026+
- Historic PTET credits claimed on prior returns remain valid

### Re-enactment legislation pending

H.F. 3127 (Davids) / S.F. 3405 (Rest) introduced in 94th Legislature (2025-2026 session) would:
- **Re-enact PTET retroactively to January 1, 2026**
- Extend expiration through 2027 (or 2029 per some versions)
- Restore credit for PTE tax paid to other states
- Address conformity questions with federal treatment

**As of April 2026, enactment status**: Bills in committee/introduction; not yet signed into law. This is a significant planning issue — Priceless clients with MN exposure need to plan for BOTH scenarios (PTET available vs. PTET expired) for TY 2026.

### Historic PTET mechanics (for pre-2026 tax years)

- **Rate**: 9.85% (top MN individual rate)
- **Tax base**: MN portion of business income tax base (S corp: MN source; partnerships: all income for resident owners from 2023 forward)
- **Election**: Annual; 50%+ ownership consent required; made by extended due date (for 2025 calendar filers: September 15, 2026)
- **Refundable credit**: To owners
- **Schedule PTE-RP**: Resident partner schedule (starting 2023)

### Impact for Priceless clients

If MN PTET is NOT re-enacted for 2026:
- MN pass-through income flows through to owners at personal level
- OBBBA SALT cap ($40K for 2026 for MAGI < $500K; reverts to $10K at MAGI > $600K) limits federal deduction
- For high-income MN pass-through owners: loss of PTET significantly raises effective federal tax on MN income

If re-enactment passes:
- Status quo; PTET election available with same 9.85% rate

**Priceless action**: Monitor HF 3127/SF 3405 status. Make 2025 PTET elections (last eligible year) aggressively for existing clients. Model both scenarios for TY 2026 planning.

## Corporate income tax

- **Rate: 9.8% flat** (among highest in US; only CA 8.84% + CT 7.5%+10%surtax are comparable for combined effective rate)
- **Base**: Federal taxable income with MN modifications
- **GILTI**: Subject to tax (H.F. 1938 change 2023; MN taxes GILTI unlike most states)
- **Dividends received deduction**: Reduced percentages per H.F. 1938
- **NOL limit**: 70% of taxable income (reduced from 80% per federal; H.F. 1938)
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor
- **Combined filing**: Required for unitary business groups (since 1982)

## OBBBA conformity

Minnesota uses **static-date conformity** to federal IRC. Each year legislature must update conformity date. For TY 2025, conformity is typically to IRC as of December 2024 (before OBBBA enactment July 2025). This means OBBBA provisions do NOT automatically flow through.

| OBBBA Provision | Federal Status | MN Conformity | Planning Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| §174 domestic R&E immediate expensing | Restored permanent | **MN likely NOT conform** until legislature updates date | Requires amortization on MN return |
| 100% bonus depreciation post-1/19/2025 | Restored permanent | **MN generally DECOUPLES** regardless | MN requires MACRS |
| §179 expensing | Enhanced | MN conforms to pre-OBBBA §179 limits (or reduces) | |
| §1202 QSBS | Enhanced ($15M) | MN conforms pre-OBBBA level (or not at all) | Possibly limited |
| TIPS/overtime/car loan interest deductions | Deductible federally | **MN NOT conform** — requires addback | |
| QBI (§199A) | Permanent | **MN NOT conform** | QBI federal-only |
| SALT cap | $40K (2026) | Personal level for MN | PTET (if re-enacted) mitigates; otherwise no workaround |

**Priceless action**: For MN pass-through clients, verify all OBBBA federal deductions are added back on MN return. This is material.

## Minnesota estate tax

Minnesota is one of 12 states with estate tax:
- **Exemption: $3,000,000** (NOT indexed for inflation; unchanged for years)
- **Rates**: Graduated 13% to 16%
- **Applies to**: MN residents' worldwide estates; nonresidents' MN-situs property
- **Portability**: MN DOES NOT have portability between spouses
- **Maine-like gap**: MN $3M exemption vs. federal $15M creates significant planning gap

### Rate structure (simplified)

| Taxable Estate | Rate (approximate) |
|---|---|
| $3,000,001 - $7,100,000 | 13% |
| $7,100,001 - $8,100,000 | 13.6% |
| Above $8,100,000 | 16% |

### Worked example — $10M MN estate

- Taxable: $10M - $3M = $7M
- Estate tax: approximately $910,000-$1,000,000 (13-14% effective) [VERIFY exact calculation]

This is a significant Priceless planning consideration for MN HNW clients.

## Sales and use tax

- **State rate: 6.875%**
- **Local**: Up to 1.5%; combined max ~8.375%

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic via federal (6 months)
- **Payment**: Must pay 90% by April 15
- **Forms**: M1 (resident); M1NR (nonresident); M3 (partnership); M8 (S corp); M2 (trust/estate)
- **Estimated payments**: Quarterly; safe harbor 100% prior (110% if AGI > $150K) or 90% current
- **E-file mandatory**: For preparers filing > specified threshold

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: MN S corp owner (high-income)
**Setup**: MN resident; S corp with $1.5M income; one shareholder (MN resident).

**2025 with PTET (last year available)**:
- PTET: $1.5M × 9.85% = **$147,750** at entity level
- Federal SALT deduction at entity level: $147,750 × 37% = $54,668 federal savings
- Owner gets $147,750 refundable credit

**2026 without PTET (if re-enactment fails)**:
- Owner pays $147,750 on personal MN return (4 brackets to 9.85%)
- OBBBA SALT cap: If MAGI > $600K, only $10K federally deductible → $137,750 × 37% = **$50,968 in lost federal deductions**
- Net effective additional federal cost: ~$50K/year

**Priceless action**: If 2026 PTET not re-enacted, the loss of PTET election adds ~$50K/year in federal tax for this client. Monitor legislation closely.

### Scenario 2: Investment income above $1M (NII Tax)
**Setup**: MN resident; $2M in portfolio income (dividends + capital gains + interest).

**Analysis**:
- MN ordinary income tax at 9.85% on $2M = $197,000
- **MN NII Tax**: 1% × ($2M - $1M) = **$10,000 additional**
- Combined MN tax on investment income: $207,000 (10.35% effective rate)
- Plus federal: LTCG 20% + NIIT 3.8% = ~23.8% federal on gains portion

**Planning**: For MN HNW clients, consider timing of investment income recognition; investment income under $1M escapes NII. Estate planning to shift investment income to lower-taxed generations.

### Scenario 3: $8M estate with MN residence
**Setup**: MN resident; $8M worldwide estate.

**Federal**: $8M < $15M exemption → $0 federal estate tax.

**MN estate tax**:
- Exemption: $3M
- Taxable: $5M
- Tax: ~$650,000-$800,000 (13-16% marginal) [VERIFY exact]

This is $650K-$800K in MN estate tax where $0 federal. Major planning gap.

**Priceless options**:
- Relocate to no-estate-tax state before death
- Lifetime gifting (MN has no gift tax; federal $19K annual exclusion + lifetime exemption available)
- Trust structures (irrevocable trusts to remove from MN estate)
- Charitable remainder or lead trusts

### Scenario 4: PTET transition planning (2025 last year)
**Setup**: MN S corp with $800K income; election being considered for TY 2025.

**Action**: Make the 2025 election (last year available). File Form PTE with TY 2025 return; election covers only 2025 unless re-enactment passes for 2026+.

**2026 planning**: Monitor HF 3127/SF 3405. If re-enacts, continue PTET. If not, owner pays personally; consider whether to:
- Continue MN residency or relocate (high rate + lost PTET + NII Tax + estate tax)
- Restructure entity (e.g., move to C corp if PTET unavailable and owner in top bracket)
- Shift operations to no-income-tax state
