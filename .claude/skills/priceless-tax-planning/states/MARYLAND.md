# Maryland — State Tax Reference

## The essentials

**Maryland made sweeping tax changes in May 2025** via the Budget Reconciliation and Financing Act of 2025 (HB 352, signed by Gov. Wes Moore May 20, 2025). The changes are **retroactive to January 1, 2025** and materially reshape Maryland's tax landscape:

1. **Two new personal income tax brackets**: 6.25% ($500K-$1M; $600K-$1.2M MFJ); 6.50% (above $1M; above $1.2M MFJ)
2. **NEW 2% capital gains surtax** on taxpayers with federal AGI > $350,000 (permanent; applies to Maryland net capital gain)
3. **Major PTET restructuring**: Effective 1/1/2026, MD resident owners of electing PTEs are taxed on their **FULL distributive share** (not just MD-source) — split calculation similar to CA
4. **3% Tech Tax**: New sales tax on data and IT services under NAICS codes 518, 519, 5132, 5415 (effective July 1, 2025)
5. **Itemized deduction phase-out**: 7.5% reduction for each dollar of federal AGI over $200K (single) / $100K (MFS)

Combined Maryland state + local top rate now reaches **9.80%** for top-bracket Montgomery County residents (6.5% state + 3.30% maximum local).

For Priceless, MD clients typically need: (1) urgent 2025-year attention due to retroactive rate changes + capital gains surtax, (2) PTET election analysis including the new CA-style split calculation for S corporations (federal single-class-of-stock risk for mixed-residency S corps), (3) relocation analysis for high-income clients given the expanded tax burden, and (4) sales tax compliance for technology service businesses.

### Local "piggyback" tax (unique Maryland feature)

Every Maryland county imposes a local income tax added to state tax (piggyback):
- **Maximum rate (2026)**: 3.30% (Dorchester County increased to 3.30% for TY 2025+)
- **Montgomery County**: 3.20%
- **Baltimore City**: 3.20%
- **Howard County**: 3.20%
- **Baltimore County**: 3.20%
- **Most counties**: 2.75-3.20%

**Combined state + local top marginal rate**:
- **9.80% (6.5% state + 3.3% local max)** for highest-income Montgomery County-area residents
- This is comparable to NY/NJ high-tax states

### 2% Capital Gains Surtax (NEW 2025 — PERMANENT)

**Trigger**: Federal AGI > $350,000 (same threshold for all filing statuses — single, MFJ, HoH)
**Rate**: 2% of Maryland net capital gain included in MD AGI
**Effective**: Tax years beginning after December 31, 2024 (retroactive)

**Combined capital gains top rate for MD residents**:
- 6.5% state + 3.3% local + 2% surtax = **11.80%** at top bracket
- Plus federal 20% LTCG + 3.8% NIIT + (4% for high-income) = ~27.8-31.8% federal
- **All-in top capital gains rate: approximately 39.6% federal + state combined**

**Surtax EXEMPTIONS**:
- Gains from sale of primary residence sold for ≤ $1.5M
- Assets held in retirement accounts (IRAs, 401(k), defined contribution plans)
- Deferred compensation plans
- **§179 property** (trade/business property deductible under §179)

**Form 502CG** (new): Calculates the 2% surtax

**Planning implication**: The §179 carveout creates asymmetric treatment — two similarly-situated taxpayers may be treated differently based on whether their business assets were §179-expensed vs. otherwise depreciated. Venable notes: "In theory, two taxpayers holding the same asset could be treated differently based on their taxable incomes."

### Standard deduction (2025+ per HB 352)

**Increased per HB 352** (retroactive):
- Single: $3,350 (up from $2,250)
- MFJ: $6,700 (up from $4,500)

### Itemized deduction phase-out (NEW 2025)

**7.5% reduction of itemized deductions** for federal AGI exceeding:
- $200,000 (single, HoH, MFJ)
- $100,000 (MFS)

**Example**: Federal AGI $300,000 → 7.5% × ($300,000 - $200,000) = $7,500 reduction in MD itemized deductions

### Personal exemption

- $3,200 per taxpayer (phased out for high income per HB 352)
- Phase-out begins AGI $100K single / $150K MFJ
- Fully phased out above AGI $150K single / $200K MFJ

### No retirement income exclusion (except Social Security)

- Social Security: FULLY exempt from MD tax
- Traditional IRA, 401(k), 403(b): taxable as ordinary income
- Military retirement: up to $12,500 exemption for age 55+; additional amounts
- Pensions from public employers: not broadly exempt

**Practical impact**: MD is much less retirement-friendly than GA ($65K) or IL (all retirement).

### Residency rules

Maryland uses domicile + statutory residency:

**Resident**:
- Domiciled in MD, OR
- Physically present in MD for > 183 days during year AND maintained an abode in MD

**Statutory residency** similar to NY's test

**Audit posture**: MD Comptroller does audit residency changes, particularly given the expanded rate structure increases stakes. Less aggressive than NY.

## Business taxes

### Corporate income tax

**Rate**: 8.25% flat (no change from prior years)
**Minimum tax**: $150
**Apportionment**: Single sales factor (since 2022)

### PTET — Maryland Pass-Through Entity Tax (MAJOR 2025 CHANGES)

**Authority**: Md. Code Ann., Tax-Gen. §10-102.1 (enacted 2020; significantly modified by HB 352, 2025)

### Rate

Rate structure (pre-2026):
- 8% on non-individual, non-corporate member income
- Otherwise at the member's applicable rate (individual rates per bracket structure)

**Post-HB 352 (effective for TY 2026+)**: Rate structure updated to reflect new 6.25% and 6.5% brackets.

### MAJOR 2026 CHANGE: Resident vs. Nonresident base calculation

**CRITICAL CHANGE effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025**:

**Pre-2026 rule**: MD PTET applied only to Maryland-source income for all owners, regardless of residency. Resident owners got only partial credit (Maryland portion), losing federal deduction on out-of-state income.

**Post-2026 rule** (per HB 352):
- **Resident owners**: PTE taxable income = full distributive share (all sources, not just MD)
- **Nonresident owners**: PTE taxable income = MD-source income only (unchanged)

**This is a "split calculation" regime similar to California and Arizona.**

### S CORPORATION SECOND-CLASS-OF-STOCK RISK (critical federal tax issue)

**The problem**: For S corporations with mixed MD-resident and non-MD-resident shareholders, the split calculation creates special allocation issues that violate IRC §1361(b)(1)(D) single-class-of-stock requirement.

**Example** (from Venable LLP 2025 analysis):
- S corp 50% MD resident shareholder, 50% non-resident shareholder
- $1M total income; $500K MD-source
- Resident pays PTET on full $500K share = $70 (hypothetical rate)
- Nonresident pays PTET on $250K MD-source share = $30
- Total PTET $100; allocated 50/50 per stock ownership = $50 each
- Resident gets $50 credit but paid $70 in actual burden; nonresident gets $50 credit but only $30 actual burden
- Unless the S corp makes special allocations (violating single-class-of-stock) or special distributions

**Federal S corp risk**: IRS could deem the differential allocation as creating "second class of stock," invalidating the S election.

**Expected fix**: Legislative revision anticipated before implementation:
- Option 1: Limit expanded PTET base to S corps with all-MD-resident shareholders
- Option 2: Make the resident-inclusive calculation elective on S corp basis
- [VERIFY]: Monitor MD General Assembly spring 2026 for clarifying legislation

**Planning action for MD S corps with mixed-residency**:
- DO NOT elect PTET until legislative fix clarified
- Partnership structures more flexible (can make special allocations)
- Consider restructuring mixed-residency S corps to all-MD or all-single-state

### Eligibility

**Eligible PTEs**:
- Partnerships
- S corporations
- LLCs treated as partnerships or S corps
- Business trusts

### Election mechanics

**Method**: Election made on Form 511 (for PTEs paying at entity level)
**Due date**: 15th day of 4th month after tax year end (April 15 for calendar year)
**Can file extension**: Form 510E (6-month extension)

**Annual election**: Made each year

### Credit to owners

**Refundable credit** to qualifying members
**Composite return prohibition**: MD PTET electing entities CANNOT also file composite returns (per MD rules)

### Estimated payments

Required quarterly if liability exceeds $1,000

### Maryland HB 352 PTET resident-source fix

This change addresses a prior planning problem: under the old rules, MD residents paid MD PTET only on MD-source income, but paid MD personal income tax on all income (including out-of-state). Result: partial PTET credit and federal deduction only on MD-source portion.

**Post-2026**: Resident owner's full income through the PTE is eligible for PTET + full federal deduction. This is beneficial — BUT only if S corp second-class-of-stock issue is resolved.

## Real estate considerations

### MD Real Property Transfer Tax
**State rate**: 0.5% of consideration (Md. Code Ann., Tax-Property §13-203)
**County recordation tax**: varies $2-$12 per $1,000 of consideration (adds ~0.2-1.2%)
**Combined Montgomery County**: ~1.8-2.0%
**Baltimore City**: ~3.0%

### Property tax
**Average effective rate**: ~1.05% of market value
**Montgomery County**: 0.95-1.2% effective
**Baltimore City**: 1.6-1.7% effective

**Homestead Credit**: annual assessment increase cap (10% statewide max; some counties lower)

### §1031 Exchanges
MD conforms to federal §1031; standard mechanics apply.

## Estate and inheritance taxes (MD has BOTH)

### MD Estate Tax
**Authority**: Md. Code Ann., Tax-Gen. §7-301 et seq.
**Exemption (2026)**: $5,000,000 (fixed at this level; NOT indexed for inflation after reaching $5M in 2019)
**Rate**: Graduated up to 16%

**Portability**: MD allows portability of unused exemption to surviving spouse (favorable — unlike MA)

### MD Inheritance Tax
**Authority**: Md. Code Ann., Tax-Gen. §7-201 et seq.
**Rate**: 10% on most transfers; several exemptions
**Exempt beneficiaries**: Spouse, children, parents, grandparents, grandchildren, siblings, step-parents (expanded 2012)
**Subject to 10% tax**: Nieces, nephews, cousins, unrelated beneficiaries, friends

**Practical impact**: MD inheritance tax only significant for non-direct heirs.

### Combined MD Estate + Inheritance interaction

Some coordination between the two taxes — credit mechanism prevents total double taxation. But the combined system makes MD estate planning more complex than states with only one tax.

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 6%
**No local rates**: Uniform statewide (unlike MD's neighbors)

### 3% Tech Tax (NEW effective July 1, 2025)

**Authority**: HB 352 (2025)
**Scope**: 3% sales tax on services in NAICS codes:
- **518**: Data processing, hosting, and related services
- **519**: Other information services
- **5132**: Software publishing (includes SaaS)
- **5415**: Computer systems design and related services (includes IT consulting, custom software)

**NOT additional to 6%**: Services subject to 3% Tech Tax are NOT also subject to standard 6% sales tax (one or the other, not both).

**Broad impact**: 
- Cloud storage (AWS, Google Drive, Wix)
- Web hosting
- Data backup
- Video streaming support
- Web search portals, online directories
- IT consulting
- Software installation
- Business software providers

**Business-to-business impact**: Most Tech Tax falls on B2B transactions — many MD businesses now face new sales tax on vendors.

### Economic nexus (Wayfair)
- $100,000 in MD sales OR 200+ transactions in prior calendar year
- Marketplace facilitator collection required

## Multi-state mechanics — DC/MD/VA complexities

### MD resident with out-of-state income
- MD taxes worldwide income
- Schedule CR (credit for tax paid to other state)
- Critical: new PTET resident-base change improves federal deductibility for out-of-state pass-through income

### Non-resident with MD-source income
- Form 505 (non-resident)
- MD-source: services performed in MD, real estate in MD, MD business allocation

### DC-MD commuter (common)
- DC-MD reciprocity: MD residents working in DC are NOT subject to DC wage tax; DC residents working in MD are NOT subject to MD withholding
- Simplifies DMV metro commuting

### MD-VA commuter
- MD-VA reciprocity: Similar to DC (wages only)
- VA residents working in MD: VA taxes wages; MD does not withhold
- Applies only to wages, not business/investment income
