# New Hampshire (NH) — State Tax File

**Major 2025 change**: The Interest and Dividends (I&D) Tax — New Hampshire's last remaining individual-level tax — was fully repealed effective January 1, 2025. NH now joins FL, NV, SD, TX, TN, WA, WY, AK as states with no individual income tax at the personal level.

New Hampshire's distinctive feature: **high-rate business taxes (BPT 7.5% and BET 0.55%)** that apply to operating businesses including S corps, LLCs, and partnerships — similar to Tennessee's F&E Tax structure. This differs from pure no-tax states like FL/TX/NV/WY/SD where pass-throughs pay nothing at the entity level.

For Priceless, NH relevance:
- **Northeast relocation destination** (from MA, NY, CT) — comparable alternative to moving to FL
- **Lower property tax than MA for equivalent homes** [VERIFY — actually NH property tax is HIGHER than MA on effective basis, but no income tax plus no sales tax compensates]
- **NO sales tax** — a rare US combination with no income tax
- **Higher property tax** — 1.61% median, among highest nationally
- **Business tax complexity** via BPT and BET for NH-operating entities

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: NONE (I&D Tax repealed effective 1/1/2025; no wage tax ever imposed)
- **Sales tax**: NONE (0%)
- **Business Profits Tax (BPT) — RSA 77-A**: 7.5% flat rate on net income; threshold $109,000 gross business income (TY 2025+)
- **Business Enterprise Tax (BET) — RSA 77-E**: 0.55% on enterprise value tax base (compensation + interest + dividends paid); threshold $298,000 (TY 2025+)
- **BET credit against BPT**: BET paid creditable against BPT liability; 10-year carryforward for credits attributable to periods ending on/after 12/31/2014
- **Meals and Rentals Tax (RSA 78-A)**: 8.5% on prepared meals, hotel rooms, short-term rentals
- **Real Estate Transfer Tax (RSA 78-B)**: $1.50 per $100 of consideration (paid by both seller AND buyer = 3% combined on transfer)
- **Capital gains**: NEVER subject to state tax (not even under old I&D Tax)
- **Estate/inheritance/gift tax**: NONE
- **Property tax**: high; median effective 1.61% (among highest in US)
- **PTET**: NOT APPLICABLE (no individual income tax remaining)

## Personal income tax — FULLY REPEALED (2025)

### Historical context: I&D Tax

New Hampshire's Interest and Dividends Tax was enacted in 1923 (RSA Chapter 77). It applied ONLY to interest and dividend income above threshold; never to wages or capital gains.

**Historical rate structure**:
- 5% for tax periods ending before December 31, 2023
- 4% for tax periods ending on or after December 31, 2023
- 3% for tax periods ending on or after December 31, 2024
- **0% (fully repealed) for tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025**

**Historical thresholds**:
- $2,400 interest/dividends for single/individual filers
- $4,800 for joint filers
- Exemptions for age 65+, blind, disabled

**Repeal mechanism**: HB 2 (2023 session), Chapter 79 of Laws 2023, signed by Governor Sununu. Originally scheduled for 2026; accelerated to January 1, 2025.

### 2024 and prior year compliance still required

**Critical for Priceless clients**:
- 2024 I&D Tax return (DP-10) still required for filing if thresholds were met
- Due April 15, 2025
- Even though tax is being repealed going forward, 2024 compliance is mandatory
- 2024 overpayments refunded unless applied to prior-year balances
- No estimated 2025 I&D Tax payments should be made (no tax to pay)

### 2025 forward — NO individual tax

For TY 2025+:
- **No DP-10 filed**
- **No estimated I&D payments**
- **No Schedule I** (previously used)
- **No state wage tax** (never existed)
- **No state tax on capital gains** (never subject to I&D; not subject now)

### Reinstatement risk (HB 503-FN)

A legislator introduced HB 503-FN in 2025 to reinstate the I&D Tax at 5% with a $20,000 threshold (significantly higher than prior $2,400/$4,800). This bill's status is [VERIFY] — similar 2024 bill (HB 1492-FN) did not advance.

**Priceless client advisory**: NH residents with significant investment income should monitor potential reinstatement but no immediate planning change needed. Capital gains would remain exempt regardless.

## Business taxes (CRITICAL — BPT and BET apply to pass-throughs)

NH's business taxes apply broadly to business organizations — including S corporations, LLCs, and partnerships — that operate in NH. This is similar to Tennessee's F&E Tax and differs from pure no-tax states.

### Business Profits Tax (BPT) — RSA 77-A

**Rate**: 7.5% flat (since TY ending on/after 12/31/2023; was 7.6% historically)
**Base**: Federal taxable income with NH modifications
**Who files**: Every business organization with gross business income > **$109,000** (TY 2025+; threshold raised from prior $92,000)
**Who pays**: All business entity types (C corps, S corps, LLCs, partnerships)

**Key NH adjustments to federal taxable income** [VERIFY full list via NH DRA instructions]:
- NH conforms to most federal provisions but adds certain state-specific adjustments
- BET paid is a credit, not a deduction
- Net Operating Loss (NOL) limitations specific to NH

**Apportionment**:
- **Single sales factor** for multi-state businesses (TY ending on/after 12/31/2022)
- Prior to 2022: Three-factor with double-weighted sales

**Unitary combined reporting**: Required for organizations operating a unitary business (RSA 77-A:1, XV)

**Filing**:
- Form NH-1120 (corporate) or NH-1065 (partnership/LLC) or NH-1120-ES/NH-1065-ES (estimates)
- **Due**: April 15 for calendar-year corps; March 15 for partnerships (aligned with federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic 7 months if 100% of BPT/BET liability paid by due date

### Business Enterprise Tax (BET) — RSA 77-E

**Rate**: 0.55% on enterprise value tax base
**Proposed reduction to 0.50%** for tax periods ending on/after 12/31/2026 via HB 155 (status [VERIFY])

**Enterprise value tax base**: Sum of:
- Compensation paid or accrued (wages, salaries, officer compensation, partnership guaranteed payments)
- Interest paid or accrued
- Dividends paid

**Threshold** (TY 2025+):
- > $298,000 gross business receipts, OR
- > $298,000 enterprise value tax base
- Threshold adjusted biennially

**BET vs. BPT interaction**:
- BET paid is **creditable against BPT** (not deductible)
- Unused BET credit carries forward 10 years (for credits attributable to periods ending on/after 12/31/2014)
- BET credit of 5 years for older credits

**Practical effect**: BET is effectively a floor tax — businesses with low profits but substantial payroll still owe BET. Profitable businesses pay BPT, with BET credit reducing net impact.

**Example**: NH S corp with:
- $500K compensation, $20K interest paid, $10K dividends = $530K enterprise value tax base
- BET: $530K × 0.55% = $2,915
- Net income: $200K profit
- BPT: $200K × 7.5% = $15,000
- BPT after BET credit: $15,000 - $2,915 = $12,085 net BPT due
- Total NH business tax burden: $2,915 + $12,085 = $15,000 (effectively equal to BPT alone)

### PTET not applicable

Since NH no longer has an individual income tax, PTET is conceptually inapplicable:
- BPT paid at entity level does not correspond to owner state tax to offset (no owner state tax to offset)
- Owners do NOT get a credit for BPT paid — BPT is the corporate-equivalent tax
- SALT cap workaround not relevant at NH level (though NH residents with other-state income may benefit from PTET elections in those states)

## Sales and use tax — NONE

New Hampshire is one of **five states** with no statewide sales tax (AK, DE, MT, OR, NH). No local sales tax either.

**For Priceless e-commerce clients selling to NH customers**: No NH sales tax obligation. However, nexus with NH may subject business to BPT/BET.

**For NH residents**: No sales tax on purchases in NH. May still owe use tax in other states if purchased for use outside NH and returning to home state (depends on other state's law).

## Property tax — HIGH

**Among highest effective property tax rates in US**.

**Effective rates**:
- State median: 1.61%
- Concord (capital): ~2.0%
- Portsmouth: ~1.3%
- Manchester: ~2.25%
- Nashua: ~2.15%
- Ski country (north): varies significantly

**Funding**:
- Primary funding source for education (NH has no general sales tax and no income tax; education funded locally)
- Significant variance by municipality

**Exemptions**:
- **Elderly exemption** (age 65+; income-limited; varies by town)
- **Blind exemption**
- **Disabled veterans exemption**
- **Solar/wind energy exemptions**
- **Conservation easement reductions**

**Property tax relief**:
- NH Department of Revenue Administration Low and Moderate Income Homeowners Property Tax Relief program (limited income threshold; refunds portion of property tax paid)
- Application window typically May-June following prior tax year

**Tax Year**: Varies by municipality; some use calendar year, others July-June fiscal

### Utility Property Tax (RSA 83-F)

Separate 6.6% tax on value of utility property (electric generation, transmission, natural gas, railroad property) administered at state level.

## Real Estate Transfer Tax (RSA 78-B)

**Rate**: $1.50 per $100 of consideration (or fraction thereof)
**Paid by**: Both seller AND buyer (each pays $0.75 per $100)
**Combined**: Effectively 3% of total consideration divided between parties

**Example**: $750,000 home sale = $11,250 total transfer tax ($5,625 each party)

**Exemptions**:
- Mortgages and deeds of trust
- Leases
- Transfers to government
- Transfer to irrevocable trust by grantor (specific conditions)
- Transfer between spouses incident to divorce

**Compared to other states**:
- Higher than most (MA has 0.456%; CT 0.75% on most sales; NY varies; FL documentary stamp $0.70/$100 buyer only)
- NH's split-liability approach is unusual

## Meals and Rentals Tax (RSA 78-A)

**Rate**: 8.5%
**Applies to**:
- Prepared meals (restaurant food)
- Hotel/motel rooms
- Short-term rentals (< 185 days per guest) — includes Airbnb/VRBO
- Car rentals (additional fee)

**Not applicable to**:
- Grocery food (unprepared)
- Long-term rentals (> 185 days same guest)

**Impact**: Priceless hospitality/STR clients: collect and remit; non-hospitality clients generally don't owe.

## Communications Services Tax (RSA 82-A)

**Rate**: 7%
**Applies to**: Two-way communications services (telephone, mobile, etc.)
**Paid by**: Consumer via provider billing

## Estate, gift, and inheritance tax

NONE at state level. NH has no estate tax, no inheritance tax, no gift tax.

Federal estate tax applies at $15M (post-OBBBA permanent). NH residents face federal-only estate tax burden.

Historical note: NH had a Legacy and Succession Tax and Estate Tax, both repealed over last 20+ years.

## Residency establishment

NH residency for domicile purposes (relevant for property tax exemptions and multi-state audit defense):

**Factors**:
- NH driver's license
- Voter registration
- Vehicle registration
- Physical presence
- Primary residence designation

**Note**: Since NH no longer has individual income tax, there's no NH tax ruling or audit risk for residency determinations at state level. Residency documentation is primarily to defend against OTHER states claiming residency (especially MA, which has aggressive departure audit practices).

**Common Priceless pattern**: MA resident relocating to NH to avoid MA's 5% + 4% millionaire surtax. MA will audit departure aggressively if significant income/assets shift around the move.

## Priceless client considerations

### Massachusetts departure to NH (primary case)
- MA has 5% base + 4% millionaire surtax ($1.1M+ income)
- NH has 0% state income tax
- Savings for high earners: up to 9% federal income differential
- MA aggressive departure audit — establish NH residency before year of tax event
- Property tax higher in NH than some MA suburbs; net still favorable for high earners

### S corporation owners resident in NH
- **BPT 7.5% on profits** at entity level (above $109K threshold)
- **BET 0.55% on compensation + interest + dividends paid** (above $298K threshold)
- BET credit against BPT softens combined effect
- No owner-level NH tax (I&D repealed)
- No PTET election (nothing to work around)

### Real estate investors
- No state income tax on rental income
- High property tax (1.6%+)
- Real estate transfer tax on purchase AND sale (~3% round-trip)
- BPT/BET if held in business entity (LLC, S corp)
- Rental held in personal name avoids BPT/BET
- Short-term rentals: 8.5% meals & rentals tax on rental receipts

### Traders with Trader Tax Status (TTS)
- Pre-repeal: I&D Tax on dividends/interest caused planning around §475 elections
- Post-repeal 2025+: No individual tax concern
- BPT risk remains IF trading activity rises to "doing business" level (fact-specific; §475 MTM election increases BPT risk)
- Non-TTS investors: no NH tax exposure

### Doctors and professional practices
- PLLCs and S corp PCs subject to BPT and BET
- Higher-compensation practices can have significant BET exposure
- Consider partnership structure alternatives (but still BPT/BET at entity level)

### Inbound from other states
- Most common from MA (aggressive departure audit consideration)
- Some from NY, CT, NJ
- Northern New England lifestyle; seasonal considerations
- Low cost of living in interior NH; coastal Portsmouth area commands premium

### Retirees
- No state income tax on retirement distributions, Social Security, pensions
- NO SALES TAX — living costs lower for consumption-focused retirement
- BUT: high property tax; may offset savings for property-heavy retirees
- Estate/gift tax absent

## Multi-state mechanics

### NH resident with out-of-state income
- No NH individual tax to credit
- File non-resident returns in other states
- BPT/BET on NH business operations still apply

### Non-resident with NH-source income
- No individual NH tax
- BPT/BET applies to NH-source business activity above thresholds
- Real estate transfer tax on NH property transactions

### NH business with multi-state operations
- Single sales factor apportionment (BPT)
- BET apportionment based on NH compensation and NH-paid interest/dividends
