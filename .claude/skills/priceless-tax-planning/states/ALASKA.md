# Alaska (AK) — State Tax File

**Unique tax regime**: Alaska is the only US state with no statewide personal income tax AND no statewide sales tax. Revenue comes primarily from oil/gas production taxes and investment earnings from the Alaska Permanent Fund. Individual residents receive an annual Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD) — $1,702 for 2024; variable year-over-year.

For Priceless purposes: Alaska is a niche concern. Most common client touchpoint is a Priceless client who has a C corporation with Alaska operations (resource extraction, tourism, fisheries, or federal government contracting) or a resident client who relocated to Alaska. Individual tax planning at the state level is minimal because no state income tax exists.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: NONE (repealed 1980 after oil revenue replaced need)
- **Corporate income tax**: YES — graduated 0% to 9.4% (applies ONLY to C corps; Alaska Stat. §43.20.011)
- **Sales tax**: NO STATEWIDE; local municipalities and boroughs may impose (rates 0-9.5%; ~1.82% weighted average)
- **PTET**: NOT APPLICABLE (no individual tax to work around)
- **Franchise tax**: NONE
- **Estate / inheritance tax**: NONE
- **Property tax**: local only (state does not levy); average effective rate 1.07%
- **Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD)**: $1,702 for 2024 [VERIFY 2025 and 2026 amounts via AK DOR]; federally taxable; not state taxable
- **Biennial report**: required for corporations and LLCs; $100 flat fee; due January 2 of odd years after initial filing
- **Business License**: $50 for 1 year or $100 for 2 years; expires December 31
- **Oil/gas production tax**: complex; Alaska Stat. §43.55; applies to producers only
- **Fisheries tax**: Alaska Stat. §43.75 (1-5%); resource-industry specific
- **Statutory residency**: Alaska defines residency by physical presence and intent; 30-day PFD qualifying residency distinct from domicile

For Priceless purposes: Unless client is a C corporation with Alaska nexus or a resident of Alaska, state-level tax planning is generally not a concern.

## Personal income tax (NONE)

Alaska repealed its individual income tax in 1980 after oil revenues made the tax unnecessary. Alaska Stat. §43.20 retains the framework for corporate tax but individual income tax provisions were repealed.

**What Alaska residents DO pay**:
- Federal income tax (no state offset to consider)
- Federal self-employment tax on SE earnings
- FICA on wages
- Federal capital gains tax

**What Alaska residents DO NOT pay**:
- State income tax on wages, investment, business income, capital gains, or retirement distributions
- State tax on the Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD) — although PFD IS federally taxable

### Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD)

**What it is**: Annual payment to every eligible Alaska resident from investment earnings on the Alaska Permanent Fund (constitutionally-established $80+ billion sovereign wealth fund).

**Eligibility requirements (abbreviated)**:
- Alaska resident for all of prior calendar year
- Intent to remain in Alaska indefinitely
- Not absent from Alaska more than 180 days during qualifying year (with various exceptions)
- No felony conviction current
- Not claiming residency elsewhere

**Recent PFD amounts**:
- 2021: $1,114
- 2022: $3,284 (includes energy relief payment)
- 2023: $1,312
- 2024: $1,702
- 2025/2026: [VERIFY via AK DOR]

**Federal tax treatment**: PFD is fully federally taxable as other income (Form 1099-MISC issued by Alaska Department of Revenue). No state tax.

**Priceless client impact**: Alaska-resident clients receive 1099-MISC annually; must report on federal return. Multiplicative effect on family returns (each qualifying family member receives PFD).

### Residency considerations

Alaska residency for tax purposes differs from Alaska residency for PFD purposes:
- **Tax residency**: no state tax to enforce; domicile-based like most states but lacks statutory teeth given no income tax
- **PFD residency**: statutory 12-month qualifying period + physical presence requirements; stricter than many states

Priceless clients who move to Alaska often do so for PFD eligibility in addition to lifestyle reasons. Physical presence requirement matters for PFD but not for state income tax (since none exists).

## Corporate income tax (Alaska Stat. §43.20)

**Applies to**: C corporations (including S corporations that elected to be taxed as C; LLCs that elected C corp status). Pass-through entities (S corps, partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships) do NOT pay Alaska corporate tax.

- Water's edge election available for multi-state/international operations

### Apportionment

**Modified three-factor with double-weighted sales** for most industries:
- Sales factor × 2 + Property factor + Payroll factor, all divided by 4
- Single sales factor available for some industries [VERIFY]
- Oil and gas producers: special modified factor (weighted sales + worldwide income inclusion under combined reporting)

### Pass-through entities

Alaska does NOT tax S corporations, partnerships, or LLCs taxed as partnerships at the entity level. Pass-through income flows directly to owners (and since Alaska has no personal income tax, Alaska does not tax it there either).

This creates a favorable environment for pass-through owners operating in Alaska: income earned in Alaska by an S corp or partnership with Alaska operations is untouched by Alaska state tax at any level.

### Credits available

- **Alaska Education Credit** (Alaska Stat. §43.20.014): 50% of first $100K of contributions to Alaska universities/colleges + 100% up to a cap. Annual cap per taxpayer.
- **Film production credit**: program details vary; [VERIFY current status — may be sunsetted]
- **Oil and gas exploration credits**: complex; industry-specific
- **Mining license tax credits**: Alaska Stat. §43.65

### Filing

- Form 6000 (Alaska Corporate Income Tax Return)
- Due date: 30 days after federal return due (April 15 for calendar year = May 15 Alaska)
- Extensions: 6 months via Form 6220
- Minimum tax: $25 annual if corporation has Alaska nexus regardless of income

## Other business taxes

### Oil and Gas Production Tax (Alaska Stat. §43.55)

Applies to producers of oil and gas in Alaska. Complex volume + value + price-based structure with various credits. Unique to resource industry; not directly relevant to typical Priceless client.

### Mining License Tax (Alaska Stat. §43.65)

Graduated tax on mining operations' net income; 0% to 7%.

### Fisheries Business Tax (Alaska Stat. §43.75)

Applies to fisheries processors:
- 3% for shore-based processors (salmon and other species)
- 5% for floating/offshore processors
- Lower rates for certain species and operations

Fishery Resource Landing Tax (Alaska Stat. §43.77): 1-3% depending on type of fishery.

These resource taxes primarily affect industrial operators, not typical Priceless pass-through clients.

### Unemployment insurance (UI)

**Wage base (2026)**: $54,200
**Rate range**: 1.00% to 5.40% for experienced employers
**New employer**: 1.99%
**Employee contribution**: 0.50% (Alaska is unusual — most states have employer-only UI)

## Sales and use tax

### No statewide sales tax

Alaska does not impose a statewide sales tax. However, approximately 100+ municipalities and boroughs impose local sales taxes:
- Anchorage: NO local sales tax
- Juneau: 5%
- Fairbanks: NO local sales tax (North Star Borough)
- Ketchikan: 6.5% (city + borough combined)
- Sitka: 6%
- Bethel: 6%
- Kodiak: 7%
- Various small communities: 0-9.5%

Average weighted combined state+local sales tax rate: 1.82%.

### Alaska Remote Seller Sales Tax Commission (ARSSTC)

**What it is**: A cooperative framework where participating Alaska municipalities delegate remote seller sales tax administration to a unified commission. Remote sellers collect and remit through one portal rather than dealing with individual local jurisdictions.

**Economic nexus (ARSSTC participants)**:
- $100,000 in gross sales OR 200+ separate transactions in participating jurisdictions in prior or current calendar year
- Marketplace facilitator provisions apply

**Non-participating jurisdictions**: Some municipalities have not joined ARSSTC; separate registration/collection may be required.

**Priceless e-commerce clients**: If selling tangible goods to Alaska customers, confirm ARSSTC registration if thresholds met. Many Alaska municipalities waive collection for below-threshold sellers; check per-jurisdiction if client operates near threshold.

## Property tax

Local level only (state does not levy). Administered by boroughs and cities.

- Anchorage: effective rate ~1.35%
- Fairbanks North Star Borough: ~1.5%
- Juneau: ~1.1%
- Statewide average effective: 1.07%

Homestead-type exemptions vary by locality. Senior and disabled veteran exemptions common.

Alaska real estate values generally below mainland US averages. Rural property assessments low.

## Estate, gift, and inheritance tax

NONE at state level. Alaska has no estate, inheritance, or gift tax.

Federal estate tax applies at $15M (post-OBBBA permanent). Alaska residents face federal-only estate tax burden.

## Priceless client considerations

### S corporation owners resident in Alaska
- No state income tax at individual level = no state planning needed
- If operating C corp in Alaska, graduated corporate tax applies at entity level
- Biennial report compliance ($100 per two-year period)
- Business license annually

### Real estate investors
- No state income tax on rental income
- Moderate property tax (1-1.5% effective)
- No state transfer tax on real estate sales
- Alaska real estate generally lower value than mainland; rental yield analysis focus

### Multi-state clients with Alaska operations
- Most common touchpoint for Priceless: client has out-of-state business activity in Alaska (federal contractors, resource extraction, tourism)
- If pass-through: no Alaska income tax concern at entity level
- If C corp: Alaska corporate tax applies on Alaska apportioned income
- Nexus rules: physical presence for corporate tax (Alaska has not adopted economic nexus for corporate tax; only sales tax economic nexus via ARSSTC)

### Retirees
- Major advantage: no state tax on Social Security, pensions, IRA distributions, or other retirement income
- Plus PFD eligibility (must meet residency requirements)
- Property tax moderate
- Climate and remoteness are lifestyle factors; PFD + no income tax is an economic attractor

### Resource industries (oil, gas, mining, fishing)
- Industry-specific taxes (production tax, mining license tax, fisheries tax) apply regardless of entity structure
- Complex credits and adjustments
- Priceless likely refers such clients to specialists in Alaska resource taxation

## Multi-state mechanics

### AK resident with out-of-state income
- No AK tax to consider at state level
- File non-resident returns in other states where income earned
- No AK credit mechanism needed (no AK tax to offset)

### Non-resident with AK-source business income
- Individual: no AK personal income tax
- Pass-through entity: AK does not tax at entity level
- C corporation: AK apportioned income subject to graduated corporate tax

### Relocation from high-tax state to Alaska
- Similar to FL/TX/NV relocation pattern
- Departing state residency rules apply (CA/NY/NJ particularly aggressive)
- Priceless coordinates documentation, travel records, life transfer
