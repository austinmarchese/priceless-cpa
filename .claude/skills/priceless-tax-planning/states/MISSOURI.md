# Missouri (MO) — State Tax File

Missouri has a **game-changing 100% capital gains subtraction** (Section 143.121) effective TY 2025+ — all federal capital gains can be subtracted from Missouri taxable income. This makes Missouri a **de facto 0% state for capital gains** for MO residents. Combined with modest 4.7% top rate on ordinary income, Missouri has become highly favorable for investment-heavy clients.

Missouri also explicitly decouples from OBBBA's TIPS, overtime, car loan interest, and enhanced senior deductions for federal tax purposes — these are NOT available on Missouri returns.

Low Priceless concentration. Some Kansas City/St. Louis clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 4.7% top rate 2025/2026 (8 graduated brackets reaching 4.7% at $9,191 taxable income — effectively flat for most filers above that threshold)
- **Capital gains**: **100% subtraction effective TY 2025+** — Missouri residents pay $0 state tax on capital gains income reported for federal purposes (unique to MO)
- **Corporate income tax**: 4.0% flat (reduced from 6.25% via prior reforms)
- **PTET**: Yes (Section 143.436); refundable credit to owners
- **Sales tax**: 4.225% state; local adds up to ~7.5% additional; combined up to ~11%; groceries taxed at reduced 1.225% state rate
- **Estate tax**: NONE
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: **Fully exempt for age 62+** (100% subtraction for Social Security and SSDI; effective TY 2024+ under SS SB 190)
- **Public pensions**: Up to $47,633 per taxpayer (2025; tied to maximum Social Security benefit)
- **Military retirement**: Fully exempt
- **Private pensions**: Up to $6,000 per taxpayer (income limits apply)
- **Standard deduction**: Conforms to federal — $15,000 single / $30,000 MFJ / $22,500 HoH (2025); $16,100 / $32,200 / $24,150 estimated 2026 (OBBBA-adjusted federal amounts)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### 100% Capital Gains Subtraction (Section 143.121) — KEY PLANNING ITEM

Effective TY 2025 (returns filed in 2026): **100% of federal capital gains income can be subtracted** on the Missouri return.

**Mechanics**:
- Starting point: Federal adjusted gross income (includes capital gains)
- Missouri modification: Subtract **full amount** of net capital gain income reported for federal purposes
- Result: $0 Missouri state tax on capital gains

**Applies to**:
- Long-term capital gains (federal §1(h))
- Short-term capital gains (federal ordinary)
- §1231 gains treated as capital
- Section 1202 QSBS to extent in federal AGI (federal exclusion already removes most from AGI)
- Section 1031 deferred gains (when eventually recognized)
- Installment sale gain as recognized

**Does NOT apply to**:
- Ordinary income (wages, business income, interest, dividends, rental — not capital gain)
- Section 1245/1250 recapture (taxed as ordinary)
- Collectibles gain (still capital; included in subtraction)

**Planning implications for Priceless clients**:
- MO-resident investment-heavy clients: Major tax advantage vs. other states
- Capital gain harvesting strategies more valuable (no MO state tax hit)
- Recommending MO residency for post-exit entrepreneurs (founders, business sellers)
- Roth conversions favorable — pay MO tax now on ordinary (4.7%), convert to tax-free growth
- Investment timing: Less urgency to bunch or defer gains; MO won't tax them
- Real estate sales: MO residents avoid 4.7% state tax on gains (federal at 20% + NIIT 3.8% still applies)

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: 100% exempt for age 62+ (SSDI fully exempt)
- **Public pensions** (federal, state, local): Up to $47,633 per taxpayer (2025; tied to max Social Security benefit)
- **Private pensions**: Up to $6,000 per taxpayer (income limits: MAGI < $32,000 single / $100K MFJ = full deduction; phased above)
- **Military retirement**: 100% exempt
- **Survivor Benefit Plan annuity**: Public pension exemption applies ($47,633)

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

- **Rate**: 4.7% (top individual rate)
- **Base**: MO-source income of electing entity
- **Election**: Annual; made on Form MO-PTE
- **Credit**: Refundable credit to owners on MO-1040
- **Estimated payments**: Required if entity liability > $500

### Worked example

S corp with $500K MO-source income (ordinary business, not capital gains); one owner:
- Entity PTET: $500K × 4.7% = $23,500 at entity level
- Federal SALT deduction: $23,500 × 37% = $8,695 federal savings
- Owner gets $23,500 refundable credit

**If the same S corp sold assets generating $500K long-term capital gain**:
- Federal AGI includes $500K LTCG
- Missouri residents: Subtract full $500K capital gain → **$0 Missouri tax** on gain
- No PTET election needed for capital gain portion (nothing to pay)
- PTET remains valuable for ordinary S corp income

## Corporate income tax

- **Rate: 4.0% flat** (reduced from 6.25% per 2018 Senate Bill 884)
- **Base**: Federal taxable income with MO modifications
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor
- **Combined filing**: Not required
- **NOL**: 20-year carryforward

## OBBBA conformity — CRITICAL MO NON-CONFORMITY

Missouri DOR has **explicitly stated** (2025 Individual Income Tax Year Changes publication) that federal OBBBA deductions are **NOT available on Missouri returns**:

|---|---|---|---|

| No Tax on Overtime (up to $12.5K single / $25K MFJ) | Federal deduction TY 2025-2028 | **NOT allowed on MO return** — addback required | MO taxes overtime premium at 4.7% |
| Car Loan Interest Deduction | Federal deduction TY 2025-2028 | **NOT allowed on MO return** — addback required | MO full tax on income without this deduction |
| Enhanced Senior Deduction ($6,000/$12,000) | Federal deduction TY 2025-2028 | **NOT allowed on MO return** | MO already has retirement exemptions |
| §174 domestic R&E restoration | Restored permanent | [VERIFY] — likely conforms | |
| 100% bonus depreciation post-1/19/2025 | Restored permanent | [VERIFY — MO historically decouples] | |

## Local earnings taxes

Kansas City and St. Louis each impose a 1% earnings tax on residents and on income earned in the city by nonresidents:
- **Kansas City**: 1% earnings tax on residents and on income earned from work performed in KC
- **St. Louis**: 1% earnings tax similar mechanics

Both are in addition to state income tax. For Priceless clients with KC/STL wage income, must file separate city earnings tax return.

## Sales and use tax

- **State rate: 4.225%**
- **Local**: Up to ~7.5% additional (combined up to ~12%); varies significantly by jurisdiction
- **Groceries**: Taxed at reduced 1.225% state rate
- **Prescription drugs**: Exempt
- **Services**: Limited service taxation

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic 6-month to October 15 via federal extension or Form MO-60
- **Payment**: Full tax due by April 15 to avoid penalty/interest
- **Forms**: MO-1040 (resident); MO-NRI (nonresident)
- **Estimated payments**: Quarterly (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15)
- **Safe harbor**: 100% prior year (110% AGI > $150K) or 90% current year

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: MO business seller (CAPITAL GAINS KEY)
**Setup**: MO-resident S corp owner; selling business for $3M long-term capital gain.

**Missouri**:
- Starting point: Federal AGI includes $3M LTCG
- Missouri modification: Subtract $3M full capital gain
- **Missouri tax on gain: $0**

**Federal**:
- LTCG 20%: $600K
- NIIT 3.8%: $114K (if MAGI > $250K MFJ)
- Total federal: $714K (on sole gain)

**Total tax**: $714K (federal only; Missouri 100% subtraction = $0)

**Compare to Kansas** (same income): KS 5.7% × $3M = $171K state tax. **Missouri saves $171K** vs. KS.

### Scenario 2: Retiree with investment portfolio
**Setup**: MO resident, 67, MFJ; $80K pension + $45K Social Security + $150K annual capital gains on portfolio.

**Analysis**:
- Social Security: 100% exempt
- Public pension: Up to $47,633 × 2 = $95,266 joint; $80K < cap, so fully exempt
- Capital gains: $150K × 100% subtraction = $0 MO tax
- Other income: $0
- **Total MO tax: $0**

This is exceptional. Missouri is among the top 3-4 retirement-friendly states when combined with 100% capital gains and Social Security.

### Scenario 3: OBBBA deductions client
**Setup**: MO-resident restaurant server; $40K wages + $18K tips + $8K overtime.

**Federal 2025 (with OBBBA)**:
- Income: $66,000
- Tips deduction: -$18,000 (OBBBA; up to $25K allowed)
- Overtime deduction: -$8,000 (OBBBA; up to $12,500 single cap)
- Standard deduction: -$15,750
- Federal taxable: $24,250

**Missouri 2025**:
- Starting point: Federal AGI $66,000 (does NOT include federal OBBBA deductions below the line if structured as above-the-line)
- Actually OBBBA tips/overtime are NEW above-the-line deductions, so federal AGI may already be reduced
- [VERIFY exact mechanic: if OBBBA deductions are "below the line," AGI still includes tips/overtime; if above-the-line, AGI is reduced by those deductions]
- **Either way**: Missouri requires addback of tips, overtime deductions
- Missouri taxable: ~$50,000 after MO standard deduction ($15,000)
- MO tax: ~$2,200 (at 4.7% effective)

**Priceless action**: Server client benefits federally but not in Missouri. Model both.

### Scenario 4: Kansas City remote worker
**Setup**: MO resident (suburbs); works remotely for KC-based employer.

**State tax**: MO 4.7% (resident tax on worldwide income)
**KC earnings tax**: 1% on wages earned for work performed in KC; **if work is performed remotely from MO suburbs OUTSIDE KC jurisdiction, NO KC earnings tax applies** — even if employer is based in KC

**Priceless action**: Verify work location is truly remote (not KC office); employer should not be withholding KC earnings tax for work performed outside KC.
