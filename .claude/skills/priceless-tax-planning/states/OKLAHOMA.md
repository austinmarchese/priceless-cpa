# Oklahoma (OK) — State Tax File

Oklahoma enacted **HB 2764 (2025)** which consolidated 6 individual income tax brackets to **3 brackets effective TY 2026** and reduced the top rate from 4.75% to **4.5%**. Bill also establishes **trigger mechanism for 0.25% reductions** when specified revenue conditions met — similar to MS and KY phase-out trajectories, potentially leading to full elimination over time.

Oklahoma corporate rate remains 4% flat (unchanged). Brackets NOT indexed for inflation.

Very low Priceless concentration. Some OKC/Tulsa clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 3 brackets effective TY 2026; top rate **4.5%** (reduced from 4.75% in 2025)
- **Corporate income tax**: 4% flat (unchanged)
- **PTET**: Yes (68 Okla. Stat. § 2385.1)
- **Sales tax**: 4.5% state + local up to 7% (combined max ~11.5%; high combined burden)
- **Estate tax**: None
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Military retirement**: 100% exempt (since 2022)
- **Other retirement**: Up to $10,000 per taxpayer exempt (age 65+ with income limits)
- **Standard deduction**: Conforms federal ($16,100 single / $32,200 MFJ for 2026)
- **Capital gains deduction**: Up to 100% for OK property held > 5 years (Oklahoma-source gains)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### OK capital gains deduction

Oklahoma offers **100% subtraction** for capital gains on sale of:
- Oklahoma-headquartered property (stock, real estate, business interests) held **more than 5 years**
- Tangible/intangible property from Oklahoma-based entities held > 2 years

This is a significant planning advantage for long-term Oklahoma-based holdings. For Priceless clients with 5+ year Oklahoma property holdings, capital gains effectively 0% for OK state purposes.

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Military retirement**: 100% exempt (2022+ effective)
- **Federal/state pensions**: Up to $10,000 per taxpayer exempt (age 65+; income limits)
- **IRA/401(k)**: Generally taxed as ordinary income (subject to $10K exemption if age 65+)

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

- **Rate**: 4.5% (top individual rate)
- **Base**: OK-source income
- **Election**: Annual
- **Credit**: Refundable

### Worked example

S corp with $500K OK income:
- Entity PTET: $500K × 4.5% = $22,500 at entity level
- Federal SALT deduction: $22,500 × 37% = $8,325 federal savings

## Corporate income tax

- **Rate: 4% flat** (unchanged by HB 2764)
- **Base**: Federal taxable income with OK modifications
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor
- **Combined filing**: Not required

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: OKC business owner with long-held interest
**Setup**: OK resident; sells interest in 10-year-old OK-based S corp; $2M LTCG.

**Analysis**:
- **OK capital gains deduction**: 100% for OK property held > 5 years → **$0 OK tax** on gain
- Federal: LTCG 20% + NIIT 3.8% on $2M = ~$476K federal
- Total tax: $476K (federal only; OK state $0)

This is highly favorable for Priceless OK clients planning business exits. Holding period critical — 5+ years for qualifying property.

### Scenario 2: OK S corp owner
**Setup**: OK resident; $400K S corp income.

**2026**:
- OK tax: $400K × 4.5% (top bracket) ≈ $18,000
- With PTET: Entity pays; federal SALT deduction $18K × 37% = $6,660 federal savings

### Scenario 3: Retiree
**Setup**: OK resident; 70; MFJ; $45K combined Social Security + $50K pension + $20K IRA distribution.

**Analysis**:
- Social Security: 100% exempt
- Pension: $10,000 × 2 = $20,000 exemption (age 65+)
- Taxable pension: $50K - $20K = $30K
- IRA: $20K (taxable)
- Total taxable: $50K
- Less MFJ standard deduction: $32,200
- Net OK taxable: $17,800
- OK tax: ~$800 (effective 1.6%)
