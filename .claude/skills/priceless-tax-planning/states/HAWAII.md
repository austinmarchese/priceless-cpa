# Hawaii (HI) — State Tax File

**Most complex bracket structure in US**: 12 brackets; top rate 11% on income > $325,000. Hawaii also has the unique General Excise Tax (GET) — not a sales tax, but a broader privilege tax on business activity.

Low Priceless concentration. Relevance: occasional second-home HNW clients, multi-state clients with HI operations (tourism, agriculture), and inbound/outbound relocation complexity.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 12 brackets; rates 1.4% to 11%
- **Top bracket**: 11% on income > $325,000 single / $650,000 MFJ
- **Standard deduction (2025)**: $4,400 single / $8,800 MFJ (doubled from $2,200/$4,400 pre-2024)
- **Corporate income tax**: Graduated 4.4% / 5.4% / 6.4% (top rate on income > $100K)
- **General Excise Tax (GET)**: 4% state + up to 0.5% county (Oahu 4.5%; Maui/Hawaii/Kauai 4.5% each)
- **PTET**: Yes (HRS §235-51.5); elective
- **Estate tax**: Yes; $5.49M exemption (indexed) [VERIFY 2026]
- **Property tax**: effective 0.27% (lowest nominal US rate on residential)
- **Transient Accommodations Tax**: 10.25% state + 3% county (combined 13.25% on short-term rentals/hotels)

## Personal income tax

### 12-bracket structure (post-2024 Act 46 expansion)

Brackets widened 2024 (Act 46 of 2024) with lowest rate bracket expanded; top bracket now applies at $325K single (up from $200K). Full bracket structure has 12 tiers:

**Single filers (2025)**:
| Range | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 - $9,600 | 1.4% |
| $9,601 - $14,400 | 3.2% |
| $14,401 - $19,200 | 5.5% |
| $19,201 - $24,000 | 6.4% |
| $24,001 - $36,000 | 6.8% |
| $36,001 - $48,000 | 7.2% |
| $48,001 - $150,000 | 7.6% |
| $150,001 - $175,000 | 7.9% |
| $175,001 - $200,000 | 8.25% |
| $200,001 - $300,000 | 9% |
| $300,001 - $325,000 | 10% |
| $325,001+ | 11% |

**2027+ planned**: Further bracket widening phased in through 2031.

### Starting point

HI begins with federal AGI with state-specific adjustments.

### Retirement income

- Social Security: fully exempt
- Pension from private sources: **fully exempt** (HI is one of few states with full pension exemption)
- Military retirement: fully exempt
- IRA/401(k): taxable at ordinary rates

**Unusually generous retirement treatment**: HI is a favorable retirement state for pension-heavy income despite high top rate on wages/business income.

### Capital gains

**Alternative Tax on Capital Gains (HRS §235-51)**: HI allows the taxpayer to calculate LT capital gains tax at 7.25% instead of ordinary rates — benefits high earners in top bracket.

## General Excise Tax (GET) — Unique

**Authority**: HRS §237

**What it is**: Broad-based privilege tax on businesses' gross income in HI. Not a sales tax — legally paid by the seller, though economically passed to consumer.

**Key differences from sales tax**:
- **Applies to services** (most services taxed, unlike most state sales taxes)
- **Applies to business-to-business transactions** (creates tax pyramiding)
- **Applies to rent** (landlords owe GET on rental income)
- **Applies to professional fees** (lawyers, consultants owe GET)
- **Applies to interest and dividends received in business context**

**Rates**:
- **Wholesale**: 0.5% (business-to-business that will resell)
- **Retail**: 4% state + county surcharge
- **Insurance commissions**: 0.15%

**County surcharges**:
- Honolulu (Oahu): 0.5% (combined 4.5%)
- Hawaii: 0.5% (combined 4.5%)
- Maui: 0% (combined 4%)
- Kauai: 0.5% (combined 4.5%)

**$100K small seller threshold**: Out-of-state businesses with only sales and no physical presence can elect alternative 0.5% tax of annual gross sales if HI gross < $100K (HRS §235-51 note (t)).

**Priceless client impact**:
- HI-based service businesses pay GET on gross receipts
- Landlords pay GET on rental income (additional to federal/state income tax)
- Creates effective tax pyramiding for B2B services
- HI-based consulting/professional firms face meaningful GET burden

## PTET (HRS §235-51.5)

**Status**: Elective
**Rate**: 11% (matches individual top rate)
**Eligible**: S corps, partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships
**Election**: Annual
**Federal SALT cap workaround**: Yes

## Corporate Income Tax

**Rates (graduated)**:
- $0 - $25,000: 4.4%
- $25,001 - $100,000: 5.4%
- $100,001+: 6.4%

**Base**: Federal taxable income with HI modifications
**Apportionment**: Single sales factor (2020+)

## Estate Tax

**Exemption**: $5.49 million (2025) [VERIFY 2026 indexed]
**Rate**: 10% to 20% graduated
**Portability**: Limited HI estate tax portability; does not fully replicate federal portability

Significant divergence from federal $15M; creates planning need for HI residents with estates $5.49M - $15M.

## Property tax

**Effective rate**: 0.27% statewide (among lowest nominally)
**Mechanism**: County-administered; state does not levy
**Honolulu County (Oahu)**: Residential rate $3.50 per $1,000 of assessed value
**Primary residence credit**: $80,000 assessed value credit reduces taxable value
**Second home / vacation rental**: Higher rates (Honolulu Residential A, $4.50-$13.50 per $1K)

## Transient Accommodations Tax (TAT)

**State rate**: 10.25% (on hotel/short-term rental gross rent)
**County surcharge**: 3% (all counties)
**Combined**: 13.25%

**Priceless STR client impact**: HI short-term rental owners collect 13.25% TAT in addition to GET (4-4.5%) and federal income tax. Total transactional tax ~17-18% on rental receipts.

## Residency

Standard domicile. HI has 200-day statutory resident provision.

## Priceless client considerations

### Second-home owners in HI
- Property tax rates HIGHER for non-primary-residence (Residential A class)
- TAT + GET if renting out (complex compliance)
- HI source income for nonresidents: tax at graduated rates; Form N-15

### Hi residents moving out
- Domicile change to FL/TX/NV/WY etc.
- HI DOR may audit if significant income event follows departure
- Document physical presence change

### Business owners in HI
- GET on gross receipts (no cost-of-goods deduction)
- PTET election captures SALT cap value
- High top marginal rate 11% + GET 4.5% = significant effective tax
- Service businesses particularly affected by GET pyramiding

### Retirees in HI
- Social Security and pensions fully exempt
- Lifestyle/climate attractors
- Cost of living very high
- IRA/401(k) taxable at ordinary rates
