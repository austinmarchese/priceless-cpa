# Louisiana (LA) — State Tax File

Louisiana executed a sweeping tax overhaul in November 2024 Third Extraordinary Session. Effective January 1, 2025: individual income tax moved from 3-bracket graduated (1.85%/3.5%/4.25%) to **flat 3%**. Corporate income tax moved from 3-bracket graduated (3.5%/5.5%/7.5%) to **flat 5.5%**. Corporate franchise tax **REPEALED effective January 1, 2026**. Sales tax increased to 5% (from 4.45%) through 2029, then reduced to 4.75% in 2030. Standard deduction tripled.

Low Priceless concentration. Some New Orleans area clients; oil/gas multistate.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: **3.0% flat** (effective 1/1/2025; replaced 1.85%/3.5%/4.25% graduated)
- **Corporate income tax**: **5.5% flat** (effective 1/1/2025; replaced 3.5%/5.5%/7.5% graduated)
- **Corporation franchise tax**: **REPEALED effective 1/1/2026** (HB 3, 2024 3rd Extraordinary Session)
- **PTET**: Yes; refundable credit; 3% rate (tracks individual rate)
- **Sales tax**: **5.0% state** (2025-2029); decreasing to 4.75% effective 1/1/2030; local adds up to 7% (combined maximum around 11-12%)
- **Standard deduction**: $12,500 single/MFS; $25,000 MFJ/HoH/QSS (2025; inflation-adjusted from 2026 per CPI)
- **Retirement income exemption**: $12,000 for age 65+ (doubled from $6,000 in 2025)
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Estate tax**: NONE (no state estate transfer tax owed since 2004)
- **Inheritance tax**: Repealed 2008
- **Gift tax**: NONE
- **Filing deadline**: **May 15** (not April 15 — Louisiana statutory deadline)

## Personal income tax

### Rate history

| Year | Structure | Top Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-2022 | 6-bracket graduated | 6% |
| 2022-2024 | 3-bracket graduated | 4.25% (on income > $50K) |
| **2025+** | **Flat** | **3.0%** (all income) |

The 2024 Third Extraordinary Session reform (Act 1, HB 10 specifically) enacted the flat rate. This reform ALSO:
- Tripled the standard deduction (from $4,500 single / $9,000 MFJ to $12,500 / $25,000)
- Doubled the retirement income exemption ($6,000 → $12,000; age 65+)
- **Eliminated the net capital gains deduction** (prior LA law allowed deduction for sales of LA-based business interests; now repealed)
- Increased sales tax to 5% through 2029

### Standard deduction and personal exemption

Prior to 2025 reform, Louisiana had a "combined personal exemption-standard deduction" that varied by filing status. Under the 2025 reform:

| Filing Status | 2025 Standard Deduction | 2026 (CPI-adjusted) |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $12,500 | ~$12,875 |
| Married Filing Separately | $12,500 | ~$12,875 |
| Married Filing Jointly | $25,000 | ~$25,750 |
| Head of Household | $25,000 | ~$25,750 |
| Qualifying Surviving Spouse | $25,000 | ~$25,750 |

Beginning January 1, 2026, the standard deduction is adjusted annually for inflation based on CPI. Exact 2026 figures published by LDR in late 2025 [VERIFY exact 2026 figures from LDR RIB].

### Capital gains deduction — REPEALED

Prior Louisiana law allowed a deduction for net capital gains from sales of LA-based businesses or interests. This was eliminated effective January 1, 2025, as part of the reform package.

**Transition rule for installment sales**: For installment sales recognized before January 1, 2025, taxpayers may continue to claim the net capital gains deduction on future installments recognized after that date. This preserves the deduction only for already-closed sales with installment payments ongoing. New sales post-1/1/2025 receive no LA capital gains preference.

**Priceless planning implication**: For LA clients selling businesses in 2024 or 2025, evaluate whether installment sale structure pre-2025 could have preserved the deduction on future payments. For sales closing in 2025+, no LA preference exists; gain taxed at ordinary 3% rate.

### Withholding

LDR guidance (Revenue Information Bulletin No. 25-008, December 23, 2024) provided updated withholding tables. Employers generally use **3.09% withholding** as a small buffer over the 3% tax rate to ensure adequate withholding through year-end.

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Retirement income exemption**: $12,000 per taxpayer (doubled from $6,000) for age 65+; CPI-adjusted annually from 2026
- **Qualified sources**: Pensions, annuities, IRA/401(k) distributions
- **Military retirement**: Fully exempt (separate from $12,000 exemption)

## Corporate income tax

- **Rate: 5.5% flat** (effective 1/1/2025; replaced 3.5%/5.5%/7.5% graduated)
- **Corporate standard deduction**: $20,000 (new for 2025; HB 2)
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor (standard)
- **Combined filing**: Not required; separate-company
- **Bonus depreciation/§179 election**: Corporations may elect to immediately expense qualified property and §174 R&E expenditures in the tax year placed in service/incurred — HB 2 allows a "bonus amortization" similar to §174 (this is LA-specific and effective for post-1/1/2025 property/expenditures)
- **NOL**: 20-year carryforward; no carryback

### Corporate franchise tax — REPEALED

The corporate franchise tax (previously imposed at $2.75/$1,000 of taxable capital exceeding $300,000, a tax on capital regardless of profitability) is **REPEALED effective for franchise tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026**.

Important mechanics:
- LA franchise tax period is based on the PRIOR year's corporate accounting period
- So the **CFT-620 filed in 2025** (for 2024 accounting period) is the **LAST franchise tax return** for most corporations
- 2026 franchise tax period (based on 2025 corporate activities): **NO franchise tax owed**
- This is a meaningful savings for LA-incorporated or LA-doing-business corporations

**Priceless client implication**: Any LA-based corporate entity that was paying franchise tax will see this obligation end. For S corps and LLCs taxed as partnerships, no franchise tax was owed previously, so no change.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

PTET pre-existed the 2024 reform and continues under revised mechanics:
- Rate: 3% flat (tracks individual rate)
- Refundable credit to owners
- Election annual, made on Form R-6922
- Covers LA income tax only; no local analog
- Graduated rates for PTE electing to be taxed as corporation were repealed (HB 10, effective 1/1/2025); flat 3% applies to all electing PTEs

### Worked example

S corp with $500,000 LA-source income; single owner:
- Entity PTET: $500,000 × 3% = **$15,000** paid by entity
- Federal deduction: $15,000 × 37% = **$5,550** federal tax savings
- Owner credit: $15,000 refundable credit
- Net LA cost: $0 (breakeven); net federal benefit: $5,550

For S corp, no federal double-tax concern (S corp PTET election per IRS Notice 2020-75 is approved). No franchise tax applies to S corps regardless.

## Sales and use tax

### Rate and transition

- **Current (2025-2029)**: 5.0% state (increased from 4.45%)
- **Beginning 1/1/2030**: Scheduled reduction to 4.75% state
- **Local**: Parishes and municipalities impose additional local sales tax; combined can reach ~11-12% in some parishes (among highest combined in US)

### Base expansion (HB 8, 2024)

HB 8 expanded the sales tax base effective January 1, 2025, to include digital products:
- Digital audiovisual works (streaming, downloads)
- Digital audio works (music downloads)
- Digital books (ebooks)
- Digital codes and applications
- Digital periodicals
- Any tangible personal property transferred electronically

Service industries NOT generally subject to sales tax in LA (unlike some other states). But digital goods base expansion affects streaming, software, and digital content businesses.

### Exemption repeals (HB 10, 2024)

Several exemptions were repealed effective 1/1/2025:
- Separately stated labor charges on property repaired outside LA
- Labor, materials, and supplies for drilling rig machinery repair
- Other industry-specific exemptions

### Vendor's compensation

Sellers remitting sales tax on time receive compensation:
- Rate: 1.5% of state sales tax collected
- Monthly cap: $750 (reduced from $1,500)

## OBBBA conformity

Louisiana generally follows rolling federal conformity, meaning post-OBBBA changes flow through automatically unless specifically decoupled. Key items:

| OBBBA Provision | Federal Status | LA Conformity | Planning Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| §174 domestic R&E immediate expensing | Restored permanent | **LA explicitly allows** — HB 2 permits immediate deduction OR "bonus amortization" election for R&E expenditures paid/incurred post-1/1/2025 | Highly favorable for LA R&D-active businesses |
| 100% bonus depreciation post-1/19/2025 | Restored permanent | **LA explicitly allows** — HB 2 allows immediate expensing of qualified property placed in service post-1/1/2025 | Very favorable; LA is one of few states with explicit bonus conformity |
| §179 expensing ($2.5M/$4M phase-out) | Enhanced | LA generally conforms | |
| §1202 QSBS ($15M cap; tiered exclusion) | Enhanced | LA conforms via base | Gain eligible for exclusion |
| QBI deduction (§199A) | Permanent | **LA NOT conform** — LA S corp owners pay 3% on full distributive share | QBI federal-only |
| SALT cap ($40,400 2026; reverts $10K 2030) | Applies federal | LA unaffected directly | PTET mitigates federal SALT cap |
| TIPS/overtime/car loan interest deduction | Deductible federally TY 2025+ | [VERIFY — LA may have decoupled per HB 2 amendments] | |

**Key point**: Louisiana's HB 2 bonus depreciation and bonus amortization provisions for corporations, S corps, partnerships, estates, and trusts (all entity types) make LA unusually favorable for immediate deduction of qualified property and R&E. This is genuinely different from most states that decouple from federal bonus.

## Estate, inheritance, and gift taxes

- **Estate tax**: The LA "estate transfer tax" remains on the books but is tied to a federal credit that was eliminated after 2004. **No LA estate tax has been owed on any estate since 2004.** Effectively zero.
- **Inheritance tax**: **Repealed 2008**
- **Gift tax**: None

Louisiana is estate-planning-friendly from a state perspective. Federal estate tax still applies at $15M (2026) threshold.

## Unique LA features

### Community property state

Louisiana is a **community property** state (one of 9 in US). Marital assets acquired during marriage are generally jointly owned. For federal tax purposes, this has implications for:
- Basis step-up: Full step-up on both halves of community property at first spouse's death (versus 50% for common-law property)
- Income allocation: Generally 50/50 on wages and business income earned during marriage
- Succession planning: Usufruct and naked ownership structures unique to LA civil code

For Priceless LA-based clients or clients with LA community property interests, coordinate with LA estate attorney; federal tax planning must account for LA civil code nuances.

### Louisiana Revised Statutes vs. Civil Code

Louisiana is unique in US as a civil-law jurisdiction (French legal heritage). Tax statutes are in Revised Statutes Title 47; property/succession rules in Civil Code. This affects trust planning (LA trust law differs from common-law states), property transfers, and succession.

### 2026 inventory tax constitutional amendment

March 2025 voters **rejected** the broader constitutional amendment (65% opposed) that would have reduced the constitutional ceiling on individual income tax rate to 3.75%, restructured LA Constitution Article VII, and made various other structural changes.

A separate amendment on **inventory tax exemption** is pending 2026 vote. If approved, parishes would have authority to exempt business inventory from local property tax. LA is one of few states that taxes business inventory as personal property. Eliminating this would be significant for manufacturers, retailers, and distributors with significant inventory in LA.

## Filing mechanics

- **Filing deadline: May 15** (NOT April 15 — LA statutory deadline)
- **Automatic 6-month extension to file** (no form required)
- **Payment deadline**: May 15 (not extended by extension)
- **Late payment penalty**: 0.5% per 30 days (capped at 25%)
- **Quarterly estimated payments**: Due April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15
- **Safe harbor**: 100% prior-year tax or 90% current-year tax
- **Forms**: IT-540 (resident), IT-540B (nonresident/part-year)

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: New Orleans S corp owner
**Setup**: S corp with $600K LA-source income; single shareholder (LA resident).

**Before reform (2024)**:
- LA tax: $600K on graduated rate (~4.25% top bracket effective on most income) ≈ $25,500
- No change from PTET election (essentially breakeven for LA; federal SALT benefit)

**After reform (2025+, 3% flat)**:
- LA tax: $600K × 3% = **$18,000** (direct savings of ~$7,500 on same income)
- With PTET: Federal SALT deduction $18,000 × 37% = $6,660 federal savings
- Net LA out-of-pocket: $18,000 (PTET credit refunds $18,000 to owner)

**Savings**: $7,500/year LA rate drop alone. PTET adds $6,660 federal savings annually.

### Scenario 2: Multi-state business with LA operations
**Setup**: Texas-based S corp with LA operations (20% of sales); $2M total income.

**Analysis**:
- LA-sourced income: $400K (20% × $2M via single sales factor)
- LA PTET: $400K × 3% = $12,000 paid at entity level
- Owner gets $12,000 credit if resident of LA; or credit against home state tax if nonresident (typical OSC mechanics)
- TX owners: TX has no income tax, so no home-state credit; LA PTET avoided by making the election (owners file LA nonresident or composite)
- Post-reform 2026: No LA franchise tax (CFT-620 filed 2025 was last)

**Planning**: Any LA-operating corporation benefits from franchise tax repeal. PTET election still valuable for federal SALT benefit.

### Scenario 3: Capital gains sale of LA business
**Setup**: LA client selling interest in family business for $5M gain; completed 2024 (before reform).

**Pre-reform (2024 sale)**:
- LA net capital gains deduction potentially available (old law)
- If business qualified: deduction up to specified threshold [VERIFY old law specifics]
- LA tax on remainder at graduated rate (4.25% top)

**Same sale 2025+**:
- Net capital gains deduction **REPEALED** — no LA preference
- Tax at flat 3% on full gain: $5M × 3% = $150,000
- Federal: LTCG 20% + NIIT 3.8% + optional PTET for federal SALT on ordinary income only

**Priceless planning**: For LA clients with large capital gains events, timing matters significantly. Pre-2025 sales (completed via installment) preserved old deduction; post-2025 sales pay 3% on full gain. Not devastating given only 3% rate, but material for $10M+ events.

### Scenario 4: Retiree relocating to LA
**Setup**: Client age 68; $80K pension + $30K Social Security; relocating from GA to New Orleans.

**LA tax analysis (MFJ with spouse age 65, $20K pension)**:
- Social Security: Fully exempt ($30K)
- Pension exemption: $12,000 × 2 = $24,000
- Taxable pension: ($80K + $20K) - $24,000 = **$76,000 taxable**
- Standard deduction: $25,000 (MFJ) 
- Net taxable: $76,000 - $25,000 = **$51,000**
- LA tax: $51,000 × 3% = **$1,530**

**Comparison**: GA 5.19% flat on similar income ≈ $2,650. LA saves ~$1,100/year. Plus no estate tax in LA.

**Additional considerations**: LA property taxes are moderate; LA sales tax high (5-12% combined). Overall retirement tax picture favorable but sales tax on consumption offsets some income tax savings.
