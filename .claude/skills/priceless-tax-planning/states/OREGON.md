# Oregon (OR) — State Tax File

Oregon has among the **highest top individual income tax rates in the US** (9.9%), combined with **Portland-area local taxes** (Multnomah County 1.5% + Metro SHS 1%) that push effective combined rates to **12-14% for Portland high earners**.

Oregon has **NO sales tax** (one of 5 no-sales-tax states) — offset by high income tax.

Oregon estate tax threshold is **$1 million** — the **LOWEST in the US** (10% to 16% rates). For Priceless Oregon clients, estate planning is critical given low threshold.

**Oregon PTET EXPIRED December 31, 2025** — similar to Minnesota situation; creates planning gap for TY 2026.

Oregon also has a **Corporate Activity Tax (CAT)** — gross receipts tax alongside corporate excise tax. Complex business tax environment.

Low Priceless concentration. Some Portland-area clients and WA/OR border remote workers.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 4 brackets — 4.75% / 6.75% / 8.75% / **9.9% top** (at $125K single / $250K MFJ 2026)
- **Portland/Multnomah**: Additional 1.5% county tax + 1% Metro Supportive Housing Services tax on high earners → up to 2.5% additional in Portland area
- **Preschool for All (PFA) tax**: 1.5% on Multnomah County income > $125K single / $200K MFJ; 3% on income > $250K single / $400K MFJ
- **Corporate excise tax**: 6.6% on first $1M; 7.6% above $1M
- **Corporate Activity Tax (CAT)**: 0.57% on taxable commercial activity over $1M (since 2020)
- **PTET**: **EXPIRED after 12/31/2025** (re-enactment possibility [VERIFY])
- **Sales tax**: **NONE**
- **Estate tax**: **YES — $1M exemption** (LOWEST threshold in US); 10-16% graduated
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Pension income**: Up to $6,250 single / $12,500 MFJ deduction (age 62+)
- **Standard deduction (2025)**: $2,835 single / $5,670 MFJ (2026: $2,910 / $5,820) — much lower than federal
- **Kicker refund**: Automatic when state revenues exceed forecast by 2%+ (returned to taxpayers)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### Effective rates

Because lower brackets cover very small income amounts, most OR residents with moderate to high incomes pay **close to 8.75-9.9% effective**. Oregon's brackets don't provide meaningful progressivity at typical income levels — essentially a flat-ish 8.75-9.9% tax on wages above ~$22K MFJ.

### Standard deduction — LOW

Oregon standard deduction is unusually low:
- 2026: $2,910 single / $5,820 MFJ
- Compare federal: $16,100 / $32,200 for 2026

**Impact**: Oregon taxable income is typically much higher than federal taxable income because the state's standard deduction is so much lower.

### Personal exemption credit

Oregon offers a $256 per-qualifying-exemption credit (2025; [VERIFY 2026]) — NOT a deduction. Phases out:
- Single: Fully phased at $100K federal AGI
- MFJ: Fully phased at $200K federal AGI

### Federal tax liability subtraction

Oregon uniquely allows a **deduction for federal income tax liability paid**:
- 2025 cap: $8,500 combined federal + foreign tax (MFJ $8,500; MFS $4,250)
- Foreign tax within that cap: $3,000 max ($1,500 MFS)
- Capped and inflation-indexed

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: 100% exempt
- **Pension income**: Up to $6,250 single / $12,500 MFJ for age 62+
- **Military retirement**: Partial exemption (follows pension deduction)

### Oregon "Kicker"

Oregon's unique constitutional provision: When state revenues exceed the forecast by 2% or more for the biennium, the excess is **returned to taxpayers as a refund** (the "kicker").
- 2024 kicker: $5.61 billion refunded
- Applied as credit against tax liability or refund
- Has returned in ~half of biennial cycles

### Capital gains

Oregon taxes capital gains as ordinary income at 4.75-9.9%. No preferential rate (unlike federal LTCG).

## Portland/Multnomah County tax layers — critical for Priceless

Portland-area residents face **3 separate tax layers**:

### 1. Multnomah County Business Income Tax
- 2% on business profits

### 2. Multnomah County Preschool for All (PFA) Tax
- **1.5%** on income > $125K single / $200K MFJ (above threshold amount)
- **3%** on income > $250K single / $400K MFJ (above that threshold)
- Since 2021

### 3. Metro Supportive Housing Services (SHS) Tax
- **1%** on income > $125K single / $200K MFJ (above threshold)
- Applies to Metro region (Multnomah, Clackamas, Washington counties)

### Combined Portland effective rate

For a Multnomah County resident earning $300K single:
- State: 9.9% × $300K (above threshold) = significant
- PFA: 1.5% on ($250K-$125K) = $1,875; 3% on ($300K-$250K) = $1,500 = $3,375
- Metro SHS: 1% × ($300K-$125K) = $1,750
- **Total OR state+local income tax effective rate: approximately 12-14%** for Portland high earners

### Washington border arbitrage

Clark County, Washington (across Columbia from Portland):
- Washington has NO income tax
- WA residents working in OR: Oregon sources wages TO OR for OR tax (convenience-of-employer not applied in OR)
- Commuters pay OR tax at 9.9% top but avoid Portland/Multnomah local layers
- Best of both: WA residence (0% income tax on non-OR income) + OR work income at OR rates

For Priceless clients considering Pacific Northwest, WA-side residency dramatically reduces state tax burden.

## Oregon estate tax — $1M exemption (LOWEST in US)

Oregon has the lowest estate tax threshold of any state:
- **Exemption: $1,000,000** (NOT indexed for inflation)
- **Rates: 10% to 16% graduated**
- **Applies to**: OR residents' worldwide estates; nonresidents' OR-situs property
- **Portability**: Oregon does NOT offer portability
- **Gift tax**: None

### Rate schedule (approximate)

| Taxable Estate (above $1M exemption) | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 - $500K | 10% |
| $500,001 - $1M | 10.25% |
| $1M - $1.5M | 10.5% |
| Increasing | Up to 16% |

### Worked example

$5M OR estate:
- Taxable: $5M - $1M = $4M
- Estate tax: Approximately $400,000-$500,000 (at ~10-12% blended)

This is a **significant planning concern** for any OR homeowner (Portland home values alone often exceed $1M; add retirement accounts and life insurance, easily hit threshold).

### Planning options

- **Credit shelter trusts (AB trusts)**: Use each spouse's $1M exemption separately at each death
- **Lifetime gifting**: Oregon has no gift tax; federal $19K annual exclusion ($38K MFJ)
- **Trust structures**: Irrevocable trusts to remove from OR estate
- **Relocation**: Move to no-estate-tax state before death
- **Life insurance trusts**: ILITs to keep insurance outside estate

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) — EXPIRED

Oregon PTET **expired for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025** (similar to MN).

- **Pre-2026**: Election available; 9.9% rate; refundable credit
- **TY 2026+**: NOT available until/unless re-enactment legislation passes
- **Re-enactment**: Possible legislative priority; [VERIFY current status]

### Impact for Priceless clients

Loss of PTET for TY 2026 is material for OR pass-through owners:
- Pre-2026: PTET allowed federal SALT deduction workaround for state income tax
- Post-2026: OR residents pay 9.9% + Portland layers directly on personal return; federal SALT cap limits deduction

For a Portland S corp owner with $500K income:
- OR state tax: $500K × 9.9% ≈ $49,500
- Portland PFA: $7,500 (at 3% on top bracket)
- Metro SHS: $3,750
- **Total OR income tax**: ~$60,750
- Federal SALT deduction: Limited to $40K (2026) if MAGI < $500K; $10K if MAGI > $600K
- **Lost federal deduction**: ~$20K × 37% = ~$7,400/year federal tax cost vs. prior PTET availability

## Corporate excise tax and Corporate Activity Tax (CAT)

### Corporate Excise Tax
- 6.6% on first $1M of OR taxable income
- 7.6% above $1M

### Corporate Activity Tax (CAT) — since 2020
- **0.57% on Oregon commercial activity** above $1M threshold
- Gross receipts based (not income)
- Applies to most businesses operating in OR

### Combined impact

Oregon business with $10M OR gross receipts, $500K net income:
- Corporate excise: $500K × 6.6% = $33,000
- CAT: ($10M - $1M threshold) × 0.57% = $51,300
- **Combined**: $84,300 on $500K net income = **16.9% effective rate**

This is extraordinarily high. Oregon's combined business tax is among the highest in US.

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Portland professional
**Setup**: Portland resident; $250K W-2 salary.

**2026 OR+local tax**:
- State: $250K at blended rates → approximately $23,000 (8.75-9.9%)
- PFA (Multnomah): 1.5% × $125K (amount between $125K and $250K) = $1,875
- Metro SHS: 1% × $125K = $1,250
- **Total OR state+local: ~$26,125** (effective 10.5%)

### Scenario 2: Oregon business owner post-PTET expiration
**Setup**: Portland resident; S corp with $600K OR-source income.

**2025 (with PTET)**:
- PTET: $600K × 9.9% = $59,400 at entity; federal SALT $59,400 × 37% = $21,978 savings

**2026 (no PTET)**:
- Owner pays on personal: $600K × 9.9% = $59,400 + Portland layers = ~$74K total
- Federal SALT cap: Only $10K deductible (if MAGI > $600K)
- Lost federal deduction: ~$64K × 37% = **~$23,680 additional federal tax**

Significant effective tax increase from PTET expiration for OR pass-through owners.

### Scenario 3: Oregon $3M estate
**Setup**: OR resident; $3M estate (includes $1.5M Portland home, $1M retirement, $500K other).

**Analysis**:
- Federal: $3M < $15M → **$0 federal**
- OR: $3M - $1M exemption = $2M taxable; approximately **$200,000 Oregon estate tax** at 10% blended

$200K Oregon estate tax for a relatively modest estate — major planning issue.

### Scenario 4: WA commuter
**Setup**: WA resident (Vancouver); works in Portland for OR employer; $150K salary.

**Analysis**:
- OR taxes nonresident wages earned in OR: $150K × ~9% effective = ~$13,500
- No Portland local tax on nonresidents (resident-based)
- WA: No income tax
- **Total**: ~$13,500 OR tax (saved Portland local layers ~$2-3K)

Moving to WA side reduces state tax by Portland local layers and by OR estate tax (if maintained WA domicile).
