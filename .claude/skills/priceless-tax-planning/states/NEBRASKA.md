# Nebraska (NE) — State Tax File

Nebraska executed one of the more aggressive state tax-reduction packages via **LB 754 (2023)**: top rate from 6.84% (pre-reform) dropping to **4.55% for 2026** and **3.99% for 2027**. Bracket structure consolidated from 4 to 3 brackets effective TY 2026. Corporate rate also reduced from 5.20% to 4.55% for 2026. **Social Security fully exempted** (100%) from TY 2025+. Military retirement and federal retirement income also fully exempt.

Very low Priceless concentration. Some Omaha/Lincoln clients; agricultural/insurance industry.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 3 brackets (2026) — 2.46% / 3.51% / **4.55% top**; 2027 scheduled top rate **3.99%**
- **Corporate income tax**: **4.55% flat (2026)**; scheduled 3.99% 2027 (reduced from 5.20% in 2025)
- **PTET**: Yes (LB 754; elective)
- **Sales tax**: 5.5% state; local up to 2.5% (combined max 8%); food exempt
- **Estate tax**: None
- **Inheritance tax**: **YES** — by county! (one of 6 states with inheritance tax); Class A (spouse/children/parents/grandchildren): exempt up to $100K then 1% above; Class B (more distant): exempt up to $40K then 11%; Class C (unrelated): exempt $25K then 15%
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: **100% fully exempt** (effective TY 2025+ per LB 754)
- **Military retirement**: 100% exempt
- **Federal pension**: 100% exempt (effective 2024 per LB 754)
- **Standard deduction 2025**: $7,900 single / $15,800 MFJ
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### IMPORTANT: Brackets are NOT inflation-adjusted

Per § 77-2715.03(3)(a), the minimum/maximum bracket amounts are adjusted for inflation — BUT the statute specifies this adjustment mechanism for pre-2018 thresholds. **The 2026 bracket thresholds established by LB 754 are FIXED** (not automatically indexed). This creates potential bracket creep over time if wages grow.

### Retirement income (LB 754 enhancements)

- **Social Security**: **100% fully exempt** effective TY 2025 (phased from 80% in 2024, 60% in 2023, 40% prior)
- **Military retirement**: **100% exempt** effective 2022 (LB 387, 2021)
- **Federal retirement (Civil Service)**: **100% exempt** effective 2024 (LB 754)
- **Nebraska National Guard income**: Excluded (LB 1023, 2024)
- **Private pensions and IRA/401(k)**: Taxed as ordinary income (no special exemption)

### Nebraska 529 plan subtraction

Up to **$10,000 per return** (not per taxpayer) for contributions to Nebraska 529 (NEST) plan. Significant benefit for education funding.

### Convenience of Employer Rule

Nebraska is one of 5 states with a **convenience of employer rule** (NE, NY, CT, DE, PA) per § 77-2733(8)(c):
- Non-resident remote worker for NE employer: NE sources the income if work is performed outside NE "for the employee's convenience" rather than employer necessity
- Reciprocal application: Other convenience-of-employer states (CT, NY) may source similar income to NE residents working for their employers

**Priceless action**: For any clients with NE employer ties or NE residents working for CT/NY/DE/PA employers, must carefully analyze convenience of employer implications.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) — LB 754

- **Rate**: Top individual rate (4.55% for 2026; 3.99% 2027+)
- **Base**: NE-source income of electing entity
- **Election**: Annual; made on entity return
- **Credit**: Refundable to owners

### Worked example

S corp with $400K NE-source income; one owner:
- 2026 PTET: $400K × 4.55% = $18,200 at entity level
- Federal SALT deduction: $18,200 × 37% = $6,734 federal savings
- Owner gets $18,200 refundable credit

- 2027 PTET: $400K × 3.99% = $15,960 at entity level (rate drop saves $2,240)

## Corporate income tax

- **2025**: 5.20% flat
- **2026**: **4.55% flat** (per LB 754)

- **Base**: Federal taxable income with NE modifications

- **NOL**: 20-year carryforward; no carryback

## Nebraska inheritance tax (important unique feature)

Nebraska is one of 6 US states (IA, KY, MD, NE, NJ, PA — 2 of these phased out) with an active inheritance tax. Key features:

### Administration

- **County-administered** (not state); each of 93 NE counties handles inheritance tax
- **Beneficiary-level** tax (not estate-level like federal)
- **Applies to**: Transfers at death to beneficiaries of NE decedent or NE-situs property
- **Payable within 12 months of death** (county court)

### Classes and rates (2025)

**Class A (immediate family)** — spouse, parents, children (including adopted), grandchildren, siblings:
- Exemption: $100,000 per beneficiary
- Rate: 1% on amount above exemption

**Class B (more distant relatives)** — nieces, nephews, aunts, uncles, step-family relationships:
- Exemption: $40,000 per beneficiary
- Rate: 11% on amount above exemption

**Class C (more distant)** — cousins, unrelated individuals, friends, non-charitable:
- Exemption: $25,000 per beneficiary
- Rate: 15% on amount above exemption

### Planning implications

- NE inheritance tax is notably lower than KY, NJ, PA versions
- Spouse fully exempt (Class A + unlimited marital deduction at federal)
- Charitable beneficiaries: Generally exempt [VERIFY specific charitable qualification]
- Priceless clients with NE-situs property: flag for inheritance tax even if client not NE resident

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Omaha small business S corp
**Setup**: NE resident; S corp with $500K NE-source income.

**2025**: $500K × 5.20% = $26,000 state tax (or PTET equivalent)
**2026**: $500K × 4.55% = **$22,750** (saves $3,250)
**2027**: $500K × 3.99% = **$19,950** (saves $6,050 vs. 2025)

Meaningful rate drops for consistently-profitable businesses.

### Scenario 2: Retiree from NE
**Setup**: NE resident; 70; MFJ with spouse 68; $80K combined pension (private) + $42K Social Security + $30K IRA distributions.

**2026 analysis**:
- Social Security: 100% exempt ($42K)
- Military retirement/federal pension: N/A (not applicable here)
- Private pension: Taxed as ordinary ($80K)
- IRA: Taxed as ordinary ($30K)
- Taxable: $110,000
- Less MFJ standard deduction: $15,800
- Less personal exemptions (if applicable): [VERIFY]
- Net NE taxable: approximately $94,200
- NE tax: approximately $4,200 (at 4.55% blended)

Compared to states with full private pension exemption: NE is less favorable than MS, PA, MO on private pensions but on par with most other states.

### Scenario 3: NE inheritance tax planning
**Setup**: NE resident decedent; $2M estate; beneficiaries are surviving spouse ($1M), 2 children ($400K each), nephew ($100K), college foundation ($100K).

**Analysis**:
- Spouse (Class A + marital): $1M exempt
- Children (Class A, 2 × $100K exemption + 1% above): Each gets $100K exempt, taxed at 1% on $300K = $3,000 each; total $6,000
- Nephew (Class B, $40K exempt + 11% above): Taxed at 11% on $60K = $6,600
- Foundation (Class C or exempt if qualifying charity): [VERIFY charity-specific treatment]; if Class C, 15% on $75K = $11,250

**Total inheritance tax**: approximately $12,000-$24,000 depending on charity treatment.

Relatively modest compared to KY ($20-40K on similar profile) due to low NE rates (1%/11%/15% vs. KY's 4-16%).

### Scenario 4: Remote worker convenience rule
**Setup**: NE resident; works remotely for Connecticut employer.

**Analysis**:
- CT has convenience-of-employer rule → CT sources NE resident's income TO CT for CT tax purposes
- NE resident taxed in both states:
  - CT: wages sourced to CT per convenience rule
  - NE: resident taxed on worldwide income
- Credit for OSC: NE allows credit for CT tax paid (up to NE rate on same income)
- Net: NE resident pays HIGHER of CT rate (6.99% top) or NE rate (4.55%/3.99%); since CT > NE, CT rate is effective rate

**Planning**: Consider establishing CT work presence (short visits) and negotiating with CT employer to move to "necessity" work location; or structure through S corp / independent contractor relationship to avoid wage-sourcing issues. Also note CT 60% Convenience Challenge Credit (2025 budget, retroactive to TY 2020) may provide relief.
