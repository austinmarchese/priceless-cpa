# Colorado (CO) — State Tax File

**TABOR-constrained flat tax state**: Colorado's 4.4% flat income tax rate is constitutionally required (TABOR — Colo. Const. Art. X, §20). Rate has been reduced multiple times as TABOR compliance mechanism (4.63% → 4.50% → 4.40%). TABOR requires voter approval for tax increases, giving significant long-term rate stability.

**Common Priceless destination**: Denver/Boulder tech ecosystem (Google, Amazon, startups); Summit/Eagle/Pitkin counties (ski resorts) for HNW residents; Colorado Springs for government contractors and retirees.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 4.4% flat (constitutionally single rate under TABOR)
- **TABOR refunds**: Annual when state revenue exceeds inflation + population growth cap
- **Corporate income tax**: 4.4% flat (same rate as individual; may be reduced midyear if TABOR surplus)
- **PTET**: Yes (SALT Parity Act; 4.4% entity rate); form DR 0104BEP
- **Sales tax**: 2.9% state + local up to 8.3% (Denver 8.81%; Aspen 9.3%; combined max ~11.2%)
- **Denver OPT**: $5.75/month employee + $4/month employer (for employees earning $500+/month)
- **Estate/inheritance/gift tax**: NONE
- **Property tax**: effective 0.48% (low; 0.60% per some sources)
- **Pension exclusion**: Ages 55-64 up to $20K; age 65+ up to $24K
- **Social Security**: partially taxable; age 65+ full exemption

## Personal income tax

### Rate: 4.4% flat

**TABOR constitutional requirement**: All taxable income must be taxed at one rate (no graduated brackets permitted; unique US structure).

**Historical rate reductions**:
- 1999-2019: 4.63%
- 2020: 4.50% (permanent reduction)
- 2022: 4.40% (temporary reduction to 4.50%)
- **2023+: 4.40% (permanent)**

**Further reduction possible**: TABOR allows legislature to temporarily reduce rate when revenue exceeds cap. Voters can also approve permanent reduction.

### Starting point: federal taxable income

Colorado uses federal taxable income as starting point (NOT federal AGI). CO additions and subtractions apply.

**Common CO subtractions**:
- Pension/annuity income: up to $20K (age 55-64); up to $24K (age 65+)
- Social Security: 65+ may qualify for full exemption if income-eligible
- Military retirement: phased-in exclusion; $10K (2026 age-dependent)
- US government bond interest
- 529 contributions

**Common CO additions**:
- State/local bond interest from other states
- [VERIFY specific CO bonus depreciation position post-OBBBA]

### Standard deduction

Colorado uses federal standard deduction directly (no separate CO deduction). $15,000 single / $30,000 MFJ (2025); $15,750/$31,500 (2026 projected).

### Capital gains

Taxed at flat 4.4%. No preferential rate. Some exclusions historically for CO-source business sales [VERIFY current status].

## PTET — SALT Parity Act (C.R.S. §39-22-343)

**Rate**: 4.4% flat (matches individual rate)
**Election**: Annual; made on CO entity return
**Eligible**: S corps, partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships
**Owner credit**: Refundable credit on CO personal return
**Federal SALT deductible**: Yes

**Form**: DR 0104BEP (PTET payment form); corresponding entity return reports election

**Planning**: Since PTET rate matches individual rate, election is tax-neutral at CO level. Value captured entirely at federal level via SALT cap workaround.

**Example**: $300K S corp owner K-1 income:
- Without PTET: $300K × 4.4% = $13,200 CO tax; no federal deduction (SALT cap)
- With PTET: Entity pays $13,200; federal deduction $13,200 × 37% = $4,884 federal savings; owner gets $13,200 CO credit

## TABOR refunds

**Mechanism**: When state revenue exceeds TABOR cap (inflation + population growth), surplus must be refunded to taxpayers or voters must approve state keeping funds.

**Refund distribution varies by year**:
- Sales tax refund (flat per-capita amount to all taxpayers)
- Temporary income tax rate reduction
- Property tax reduction
- State tax credit on income tax return

**Recent refunds**:
- TY 2022: large refund via flat $750 per taxpayer distribution (also rate reduction to 4.5% retroactive)
- TY 2023-2024: refunds via six-tier structure based on income
- TY 2025-2026: [VERIFY current TABOR mechanism]

**Priceless client planning**: TABOR refunds are federally taxable as other income. Coordinate federal reporting.

## Corporate income tax

**Rate**: 4.4% flat (same as individual)
**Base**: Federal taxable income with CO modifications
**Apportionment**: Single sales factor (post-2009 phase-in complete)
**Combined reporting**: Required for unitary groups
**TABOR surplus reduction**: CO DOR can announce midyear reduced rate if TABOR revenue triggers

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 2.9% (low)
**Local rates**: 0-8.3% (high variability; resort towns highest)
**Combined max**: 11.2% (some Breckenridge/Vail area locations)
**Food for home consumption**: exempt from state tax; may be subject to local

**Major markets**:
- Denver: 8.81% combined
- Boulder: 8.845%
- Colorado Springs: 8.2%
- Aurora: 8.0%
- Aspen: 9.3%

**Home rule cities**: Many CO cities (Denver, Boulder, Colorado Springs) self-collect sales tax and have their own economic nexus rules in addition to state. Complex multi-jurisdictional filings.

**Economic nexus (state level)**:
- $100,000 in CO retail sales in prior or current calendar year
- Marketplace facilitator collection required
- Home rule cities: separate thresholds possible

**SUTS (Sales & Use Tax System)**: CO DOR online portal simplifies multi-jurisdictional filings for state-collected localities; does NOT handle home rule cities.

## Denver Occupational Privilege Tax (OPT)

**Authority**: Denver Revised Municipal Code Sec. 53-32
**Employee tax**: $5.75/month for employees earning $500+/month in Denver
**Employer tax**: $4.00/month per qualifying employee
**Filing**: Quarterly via Denver eBiz Tax Center

Several other CO cities have similar "head taxes" (Aurora, Sheridan, Glendale, Greenwood Village). Not income-based; flat per-employee.

## Property tax

**Effective rate**: 0.48-0.60% (below national average)
**Assessment**: Residential at 6.7%-6.95% of actual value (TABOR-regulated); commercial 29%
**Mill levies**: Local (school districts, counties, special districts)
**Senior homestead exemption**: 50% of first $200K ($100K exemption) of primary residence for 65+ residents of 10+ years
**Gallagher Amendment**: Repealed 2020 (removed residential/commercial tax ratio requirement)

## Estate, gift, and inheritance tax

NONE. Colorado repealed its estate tax in 2005 (no CO estate tax post-2004 decedents).

## Residency

Standard domicile factors. CO has no bright-line day count for state tax (unlike NY 183-day rule). Domicile-based.

## Priceless client considerations

### Denver/Boulder tech and startup clients
- QSBS sales: CO conforms to §1202; gains excluded
- Equity compensation (RSUs, ISOs): CO taxes at ordinary rate
- Pre-IPO relocation planning: less compelling vs. CA→FL (save only 4.4% vs. save 13.3%)

### S corporation owners resident in CO
- 4.4% entity rate at PTET level matches individual
- PTET election captures federal SALT deduction value
- Standard compliance on Forms 104, 106, DR 0104BEP

### Real estate investors
- Low CO property tax (0.48% effective)
- 4.4% CO income tax on rental income
- No state transfer tax (local RETT may apply)
- Home rule city sales tax complexity for short-term rentals

### Mountain resort / second home
- Summit County (Breckenridge), Eagle County (Vail), Pitkin County (Aspen), San Miguel County (Telluride)
- High property values; effective rates moderate (0.5-0.7%)
- Short-term rental regulations tightening in many resort towns
- Sales tax high in resort counties (9-11% combined)

### Retirees
- Social Security partially taxed (age 65+ may qualify for full exemption)
- Pension/401(k)/IRA: $20K-$24K exemption by age
- Military retirement: phased-in exclusion
- Moderate property tax; low CO rate
- Climate/lifestyle factor

### Tax-driven relocations
- Less common than CA→FL or NY→FL (CO's 4.4% is not bad)
- CO→FL or CO→NV sometimes occurs for HNW exits
- Common inbound from CA, NY for tech/lifestyle, not tax

## Multi-state mechanics

### CO resident with out-of-state income
- Credit for taxes paid to other states (C.R.S. §39-22-108)
- Form 104 with Schedule C

### Non-resident with CO income
- Form 104PN (part-year) or Form 104NR
- CO tax on CO-source income only

### Multi-city CO operations
- Home rule cities separate filing from state
- SUTS simplifies state-collected localities only
- Denver OPT flat tax applies to Denver employees
