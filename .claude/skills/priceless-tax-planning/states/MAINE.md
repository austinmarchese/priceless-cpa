# Maine (ME) — State Tax File

Maine has a progressive 3-bracket income tax (5.8%/6.75%/7.15%) that applies to income earned by Maine residents on a worldwide basis. Unlike many southern states, Maine has NOT moved toward flat-rate or rate-reduction reforms. Estate tax threshold is $7.16M for 2026 (well below federal $15M exemption). The pension income deduction for retirees is generous but capped.

Very low Priceless concentration. Occasional vacation home or coastal property owners; remote workers.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 3-bracket graduated 5.8% / 6.75% / 7.15% (2026 brackets inflation-adjusted by 1.303 factor on low end, 1.298 on high end)
- **Corporate income tax**: 3.50% to 8.93% (4 brackets); top bracket on corporate income > $3.5M
- **PTET**: Yes (elective)
- **Sales tax**: 5.5% state; NO LOCAL sales tax (uniform); 8% on prepared food; 9% on lodging; 10% on car rentals
- **Estate tax**: **YES — $7.16M exemption 2026**; graduated 8-12% rates
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None (federal only; $19K annual exclusion 2026)
- **Standard deduction 2025**: $15,000 single/MFS; $30,000 MFJ; $22,500 HoH (equals federal)
- **Personal exemption 2025**: $5,150 per taxpayer (ME-specific; inflation-adjusted)
- **Pension income deduction 2025**: up to $48,216 per taxpayer (increased from prior levels)
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Property tax**: Average 1.09% effective
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms to federal); automatic 6-month extension to October 15

## Personal income tax

### Brackets (2026 — inflation-adjusted)

Maine inflation-adjusts brackets annually using a cost-of-living adjustment factor. For 2026, the factor is 1.303 on low dollar amounts and 1.298 on high dollar amounts, applied to 36 M.R.S. § 5111's base figures.

### Standard deduction and personal exemption

Maine conforms standard deduction amounts to federal amounts (though has own inflation adjustment). 2025 figures:
- Single/MFS: $15,000
- MFJ/QSS: $30,000
- HoH: $22,500
- Additional standard deduction for age 65+ or blind: additional amounts apply [VERIFY]

Personal exemption: $5,150 per taxpayer (2025); 2026 adjusted by 1.279 factor ≈ **$5,275** for 2026

### Retirement income treatment

Maine provides a **pension income deduction** of up to **$48,216 per taxpayer** (2025). This is a significant state-specific benefit for retirees:
- Applies to: Qualified pension, 401(k), 403(b), IRA distributions
- Per-taxpayer cap (so MFJ couple with each retiree's pension can get $96,432 deduction)
- Phaseout begins at specified income thresholds [VERIFY 2025 phaseout threshold]
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt (not counted against $48,216 cap; unlimited)
- **Military retirement**: Fully exempt (not counted against pension cap)

Maine's retirement treatment has gotten more favorable over time. The pension deduction cap was increased via 2022-2023 legislation.

### Federal itemization

Maine allows either standard deduction OR itemized deductions (similar to federal). Maine itemized deductions follow federal Schedule A with some ME-specific modifications (state taxes not deductible; some other adjustments).

### Capital gains

Maine taxes capital gains as ordinary income at 5.8%/6.75%/7.15% brackets. No preferential capital gains rate.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

Maine enacted an elective PTET effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2022. Key features:
- **Rate**: Highest marginal rate (7.15%)
- **Election**: Annual; made on entity's return
- **Credit**: Refundable credit to owners
- **Estimated payments**: Quarterly if entity PTET liability > $1,000
- **Forms**: Form 1040ME-PTE (entity level); Schedule NR-CR (nonresident credits)

### Worked example

S corp with $500K Maine-source income; single resident owner:
- Without PTET: Owner's Maine tax ~ $33,000 (at top bracket 7.15%)
- With PTET: Entity pays $500K × 7.15% = $35,750; federal deduction at entity level saves $35,750 × 37% = $13,228; owner gets $35,750 refundable credit
- Net Maine cost: $0; federal savings: $13,228

## Maine estate tax

Maine is one of 12 states with an active estate tax, and Maine's exemption ($7.16M for 2026) is significantly **below the federal exemption** ($15M). This creates a planning gap where Maine estate tax can apply even when no federal estate tax is owed.

### 2026 exemption: $7.16M

Indexed annually. 2024 was $6.8M; 2025 was $6.94M; 2026 is $7.16M.

### Rate schedule (graduated 8-12%)

| Taxable Estate (above exemption) | Base Tax | Marginal Rate |
|---|---|---|
| $0 - $3,000,000 | $0 | 8% |
| $3,000,001 - $6,000,000 | $240,000 | 10% |
| Above $6,000,000 | $540,000 | 12% |

### Worked example — $11.56M Maine estate

- Taxable estate: $11.56M - $7.16M exemption = $4.40M
- Base tax at $3M bracket: $240,000
- Marginal 10% on ($4.40M - $3M) = $140,000
- **Total Maine estate tax: $380,000**

Federal estate tax: $11.56M < $15M federal exemption → $0 federal. But Maine collects $380K. This is the planning gap.

### Planning implications

- Maine QTIP elections can reduce exposure
- Lifetime gifting within federal annual exclusion ($19K/donee 2026)
- Portability: Maine DOES NOT have portability between spouses (federal does); use of first spouse's exemption must be elected at first death
- Credit shelter trusts valuable for Maine residents
- Real estate in Maine (including seasonal/vacation homes) is subject to Maine estate tax for nonresidents too

## Sales and use tax

- **Rate: 5.5%** state; **NO LOCAL** sales tax
- Uniform across state (simple)
- **Groceries (unprepared food)**: Exempt
- **Prescription drugs**: Exempt
- **Most clothing**: Exempt
- **Prepared food**: 8% (higher rate)
- **Lodging**: 9%
- **Car rentals**: 10%
- **Service tax**: Not generally imposed (most services exempt)

## Residency

Maine uses standard domicile + statutory residency tests:
- **Domicile**: Permanent home, intent to remain
- **Statutory**: >183 days physical presence PLUS permanent place of abode

For Priceless clients with Maine vacation/seasonal property:
- Physical presence matters; >183 days triggers residency even if domiciled elsewhere
- Maine has aggressive residency audits for high-value seasonal property owners
- "Green card" residency rules apply: claiming Maine residency for any state purpose (driver's license, voting, homestead) can trigger income tax residency

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic 6-month to October 15 (no form required if federal extension)
- **Payment**: Must pay 90% of total tax by April 15 to avoid late payment penalty
- **Forms**: 1040ME (resident); 1040ME-NR (nonresident)
- **Estimated payments**: Quarterly (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15)
- **Safe harbor**: Lower of 100% prior year (110% if AGI > $150K) or 90% current year

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Miami client with Maine vacation home
**Setup**: Client domiciled FL; owns $3M oceanfront property in Kennebunkport; spends 90 days/year there.

**Analysis**:
- FL resident: FL has no income tax
- Not statutory resident of Maine (< 183 days)
- Maine tax only on ME-source income (rental, etc.)
- Maine property tax applies; estate tax applies to Maine real estate for nonresidents

**Estate planning**:
- If client's worldwide estate is $10M (including $3M ME property): No federal estate tax ($10M < $15M)
- **Maine estate tax on Maine-sourced portion**: $3M ME property; $3M < $7.16M exemption → $0 Maine estate tax
- If worldwide estate is $20M with $5M in ME: Maine estate tax applies (Maine-sourced portion taxed by ME on its own exemption test)
- If worldwide estate is $20M with $10M in ME: Maine-sourced portion $10M; exceeds $7.16M exemption → Maine tax applies at 8-12% on $2.84M excess = ~$227K

### Scenario 2: Maine remote worker for out-of-state employer
**Setup**: Maine resident working remotely for Massachusetts employer.

**Analysis**:
- Maine resident = Maine tax on all income (worldwide)
- Massachusetts convenience of employer rule: MA does NOT have convenience rule; taxes based on work location
- Worker physically in Maine → MA does not tax the wages
- Maine taxes at 5.8-7.15%; no credit needed

**Action**: Verify MA employer is NOT withholding MA tax. If so, request employer stop and file MA non-resident for refund.

### Scenario 3: Retiree choosing Maine
**Setup**: MFJ couple, both 66; $100K combined pension income; $40K combined Social Security.

**Analysis**:
- Social Security: Fully exempt ($40K)
- Pension deduction: $48,216 × 2 = $96,432 joint
- Remaining pension: $100,000 - $96,432 = $3,568 taxable
- Standard deduction (MFJ): $30,000
- Personal exemptions (2 × $5,150) = $10,300 [VERIFY applicability]
- Net taxable: Very low; tax ~ $0 to $200

Maine is quite retirement-friendly with the $48,216 per taxpayer pension deduction.

### Scenario 4: Sale of seasonal business
**Setup**: Maine-based S corp (lobster business); $2M sale; single owner/operator, Maine resident.

**Analysis**:
- Capital gains taxed as ordinary in ME at 7.15%
- PTET election: $2M × 7.15% = $143,000 Maine tax
- Federal: LTCG ~ 20% + NIIT 3.8% on $2M = $476,000 federal
- With PTET: Maine tax paid at entity level; federal SALT deduction of $143K × 37% bracket = ~$53,000 savings

**Planning**: PTET election provides meaningful federal SALT benefit on large ME income events.
