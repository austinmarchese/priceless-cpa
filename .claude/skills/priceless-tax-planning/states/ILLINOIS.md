# Illinois — State Tax Reference

## The essentials

Illinois has a flat 4.95% personal income tax (per IL Constitution Art. IX §3(a) — the state's constitution prohibits graduated income tax without constitutional amendment; 2020 ballot proposition to allow graduated tax was rejected). Combined with Chicago's and Cook County's tax environment, Illinois has higher effective taxes than the flat rate suggests.

Illinois's pass-through entity tax (PTE tax) was enacted in 2021 as a SALT cap workaround. Originally scheduled to sunset December 31, 2025, legislation enacted December 12, 2025 removed the sunset — **Illinois PTE tax is now PERMANENT**. This is a major planning factor for Illinois S corp and partnership clients.

Illinois corporate income tax is 9.5% total (7% income tax + 2.5% personal property replacement tax), among the highest state corporate rates. Illinois has lower property tax than New Jersey but property taxes in Chicago/Cook County and affluent suburbs are significant. Sales tax is 6.25% state + substantial local additions.

For Priceless, Illinois clients typically need: (1) PTE tax election evaluation (made permanent post-December 2025), (2) attention to Illinois's specific add-back/modification rules for federal depreciation differences, and (3) coordination for multi-state businesses with Illinois presence.

## Personal income tax

### Rate structure (2026)

**Flat rate**: 4.95% of net income

Verified via Tax Foundation (February 2026) and Illinois Department of Revenue.

### No brackets, no standard deduction (structurally)

Illinois uses federal adjusted gross income as starting point with Illinois modifications (per 35 ILCS 5/203).

**Personal exemption (2026)**: $2,925 per person (indexed annually; per 35 ILCS 5/204)
**Senior/disabled additional exemption**: $1,000

**No standard deduction** — Illinois does not have one.

**Income calculation**:
- Start with federal AGI
- Add back: federal depreciation differences (see conformity matrix)
- Subtract: Illinois subtractions (retirement income, certain municipal bond interest, etc.)
- Result: Illinois base income
- Apply 4.95% rate

### Retirement income advantages (NOTABLE)

Illinois does NOT tax retirement income:
- Social Security: not taxed
- Pension income (qualified plans): not taxed
- IRA and 401(k) distributions: not taxed
- Annuity income from qualified plans: not taxed

This is a significant advantage for Illinois retirees compared to most states (Illinois is one of few states with complete retirement income exemption). Many retirees choose to stay in Illinois rather than relocate to FL purely for this reason.

### Residency rules

Illinois uses "resident" vs. "non-resident" per 35 ILCS 5/202:

**Resident**: Domiciled in Illinois for any portion of year
**Part-year resident**: moved into or out of Illinois during year
**Non-resident**: earned Illinois-source income without being Illinois resident

**Domicile test (35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20))**: 
- Principal home
- Intent to make Illinois permanent home
- Physical presence

**No strict day-count statutory residency rule** like NY (which has 183-day + permanent place of abode test). Illinois residency audit posture is less aggressive than NY but still active.

### Chicago and Cook County local considerations

Chicago has various use-based taxes (amusement tax, lease tax, etc.) but no city income tax on wages. Cook County does not impose additional income tax.

Chicago Home Rule Unit (Cook County part of Chicago) imposes:
- 1.25% transaction tax on non-titled personal property
- Various amusement / facility use taxes
- No broad income tax

## Business taxes

### Corporate income tax

**Rate structure**: 7% Illinois corporate income tax + 2.5% Personal Property Replacement Tax (PPRT) = 9.5% combined

**Authority**: 35 ILCS 5/201

**PPRT (2.5%)**: Separately tracked; goes to local governments in lieu of pre-1979 personal property tax

**Total 9.5% rate**: Among highest state corporate rates in nation

### Personal Property Replacement Tax (PPRT) — unique Illinois feature

Applies to:
- Corporations: 2.5% on net income (part of 9.5% combined rate above)
- S corporations: 1.5% on net income (in addition to any other S corp considerations)
- Partnerships: 1.5% on net income
- Trusts: 1.5%

**Important**: S corporations and partnerships pay 1.5% PPRT at entity level (in addition to pass-through to owners). This is separate from the elective PTE tax.

### PTE Tax (Illinois pass-through entity tax) — MADE PERMANENT 12/12/2025

**Authority**: 35 ILCS 5/201(a), as amended by Public Act [VERIFY specific PA citation, December 12, 2025 legislation removing sunset]

**Originally**: Enacted 2021 as SALT workaround; scheduled to sunset December 31, 2025
**December 12, 2025**: Illinois legislature enacted legislation removing sunset — PTE tax is now PERMANENT
**2026 and beyond**: PTE tax election available indefinitely

### Rate

**4.95% on Illinois taxable income** (same as personal flat rate)

Applied to partnership or S corp's Illinois-allocated net income.

### Eligibility

**Electing entity can be**:
- Partnerships (including LLCs treated as partnerships)
- S corporations
- Does not apply to sole proprietorships or single-member LLCs disregarded for federal

### Election mechanics

**Method**: Election made on timely-filed original Illinois return (Form IL-1065 for partnerships; Form IL-1120-ST for S corps) via checkbox on the return

**Unlike NY**: No separate pre-election deadline; made with the tax return

**Deadline**: Original return due date including extensions (March 15 for calendar year entities; September 15 if extended)

**Revocation**: Cannot be revoked after election made on return

**Annual**: Must be made each year; no multi-year election

### Credit to owners

**Flow-through mechanics**:
- Electing entity pays 4.95% PTE tax on Illinois taxable income
- Each partner/shareholder receives credit equal to their share of PTE tax paid
- Credit claimed on individual's Illinois return (Form IL-1040)
- Credit is refundable (can exceed individual liability)

**Important**: Illinois PTE tax allows credit to flow to residents AND non-residents of Illinois (on their portion of Illinois-source income)

### Estimated payments

Required if PTE tax expected to exceed $500:
- Quarterly estimated payments
- Due dates aligned with federal (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15)
- Form IL-1120-ST-V or IL-1065-V for payments

### Interaction with post-OBBBA SALT cap

With OBBBA $40,400 cap + $505K MAGI phase-out:

**Illinois residents below $505K MAGI**:
- Illinois 4.95% rate means moderate-income clients generate lower state tax than in NY/CA/NJ
- $40K cap may absorb most IL tax + property tax for middle income
- PTE election still beneficial for S corp/partnership owners with significant pass-through income

**Illinois residents > $505K MAGI**:
- SALT phase-out reduces cap to $10K at MAGI > $565K
- PTE election becomes essential
- Even with 4.95% flat rate, $500K+ AGI client has $25K+ IL tax; with property tax, hits cap quickly

**Illinois business + federal combined analysis**:
For $1M AGI IL resident S corp owner with $50K IL tax (combined 4.95% + 1.5% PPRT already owed by entity):
- Without PTE election: $50K tax paid personally. Federal SALT cap $10K (phase-out). Lost: $40K × 37% = $14,800
- With PTE election: Entity pays $49.5K PTE tax (4.95% × $1M); fed deducted. Savings: $49.5K × 37% = $18,315
- Owner receives credit on IL return: $49.5K offsets IL individual tax
- **Net benefit**: approximately $15,000+ federal savings

### Illinois entity-level S corp 1.5% tax

S corps also pay 1.5% PPRT at entity level (separate from PTE election):
- Cannot be avoided by PTE tax election
- Paid on Form IL-1120-ST
- Affects cash flow and reasonable compensation planning

## Real estate considerations

### Illinois Real Estate Transfer Tax
**State rate**: $1 per $1,000 of consideration (0.1%) — **relatively low**
**County**: $0.25 per $500 (Cook County; most counties no additional)
**Chicago**: Additional $5.25 per $500 for properties $3M+ ("mansion tax" passed by voters in March 2024)

### Property tax
**Cook County (Chicago)**: effective rate 2.0-2.5% of market value
**DuPage, Lake, Kane (suburbs)**: 1.8-2.3% effective
**Downstate**: typically 1.5-1.8%

**Homestead exemption (Illinois)**:
- General homestead: $6,000 equalized assessed value reduction (primary residence)
- Senior exemption (age 65+): additional $8,000 EAV reduction
- Senior freeze: income-limited freeze on EAV for income < $65K (indexed)
- Longtime resident: income-limited expanded exemption

**Cook County alternative assessment**: complex 10% assessment for residential, 25% for commercial/industrial

### §1031 Exchanges

Illinois conforms to federal §1031. No state-specific clawback.

## Estate, gift, and inheritance taxes

### Illinois Estate Tax
**Exemption (2026)**: $4 million (per 35 ILCS 405/1 et seq.) — NOT indexed for inflation (unlike federal)
**Rate**: Graduated up to 16%

**Cliff effect similar to NY**: If gross estate exceeds $4M exemption, entire estate can become taxable (depending on tax computation).

**Pre-death planning critical**: Many Illinois residents face state estate tax without facing federal estate tax (federal $15M vs. IL $4M).

**Federal estate tax**: $15M (post-OBBBA permanent). Illinois clients face state-only estate tax between $4M and $15M, plus federal above $15M.

### Gift tax
Illinois has NO state gift tax. But Illinois estate tax includes gifts within 3 years of death in taxable estate computation.

### Inheritance tax
Illinois has NO separate inheritance tax.

### Planning implications for Illinois clients

Illinois estate tax is one of the most aggressive state estate taxes:
- $4M exemption (lower than federal $15M)
- Not inflation-indexed (exemption doesn't grow)
- 16% top rate

Most Priceless Full-Wealth clients in Illinois need state estate tax planning — even at net worth levels far below federal exemption threshold. Common strategies:
- Irrevocable trusts (ILITs, SLATs, GRATs)
- Charitable vehicles (foundations, DAFs)
- Out-of-state domicile change (FL, NV, TN commonly)

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 6.25%
**Local rates**: Home Rule municipalities add up to 4.75%; non-Home-Rule up to 1.25%
**Chicago combined**: 10.25% (one of highest major city rates)
**Cook County suburbs combined**: 9-10%

**Economic nexus (Wayfair)**:
- $100,000 in IL sales OR 200+ transactions in prior or current year
- Marketplace facilitator collection required

**Illinois service tax**: Limited — Illinois historically taxed tangible goods broadly; select services (amusement, hotel) have separate tax regimes

## Priceless client considerations

### S Corporation owners
- PTE tax election (now permanent post-12/12/2025) highly valuable for higher-income clients
- 1.5% PPRT at entity level (cannot be avoided)
- 4.95% flat personal rate plus PTE election optimizes federal deductibility
- IL S corp compliance: Form IL-1120-ST annually

### Real estate investors
- Relatively low transfer tax (0.1% state)
- Chicago mansion tax for $3M+ properties (2024)
- High Cook County property taxes
- No state capital gain preference (taxed at 4.95%)

### Professional practices
- 4.95% flat rate applies uniformly
- PTE election available if structured as S corp or partnership
- IL does not restrict professional entity types (unlike some states)

### Software / AI / tech
- 4.95% flat rate lower than coastal states
- Chicago tech ecosystem (1871, University of Chicago, etc.)
- R&D credit: Illinois EDGE credit and Illinois R&D credit (6.5% of increase over base)
- QSBS: federal benefit flows through to IL residents [VERIFY IL §1202 conformity]

### High-income retirees
- Major Illinois advantage: NO TAX on retirement income (pension, 401(k), IRA, Social Security)
- Many retirees choose to stay in IL rather than relocate
- State estate tax offsets some of this advantage (plan accordingly)

### Estate planning clients
- $4M state exemption creates planning need at lower net worth than federal
- Pre-death gifts to use federal exemption while state exemption lower
- Out-of-state trust situs (South Dakota, Nevada, Delaware) considered for dynasty trusts
- Domicile change to FL/TX/NV for ultimate escape from IL estate tax

## Multi-state mechanics

### IL resident with out-of-state income
- IL taxes worldwide income at 4.95%
- Credit for tax paid to other state (Schedule CR)
- Credit limited to lesser of other state tax paid OR IL tax on that income

### Non-resident with IL-source income
- Form IL-1040NR
- IL-source income: services performed in IL, real estate in IL, Illinois business allocation

### Partial-year residents
- Schedule NR allocates income by residency period
- IL approach: income before move + IL-source income after move

### Out-of-state S corp with IL operations
- IL apportionment for income
- Non-resident shareholders: IL withholding via composite or individual nonresident return
- IL PTE tax election may still apply if entity has IL activity
