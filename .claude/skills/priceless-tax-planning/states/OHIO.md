# Ohio (OH) — State Tax File

Ohio enacted **HB 96 (2025)** signed June 30, 2025 by Governor DeWine, effective September 30, 2025. Ohio transitions from graduated 2-bracket nonbusiness income tax (2.75% / 3.125%) to **flat 2.75%** effective January 1, 2026 — joining 14 other flat-rate states. At 2.75%, Ohio becomes the **second-lowest flat-rate state** behind Arizona (2.5%).

Ohio is unique in separating **nonbusiness income** (wages, interest, dividends) from **business income** (sole prop, partnership, S corp) — business income still taxed at flat 3% after $250,000 Business Income Deduction (BID). BID not changed by HB 96.

**Ohio has NO corporate income tax** — instead uses **Commercial Activity Tax (CAT)** (gross receipts tax on businesses).

Low Priceless concentration. Some Columbus/Cincinnati/Cleveland clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax (nonbusiness)**: **Flat 2.75% effective TY 2026** on income above $26,050 exemption (frozen, not inflation-indexed)
- **Business income tax**: **Flat 3%** after **$250,000 Business Income Deduction (BID)** per taxpayer
- **Corporate income tax**: **NONE** — replaced by Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) on gross receipts
- **Commercial Activity Tax (CAT)**: 0.26% on gross receipts over $3M (threshold increased 2024 from $1M)
- **PTET**: Yes (Ohio Rev. Code § 5747.38)
- **Sales tax**: 5.75% state; local up to 2.25% (combined max 8.00%)
- **Estate tax**: None (repealed 2013)
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt (not included in Ohio AGI)
- **Municipal income tax**: **2,000+ Ohio municipalities impose local income tax** — rates 0.5% to 3% (Columbus 2.5%, Cleveland 2.5%, Cincinnati 1.8%); complex RITA and CCA administration
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

## Personal income tax — HB 96 changes

### 2025 (transition year) — 2-bracket structure

| Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 - $26,050 | 0% |
| $26,051 - $100,000 | 2.75% |
| Above $100,000 | 3.125% |

### 2026+ (FLAT under HB 96)

| Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 - $26,050 | 0% (exempt) |
| Above $26,050 | **2.75% flat** |

**Key changes effective TY 2026**:
1. Top bracket (3.125%) **eliminated** — savings for high-income nonbusiness filers
2. $26,050 threshold **frozen** (not inflation-indexed; bracket creep over time)
3. MAGI > **$500,000**: Phase-out of personal, spousal, dependent exemptions AND joint-filing credit

### Worked example — high earner

Ohio resident MFJ, $600K W-2 wages (nonbusiness income):

**2025**:
- First $26,050: $0
- $26,051-$100,000 = $73,949 × 2.75% = $2,034
- $100,001-$600,000 = $499,999 × 3.125% = $15,625
- Total: $17,659
- Plus: personal exemptions at full value

**2026**:
- First $26,050: $0
- $26,051-$600,000 = $573,949 × 2.75% = $15,784
- Total: $15,784 (saves $1,875 from rate drop)
- BUT: MAGI $600K > $500K → lose personal, spousal, dependent exemptions; lose joint filing credit
- Net impact depends on prior exemption claim amount; may be slight loss or slight gain

### Business Income Deduction (BID) — unchanged by HB 96

Ohio's BID is a major feature for pass-through owners:
- **$250,000 deduction** per taxpayer from business income
- Business income **above $250,000** taxed at separate flat **3%**
- Nonbusiness income (wages, interest, dividends, capital gains) taxed at 2.75% (2026)

### BID worked example

Single Ohio resident; $500K total income: $150K W-2 wages + $350K S corp business income.

**2026 analysis**:
- Nonbusiness income (W-2): $150K - $26,050 exemption = $123,950 × 2.75% = $3,409
- Business income: $350K - $250,000 BID = $100,000 × 3% = $3,000
- Total Ohio tax: $6,409

**Effective rate on total $500K: 1.28%** — remarkably low for combined wage + pass-through income.

### Ohio EITC

30% of federal EITC (nonrefundable). Relatively high rate but nonrefundability limits benefit for lowest-income filers.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

Ohio PTET election available under Ohio Rev. Code § 5747.38.

- **Rate**: 3% (tracks business income rate for 2026)
- **Base**: Entity's business income (not nonbusiness)
- **Election**: Annual
- **Credit**: Refundable to owners

Because business income already benefits from $250K BID deduction plus flat 3% rate, Ohio PTET provides less dramatic benefit than in high-tax states. Useful primarily for federal SALT deduction mechanics for high-business-income clients.

## Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) — Ohio's gross receipts tax

Ohio's CAT replaces corporate income tax for most purposes:
- **Rate**: 0.26% on gross receipts above $3,000,000 (threshold increased TY 2024 from $1M)
- **Base**: Gross Ohio-sourced receipts (not net income)
- **Minimum tax**: $150 annual minimum for businesses with $150K-$1M taxable gross receipts
- **Exemption**: Below $1M taxable gross receipts (though filing still required to claim exemption)
- **Applies to**: All business forms (C corp, S corp, partnership, LLC, sole prop)

### Priceless implications

Ohio CAT is a significant consideration for high-revenue businesses with low margins:
- $10M gross receipts × 0.26% = $26,000 CAT (regardless of profitability)
- Same company's net income of $300K would produce $9K at 3% ordinary rate (huge CAT over-cost)
- Service businesses and low-margin industries disproportionately affected

## Municipal income tax — critical Ohio complexity

Ohio has **2,000+ local taxing jurisdictions** imposing income tax:

### Key cities
| City | Resident Rate | Non-Resident (work location) |
|---|---|---|
| Columbus | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Cleveland | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Cincinnati | 1.8% | 1.8% |
| Dayton | 2.5% | 2.5% |
| Toledo | 2.25% | 2.25% |
| Akron | 2.5% | 2.5% |

### Administration

- **RITA** (Regional Income Tax Agency): Administers for ~300 municipalities
- **CCA** (Central Collection Agency): Administers for Cleveland and several others
- **Municipal direct filing**: Some large cities (Columbus) administer directly

### Credits for work outside residence

Most Ohio municipalities provide **credit for taxes paid to the municipality of work**:
- Full credit (100%) in most cities
- Partial credit in some
- Specific rules vary

For Priceless clients with Ohio wage income: Local tax can add 1.8-3% on top of state 2.75%, making effective state+local rate 4.55-5.75% before federal.

### Post-pandemic remote work issues

Ohio's HB 110 (2021) established specific remote work rules:
- Employees working remotely (not at employer's location) generally owe tax to their residence municipality, not employer location
- Significant tax litigation pending on retroactive application
- Priceless must analyze each client's 2024-2026 remote work pattern

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Columbus S corp owner
**Setup**: Columbus resident; $400K S corp income (pass-through).

**2026 Ohio state tax**:
- Business income $400K - $250K BID = $150K × 3% = $4,500
- Nonbusiness income (assume minimal): ~$0

**Columbus municipal tax**: S corp distributions generally NOT subject to local city tax for residents (unless treated as wages through reasonable compensation); W-2 wages are subject.
- If owner takes $100K reasonable compensation: $100K × 2.5% = $2,500 Columbus local
- Distributions: Not subject to Columbus tax for residents (Columbus excludes pass-through distributions from resident tax base [VERIFY specific local ordinance])

**Total Ohio state+local tax**: approximately $7,000 on $400K total income (1.75% effective rate). Very favorable.

### Scenario 2: Cleveland professional
**Setup**: Cleveland resident; $250K W-2 salary; works in Cleveland office.

**2026**:
- Ohio state: $250K - $26,050 = $223,950 × 2.75% = $6,159
- Cleveland city: $250K × 2.5% = $6,250
- Combined state+local: **$12,409 effective 4.96%**

Ohio state+local tax is meaningfully higher than just state rate alone due to municipal layer.

### Scenario 3: CAT-impacted business
**Setup**: Ohio LLC; $8M gross receipts; $400K net income (5% margin).

**Analysis**:
- **CAT**: $8M × 0.26% = $20,800 (gross receipts based)
- **Effective rate on net income**: $20,800 / $400K = **5.2% effective**
- Plus owner tax on pass-through income

CAT heavily impacts low-margin businesses. Priceless clients with high-revenue / low-margin Ohio operations should consider CAT carefully.

### Scenario 4: High-income Ohio family losing exemption
**Setup**: MFJ with MAGI $650K; 2 dependents.

**2025**:
- Exemptions: $1,900 (>$80K AGI) × 4 = $7,600 deduction before tax
- Joint filing credit: $88
- Full benefit of exemptions

**2026**:
- MAGI > $500K → exemptions phased OUT entirely; joint filing credit eliminated
- Lost deductions: $7,600 × 2.75% = $209 additional tax
- Lost joint filing credit: $88 additional tax
- **Additional state tax: ~$297**
- Offset by rate drop: $4,600 income range (old 3.125% vs. new 2.75%) × 0.375% = $17 savings (minimal)

Net: High earners lose slightly from exemption phase-out; marginal rate drop doesn't fully offset for those already above 3.125% bracket.
