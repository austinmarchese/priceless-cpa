# Michigan (MI) — State Tax File

Michigan has a **constitutionally-required flat income tax** (Mich. Const. Art. IX § 7) at 4.25% for 2026. A 2015 revenue-trigger mechanism temporarily reduced the rate to 4.05% for tax year 2023 only; the rate reverted to 4.25% in 2024 and remains 4.25% for 2025 and 2026 because trigger conditions have not been met.

24 Michigan cities levy **local city income taxes** — notably Detroit at 2.4% resident / 1.2% non-resident. This adds to Priceless analysis for Detroit-area employees.

Michigan has a **Flow-Through Entity (FTE) Tax** (Michigan's PTET) at the same 4.25% rate, providing federal SALT cap workaround.

Low Priceless concentration. Some Detroit-metro automotive or supplier clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: **4.25% flat** (2026; confirmed by Michigan Treasury April 2026)
- **Corporate income tax**: 6.00% flat
- **Flow-Through Entity Tax (FTE)**: 4.25% flat (tracks individual rate)
- **Sales tax**: 6% state; NO LOCAL sales tax (uniform)
- **Estate tax**: NONE (never had one; repealed 2002)
- **Inheritance tax**: None (never had one)
- **Gift tax**: None
- **City income tax**: Detroit 2.4% resident / 1.2% non-resident; other cities up to 1% resident / 0.5% non-resident
- **Personal exemption**: $5,600 approximate for 2025; per dependent; inflation-adjusted
- **Retirement income exemption**: Tier-based by birth year (see below); 100% implementation for Tier 3 in 2026
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt (all ages)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

## Personal income tax

### Constitutional flat rate

Michigan's state constitution (Art. IX § 7) prohibits graduated income tax rates. Any attempt to impose graduated rates would require constitutional amendment via statewide voter referendum. This structural constraint means Michigan cannot easily reform to progressive taxation.

### Rate trajectory

| Year | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2007-2022 | 4.25% | Long-standing rate |
| **2023** | **4.05%** | Triggered reduction (2015 law; conditions met) |
| **2024-2026** | **4.25%** | Reverted to 4.25% (trigger was one-year; subsequent conditions NOT met) |

**The 2023 drop was temporary** — the 2015 revenue trigger mechanism (MCL 206.51(1)(c)) provides for automatic reduction if:
- General Fund revenue growth exceeds inflation × 1.425

For FY2024 and FY2025, conditions were NOT met. Michigan Treasury confirmed April 15, 2026 that 2026 rate stays at 4.25%.

### Annual determination

Each year, the State Treasurer, Senate Fiscal Agency Director, and House Fiscal Agency Director jointly publish the rate determination following the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR) for the prior fiscal year. This happens mid-April annually.

### Standard deduction

Michigan does NOT have a standard deduction in the traditional sense. Instead:
- **Personal exemption**: ~$5,600 per taxpayer/dependent for 2025 [VERIFY 2026 figure; typically inflation-adjusted]
- Michigan taxable income starts with federal AGI, applies ME-specific additions/subtractions

### Retirement income (tiered system — "Lowering MI Costs" 2023 phase-in)

Michigan retirement income treatment is unusually complex, based on taxpayer's birth year:

**Tier 1 (born before 1946)**:
- All public pension income: exempt
- Private pension: Up to $56,961 single / $113,922 joint (2025 figures; inflation-adjusted) exempt

**Tier 2 (born 1946-1952)**:
- Pension deduction: $20,000 single / $40,000 joint after reaching age 67

**Tier 3 (born after 1952)** — the "Lowering MI Costs" phase-in:
- 2023: 25% of Tier 2 amounts = $5,000 single / $10,000 joint
- 2024: 50% of Tier 2 amounts = $10,000 single / $20,000 joint
- 2025: 75% of Tier 2 amounts = $15,000 single / $30,000 joint
- **2026: 100% of Tier 2 amounts = $20,000 single / $40,000 joint** (full phase-in)

**Social Security**: Exempt for all tiers, all ages, unlimited amount.

**Military retirement**: Fully exempt since 2016 regardless of birth year.

### Local city income taxes

24 Michigan cities levy their own income tax under the Uniform City Income Tax Ordinance (MCL 141.631 et seq.). **Detroit** is the most significant for Priceless clients:

| City | Resident Rate | Non-Resident Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Detroit | 2.4% | 1.2% |
| Grand Rapids | 1.5% | 0.75% |
| Highland Park | 2.0% | 1.0% |
| Saginaw | 1.5% | 0.75% |
| Flint | 1.0% | 0.5% |
| Lansing | 1.0% | 0.5% |
| Most others | 1.0% | 0.5% |

Detroit's higher rates (2.4% / 1.2%) are set by state statute specifically for Detroit; other cities are capped at 1.0% resident / 0.5% non-resident.

**Combined state + Detroit resident tax: 4.25% + 2.4% = 6.65%**

City income tax is in ADDITION to state income tax. Filed on separate city returns. Detroit residents file Form D-1040.

### Capital gains

Michigan taxes capital gains at the same flat 4.25% rate as ordinary income. No preferential treatment.

## Flow-Through Entity Tax (FTE)

Michigan's PTET is the **Flow-Through Entity Tax**, enacted December 2021 and effective retroactively to January 1, 2021.

### Mechanics

- **Rate**: 4.25% (same as individual rate)
- **Eligible entities**: S corporations, partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships or S corps
- **Tax base**: Michigan portion of "business income tax base" attributable to direct members that are individuals, fiduciaries, or other flow-through entities (NOT applicable to portion attributable to C corps, insurance companies, or financial institutions)
- **Election**: 3-year binding election; once made, continues for election year plus 2 subsequent years
- **Election deadline**: **September 30 of the calendar year following the election tax year** (i.e., for 2025 tax year, election deadline is September 30, 2026)
- **Annual return**: Due March 31 of year following tax year (for 2025: March 31, 2026)
- **Election method**: Michigan Treasury Online (MTO) only; no other method constitutes valid election
- **Quarterly estimated payments**: Required if liability > $800/year; due April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15

### Critical deadline mechanics

- Annual return: March 31, 2026 (for TY 2025)
- **Election deadline: September 30, 2026** (very late — can be after return due date)
- **If election made AFTER March 31**: Full FTE tax liability for first year of election due on the day of election (lump sum)
- Estimated payment safe harbor: 90% of current year OR prior year × 3/4 by Q3

### OBBBA impact on FTE election

The Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) raised the federal SALT cap from $10,000 to $40,000 for TY 2025-2029 (with phase-out beginning at $500K MAGI; back to $10K at $600K+).

**For Michigan clients**:
- Low/moderate income (MAGI < $500K): The SALT cap raise may eliminate the FTE election benefit — full state tax is now federally deductible on the personal return
- High income (MAGI > $600K): SALT cap is back to $10K; FTE election still highly valuable
- In between: Requires modeling

**Further complication**: FTE election is 3-year binding. A 2026 election covers 2026-2028. But SALT cap reverts to $10K in 2030. A 2028 election would cover 2028-2030, straddling the favorable and unfavorable federal SALT regimes.

### Worked example

S corp with $750K Michigan-source income; single owner (Michigan resident):
- Without FTE: Owner pays $750K × 4.25% = $31,875 on personal return
- With FTE: Entity pays $31,875; federal SALT deduction at entity level saves $31,875 × 37% = $11,793 federally
- Owner gets $31,875 refundable credit
- **Net federal benefit**: $11,793 (assuming SALT cap would otherwise limit deductibility)

**Under OBBBA SALT cap increase**: If owner's MAGI is $450K: SALT cap of $40K could absorb the $31,875 state tax at personal level. FTE election provides little federal benefit. At MAGI $700K+: SALT cap reverts to $10K (personal level can only deduct $10K); FTE election preserves full $31,875 federal deduction.

## Corporate income tax (CIT)

Michigan Corporate Income Tax (2012 reform; replaced prior Michigan Business Tax):
- **Rate**: 6.00% flat
- **Applies to**: C corporations only (not flow-through entities)
- **Tax base**: Federal taxable income with Michigan modifications
- **Apportionment**: Single sales factor
- **Combined filing**: Optional group filing (not required)
- **Minimum tax**: None
- **NOL**: 20-year carryforward; no carryback

## Sales and use tax

- **Rate: 6%** state; **NO LOCAL** sales tax (uniform statewide)
- **Food (unprepared)**: Exempt
- **Prescription drugs**: Exempt
- **Services**: Generally exempt (limited service taxation)
- **Digital products**: Taxable (state taxes digital goods)

## Unique Michigan items

### Homestead Property Tax Credit

Michigan offers a Homestead Property Tax Credit for homeowners (and renters) with property taxes > specified % of household income. Credit available on MI-1040CR. Relevant for Priceless clients with modest-income retirees.

### Proposal A property tax cap

Michigan Proposal A (1994) caps annual assessment increases at 5% or CPI, whichever is lower — keeps property taxes stable for long-time owners. "Uncapping" on sale can cause significant property tax increases for new buyers.

### Principal Residence Exemption (PRE)

Owner-occupied homes exempt from 18-mill school operating tax (typically 25-35% of total property tax). Must file for PRE; applies to primary residence only.

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic via federal extension; 6 months
- **Payment**: Must pay by April 15 to avoid penalty
- **Forms**: MI-1040 (resident); MI-1040 Schedule NR (non-resident)
- **City returns**: Separate; e.g., Detroit D-1040
- **Estimated payments**: Quarterly; safe harbor 100% prior year or 90% current year

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Detroit-based S corp owner
**Setup**: Detroit resident; S corp with $600K MI-source income; W-2 comp $200K.

**Analysis**:
- State income tax (4.25%): $600K × 4.25% = $25,500 (if no FTE) or FTE election
- **Detroit city income tax**: Applies to W-2 wages only (not S corp distributions for residents) → $200K × 2.4% = $4,800
- FTE election: Covers state only; does not cover Detroit city tax
- Federal SALT deduction: State + Detroit = $25,500 + $4,800 = $30,300; depending on MAGI, fully or partially deductible under OBBBA SALT cap

**Planning**: Ensure Detroit city tax is properly withheld/paid; separate city return required. FTE election valuable if MAGI > $600K.

### Scenario 2: Auto industry consultant (remote)
**Setup**: MI resident; consulting for FL auto supplier; 1099 income $400K/year.

**Analysis**:
- Michigan resident taxed on worldwide income
- Florida has no income tax; no FL filing required
- MI: $400K × 4.25% = $17,000 state tax
- No city tax if not residing in one of 24 cities
- Self-employment: Schedule SE federal; MI doesn't impose separate SE tax
- Consider S corp election + FTE for federal SALT benefit

### Scenario 3: Retired autoworker (Tier 3)
**Setup**: MFJ, both age 67, born 1957; $60K combined pension + $45K combined Social Security.

**2026 analysis (Tier 3 at 100%)**:
- Social Security: Fully exempt ($45K)
- Pension deduction: $40,000 (MFJ Tier 3 at 100% in 2026)
- Taxable pension: $60K - $40K = $20,000
- Less personal exemptions: ~$11,200 (MFJ 2 x $5,600)
- Net taxable: ~$8,800
- MI tax: $8,800 × 4.25% = **$374**

**Same couple 2025** (Tier 3 at 75%): Pension deduction $30,000; taxable $30,000 pension - $11,200 exemptions = $18,800; MI tax $799.

The 2026 full-phase-in saves this couple $425/year vs. 2025.

### Scenario 4: Michigan-to-Puerto Rico departure
**Setup**: Client relocating from Bloomfield Hills MI to PR under Act 60.

**MI departure checklist**:
- File final MI-1040 as part-year resident
- Establish PR bona fide residency (IRC §937 three-test: presence, tax home, closer connection)
- Break MI ties: voter registration, driver's license, dispose of MI homestead exemption if applicable
- Property still in MI: continues to generate MI-source income (rental, gains on sale)
- PTET/FTE election for remaining MI business interests

MI has no exit tax but requires clean residency break. PR is attractive post-departure; no state exit tax concerns.
