# Alabama (AL) — State Tax File

**Low-rate state with unusual features**: Alabama's 5% top individual income tax rate is relatively low, but the state has distinctive characteristics — particularly the **federal income tax deduction** (constitutionally protected) that allows residents to reduce state taxable income by federal taxes paid. Not a common Priceless client state; relevance mostly through multi-state operations or FL-bound relocation clients with AL ties.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 2% / 4% / 5% graduated (3 brackets; very low thresholds)
- **Top bracket threshold**: Effectively $3,000 single / $6,000 MFJ (above which 5% applies) — so virtually all working Alabamians pay the top rate
- **Federal income tax deduction**: Allowed as subtraction from AL income (constitutional — Amendment No. 225)
- **Corporate income tax**: 6.5% flat
- **Business Privilege Tax (BPT)**: graduated by net worth; $0.25 to $1.75 per $1,000 net worth; $50-$15,000 range; exempt if tax due ≤ $100
- **PTET**: Yes (Ala. Code §40-18-24.2); 9.3% flat elective rate
- **Sales tax**: 4% state + up to 7.5% local (combined max ~13.5% — among highest in nation)
- **Estate/inheritance/gift tax**: NONE
- **Property tax**: very low; effective 0.41%
- **Retirement income**: Social Security fully exempt; state/federal pensions exempt; military retired pay exempt

## Personal income tax

### 2025/2026 brackets

**Single filers**:
| Bracket | Income Range | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0 - $500 | 2.0% |
| 2 | $501 - $3,000 | 4.0% |
| 3 | $3,001+ | 5.0% |

**MFJ filers**:
| Bracket | Income Range | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0 - $1,000 | 2.0% |
| 2 | $1,001 - $6,000 | 4.0% |
| 3 | $6,001+ | 5.0% |

**Practical reality**: With the top bracket starting at only $3,000 single / $6,000 MFJ, Alabama functions as nearly a flat 5% tax for most working residents. The graduated brackets are essentially historical relics.

### Federal income tax deduction (unique)

Alabama allows taxpayers to **deduct federal income taxes paid** from Alabama taxable income. This is constitutionally protected (Alabama Amendment No. 225) and unique among US states (only a handful have similar provisions).

**Effect**: Higher-income filers paying substantial federal tax see significant AL tax reduction. For example:
- $200K federal taxable income → $30K federal tax → AL deduction $30K
- $170K AL taxable income × 5% = $8,500
- Without deduction: $200K × 5% = $10,000
- Deduction saves $1,500 (15% state tax reduction)

**Priceless planning note**: Federal income tax deduction reduces effective AL rate below nominal 5%. For high-income clients, effective AL rate typically 3.5-4.0%.

### Standard deduction and exemptions

- **Standard deduction (2025)**: Varies by income; range $2,500 to $7,500 [VERIFY 2026]
- **Personal exemption**: $1,500 single / $3,000 MFJ
- **Dependent exemption**: $1,000 per dependent

### Capital gains

Taxed as ordinary income. No preferential rate.

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: fully exempt
- **Federal government pensions**: fully exempt
- **State/local government pensions**: fully exempt
- **Military retired pay**: fully exempt
- **Private pensions, IRA, 401(k)**: TAXABLE at ordinary rates
- **Defined benefit plan distributions**: first $6,000 exempt for age 65+

## PTET — Pass-Through Entity Elective Tax (Ala. Code §40-18-24.2)

**Rate**: 9.3% flat (at entity level)
**Election**: Annual; must elect each year
**Available to**: S corporations, partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships
**Owner credit**: Refundable credit on personal AL return
**Federal SALT cap workaround**: Yes — entity-level tax deductible federally per IRS Notice 2020-75

**Election timing**: On or before due date of AL return (including extensions)

**When to elect**:
- Owners in 5% AL bracket (most working owners)
- SALT cap binding on federal return
- Pass-through entity has clear K-1 allocations
- BUT: 9.3% AL PTET rate > 5% individual rate for most earners, so election results in HIGHER AL tax paid (with full federal deduction)

**PTET math**:
- Without PTET: Owner pays 5% AL on K-1 income; $200K × 5% = $10K AL tax; no federal deduction (SALT cap)
- With PTET: Entity pays 9.3% × $200K = $18.6K AL; federal deduction for entity = $18.6K × 37% = $6,882 federal savings; owner gets AL credit $18.6K (refundable portion back)

**Net effect**: At AL rate 5% and federal rate 37%, PTET election slightly negative for most clients (AL tax paid 9.3% vs. AL credit 5% + refund differential; federal savings may not offset). **Carefully model before electing** — AL PTET math less favorable than most states where PTET rate matches individual rate.

## Corporate income tax

**Rate**: 6.5% flat
**Base**: Federal taxable income with AL modifications
**Federal tax deduction**: Allowed (same as individual)
**Apportionment**: Single sales factor (post-2021 reform)

**Effective rate after federal deduction**: Approximately 4.5-5.0% for most C corps.

## Business Privilege Tax (BPT)

**Applies to**: Corporations, LLCs, LPs (all entity types doing business in AL or organized under AL law)
**Calculation**: Based on net worth apportioned to AL
**Rate**: Graduated from $0.25 to $1.75 per $1,000 of net worth
**Minimum**: $50 (formerly $100; reduced by Act 2022-252)
**Maximum**: $15,000 for most; $3M for financial institutions/insurance; $500 for Family LLEs

**Full exemption**: BPT tax due ≤ $100 (Act 2022-252 effective 2024+)

**Filing**:
- Form CPT (C corps, banks, insurance, REITs, trusts)
- Form PPT (S corps, LLCs, partnerships, disregarded entities)
- Due: April 15 or 15th day of 4th month after year end; automatic extension if federal extension granted

**Annual report**: Eliminated October 1, 2024 by HB 230 — no separate annual report filing.

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 4% (one of lowest in US)
**Local rates**: 0% to 7.5% additional
**Combined maximum**: ~13.5% (some Alabama municipalities among highest combined rates in US)
**Statewide average combined**: 9.29%

**Major cities**:
- Birmingham: 10%
- Mobile: 10%
- Huntsville: 9%
- Montgomery: 10%

**Economic nexus (post-Wayfair)**:
- $250,000 in AL retail sales in prior calendar year
- Marketplace facilitator collection required
- Simplified Sellers Use Tax (SSUT) program: 8% flat rate for remote sellers (optional alternative to county-by-county)

## Property tax

**Effective rate (statewide average)**: 0.41% (among lowest in US)
**Mechanism**: Assessed value × millage rate (no state property tax; county/municipal)
**Homestead exemption**: $4,000 primary residence (federal assessed value)
**Current-use valuation**: Agricultural/forest property taxed at current-use, not market value
**Millage rates**: Highly variable by county

## Estate, gift, and inheritance tax

NONE at state level. Alabama has no estate tax, no inheritance tax, no gift tax.

## Residency

Standard domicile factors. AL source income for nonresidents covered by Ala. Code §40-18-14.

## Priceless client considerations

### S corporation owners resident in AL
- Effective rate ~4% after federal deduction
- PTET election 9.3% usually NOT favorable (math unfavorable vs. direct payment)
- BPT on entity level (low for most)
- Sales tax high for retail clients

### Multi-state pass-through owners with AL operations
- PTET by AL entity potentially useful if non-AL members are from states with PTET credits
- Evaluate carefully; AL math less favorable than peer states

### Retirees in AL
- Very favorable: Social Security, pensions, military pay all exempt
- Low property tax
- High sales tax burden on consumption
- No estate/gift tax

### Real estate investors
- Low property tax on AL real estate
- AL income tax on rental income (5% top rate; federal deduction softens)
- No estate tax
- Standard apportionment for multi-state operations

## Multi-state mechanics

### AL resident with other-state income
- AL allows credit for taxes paid to other states (Ala. Code §40-18-21)
- File AL-40 with Schedule CR (credit calculation)
- Federal income tax deduction applies (allocated)

### Non-resident with AL income
- File Form 40NR
- AL tax on AL-source income only
- No credit on AL return for taxes paid to home state
