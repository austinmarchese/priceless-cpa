# District of Columbia (DC) — State Tax File

**High-tax federal city** with unusual structure: DC is not a state, but imposes state-level income, sales, and business taxes. Congress retains ultimate oversight of DC's tax laws, though the DC Council has substantial autonomy.

For Priceless, DC relevance is limited — small population, most Priceless clients would be in surrounding MD or VA suburbs (which have separate tax regimes). Most common DC engagement: federal contractor employees, lobbying/trade association professionals, some doctors/lawyers with DC practices.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: Progressive 4% / 6% / 6.5% / 8.5% / 9.25% / 9.75% / 10.75%
- **Top bracket**: 10.75% on income > $1M
- **Corporate income tax (franchise tax)**: 8.25%
- **Unincorporated Business Franchise Tax**: 8.25% — **unique DC tax on sole proprietors, partnerships, and LLCs with > $12,000 DC gross income** (no individual income tax credit available)
- **PTET**: NO (DC has not enacted PTET — unusual gap)
- **Sales tax**: 6% state rate (0% local supplement)
- **Estate tax**: Yes; $4.71M exemption (2025 indexed); 11.2%-16% rates
- **Property tax**: effective 0.55% (low); higher nominal rates; homestead deduction
- **Deed recordation tax**: 1.1% buyer / 1.1% seller on real estate transfers (>$400K property subject to higher rate)

### Starting point

DC uses federal AGI with DC additions and subtractions. Standard deduction follows federal.

### Capital gains

Taxed as ordinary income. No preferential rate.

### Retirement income

- Social Security: fully exempt
- Military retirement: fully exempt
- Other pensions: taxable at ordinary rate

## Unincorporated Business Franchise Tax (UBT) — Critical DC Feature

**Authority**: D.C. Code §47-1808 et seq.

**Applies to**: Unincorporated businesses (sole proprietorships, partnerships, SMLLCs, LLCs taxed as partnerships) with > $12,000 DC gross income

**Rate**: 8.25% on DC taxable income (same rate as corporate franchise tax)

**No individual credit**: Owners do NOT receive credit on personal DC return for UBT paid at entity level. **This is effectively a double tax** on DC business income — entity pays 8.25% UBT, individual pays up to 10.75% on K-1/Schedule C distributive share.

**Why this matters for Priceless clients**:
- DC sole proprietors with > $12K DC gross income face UBT
- DC partnerships face UBT
- Professional services (law firms, consulting firms) heavily affected
- Combined effective marginal rate for high-earning DC pass-through owners: 19%+ (8.25% UBT + 10.75% personal)

**Entity structure planning**:
- S corporation converts to corporate tax treatment — S corp does NOT pay UBT (subject to regular corporate franchise 8.25%, which functions the same)
- BUT: S corp owners still owe personal income tax
- C corporation: 8.25% corporate + qualified dividend federal tax when distributed
- No structure fully avoids DC tax burden for DC-sourced business income

**Nonresident business owners**: Partial UBT if DC-sourced income; apportionment applies

### Unincorporated Business 80% exclusion for certain passive income

UBT calculation excludes 80% of income from personal services (very narrow category applying to certain professionals) — preserves some professional income from UBT double-tax. [VERIFY current scope]

## NO PTET — Significant planning gap

**DC has NOT enacted a Pass-Through Entity Tax** — highly unusual for a high-tax jurisdiction. This creates the double-tax UBT issue with no SALT-cap workaround.

Possible reasons DC has not adopted PTET:
- Complex interaction with federal budget agreements
- Limited legislative bandwidth
- Political sensitivities

Priceless clients with DC-source pass-through income face full federal SALT cap hit on DC taxes paid (unlike residents of 36+ states with PTET workarounds).

## Corporate Franchise Tax (D.C. Code §47-1801)

**Rate**: 8.25% on corporate net income
**Base**: Federal taxable income with DC modifications
**Apportionment**: Single sales factor
**Combined reporting**: Unitary combined reporting required

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 6%
**No local supplement**: DC does not have sublocal sales taxes
**Food**: exempt (most grocery food)
**Restaurant meals**: 10% (includes state sales tax)
**Hotel/lodging**: 14.95%
**Parking**: 18%
**Alcoholic beverages at restaurants**: 10%

**Economic nexus**:
- $100,000 in DC gross receipts OR 200 transactions

## Property tax

**Nominal rate**: Around 0.85% of assessed value ($0.85 per $100 assessed) for residential
**Effective rate**: 0.55-0.70% (lower due to assessment discounts)
**Homestead deduction**: $87,050 reduction in assessed value for primary residence ($75,000 + senior increase)
**Senior citizen discount**: Additional 50% property tax reduction for 65+ with income < $134,550
**Commercial rate**: Higher ($1.65 per $100 typical)

## Estate tax

DC Estate Tax applies:
- **Exemption**: $4,710,800 (2025; indexed) [VERIFY 2026]
- **Rate**: 11.2% to 16% graduated
- **Cliff-like structure**: estates above exemption face tax on full value
- **No portability**: DC estate tax exemption not portable to surviving spouse (unlike federal)

Estate tax exemption significantly lower than federal $15M — creates planning issues for DC residents with estates $4.71M-$15M.

**No gift tax**; no inheritance tax.

## Deed Recordation and Transfer Tax

**Recordation tax**: 1.1% on deed value < $400K; 1.45% on $400K+
**Transfer tax**: 1.1% on deed value < $400K; 1.45% on $400K+
**Total**: 2.2% of consideration (< $400K) or 2.9% (≥ $400K)
**Paid by**: Typically split between buyer and seller (customary)

## Residency

Physical presence and domicile factors. DC tax residency for individuals generally based on:
- DC driver's license
- DC voter registration
- Physical presence > 183 days
- DC domicile

## Priceless client considerations

### DC pass-through business owners
- UBT creates double tax (8.25% entity + 10.75% personal = effective 19% combined on DC-source income)
- No PTET workaround
- Consider C corp conversion ONLY for high-retained-earnings businesses (federal double tax may exceed DC savings)
- S corp structure does not avoid entity-level 8.25%
- **Residency change to MD/VA suburbs** if practical for individual owners — significant savings

### DC Estate tax planning
- $4.71M exemption vs. $15M federal creates planning need
- Consider gifting strategies (no DC gift tax)
- Domicile change to non-estate-tax state (FL, VA) before death
- Virginia has no estate tax; Maryland has estate + inheritance tax (different issue)

### DC real estate
- Moderate effective property tax (0.55%)
- Homestead + senior deductions meaningful
- Deed recordation/transfer taxes significant (2-3% combined)

### Federal government employees
- Federal pensions (CSRS/FERS) taxable at DC rate
- Military retirement: fully exempt
- Consider residence optimization (DC vs. VA vs. MD)

## Multi-state mechanics

### DC resident with other-state income
- Credit for taxes paid to other states

### Non-resident with DC income
- Form D-40 (resident form still used but prorated) OR Form D-40B (nonresident refund claim if overwithheld)
- Nonresidents do NOT owe DC income tax on wages (unlike most states) — **unique DC "sojourn" rule**: only DC domiciliaries owe DC income tax
- Federal workers commuting to DC from VA/MD owe only home state tax, not DC
- Pass-through business income to DC nonresident: still subject to UBT at entity level
