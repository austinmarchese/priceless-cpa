# Kentucky (KY) — State Tax File

Kentucky is on an aggressive trajectory to eliminate individual income tax entirely. Via HB 8 (2022), a trigger mechanism automatically reduces the rate by 0.5% increments when revenue conditions are met. Rate was 5% pre-reform, dropped to 4.5% (2023), 4.0% (2024-2025), and **3.5% effective January 1, 2026** per HB 1 (2025). Legislative leadership has stated goal of reaching 0% but acknowledged difficulty below 3%. Kentucky is also a flat-tax state and has repealed estate tax.

Low Priceless concentration. Some Louisville-area clients and multi-state remote workers. Kentucky is also reciprocal with IL, IN, MI, OH, VA, WV, WI (wages-only reciprocity).

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 3.5% flat (effective 1/1/2026; down from 4% in 2025)
- **Corporate income tax**: 5.0% flat (unchanged)
- **PTET**: Yes (HB 5, 2023; KRS 141.209); refundable credit on Form PTET-CR
- **Sales tax**: 6% state (no local add-ons; uniform statewide)
- **Estate tax**: NONE (eliminated)
- **Inheritance tax**: YES but only on distant/non-family beneficiaries; Class A (immediate family) fully exempt; Class B (siblings, nieces/nephews) with exemptions and graduated 4%-16% rates; Class C (non-family) with lower exemptions and 6%-16% rates
- **Retirement income exclusion**: $31,110 per taxpayer (2025; $31,110+ for 2026 — [VERIFY 2026 figure]; inflation-adjusted)
- **Social Security**: Fully exempt
- **Standard deduction**: $3,270 (2025); $3,360 (2026) — NOT tied to federal; Kentucky-specific
- **Local occupational/license taxes**: 0.5%-2.5% of gross wages; applies in 100+ counties and cities; separate from state income tax (critical for compensation planning)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms to federal); automatic 6-month extension to file; 75% pay-by requirement

## Personal income tax

### Rate trajectory

| Year | Rate | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-2019 | 5% graduated (top) | Pre-reform graduated structure |
| 2019-2022 | 5% flat | HB 366 (2018 session) — major reform to flat |
| 2023 | 4.5% | HB 8 (2022) trigger ratified |
| 2024-2025 | 4.0% | HB 8 trigger continued |
| **2026** | **3.5%** | **HB 1 (2025) — ratified trigger conditions** |
| 2027+ | TBD | Further trigger-based reductions if revenue conditions met |

The trigger mechanism under HB 8 requires (1) General Fund year-end balance greater than the rainy-day fund balance, and (2) General Fund receipts exceed previous year's receipts plus $450M. Both conditions must be met, and legislature must ratify each reduction.

FY2024 conditions were met comfortably: $2B+ surplus and $5B+ rainy-day fund. Legislature ratified in 2025 via HB 1. FY2025 conditions status [VERIFY — will drive potential 2027 reduction to 3.0%].

### Filing statuses and standard deduction

Kentucky uses the same federal filing statuses. Standard deduction is **per taxpayer** (not per return) and applies identically regardless of filing status:
- 2025: $3,270
- 2026: $3,360
- Single, MFJ, MFS, HoH: all same

This means MFJ files one return with **two** standard deductions ($6,720 for 2026). This is a critical distinction from federal treatment.

Itemized deductions are NOT available for most Kentucky filers (since the 2018 reform). Kentucky generally only allows adjustments starting from federal AGI; no schedule of itemized deductions.

### Retirement income exclusion (KRS 141.019)

Up to $31,110 per taxpayer of retirement income from qualified sources is excluded:
- Social Security: **fully exempt** (unlimited; not counted against $31,110 cap)
- Pension and annuity income
- IRA and 401(k) distributions
- Military retirement
- Disability retirement

The $31,110 figure is indexed annually [VERIFY 2026 exact figure; Kentucky DOR typically publishes in late Q4]. This is significant — KY retirees with $31K+ pensions pay no state income tax on that income. For a MFJ retired couple each with $31,110 of pension income, total exclusion is $62,220.

### Tax credits (common)

- **Personal tax credit**: $40 for age 65+; additional $40 for legally blind; $20 for KY National Guard
- **Family Size Tax Credit**: For low-income filers; phased by income relative to federal poverty level
- **Education Tuition Credit**: 25% of federal American Opportunity Credit or Lifetime Learning Credit
- **Child/Dependent Care Credit**: 20% of federal credit
- **Angel Investor Tax Credit**: Up to 40% of investment in certified KY small business
- **Historic Rehabilitation Credit**: Up to 30% of qualified expenses for certified historic structures (KRS 171.396); transferable

### Occupational license taxes (local)

Kentucky is unusual: most counties and many cities impose **occupational license taxes** on wages and net profits:
- Rates: 0.5% to 2.5% of gross wages
- Applies where work is performed (not residence)
- Louisville Metro: 1.25% (city) + 0.2% (mass transit) = 1.45% combined
- Lexington: 2.25%
- Jefferson County (overlap with Louisville): additional 0.2% school tax
- Approximately 100+ jurisdictions impose occupational taxes

For Priceless remote-worker clients with KY ties, these local occupational taxes are frequently missed. Must check employer withholding and proper payment to correct jurisdiction.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) — HB 5 (2023)

### Mechanics

- Enacted by HB 5, 2023 session; effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2022 (retroactive)
- Electing entities pay Kentucky tax at entity level at individual rate (3.5% for TY 2026)
- Individual owners claim **refundable credit** on Form PTET-CR
- Election is annual; made on Form PTE (the PTE return)
- Covers state income tax only; does NOT cover local occupational taxes (separate obligation)

### Election timing and administration

- Election made when filing Form PTE (the partnership/S corp return)
- Original return deadline: April 15 (calendar year); extensions available
- No separate election form required — simply check the PTET box on Form PTE
- Estimated payments required quarterly if entity PTET liability > $5,000 [VERIFY threshold — typical state pattern]
- Credit to owners is **refundable** (favorable)

### Worked example

S corp with $1,000,000 KY-source ordinary income; one 100% owner:
- Entity level PTET: $1,000,000 × 3.5% = **$35,000 paid by entity**
- Federal deduction: Entity gets $35,000 §164 SALT deduction (per IRS Notice 2020-75)
- Federal tax savings (37% bracket): $35,000 × 37% = **$12,950**
- Owner credit: $35,000 refundable credit against KY individual tax
- Owner net KY position: breakeven (paid $35,000 via PTET, gets $35,000 credit back)

Net cost of PTET: $0 (Kentucky); net benefit: $12,950 (federal). PTET is essentially "free federal deduction" for Kentucky pass-through owners.

### Multi-state considerations

- Kentucky allows credit for PTET paid to other states by KY resident owners (confirms via Schedule ITC; typical OSC mechanics)
- Out-of-state S corp with KY-resident owner: OSC paid to other state; KY allows credit up to KY rate (3.5%)
- KY S corp with nonresident owners: nonresidents can claim PTET credit but must file KY nonresident return (or be included in composite)

## Corporate income tax

- Rate: **5.0% flat** (unchanged)
- Base: Modified federal taxable income; single sales factor apportionment
- Combined filing: Not required; separate-company filing with related-party addback for intangible/interest expenses
- NOL carryforward: 20 years; no carryback
- Corporate standard deduction or exemption: None

## Inheritance tax (not estate tax)

Kentucky is one of 6 states with an inheritance tax (beneficiary-side, not estate-side). Important distinctions:

### Classes of beneficiaries

**Class A (fully exempt)**: Spouse, parents, children (including adopted), grandchildren, siblings (per 1995 amendment that added siblings), stepchildren.

**Class B (partially exempt, 4%-16%)**:
- Exemption: $1,000
- Rates: 4% first $10K over exemption; up to 16% over $200K
- Includes: nieces, nephews, half-siblings, daughters/sons-in-law, aunts, uncles, great-grandchildren

**Class C (most taxed, 6%-16%)**:
- Exemption: $500
- Rates: 6% first $10K over exemption; up to 16% over $200K
- Includes: all other beneficiaries (cousins, unrelated individuals, domestic partners not married)

### Planning implications

- Siblings are Class A (fully exempt) — favorable
- Domestic partners/long-term unmarried partners are Class C — unfavorable
- Nephews/nieces are Class B — moderate
- Non-family beneficiaries (charity, friends) at 16% top rate for large bequests

Priceless estate planning should flag Class B/C beneficiaries for tax burden. Charitable bequests are NOT inheritance-tax-free in Kentucky (unlike federal estate tax) — but charitable organizations themselves are often exempt beneficiaries [VERIFY charity-specific treatment].

## Sales tax

- **State rate: 6%** (flat, uniform)
- **No local sales tax** — uniform 6% across state
- **2022 expansion**: HB 8 (2022) expanded sales tax base to include 35+ services previously exempt (fitness, personal care, entertainment, certain professional services, digital products, streaming, etc.)
- **2023 further expansion**: HB 8 added additional services and digital goods
- Food (unprepared): Exempt
- Prescription drugs: Exempt
- Services base expansion is ongoing; legislature has signaled further base expansion in 2026 as income tax elimination pathway

## Reciprocity agreements

Kentucky has wages-only reciprocity with:
- **Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin**

Mechanics: KY resident employee working in reciprocal state pays tax to KY only (not work state). Work state employer withholds KY tax (or no tax if employee certifies via reciprocity form). Self-employment income, rental income, and business income NOT covered by reciprocity.

For Priceless KY-based clients working remotely across state lines, reciprocity can simplify but never eliminates need for careful state analysis.

## Filing mechanics

- Filing deadline: April 15 (aligned with federal)
- Automatic 6-month extension to file (no form required if federal extension filed)
- **75% pay-by rule**: Must pay at least 75% of total tax by April 15 to avoid late-payment penalty
- Minimum late payment penalty: $10
- Quarterly estimated payments: Due April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15
- Safe harbor: 100% of prior-year tax OR 70% of current-year tax (Kentucky-specific; federal uses 100%/90%)
- Forms: 740 (resident), 740-NP (nonresident/part-year), 740-NP-R (reciprocal state residents)

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Louisville-area S corp owner
**Setup**: S corp with $800K net income; single shareholder (KY resident).

**2025 (4% rate)**:
- Without PTET: $800K × 4% = $32,000 KY tax; federal SALT deduction $32,000 × 37% = $11,840 savings; net benefit tied to federal bracket
- With PTET: Entity pays $32,000; federal deduction at entity level saves $11,840; owner gets $32,000 refundable credit

**2026 (3.5% rate)**:
- Without PTET: $800K × 3.5% = $28,000
- With PTET: Entity pays $28,000; federal deduction saves $10,360; owner gets $28,000 refundable credit

**Planning note**: 0.5% rate drop = $4,000 savings on $800K of income. Modest but meaningful. For large pass-throughs, rate drops aggregate quickly.

**Also check**: Louisville Metro occupational tax 1.45% × wages (not net profit for S corp shareholders; depends on compensation mix). If owner takes $300K W-2: $300K × 1.45% = $4,350 local occupational. Distribution income not subject.

### Scenario 2: Remote worker with KY tie
**Setup**: Client works remotely from Louisville for California employer.

**Analysis**: 
- KY resident pays KY tax on worldwide income
- KY has NO reciprocity with California
- California generally does NOT tax nonresident wages for remote work (post-Multistate Tax Compact revisions)
- Client files KY 740; pays KY 3.5% (2026) on wages
- CA convenience of employer rule does NOT apply (CA doesn't have one for wages)
- No credit needed; no double tax

**Priceless action**: Verify CA employer is NOT withholding CA tax. If so, employer should stop and refund; client files KY only.

### Scenario 3: Retirement income planning
**Setup**: MFJ couple (both 65+); $90K combined pension income; $40K Social Security.

**Analysis**:
- Social Security: Fully exempt in KY ($40K)
- Pension exclusion: $31,110 per taxpayer = $62,220 joint
- Remaining pension: $90K - $62,220 = $27,780 taxable
- KY tax 2026: $27,780 × 3.5% = $972
- No standard deduction reduces this further (but each spouse has $3,360 standard deduction for 2026; standard deduction reduces income BEFORE calculating tax)
- After $6,720 standard deduction: $27,780 - $6,720 = $21,060 taxable; KY tax = $21,060 × 3.5% = $737

Kentucky is highly retirement-friendly. Low flat rate, generous exclusions, no estate tax.

### Scenario 4: Inheritance tax planning
**Setup**: KY decedent; $2M estate; beneficiaries are surviving spouse ($1M), daughter ($500K), nephew ($300K), long-term unmarried partner ($200K).

**Analysis**:
- Spouse (Class A): $1M fully exempt
- Daughter (Class A): $500K fully exempt
- Nephew (Class B): $300K - $1,000 exemption = $299,000 taxable; tax ~ $21,000 [VERIFY exact bracket calculation]
- Unmarried partner (Class C): $200K - $500 exemption = $199,500 taxable; tax ~ $20,000 [VERIFY exact bracket calculation]

Total inheritance tax: ~$41,000. Advance planning (e.g., lifetime gifts during decedent's life, life insurance owned by beneficiary, trust structures to reclassify beneficiary) could materially reduce.
