# Montana (MT) — State Tax File

Montana enacted significant 2025 reforms via **HB 337** (signed April 28, 2025): top rate reduced from 5.9% to **5.65% for 2026** and **5.4% for 2027**; lower bracket expanded; EITC doubled from 10% to **20% of federal EITC** effective TY 2026. Montana uses a 2-bracket structure (4.7% lower / top rate) — same thresholds for all filing statuses.

Montana preserves **preferential long-term capital gains rates** (3.0% / 4.1%) — unique among 2-bracket states.

**Montana has NO GENERAL SALES TAX** (one of only 5 states).

Very low Priceless concentration. Occasional Bozeman/Big Sky HNW property clients.

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax**: 2 brackets — 4.7% / **5.65% (2026)** / 5.4% (2027)
- **Long-term capital gains**: **Preferential rates 3.0% (lower) / 4.1% (upper)** — maintained under HB 337 with brackets matched to ordinary
- **Corporate income tax**: 6.75% flat (unchanged by HB 337)
- **PTET**: Yes (§ 15-30-2301 et seq.)
- **Sales tax**: **NONE** (MT is one of 5 no-sales-tax states)
- **Estate tax**: None
- **Inheritance tax**: None
- **Gift tax**: None
- **Social Security**: Partially taxable (phase-out based on income)
- **Standard deduction 2026**: Conforms to federal — $16,100 single / $32,200 MFJ (OBBBA-adjusted)
- **EITC**: **20% of federal EITC effective TY 2026** (up from 10% in 2025)
- **Property tax**: Average 0.74% — moderate; HB 231/SB 542 2025 reforms split primary residence (tiered, capped 1.9%) from second homes/short-term rentals (flat 1.9%)
- **Filing deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)

### HB 337 (2025) — "Montana Cuts" Package

Signed by Governor Gianforte April 28, 2025. Key provisions:

1. **Rate reductions**:
   - 2026: Top rate 5.9% → 5.65% (ordinary) and 4.1% (capital gains)
   - 2027: Further reduction to 5.4% (ordinary) and [VERIFY capital gains]

2. **Bracket expansion**:
   - Lower bracket (4.7% / 3.0%) significantly widened
   - More income falls into lower bracket (reducing effective rate for middle-income taxpayers)

3. **EITC doubling**:
   - 10% of federal EITC → **20% effective TY 2026**
   - Benefits low/moderate-income working families

4. **Bracket inflation adjustment from 2028**:
   - Modified inflation factor begins 2028
   - Prevents bracket creep over time

**Fiscal impact**: Governor estimates $750M+ in permanent income tax cuts once fully implemented. Analysis by Montana Budget & Policy Center estimates disproportionate benefit to top 20% (~67% of benefit).

### Standard deduction and personal exemption

Montana starts from **federal taxable income** (not federal AGI — unique among states). Montana conforms to federal standard deduction:
- 2025: $15,750 single; $31,500 MFJ (federal TY 2025 post-OBBBA)
- 2026: $16,100 single; $32,200 MFJ (federal 2026 inflation-adjusted)

**Note**: Because MT starts from federal taxable income, both standard deduction AND federal personal exemption (if any) flow through. Federal personal exemption remains $0 under OBBBA (permanently); no MT impact.

### Retirement income

Montana retirement income treatment is more complex than some states:
- **Social Security**: Taxed based on income; full exemption only below modest AGI thresholds; partial phase-out; NOT fully exempt
- **Pensions**: Partial exclusion available (~$4,910 per taxpayer 2025; inflation-adjusted) [VERIFY 2026 figure]
- **Military retirement**: Partial exemption; full subtraction for service-connected disability pensions
- **IRA/401(k) distributions**: Taxed fully (no special exemption)

Montana is less retirement-friendly than many no-sales-tax states due to partial SS taxation and limited pension deduction.

### Montana capital gains credit

**CRITICAL MT FEATURE**: Montana offers a **~2% capital gains credit** on qualifying sales of Montana property or Montana-based businesses. Combined with preferential LTCG rates (3-4.1%), effective rate on qualifying MT property/business sales:
- 3.0% LTCG - 2% credit = 1.0% effective (low bracket)
- 4.1% LTCG - 2% credit = 2.1% effective (high bracket)

**Priceless planning**: For MT property sales, aggressively claim capital gains credit. For business sale structures, route through MT where qualifying criteria met.

## Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET)

- **Rate**: 5.9% (2025); 5.65% (2026); 5.4% (2027) — tracks top ordinary rate
- **Base**: MT-source income
- **Election**: Annual
- **Credit**: Refundable to owners

## Corporate income tax

- **Rate: 6.75% flat** (unchanged by HB 337)
- **Base**: Federal taxable income with MT modifications
- **Apportionment**: 3-factor with double-weighted sales (not single sales factor)
- **Combined filing**: Water's-edge unitary combined required for multistate
- **NOL**: 10-year carryforward; 3-year carryback (unusual — many states have no carryback)

## Property tax (HB 231/SB 542 2025 reform)

Major property tax reform effective 2026:

### Primary residences and long-term rentals
- Tiered rate structure (progressive by value)
- **Top tier capped at 1.9%** (reduced from 2.2%)
- Benefits typical homeowners; significant reductions at middle home values

### Second homes and short-term rentals
- **Flat 1.9% rate** on full value
- Applies to ALL value tiers (no progressive benefit)
- Designed to disproportionately affect out-of-state owners of vacation properties

**Priceless implication**: For MT vacation homes (including client second residences), property tax effective rate is now 1.9% flat across the full value — significantly higher effective rate for high-value properties than primary-residence owners.

### Example — $2M Big Sky vacation home
- Pre-reform (2025): Tiered to 2.2% top, various tier effective → ~1.7-2.0%
- Post-reform (2026): Flat 1.9% on full value = **$38,000/year property tax**
- If classified as long-term rental: Could qualify for tiered primary-residence treatment → significant savings

For Priceless clients with MT vacation property: Consider long-term rental classification (12+ month leases) if feasible to reduce property tax burden.

## Filing mechanics

- **Deadline**: April 15 (conforms federal)
- **Extension**: Automatic 6 months via federal
- **Payment**: Must pay by April 15 to avoid penalty
- **Forms**: Form 2 (resident); Form 2NR (nonresident)
- **Estimated payments**: Required if MT liability > $500; quarterly
- **Safe harbor**: 100% prior year or 90% current year

## Priceless client scenarios

### Scenario 1: Bozeman tech entrepreneur selling startup
**Setup**: MT-resident founder; selling MT-based company for $5M LTCG; company based in MT (qualifies for MT capital gains credit).

**Analysis**:
- Federal: $5M LTCG × 20% = $1M federal (plus NIIT ~$190K)
- MT LTCG rate: 4.1% top × $5M = $205,000
- MT capital gains credit: 2% × $5M = $100,000 credit
- **Net MT tax: $105,000** (effective 2.1% rate)

**Compare to MO (100% capital gains subtraction)**: MO tax $0. MT tax $105K. MO saves $105K.
**Compare to CA (ordinary rate on capital gains)**: CA tax $5M × 13.3% = $665K. MT saves $560K vs. CA.

MT is favorable vs. high-tax states but less favorable than MO/TX/FL/WA for capital gains. For serial entrepreneurs, consider MO or no-income-tax state residency before large exits.

### Scenario 2: Montana second-home owner (post-2026 property tax reform)
**Setup**: FL-resident client; owns $3M Big Sky vacation home; not rented.

**Analysis**:
- Income tax: FL has none; MT nonresident only on MT-source income
- **Property tax (2026)**: $3M × 1.9% flat = **$57,000/year** (vs. ~$45-50K pre-reform)
- No MT income tax filing unless MT-source income

**Mitigation**: If client converts to long-term rental (12+ months), qualifies for tiered primary-residence treatment → property tax reduced potentially 30-50%. But adds rental business complexity and may trigger MT income tax filings for rental income.

### Scenario 3: Mixed-rate MT filer
**Setup**: MT resident; $200K ordinary income + $100K long-term capital gains.

**2026 analysis**:
- Ordinary: $200K at blended bracket → approximately $180K at 5.65% = $10,170 (plus lower bracket portion)
- Approximate ordinary MT tax: ~$11,000
- LTCG: $100K at 4.1% = $4,100
- If MT property/business: -2% credit = -$2,000 → $2,100
- **Total MT tax**: approximately $13,100

### Scenario 4: Working family with EITC
**Setup**: MT resident; MFJ with 2 kids; $45K combined income; qualifies for federal EITC of $5,000.

**2025 (10% MT EITC)**:
- Federal EITC: $5,000
- MT EITC: 10% × $5,000 = $500 refundable
- Total credit: $5,500

**2026 (20% MT EITC)**:
- Federal EITC: $5,000
- MT EITC: 20% × $5,000 = **$1,000** refundable
- Total credit: $6,000

**HB 337 benefit to this family**: Extra $500/year refundable state credit starting 2026.
