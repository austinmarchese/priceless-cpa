# Arkansas (AR) — State Tax File

**Aggressive rate reduction state**: Arkansas has reduced individual income tax rates repeatedly since 2021. Top rate dropped from 6.9% (2021) → 5.9% → 5.5% → 4.9% → 4.7% → 4.4% → 3.9% (2026). Corporate rate also reduced to 4.3%.

Low Priceless client concentration. Most likely engagement: multi-state client with AR operations (Walmart/Tyson Foods supply chain, Bentonville tech ecosystem).

## Quick reference

- **Personal income tax top rate**: 3.9% (2026; down from 4.4% in 2025)
- **Structure**: Two-tier; lower income 0% bracket; most income at 3.9%
- **Corporate income tax**: 4.3% (top rate; graduated)
- **PTET**: Yes (Ark. Code Ann. §26-65); elective
- **Sales tax**: 6.5% state + local up to 5.125% (combined max ~11.625%)
- **Estate/inheritance/gift tax**: NONE
- **Property tax**: effective 0.62% (moderate-low)
- **Food sales tax**: 0.125% (reduced from historical higher rate)
- **Standard deduction (2025)**: $2,340 single / $4,680 MFJ (indexed) [VERIFY 2026]

## Personal income tax

### Rate structure (2026)

Arkansas uses a tiered approach depending on income level. For most filers:

**Higher-income tables (AGI > $92,300 approx)**:
- Graduated rates collapsing to top rate of 3.9% (reduced from 4.4% in 2025)

**Lower-income tables (AGI ≤ $92,300 approx)**:
- Multiple brackets: 0%, 2%, 3%, 3.4%, then 3.9%
- Effective graduated structure with flat top

**Rate reduction history**:
- 2021: 6.9% top rate
- 2022: 5.9%
- 2023: 5.5%
- 2024: 4.9% → 4.7% (mid-year via Act 532)
- 2025: 4.4%
- **2026: 3.9%** (per continuing legislative trajectory)

Further reductions anticipated but contingent on revenue triggers.

### Federal income tax deduction

Arkansas allows taxpayers to deduct federal income tax paid (similar to Alabama and a few other states — though this is being phased out in some conforming states).

### Capital gains

30% of net long-term capital gains excluded from Arkansas income. Remaining 70% taxed at ordinary rates.

**Example**: $1M LT capital gain
- $300K excluded (30%)
- $700K × 3.9% = $27,300 AR tax
- Effective AR rate on capital gains: 2.73%

### Retirement income

- **Social Security**: 100% exempt
- **Military retirement**: 100% exempt
- **Federal civil service retirement**: $6,000 exempt
- **State/local government retirement**: $6,000 exempt
- **Private pension/401(k)/IRA**: $6,000 exempt
- Age 65+ additional exemption

### Standard deduction and exemptions

- **Standard deduction (2025)**: $2,340 single / $4,680 MFJ [VERIFY 2026 indexed amount]
- **Personal tax credit**: $29 per taxpayer
- **Dependent tax credit**: $29 per dependent
- **Low income tax credit**: for lower-income filers

## PTET — Pass-Through Entity Tax (Ark. Code Ann. §26-65)

**Rate**: Matches individual top rate (3.9% for 2026)
**Election**: Annual; irrevocable once made for the year
**Eligible**: S corps, partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships
**Owner credit**: Refundable credit on AR personal return
**Federal SALT deductible**: Yes (Notice 2020-75)

**Planning consideration**: Low AR rate (3.9%) means federal SALT cap savings modest relative to higher-tax states. Still worth electing if SALT cap binding for owner.

## Priceless client considerations

### Multi-state pass-through owners with AR operations
- Low AR rate (3.9%) = low AR tax burden
- PTET election if federal SALT cap binding
- 30% capital gains exclusion is unique AR advantage

### Capital gains planning
- 30% exclusion from AR income makes AR favorable for asset sales
- Effective rate on LT gains: 2.73% (70% × 3.9%)
- Comparable to WA (7% on capital gains above $250K) and below most states

### Business owners
- Aggressive rate reduction trajectory
- Federal income tax deduction reduces effective AR rate
- Corporate rate 4.3% relatively low

## Multi-state mechanics

### AR resident with out-of-state income
- Credit for taxes paid to other states
- Schedule AR1000TC

### Non-resident with AR income
- Form AR1000NR
- AR tax on AR-source income only
