# Georgia — State Tax Reference

## The essentials

Georgia transitioned from a graduated tax system (1-5.75%) to a flat tax in 2024. For 2026, the flat rate is 5.19% (HB 111 signed April 2025, retroactively effective January 1, 2025). A further reduction to 5.09% was scheduled for 2026 but the revenue trigger was NOT met — per Tax Foundation errata, the 2026 rate remains 5.19%. Future reductions to 4.99% are scheduled if revenue benchmarks are met.

Georgia's flat rate applies uniformly to both personal and corporate income (corporate also 5.19%). PTET election is available via HB 149 (2022); election made on entity's tax return (Form 600S for S corps, Form 700 for partnerships).

Georgia is strategically important for Priceless because of the state's flagship **film tax credit program** — Georgia's transferable film credits constitute the most active secondary market in the country. Priceless clients in high-income states frequently purchase Georgia film credits at discount to offset their Georgia tax liability. See `tax-strategy/capital-deployment/MOVIE-TAX-CREDITS.md` for the purchase-side strategy.

For Priceless, GA clients typically need: (1) PTET election analysis — straightforward since rate equals personal rate, (2) R&D credit evaluation (Georgia has competitive credit), (3) retirement income exemption planning for 62+/65+ clients, and (4) film tax credit analysis if operating in film/production or purchasing credits for state tax offset.

## Personal income tax

### Rate structure (2026)

**Flat rate**: 5.19% of Georgia taxable income

Verified via:
- Tax Foundation (February 2026 snapshot)
- Georgia Department of Revenue (dor.georgia.gov)
- HB 111 (2025) enacted April 2025, effective July 1, 2025, retroactive to January 1, 2025

**Future rate schedule** (subject to revenue triggers under state law):
- 2026: 5.19% (confirmed; reduction to 5.09% did NOT trigger)
- 2027: potentially 5.09% if revenue triggers met [VERIFY annually]
- Long-term target: 4.99% (statutory target under HB 1437 / HB 111)

### Standard deduction (2026)
- Single / Head of Household / MFS: $12,000
- MFJ: $24,000
- **[VERIFY 2026 indexing]**: These amounts were effective 2024-2025; 2026 increases likely but verify via GA DOR

### Dependent exemption
- $4,000 per dependent (per O.C.G.A. §48-7-26)

### Retirement income exclusion (MAJOR GEORGIA ADVANTAGE)

Georgia offers substantial retirement income exclusions:

**Age 62-64**: Up to $35,000 of retirement income excluded per taxpayer
**Age 65+**: Up to $65,000 of retirement income excluded per taxpayer (HB 266 increased military retirement exclusion; general retirement exclusion increased progressively)

**Qualifying income**:
- Social Security (note: Social Security already fully excluded from Georgia tax)
- IRA and 401(k) distributions
- Pension income
- Qualified annuities
- Dividends and interest from retirement-qualified accounts
- Capital gains up to limited portion

**Practical impact**: Georgia is competitive with Florida for retirees in specific income bands. Clients with $65K/year retirement income pay effectively 0% Georgia income tax; larger retirement income portfolio still benefits significantly.

### Residency rules

Georgia uses a facts-and-circumstances test per O.C.G.A. §48-7-1(10):

**Resident**: Domiciled in Georgia, OR resided in Georgia for 183+ days in a calendar year

**Key factors**:
- Physical presence
- Driver's license
- Voter registration
- Vehicle registration
- Principal residence location

**Audit posture**: Less aggressive than NY or CA. Georgia DOR audits residency but typically not with the resource commitment of those states.

**Inbound clients from high-tax states**: Atlanta metro (Buckhead, Dunwoody, Alpharetta) receives significant inbound migration. Establish clean Georgia residency similar to Florida: driver's license, voter, vehicle, homestead exemption where applicable.

## Business taxes

### Corporate income tax

**Rate**: 5.19% (flat; matches personal rate per HB 111 2025)

**Apportionment**: Single sales factor (since 2008)

**Net worth tax**: $10 - $5,000 based on net worth of corporation (separate from income tax)

**Filing**:
- Form 600 (C corporations)
- Form 600S (S corporations) — electing PTET indicated on this form
- Form 700 (partnerships)

### S Corporation entity-level considerations

Georgia has no separate S corp entity-level tax (unlike California's 1.5%). S corps pay at 5.19% if PTET elected, or pass through to shareholders at individual level.

### PTET — Georgia Pass-Through Entity Tax (HB 149)

**Authority**: O.C.G.A. §48-7-21 as enacted by HB 149 (2022); applicable to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2022

**Rate**: 5.19% of Georgia taxable income (matches personal flat rate; automatically tracks any future rate changes)

**Eligibility**:
- Partnerships (including LLCs taxed as partnerships)
- S corporations
- Multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships

**NOT eligible**:
- Sole proprietorships
- Single-member LLCs disregarded for federal purposes
- Publicly-traded partnerships
- Entities with corporate owners (only individuals, trusts, and estates as owners can elect)

### Election mechanics

**Method**: Election made on timely-filed entity return via checkbox
- S corps: Form 600S
- Partnerships: Form 700

**Deadline**: Original due date OR extended due date of entity return

**Irrevocability**: Election is IRREVOCABLE after the applicable due date passes

**Annual**: Must be made each year

### Estimated payments

Electing entity treated like C corporation for estimated payment purposes:
- Form 602-ES or Georgia Tax Center electronic payments
- Quarterly (April 15, June 15, September 15, December 15 for calendar year)

**Safe harbor**: 100% of prior year PTET liability OR 100% of current year (C corp rules apply)

### Credit to owners

**PTEDED** treatment on individual return:
- Owners enter allocable share of income taxed at entity level as SUBTRACTION from Georgia taxable income
- On Form 500, Schedule 1, Line 12, with description "PTEDED"
- Losses entered as ADDITION (Line 5, description "PTEADD")

**Not a credit mechanism (distinct from NY, CA approaches)**: Georgia PTET reduces owner's Georgia taxable income, doesn't give a credit offset. Result is same at individual level — owner pays no additional Georgia tax on PTET-taxed income.

### Post-OBBBA SALT cap interaction

With OBBBA $40,400 cap + $505K MAGI phase-out:

**GA residents below $505K MAGI**:
- Flat 5.19% combined with modest property tax (1.0% effective average) means middle-income GA clients often fit within $40K cap
- PTET election still beneficial if state+local exceeds cap
- Standard analysis: run the math

**GA residents > $505K MAGI**:
- SALT cap phases down; PTET becomes essential
- Even at 5.19%, a $1M AGI client has $50K+ GA income tax
- PTET election preserves full federal deduction

**Typical GA S corp owner, $1M AGI, $50K GA income tax**:
- Without PTET: $10K federal deduction (phase-out to floor at $565K+ MAGI). Lost: $40K × 37% = $14,800
- With PTET: Entity pays $51.9K (5.19% × $1M PTE income); federally deducted. Savings: $51.9K × 37% = $19,203
- Owner subtracts $1M from GA taxable income (PTEDED) → no additional GA tax
- **Net benefit: approximately $15,000 federal savings**

## Georgia Film Tax Credit (flagship program)

Georgia's film tax credit is the flagship state credit program and is directly relevant to multiple Priceless strategies.

### The credit mechanics (per O.C.G.A. §48-7-40.26)

**Credit rate**: 20% of qualified production expenditures + additional 10% "Peach Logo" bonus for qualifying promotional inclusion (total up to 30%)

**Qualified production expenditures**:
- Wages, salaries to Georgia residents working on production
- Payments to Georgia-resident vendors for goods/services
- Costs incurred in Georgia
- Minimum spend: $500,000 per production

**Transferability**: **Credits are fully transferable** — this creates the active secondary market for Georgia film credits

### Secondary market for credit purchase (Priceless client application)

Production companies often generate more Georgia film credits than they can use against their own Georgia tax liability. They sell credits at discount (typically 85-92 cents on dollar) through brokers.

**Priceless client application**:
- GA-resident clients (or non-GA residents with GA tax liability via S corp/partnership) can purchase GA film credits
- Pay ~88-90 cents on dollar for $1 of GA tax offset
- Net benefit per $100K credit purchased: ~$8-12K state tax savings
- Federal capital gain treatment applies on credit application (per Rev. Rul. 2003-59)

See `tax-strategy/capital-deployment/MOVIE-TAX-CREDITS.md` for full strategy mechanics.

### Broker roster

Priceless maintains vetted broker relationships for Georgia film credit purchases. Credit broker diligence includes:
- Verification of production company certifications
- Georgia DOR credit validation
- Transfer documentation compliance
- Pricing transparency

### Application to Priceless client workflow

When GA-resident (or GA-allocated) Priceless client has significant GA tax liability:
1. Quantify projected GA tax
2. Evaluate economic benefit of credit purchase (state savings net of federal tax on gain)
3. Vetted broker selection
4. Purchase transaction execution
5. Application on GA return
6. Form 8949 federal reporting of gain

## Real estate considerations

### Real Estate Transfer Tax
**Rate**: $0.10 per $100 of consideration (0.1%) — low rate compared to NY/CA

### Property tax
**Homestead exemption**: $2,000 basic exemption; counties may offer additional
**Effective property tax rate**: average ~0.9% of market value (below national average)
**Floating homestead exemption**: Atlanta area counties offer assessment caps

### §1031 Exchanges
Georgia conforms to federal §1031; standard mechanics apply.

## Estate, gift, and inheritance taxes

**Georgia estate tax**: None (repealed effective 2004)
**Georgia inheritance tax**: None
**Georgia gift tax**: None

Federal estate tax applies at $15M exemption (post-OBBBA permanent). Georgia residents face federal-only estate tax burden.

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 4% (one of lowest state rates nationally)
**Local rates**: Counties add 2-4% (ranges widely)
**Atlanta metro combined**: 7-8%

**Economic nexus (Wayfair)**:
- $100,000 in GA sales OR 200+ transactions
- Marketplace facilitator collection required

## Priceless client considerations

### S Corporation owners (Atlanta metro focus)
- PTET election available and typically beneficial for pass-through income above ~$300K
- No separate state-level S corp tax (unlike CA's 1.5%)
- Reasonable comp analysis standard (federal SS wage base)
- Annual state return filing standard

### Real estate investors
- Low transfer tax (0.1%)
- Moderate property tax
- §1031 conforms cleanly
- Atlanta growth market; strong rental yields relative to acquisition cost

### Professional practices (doctors, lawyers, consultants)
- 5.19% flat rate significantly lower than NY/CA/NJ
- Atlanta legal/medical/finance market depth
- Standard PC / PLLC structures
- PTET works well for professional practices

### Software / AI / tech companies
- R&D credit: Georgia R&D Tax Credit up to $1 million per year (15% of qualified GA research expenses above base); extends offset of payroll withholding for certain qualifying businesses
- Atlanta tech corridor (Georgia Tech ecosystem)
- QSBS: federal benefit flows through; [VERIFY post-OBBBA state conformity]
- Lower cost base than CA for comparable talent

### Film and media production
- 20-30% film tax credit (largest in country by total issued)
- Active secondary market — direct strategy for credit holders
- Purchase-side strategy for clients with GA tax liability

### Retirees (62+, 65+)
- $35K exclusion at 62-64; $65K at 65+ makes Georgia competitive with Florida for retirees in certain income bands
- Below $65K/year retirement income: effectively 0% GA tax
- Above $65K: scaling value decreases

## Multi-state mechanics

### GA resident with out-of-state income
- GA taxes worldwide income at 5.19%
- Other State Tax Credit (Schedule CR) for tax paid to other state
- Credit limited to lesser of other state tax OR GA tax on that income

### Non-resident with GA-source income
- Form 500NR
- GA-source: services performed in GA, real estate in GA, GA business allocation
- Film credit purchases can be used to offset GA liability of non-residents with GA-source income

### Partial-year residents
- Form 500 with Schedule NR allocating income by residency

### Inbound relocation from NY/NJ/CA
- Less aggressive audit posture than those states
- Still establish clear GA residency (driver's license, voter, vehicle)
- No state estate tax advantage
- Retirement income exclusion benefit
