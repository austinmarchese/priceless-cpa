# Jewelry Stores Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's jewelry retail niche — independent jewelry stores, custom design shops, family-owned jewelers. Inventory-heavy, margin-stable, often multigenerational.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Retail sales (diamonds, gold, gemstones, watches), custom design/manufacturing, repair, appraisal. Some stores have rental (for special occasions) or buy-back programs.

**Cost structure**: COGS 45-60%, labor 15-25%, rent 5-10%, insurance 3-8%, marketing 3-8%.

**Margin profile**: Gross 40-55%, net 8-20%.

**Unique feature**: Inventory often appreciates (gold, diamonds). LIFO treatment uniquely valuable.

## Key tax considerations

### 1. Inventory method (LIFO specifically)

**LIFO (Last-In, First-Out)**: Match newest costs against current revenue. In rising-cost environment (gold, precious metals over long term), LIFO defers tax by keeping older, lower-cost layers on balance sheet.

**LIFO election**: Form 970, attached to original return in first year. Irrevocable without IRS consent.

**LIFO conformity rule**: Financial statements must also use LIFO (with exceptions for certain footnote disclosures).

**Price index**: Use dollar-value LIFO with industry price index (IPIC method often practical).

**Value**: For $500K-$2M inventory stores, LIFO reserve can grow $50K-$300K+ over years, representing deferred tax at 21-37% rates.

**Recapture on exit**: LIFO layers reverse on liquidation/sale. Plan for eventual recapture in exit strategy.

### 2. Specific identification method

For high-value unique items (engagement rings, estate pieces), specific identification allowed. SKU-level tracking. Common method for jewelry but prevents LIFO.

**Planning decision**: LIFO (across fungible inventory) vs. specific identification. Often mixed — LIFO for fungible gold/chain, specific ID for unique gemstones.

### 3. Consignment inventory

- Jewelry store displays items on consignment from wholesaler/designer
- Not inventory of store (not owned)
- Revenue = commission on sale
- Contrast with purchased inventory

Must separately track; common audit/reconciliation issue.

### 4. Theft losses (§165)

§165 business casualty and theft losses generally deductible. Requirements:
- Actual loss (documented — police report, inventory count reconciliation)
- Deducted in year of discovery
- Reduced by insurance recovery

Jewelry stores have elevated theft risk; documentation hygiene matters.

### 5. Gold/silver purchase reporting

Jeweler buying precious metals from customers:
- Form 8300 reporting for cash transactions >$10,000
- AML/KYC compliance (varies by state, some states have specific precious metals dealer requirements)
- 1099-MISC to sellers may apply in some cases

### 6. Custom work / project accounting

Custom pieces:
- Time + materials tracking
- Deposit accounting (customer deposit liability vs. revenue)
- Revenue recognized on delivery typically

## Strategies to run

- **LIFO election evaluation**: For stores with $250K+ inventory, often meaningful value
- **QBI Optimization**: Non-SSTB, full deduction
- **Reasonable comp**: Owner benchmarks
- **Retirement plan**: Solo 401(k) or group 401(k)
- **PTET election**
- **§179 on display cases, security systems, safes**: All §1245 property, §179-eligible
- **Hiring children**: Very common in family jewelers

## Red flags

- No LIFO election despite long-term inventory appreciation
- Consignment inventory mixed with owned inventory
- Security system capital purchase expensed
- No §8300 reporting system for cash transactions
- Customer deposits recognized as revenue prematurely
