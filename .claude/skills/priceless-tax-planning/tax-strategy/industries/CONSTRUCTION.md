# Construction Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's construction niche — general contractors, specialty trades (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roofing), home builders, commercial construction. Typical range: $1M-$10M revenue.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Contract-based. Time-and-materials, fixed-price, cost-plus. Residential, commercial, or mixed.

**Cost structure**: Materials (25-45%), labor (20-35%), subcontractors (10-30%), equipment (5-10%), overhead (5-15%). Gross margin 20-35%, net margin 5-15%.

**Growth pattern**: Project-driven. Cash flow lumpy due to retention, change orders, payment timing.

**Tax character**: Non-SSTB (full QBI available). Accrual or completion method for long-term contracts (§460).

## Key post-OBBBA provisions affecting construction

### 100% bonus depreciation permanent

Heavy equipment, vehicles, tools, computers — all bonus-eligible. For contractors acquiring equipment, massive first-year deductions.

### §179 expensing raised to $2.5M / $4M phase-out

Alternative to bonus. $179 preserved for §1245 property AND qualified improvement property (including roofs, HVAC, fire, security on non-residential real property).

### §179D Energy Efficient Commercial Buildings Deduction — TERMINATING

**Critical**: Construction begins BEFORE June 30, 2026 required. For energy-efficient commercial projects, must break ground by cutoff.

Up to $5.81/sq ft deduction (with prevailing wage + apprenticeship requirements).

Often allocable to designer/architect/engineer for government-owned buildings — potential additional revenue stream.

### §45L Energy Efficient New Home Credit — TERMINATING

Home builders: $2,500 per Energy Star dwelling; $5,000 per Zero Energy Ready Home (ZERH). Home must be acquired by buyer BEFORE June 30, 2026. Plan accordingly.

### §174 R&D expensing restored (domestic)

Construction companies with innovative methods, BIM-heavy design work, or modular/prefab development may have §41/§174 exposure. Often missed.

## Common tax pain points

### 1. Long-term contract accounting (§460)

**Small contractor exemption**: Average gross receipts under $30M over prior 3 years (OBBBA indexed threshold; pre-OBBBA was $25M-$29M depending on year).

**Under exemption**: Completed contract method (revenue and cost recognized at completion); OR cash method; OR accrual with simplified rules.

**Over exemption**: Percentage of completion method required (revenue recognized as costs incurred; look-back interest if estimate off).

**Home construction contracts** (residential): Completed contract always allowed regardless of size.

**Planning moves**:
- Method change to simplified method for growing contractors approaching threshold
- Cost estimate accuracy critical (affects revenue recognition timing)
- Contract structure (splitting into phases, separate contracts for separable units)

### 2. Job costing accuracy

- Materials: invoice tracking, job allocation
- Labor: time-tracking by project (often via payroll system with project tags)
- Subcontractor costs: 1099 reporting + job allocation
- Equipment: usage allocation (often via cost-per-hour internal charge)
- Overhead: burden rate calculation

**Common error**: Overhead not properly allocated. Jobs look profitable but company isn't. Or overhead over-allocated and discouraging bidding.

### 3. Worker classification

Construction heavily relies on subcontractors. Classification complex:
- True subcontractors (independent trades): 1099
- De facto employees misclassified as 1099: reclassification risk + backpayroll taxes
- §530 safe harbor: good-faith classification if consistent

**IRS and DOL both aggressive in construction sector**. State DOLs especially.

### 4. Sales tax on materials

- Contractor buys materials, uses on customer job
- Depending on state: contractor pays sales tax on purchase (and can't charge separately), OR contractor buys tax-exempt and collects on sale to customer
- Multi-state work: nexus in each state where projects performed
- Resale certificate vs. exempt certificate mechanics

### 5. Retention receivables

Typical: 10% of contract price held back until project completion and punch list resolution. 

**Accounting impact**: Revenue recognized but cash not received. Watch aged receivables for write-off candidates if retention not collectible.

### 6. Workers' comp and liability insurance retrospective

Workers' comp premiums calculated on estimated payroll; audited at year-end to actual payroll. Can result in large additional premium or refund. Plan for liability.

## Strategies to run

### For every construction client

- **Equipment acquisition planning**: §179 or bonus depreciation for current-year purchases
- **QBI Optimization**: Non-SSTB; strong candidate for W-2/UBIA limit if above threshold
- **Reasonable comp review**: Owner-GC benchmarks in RCReports
- **Method accounting review** (long-term contracts): ensure compliance with §460 and small contractor thresholds

### Situational

- **R&D Credit**: For innovative methods, BIM work, modular/prefab construction, unique engineering. Often missed.
- **Cost segregation**: If client owns shop, yard, office building.
- **§179D**: If doing commercial energy-efficient work. Cutoff looming (June 30, 2026).
- **§45L**: If building new energy-efficient homes. Cutoff June 30, 2026.
- **PTET election**: In applicable states.

### Family businesses

- **Hiring children**: Real jobs common in construction (helper, supplies runner, office clerk, basic labor). Document thoroughly.
- **Spousal employment**: Office management often genuinely performed.

## Red flags

- §460 percentage-of-completion required but completed-contract used (or vice versa)
- Equipment expensed 100% straight-line but no §179 or bonus election
- No R&D credit despite demonstrably innovative methods
- Subcontractors misclassified (employee-level control, no 1099, no contract)
- Sales tax on materials mishandled multi-state
- Owner reasonable comp far below benchmarks
