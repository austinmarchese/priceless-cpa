# Real Estate Owner Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's real estate investor niche — long-term rental (LTR), short-term rental (STR), mixed portfolios, and real estate professional status candidates. From single-property owners to multi-property portfolios.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Rental income, property appreciation, occasional value-add renovation profits. LTR: predictable monthly rent. STR: higher per-night, higher turnover costs, more active management.

**Cost structure**: Depreciation dominates deductions (often 50-100% of operating cash flow); mortgage interest; property management (8-15% of rent); maintenance and repairs; insurance; property tax.

**Tax character**: Generally passive activity (§469) unless material participation or REPS. Passive losses suspended until passive income or disposition.

**Growth pattern**: Portfolio acquisition-driven. Clients often add 1-3 properties per year in growth phase.

## Post-OBBBA critical changes for real estate

### Bonus depreciation (100% permanent)

**Pre-OBBBA**: Bonus depreciation phasing down (80% in 2023, 60% in 2024, 40% in 2025, 20% in 2026, 0% in 2027).

**Post-OBBBA**: 100% bonus depreciation PERMANENT for property placed in service after January 19, 2025.

**Transitional rules**: For property placed in service Jan 1, 2025 - Jan 19, 2025, taxpayer can elect 40% or 60% bonus. After Jan 19, 2025: 100%.

**Impact**: Massive. Combined with cost seg, first-year deductions on acquired property can equal or exceed purchase price of 5/7/15-year components.

### §163(j) business interest limitation (back to EBITDA)

**Post-OBBBA**: §163(j) reverted to EBITDA basis (allows add-back of depreciation, amortization, depletion).

**Why it matters for real estate**: Leveraged real estate with substantial depreciation had reduced interest deductibility under EBIT-only rule (pre-OBBBA recent version). EBITDA reversion restores full or near-full interest deductibility for most investors.

**Real estate trade/business election**: §163(j)(7)(B) — electing out of §163(j) for real property trades/businesses. Election is IRREVOCABLE. Trade-off: no §163(j) limit, but must use ADS (slower depreciation). OBBBA's EBITDA reversion often makes this election less necessary.

### §179 expensing ($2.5M / $4M phase-out)

Available for qualified improvement property, roofs, HVAC, fire protection, security systems on non-residential real property. Useful for commercial property owners.

### Opportunity Zones permanent

OBBBA made QOZ permanent. For clients with large capital gains (QSBS exits, business sales, concentrated stock), QOZ investment defers and partially excludes gain.

## Common tax pain points

### 1. Passive activity loss suspension

Rental real estate is per se passive under §469, with two exceptions:
1. Material participation under §469(c)(7) — real estate professional status
2. STR 7-day average stay exception (§1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A))

**Common problem**: Client has W-2 or active business income plus rental losses. Losses suspend. "Paper losses" from depreciation provide no immediate tax benefit.

**Solutions**:
- REPS if qualifying (or spouse qualifying)
- STR 7-day average (treat as non-passive)
- Grouping election (§1.469-4) to combine activities
- Wait for disposition (§469(g) triggers all suspended losses)
- Find passive income to absorb (other rentals, portfolio companies)

### 2. Material participation and REPS

REPS (Real Estate Professional Status) requires:
- More than 50% of personal services performed in real property trades/businesses, AND
- More than 750 hours of services in real property trades/businesses
- Per Form 1040 taxpayer (or spouse for joint filers)

If qualifying, real estate losses are non-passive — can offset ordinary income.

**Documentation critical**: Contemporaneous time log. IRS routinely challenges. Tax Court cases frequently lost on documentation quality.

**Typical candidates**:
- Spouse of high-income W-2 earner (spouse manages portfolio, qualifies REPS, real estate losses flow against spouse's W-2 income too — MFJ benefit)
- Full-time real estate investor
- Real estate agent or broker with substantial hours

### 3. STR 7-day average stay exception

Short-term rentals averaging ≤7 days per stay are NOT per se passive. Owner must still materially participate (500+ hours, or one of other 6 material participation tests) to treat losses as non-passive.

**Documentation**:
- Average stay calculation (year total stays / year total rentals)
- Material participation log (hours)

**Typical STR operator**: Manages one or two Airbnb/VRBO properties, spends 300+ hours per year on management, marketing, cleaning coordination. Often qualifies as material participant for a single property.

**Aggregation**: Multiple STRs can be aggregated for material participation test via §469(c)(7) grouping election.

### 4. Cost segregation timing

**Standard building depreciation**: Residential 27.5 years; non-residential 39 years; straight-line.

**Cost segregation** identifies components as 5/7/15-year property:
- 5-year: carpet, appliances, removable fixtures, specialty equipment
- 7-year: certain furniture, equipment
- 15-year: land improvements, certain exterior fixtures

**Typical reclassification**: 20-35% of building basis for residential; 25-40% for commercial.

**With 100% bonus depreciation (post-OBBBA, permanent)**: All reclassified 5/7/15-year property deductible in year 1.

**Example**: $1M residential property acquired 2026, cost seg study identifies $250K as 5/7/15-year. Year 1 deduction: $250K (bonus) + $1,000/year-ish standard on the 27.5 life on remaining $750K. Compare to $36K/year straight-line without cost seg.

**Sequencing**: Cost seg typically worth it if property basis >$500K and client has income to absorb deduction.

### 5. §1031 Like-Kind Exchange

Post-TCJA: Only REAL PROPERTY qualifies (not personal property). Remains valuable for real estate.

**Requirements**:
- 45 days to identify replacement property
- 180 days to close
- Qualified intermediary must hold funds
- Like-kind (real for real — any real property for any real property, with limited exceptions)

**Boot**: Non-like-kind property or cash received triggers partial recognition.

**Timing**: Client must engage QI BEFORE closing on relinquished property. Cannot be set up retroactively.

### 6. Self-rental rules (§469)

Property leased to business in which taxpayer materially participates: Net income treated as non-passive; net loss treated as passive.

**Why it matters**: Owner of operating business who also owns the real estate leased to that business — common structure. Self-rental rules prevent using rental losses from this structure against active business income. But also prevent rental income from this structure from being passive income that could absorb other rental losses.

### 7. Safe harbor for §199A QBI rental treatment

Rental real estate qualifies as trade/business for QBI (§199A) if:
- Safe harbor (Notice 2019-07): separate books, 250+ hours of services
- General §162 standard: regular and continuous activity

See QBI-OPTIMIZATION for details.

## Strategies to run

### For every real estate owner client

- **Cost segregation evaluation** for any property >$500K basis
- **Depreciation methodology review** (bonus depreciation capture, proper class lives)
- **QBI safe harbor documentation** if rental income is net positive
- **§163(j) compliance** post-EBITDA reversion
- **§1031 exchange readiness** (QI relationships, 45/180 day awareness)

### Situational

- **REPS qualification** if owner or spouse has hours
- **STR 7-day exception** if short-term rental
- **Grouping election** if multiple properties with aggregation benefit
- **QOZ investment** for large capital gain realization
- **Cost seg catch-up** (§481(a) change of accounting method) for prior-year property without cost seg

### Portfolio-level

- **Entity structure review** (should each property be own LLC? single aggregated LLC? series LLC? depends on state and liability structure)
- **Financing structure**: purchase-money vs. cash-out refi; basis implications
- **Exit planning**: installment sale, §1031 chain, charitable remainder trust, or hold-to-step-up

## Deliverable tailoring

Real estate memo should emphasize:
- Cash flow vs. tax loss reconciliation (the "paper loss" vs. real economics conversation)
- Multi-year tax modeling (this year vs. next year given bonus depreciation timing, cost seg sequencing)
- Portfolio-level picture (not individual property)
- Passive loss carryforward tracking
- REPS documentation standards if applicable

## Red flags to watch

- Depreciation claimed but no cost seg or straight-line at 27.5 years only (often leaving 20-30% of first-year deduction on the table)
- Rental losses not suspended despite client having high other income and no REPS (potential understatement)
- STR treated as passive despite 7-day average (missed opportunity)
- §1031 exchange attempted without QI (fails the exchange)
- Self-rental not properly identified
- §163(j) limitation not considered for highly leveraged portfolios
- No safe harbor documentation for QBI rental
