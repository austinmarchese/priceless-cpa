# Software / AI Companies Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's tech niche — bootstrapped SaaS, AI-first startups, software consultancies, and profitable mid-market software operators. Biggest strategic opportunity: R&D credit capture.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Subscription (SaaS), usage-based (AI API), license, services, or hybrid.

**Cost structure**: Engineering wages 40-60% of total operating cost; AI inference costs 10-40% (for AI companies); sales/marketing 10-30%; G&A 5-15%.

**Margin profile**: SaaS gross margins 70-85%; AI-native 40-70% (inference costs); net margins range from -50% (growth-phase VC-backed) to +30% (profitable bootstrapped).

**Growth pattern**: Bootstrapped: compound ARR growth; VC-backed: hypergrowth with losses. Both have material tax planning opportunities, but different strategies apply.

## Post-OBBBA critical changes for software/AI

### §174 R&D expensing RESTORED for domestic R&D

**Pre-OBBBA (since 2022)**: All R&D required capitalization and 5-year amortization (15 years for foreign).

**Post-OBBBA (for tax years after 2024)**: Domestic R&D restored to immediate expensing OR election to amortize ≥60 months.

**Foreign R&D**: Still requires 15-year amortization. Important for companies with any offshore development.

**Impact**: For software companies with significant engineering headcount, this is a massive cash flow improvement. A $2M annual engineering spend that was spread over 5 years now fully deducts in year one.

**Transition**: Taxpayers with 2022-2024 capitalized R&D may have §481(a) adjustment or other transition treatment. Per IRS guidance (monitor for Treasury regs) — partner review required.

### §41 R&D Credit unchanged (but newly coordinated with §174 expensing)

Credit remains 20% of QREs over base (Regular method) or 14% ASC method. With §174 immediate expensing restored, after-tax value of R&D increases.

**Payroll tax offset (§41(h))**: Qualified small businesses (under $5M gross receipts current year; no gross receipts 5+ years prior) can offset up to $500K against payroll taxes. Critical for pre-revenue startups.

### QSBS (§1202) enhanced under OBBBA

- Tiered holding: 3yr/50%, 4yr/75%, 5yr/100%
- $15M cap (up from $10M) for post-July 4, 2025 acquisitions
- $75M gross asset threshold (up from $50M)
- 28% rate on unexcluded portion

See `strategies/QSBS-SECTION-1202.md` — already fully updated.

### §162(j) back to EBITDA

For software companies with any debt financing (convertible notes, venture debt), EBITDA reversion improves interest deductibility.

## Common tax pain points

### 1. R&D Credit missed or undersized

**The opportunity**: Software engineering wages are typically qualified research expenses (QREs). Companies with $500K-$5M engineering spend routinely miss $50K-$500K annual federal credits plus state credits.

**Qualified activities (4-part test)**:
1. **Permitted purpose**: New or improved business component (software functionality, algorithm, UI, performance improvement)
2. **Elimination of uncertainty**: Uncertain whether/how goal achievable
3. **Process of experimentation**: Systematic trial, refinement, evaluation
4. **Technological in nature**: Hard sciences (computer science counts)

**Qualifying software work**:
- New product feature development
- Significant performance improvements
- Security/scalability architecture work
- Machine learning model development (classic QRE)
- Integration/compatibility work (with limits — some routine)
- Database design and optimization

**NOT qualifying**:
- UI/UX polish without uncertainty
- Routine bug fixes
- Content creation
- Marketing site development
- Duplicative work

**Documentation required**:
- Employee time tracking by project/activity type
- Project descriptions (component, uncertainty, experimentation, technological nature)
- Contemporaneous engineering documentation (JIRA tickets, design docs, code review)
- Payroll records for QRE calculation
- Contractor agreements (for outsourced dev — 65% of contractor cost qualifies if U.S.-based, 0% if foreign)

**Priceless approach**: For clients with >$250K engineering spend, recommend R&D Credit Study as separate engagement. Fee: $5K-$20K. Typical credit: $50K-$500K+. Alternative: partner with specialty R&D credit firm (referral).

### 2. Foreign R&D sourcing

If development is offshore (India, Eastern Europe, LatAm), the §174 15-year amortization still applies. Documentation of where development is performed is critical.

**Planning consideration**: Where possible, structure contracts and documentation to demonstrate U.S.-based activities where applicable. Domestic R&D wages of U.S. employees: immediate expensing. Foreign contractor payments: 15-year amortization.

### 3. Stock-based compensation

**ISOs (Incentive Stock Options)**:
- No regular income tax at exercise if holding periods met (1 year from exercise + 2 years from grant)
- AMT at exercise on bargain element (spread between FMV and exercise price)
- Long-term capital gain on qualifying disposition
- Disqualifying disposition: ordinary income on spread at exercise + capital gain on appreciation

**NSOs (Non-qualified Stock Options)**:
- Ordinary income at exercise on bargain element
- Withholding required (employer obligation)
- Capital gain on subsequent appreciation (hold > 1 year for LTCG)

**RSUs (Restricted Stock Units)**:
- Ordinary income at vesting (fair market value)
- Employer withholds
- Capital gain on post-vest appreciation

**§83(b) election**:
- For founder/early employee stock that vests
- Election to recognize FMV at grant (usually nominal) rather than at vesting
- 30-day election deadline (IRC §83(b))
- Missed election = ordinary income at each vest tranche on FMV (often millions)

**Common inherited problem**: Founder granted restricted stock at incorporation, never filed §83(b). Years later when vesting occurs at high FMV, ordinary income treatment. Massive tax hit.

### 4. Multi-state nexus for remote workforce

Post-COVID, remote engineering teams across multiple states are routine. Each state where an employee works creates:
- State income tax nexus (corporate or pass-through)
- State payroll tax obligations
- Potential state sales tax nexus (Wayfair)
- State apportionment complexity

**Common problem**: Delaware C Corp with remote employees in 15 states, but only filing state income tax in 2-3 states. Retroactive exposure.

### 5. Deferred revenue / subscription revenue recognition

For SaaS companies:
- Annual contracts billed upfront = deferred revenue on balance sheet
- Revenue recognized over subscription period
- Cash tax impact differs from GAAP revenue
- §451(b) / ASC 606 coordination

Common issue: cash-basis tax return not aligning properly with accrual-basis GAAP financials. Misses deferred revenue / prepaid asset timing differences.

### 6. AI-specific: GPU / inference cost characterization

For AI companies:
- Cloud GPU spend (AWS, GCP, Azure): operating expense (cost of revenue typically)
- Proprietary GPU hardware purchase: §179 or bonus depreciation candidate
- Model training as R&D: §174 and §41 eligible
- Model inference: usually operating cost, not R&D
- Training data acquisition/licensing: evaluate §174 vs. capitalization

### 7. Payroll tax offset for startups

For startups with no prior 5-year revenue and under $5M current year:
- Elect to use §41 R&D credit against FICA (employer portion) and Medicare
- Up to $500,000 offset per year
- Helpful for pre-revenue startups that can't use income tax credit

Election made with Form 6765 in year credit generated; applied against quarterly 941s in following year.

## Strategies to run

### For every software/AI client with engineering headcount

- **R&D Credit feasibility assessment**: Even a lightweight review. Often $50K+ missed credit.
- **§174 expensing verification**: Confirm domestic expensing applied for 2025+
- **Stock-based comp review**: §83(b) elections, AMT projections, ISO/NSO mix optimization
- **QBI Optimization** (for S Corp operating entities): non-SSTB; full deduction with W-2/UBIA if above threshold
- **Multi-state nexus mapping**: Where do your employees live?

### For growth-stage companies (VC-backed C Corp)

- **QSBS timing**: Ensure C Corp status locked in before key funding round; gross asset threshold tracking ($75M)
- **Payroll tax offset election** (§41(h)) if qualifying
- **§162(l) / health insurance** for owner-employees (S Corp mechanics)
- **Retention planning**: employment-type stock-based comp strategies

### For profitable mature software companies

- **Defined Benefit / Cash Balance Plan**: For owner-operators, shelter $100K-$300K+
- **Mega Backdoor Roth**: If plan supports after-tax + in-service distribution
- **PTET election** (state-dependent)
- **Charitable giving with appreciated stock**: if company stock has appreciated, DAF funded with stock preserves basis and avoids cap gains

### Exit planning

- **QSBS optimization**: 3/4/5 year timing for maximum exclusion
- **Founder stock diversification**: structured around §1202 exclusion
- **Charitable remainder trust**: for large concentrated stock positions
- **Installment sale structure**: if seller financing
- **§1045 rollover**: QSBS reinvestment into new QSBS within 60 days defers gain

## Deliverable tailoring

Software/AI memo should emphasize:
- R&D credit quantification (if feasible credit study recommended)
- Multi-year model showing compounding of retirement/QSBS benefits
- Stock-based compensation tax timing
- Multi-state exposure
- Clear separation of federal vs. state impact

## Red flags to watch

- No §41 R&D credit claimed despite significant engineering headcount
- §174 still capitalized post-2024 (missed immediate expensing for domestic)
- Foreign R&D not identified (15-year amortization not applied)
- §83(b) election missed on founder stock
- Stock option exercises without AMT impact analysis
- Remote employees in multi-state without nexus compliance
- Cash-basis tax with deferred revenue misalignment
- Converting S Corp to C Corp late (missing QSBS clock)
