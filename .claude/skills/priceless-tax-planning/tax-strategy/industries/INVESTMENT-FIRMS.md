# Investment Firms (RIAs / Wealth Managers) AS CLIENTS

**IMPORTANT scope note**: This file addresses Priceless CPA serving RIAs/wealth managers as TAX CLIENTS. Priceless CPA does NOT provide investment advisory services. Referral relationships with insurance affiliate and RIA affiliate (see REFERRAL-DISCLOSURE-FRAMEWORK.md) are for CLIENTS of Priceless, not for RIA clients' clients.

Priceless CPA's RIA/wealth management niche — independent RIAs, wealth management firms, investment advisors. High-income, SSTB-classified, retirement-planning-heavy profile similar to medical practices.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: AUM fees (typically 0.5-1.5% of assets), financial planning fees, performance fees (for some), occasional commission (for hybrid BD-RIA).

**Cost structure**: People (advisors, ops, compliance) 40-60%; tech/platform 8-15%; office 5-10%; compliance 3-8%; marketing 3-8%. Net margins 30-55% for mature firms.

**Growth pattern**: AUM accumulation (compounding growth), book building, acquisition, team expansion.

**Tax character**: SSTB (financial services). Critical implication.

## Critical: SSTB classification consequences

§199A(d)(2)(A) SSTBs include: "any trade or business involving the performance of services in the fields of health, law, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services, brokerage services, or any trade or business where the principal asset of such trade or business is the reputation or skill of 1 or more of its employees..."

RIAs/wealth managers squarely SSTB. Above threshold phase-in, QBI deduction = $0.

**Thresholds and phase-in** (same as other SSTBs):
- 2025: $197,300 single / $394,600 MFJ; phase-in +$50K/+$100K
- 2026: ~$203K single / ~$406K MFJ (per Rev. Proc. 2025-32); phase-in widened to +$75K/+$175K

**Above phase-in (common for mature RIA owners)**: QBI deduction gone entirely.

**Only recovery path**: Reduce taxable income below or into phase-in. Same playbook as medical practices — DB/CBP plans become the dominant strategy.

## Key tax pain points

### 1. Retirement planning priority

Just like physicians, RIA owners have compressed high-earning years and SSTB QBI phase-out. Retirement plans are DISPROPORTIONATELY VALUABLE.

**Plan stack for mature RIA (owner age 45+)**:
1. Safe Harbor 401(k) + Profit-Sharing: up to §415(c) $72K (2026) + catch-up
2. Cash Balance Plan: $100K-$300K depending on age
3. Mega Backdoor Roth: additional after-tax capacity
4. Backdoor Roth IRA
5. HSA
6. Spousal employment (if spouse in firm)
7. Hiring children

Combined: $250K-$500K+ annual sheltering possible.

**DB Plan consideration**: Not just for owner. Can include key employees. Design complexity, but for $1M+ revenue firms with multiple owners/senior advisors, cash balance plans often the most value-per-dollar.

### 2. Deferred compensation

- §409A compliance critical for non-qualified deferred compensation
- Signing bonuses with vesting
- Performance-based deferred comp
- Profit-sharing plans with non-qualified component

**Common issue**: §409A violations trigger immediate income inclusion + 20% additional tax + interest. Structured correctly from start, avoids years of pain.

### 3. Book of business valuation

RIA revenue is highly valuable due to recurring nature. Business sale treatment:
- Goodwill vs. customer list (both §197 intangibles to buyer)
- Seller generally capital gain on sale of intangibles (generally qualifies)
- Earnout structures common — tax character of earnout critical

**Exit planning**: 3-5 years pre-sale, structure for tax-efficient exit. Installment sales, ESOPs (uncommon for RIA size but possible), or roll-up sale structures.

### 4. Carried interest (for PE-backed RIAs or multi-advisor structures)

§1061 (3-year holding for LTCG treatment). Post-OBBBA: structure generally maintained. Specific application to RIA context usually limited unless firm has proprietary investment vehicles.

### 5. Performance vs. management fee timing

- Management fee: typically quarterly or monthly, accrual straightforward
- Performance fee: at year-end or contract anniversary, contingent on performance hurdle
- Revenue recognition timing affects tax year

### 6. Custodian receivable/payable

AUM fees deducted directly from client accounts and remitted monthly by custodian. Creates receivable during month; received shortly after.

### 7. Client solicitation and marketing

- Marketing for RIA tightly regulated by SEC Marketing Rule
- Referral fee arrangements with other professionals (CPAs, attorneys) — disclosure + documentation
- Testimonials and endorsements (post-2021 new rules)

## Strategies to run

### Near-automatic for every RIA client

- **Reasonable comp review**: Owner-advisor benchmarks
- **Retirement plan evaluation**: DB/CBP for high-income mature firms
- **QBI SSTB positioning**: Can we get into or below phase-in?
- **PTET election**

### For mature profitable RIAs

- **DB/Cash Balance Plan**: Priority #1. Can shelter $100K-$300K+ per owner.
- **Mega Backdoor Roth**: If 401(k) supports
- **Charitable giving optimization**: DAF with appreciated assets (DAF bypasses most of 0.5% floor math; 35% cap partially applies)
- **§163(j) analysis**: Post-OBBBA EBITDA reversion for any debt

### Exit planning

- **3-5 years pre-sale**: Structure review for tax-efficient exit
- **QSBS consideration**: If firm structured as C Corp (uncommon but possible)
- **Installment sale structures**
- **Charitable remainder trusts** for concentrated owner wealth

## Deliverable tailoring

RIA memo should emphasize:
- SSTB phase-out impact and recovery path
- DB Plan compelling value (quantify dollar benefit with double-benefit SSTB restoration math)
- Multi-year planning horizon (retirement savings runway)
- Specific QBI restoration calculations

## Red flags

- High-income owner with no DB/CBP plan (missed opportunity often $20K-$100K/year)
- Owner below reasonable comp benchmark
- §409A-noncompliant deferred comp
- No exit planning if founders approaching retirement
- State filings not current (RIA registration state-specific)
