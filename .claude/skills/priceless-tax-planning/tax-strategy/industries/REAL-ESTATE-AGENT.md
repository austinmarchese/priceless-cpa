# Real Estate Agents Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's real estate agent niche — solo agents, team leaders, small brokerages. Commission-based 1099 earners with meaningful planning upside.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Commission income — typically 2.5-3% of sale price, split with broker. Agent nets 50-80% of their side of commission after brokerage split.

**Cost structure**: Brokerage splits (20-50%), marketing (5-15%), vehicle (5-10%), E&O insurance, MLS/NAR dues, continuing education, office/admin.

**Tax character**: Non-SSTB (performance-based, not "services"). Full QBI available.

**Income pattern**: Lumpy — deals close, income comes in chunks. Cash flow management critical.

## Critical tax characteristic: non-SSTB status

Real estate agents are NON-SSTB for §199A purposes. Unlike attorneys, CPAs, doctors — real estate sales is a performance-based service. Full 20% QBI deduction available.

**Common misclassification**: Some preparers incorrectly treat real estate agents as SSTB. Wrong. Cite §199A(d)(2) and regulatory treatment.

## Key strategies

### 1. S Corp election timing

**Break-even analysis**:
- Sole prop / SMLLC: all net income subject to SE tax (15.3% up to SS wage base + 2.9% Medicare + 0.9% additional Medicare)
- S Corp: only W-2 wages subject to FICA; distributions not
- S Corp costs: separate payroll, tax return, state filings, payroll service

**Typical break-even**: Net income ~$100K-$150K where S Corp payroll tax savings exceed additional admin costs.

**For consistent $200K+ earners**: S Corp almost always wins.

### 2. S Corp reasonable comp (post-election)

RCReports has agent-specific benchmarks. Typical range:
- Solo agent: $50K-$100K W-2 depending on total net and role mix
- Team leader with sub-agents: higher (includes management component)

**Common issue**: Agents on Facebook/YouTube advising ultra-low W-2 and max distribution. This is reclassification-risk territory.

### 3. Vehicle deduction

**Standard mileage** (2026 rate TBD; 2025 rate was 70¢/mile):
- Simple, no receipt requirements
- Capped value on luxury vehicles

**Actual expense**:
- Depreciation + fuel + insurance + maintenance + repairs × business use %
- Often larger deduction for expensive vehicles
- Must track business vs. personal use
- §179 or bonus depreciation on acquisition (subject to luxury auto limits)

**Luxury auto limits** (2026, approximate; verify against Rev. Proc.):
- Passenger auto: ~$20,400 year 1 limit if bonus depreciation; ~$12,200 without bonus (pre-OBBBA; post-OBBBA verify current)
- SUVs/trucks >6,000 lbs GVW: higher limit, potentially full §179 on business portion

**Common error**: Agent with Lexus RX deducting full purchase price. If under 6,000 lbs GVW, luxury auto limit caps annual deduction.

### 4. Home office

§280A(c) home office deduction:
- Regular and exclusive use
- Principal place of business OR where clients regularly met
- Standard method ($5/sq ft up to 300 sq ft, max $1,500) OR actual method (% of home expenses)

**For S Corp owner**: Use accountable plan (§62) rather than home office on personal return. Employer reimburses employee for business use of home. Deductible to S Corp, not income to owner.

### 5. Marketing and listing expenses

Common deductible categories:
- Photography (listing photos, headshots)
- Videography, drone footage
- Staging expenses
- Open house expenses (signs, refreshments, brochures)
- Digital marketing (Facebook/Instagram/Google ads)
- Print marketing (business cards, postcards, flyers)
- Client appreciation (gifts to past clients — limited to $25/client)
- Lead generation services (Zillow leads, OpCity, etc.)

### 6. Retirement savings

Solo 401(k) strong fit:
- Employee deferral $24,500 (2026)
- Employer contribution 25% of W-2 wages (S Corp) or ~20% net SE (sole prop)
- Total up to §415(c) $72K (2026)
- Roth option available

SEP IRA: simpler; employer only; triggers pro-rata rule for backdoor Roth (major downside).

**For high-earning consistent agents**: DB plan consideration after age 45.

## Deliverable tailoring

Real estate agent memo should emphasize:
- S Corp election math (if not yet elected)
- Vehicle deduction method comparison
- Marketing expense categorization
- Retirement savings projection
- Clear cash flow picture (income lumpy but planning consistent)

## Red flags

- Sole prop at $300K+ net income (missed S Corp savings)
- S Corp with ultra-low reasonable comp (reclassification risk)
- Standard mileage deducted despite luxury vehicle
- §179 taken on vehicle exceeding luxury auto limit
- QBI denied on assumption agent is SSTB (error)
- No retirement plan despite consistent high income
- Home office deducted on personal return for S Corp owner (wrong vehicle)
