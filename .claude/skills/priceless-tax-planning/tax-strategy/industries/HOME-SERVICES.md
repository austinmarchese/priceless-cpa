# Home Services Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's home services niche — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pest control, landscaping, cleaning services, handyman/general home repair. Route-based service delivery with trucks/vans in the field.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Service calls, installation/replacement projects, maintenance contracts (recurring), sometimes retail/supply.

**Cost structure**: Labor 30-45%, materials/parts 15-25%, vehicle/fleet 8-15%, marketing 5-15%, overhead 5-15%. Gross margins 35-55%, net 8-18%.

**Growth pattern**: Route density, team expansion, geographic expansion, acquisition of smaller operators.

**Tax character**: Non-SSTB, full QBI available.

## Key tax pain points

### 1. Vehicle fleet depreciation

Home services clients typically run 5-20 service vehicles (trucks, vans, trailers). 

**Post-OBBBA**: 100% bonus depreciation permanent for vehicles placed in service after Jan 19, 2025. Fleet purchases can generate large first-year deductions.

**Luxury auto limits**: Apply to passenger autos under 6,000 lbs GVW.
- Most work vehicles (F-250, cargo vans, box trucks) exceed 6,000 lbs GVW and qualify for full §179 or bonus depreciation on business portion.

**SUVs 6,000-14,000 lbs GVW**: §179 limited to ~$30,500 per vehicle for 2025 (indexed); bonus depreciation can apply for balance.

### 2. Payroll complexity

- Base hourly + overtime
- Commission on service tickets (tier-based performance)
- On-call pay, travel pay, billable hour structures
- Spiffs on specific SKU sales
- Bonus pools

Payroll system must handle all variants; many small operators using basic payroll under-track components, creating P&L distortion.

### 3. Parts inventory

Typically stocked in vehicles and at warehouse:
- Truck stock (rolling inventory)
- Warehouse inventory (bulk purchases)
- Job-specific orders

Cost of goods sold accuracy depends on consistent inventory methodology.

### 4. Workers' comp

High-risk trades have high workers' comp rates (5-15% of payroll for roofing, HVAC, electrical). Retrospective audits can create year-end surprises.

### 5. WOTC Credit opportunity

Hiring often from target groups:
- Veterans
- Long-term unemployed
- Ex-felons (reentry programs)
- Vocational rehab referrals

Credit up to $2,400/employee (or $9,600 for certain veterans).

**Critical**: Form 8850 must be submitted before/at hiring date. State workforce agency certification follows. Often missed because of procedural step.

### 6. Franchise royalty (for franchisees)

Franchisees of national brands (ServiceMaster, Molly Maid, Chem-Dry, etc.):
- Initial franchise fee: capitalized and amortized 15 years (§197)
- Ongoing royalties: operating expense (typically 5-8% of revenue)
- Marketing/tech fees: operating expense
- Territory expansion fees: additional §197 intangibles

## Strategies to run

- **Vehicle fleet planning**: §179/bonus depreciation on acquisitions
- **QBI Optimization**: Non-SSTB, full deduction available
- **Reasonable comp**: Owner-operator benchmarks
- **WOTC Credit**: Pre-screening process at hiring
- **Retirement plan**: Solo 401(k) (solo) or group 401(k) + Safe Harbor for staffed operations
- **PTET election**
- **Cost seg** if owning shop/warehouse
- **Hiring children**: Common (dispatch, parts runner, basic cleaning, social media)

## Red flags

- Vehicle deductions on luxury auto limits when work vehicles qualify for full §179
- WOTC not claimed despite hiring from target groups
- No S Corp election at $300K+ net income
- Reasonable comp unreasonable (either direction)
- Parts inventory not tracked consistently
- No R&D consideration if innovative methods used
