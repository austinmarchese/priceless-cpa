# Doctors / Medical Practices Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's medical professional niche — physicians (MD/DO), dentists, specialists, and medical group partners. High-income, SSTB-classified, retirement-savings-heavy planning profile.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Fee-for-service, insurance reimbursement, occasional cash-pay specialties (cosmetic surgery, functional medicine, concierge). Specialty-driven margin variation.

**Cost structure**: Provider compensation (30-50%), staff (15-25%), rent (5-10%), supplies (5-15%), malpractice/insurance (3-8%), other overhead (5-15%).

**Margin profile**: Solo practice net to owner (after salary) often 20-35%. Group practices: depends on compensation structure.

**Growth pattern**: Often plateau at single-practice capacity. Growth via additional providers, ancillary services (imaging, lab, procedures), or geographic expansion.

**Retirement planning profile**: Late starters (medical training delays). Often age 35-40 before significant savings begin. High earning years compressed into 20-25 year window. Retirement savings is TOP priority.

## Critical tax characteristic: SSTB classification

**§199A specified service trade or business (SSTB)**: Health services are enumerated SSTBs (§199A(d)(2)(A)).

**Practical implication**: QBI deduction fully phases out above threshold.

**Thresholds**:
- 2025: $197,300 single / $394,600 MFJ
- 2026: ~$203,000 single / ~$406,000 MFJ (exact per Rev. Proc. 2025-32)

**Phase-in range** (OBBBA widened for 2026):
- 2025: threshold to +$50K single / +$100K MFJ
- 2026+: threshold to +$75K single / +$175K MFJ

**Above phase-in range**: QBI deduction = $0 for SSTB.

**Planning implication**: Reducing taxable income into or below phase-in range RESTORES partial or full QBI deduction. This is the single biggest lever for most medical practice owners.

**Practice math for MFJ physician, $600K taxable income (2026)**:
- Above phase-in ($406K + $175K = $581K)
- QBI = $0
- Drop TI to $560K via DB Plan $40K contribution → back into phase-in
- QBI partially restored (depending on where in range)
- Direct benefit: DB deduction $40K × 37% = $14,800
- Indirect benefit: QBI partial restoration, another $10K-$25K

**This double-benefit math makes DB Plans particularly valuable for medical practice owners.**

## Common tax pain points

### 1. QBI phase-out (SSTB)

Most above-threshold physician owners lose QBI entirely. Strategies to reduce taxable income become disproportionately valuable.

**Typical pattern**: Single-physician S Corp with $700K net. Reasonable comp $300K, distributions $400K. W-2 + K-1 total ~$700K household. Well above SSTB phase-in. QBI = $0.

**Planning path**: DB Plan at age 50 can shelter $100K-$200K+, potentially dropping household income into phase-in, restoring partial QBI.

### 2. Reasonable compensation pressure

Physicians have strong RCReports benchmark data. IRS reclassification risk higher than other industries because:
- Well-documented professional comp benchmarks exist
- IRS specialist examiners for medical audit groups
- High distribution ratios trigger attention

**Common inherited issue**: Client's prior CPA set W-2 very low to minimize payroll tax, with massive distributions. Reclassification risk real.

**Priceless approach**: RCReports analysis annually, often at higher end of reasonable range to establish defensibility. Accept higher payroll tax cost for audit defense value.

### 3. Self-rental (practice real estate)

Common structure: doctor owns office building via separate LLC, practice S Corp leases from LLC.

**§469 self-rental rules**:
- Net rental income: non-passive (can't absorb other passive losses)
- Net rental loss: passive (can't offset active income)
- Can create planning asymmetry

**§163(j) election for real property trade/business**:
- Elect out of §163(j) (no interest limitation)
- Must use ADS (slower depreciation)
- IRREVOCABLE election
- Post-OBBBA EBITDA reversion may make election less necessary

**Cost segregation**: Self-rental properties typically excellent candidates. 20-35% of building cost reclassifiable to 5/7/15-year. With 100% permanent bonus depreciation (post-OBBBA), massive first-year deductions.

### 4. Retirement plan setup

Medical practices are high-income, SSTB, with late-starting retirement savings horizons. Retirement planning is DISPROPORTIONATELY IMPORTANT.

**Plan stack for mature practice (owner age 45+)**:
1. 401(k) / Profit-Sharing Plan: $70-72K total (§415(c) cap) + catch-up
2. Cash Balance or DB Plan: $100K-$300K depending on age
3. Mega Backdoor Roth (if plan supports): additional after-tax capacity
4. Backdoor Roth IRA: $7,500 + $1,100 catch-up (2026)
5. HSA: $8,750 family (2026) if HDHP
6. Spousal employment: additional capacity if spouse works in practice
7. Hiring children: transfer $16,100/child into 0% bracket

**Combined sheltering**: $250K-$500K+ possible for mid-career physician with full stack.

### 5. Malpractice insurance

- Premiums paid by practice: deductible business expense
- Premiums paid personally: not deductible unless through §162(l) for self-employed health (limited)
- Tail coverage at retirement: timing of deduction

### 6. CME and professional fees

Continuing medical education:
- Conference registration: deductible
- Travel to conferences: deductible with documentation (meals 50%, lodging 100%)
- Required CME hours: treat as professional development
- Personal travel attached to CME conference: careful allocation

Licensing fees, board certification, society memberships: generally deductible business expenses.

### 7. Payroll compliance

Medical practices have complex payroll:
- Physician W-2 with specific comp structure
- Mid-level providers (PAs, NPs) on various comp structures
- Hourly clinical staff
- Tip/bonus structures in some specialties
- §105 HRA or HSA coordination
- Various benefits (DI, life, malpractice)

## Strategies to run

### For every medical practice client

- **RCReports reasonable comp analysis**: Annual update. Benchmark defensibility critical.
- **Retirement plan evaluation**: If no DB/Cash Balance Plan and owner age 45+ with consistent income, STRONGLY recommend plan design consultation
- **QBI phase-in positioning**: Model taxable income against SSTB phase-in. Quantify dollar value of reducing TI.
- **PTET election** if state applies
- **Cost seg** if practice owns real estate

### For practice owners approaching $500K+ household income

- **DB/Cash Balance Plan**: Priority #1 strategy. Dollar value can exceed all other strategies combined.
- **Mega Backdoor Roth**: If 401(k) supports. Adds $25K-$45K/year Roth capacity.
- **Backdoor Roth IRA**: Income above direct threshold; indirect route.
- **Charitable bunching with DAF**: Post-OBBBA 0.5% floor and 35% cap apply, but DAF bunching mostly preserves benefit.

### Exit planning

- **Practice sale vs. dissolution**: Sale structure (stock vs. asset), tax character
- **Retirement benefit continuation**: §409A compliance for deferred comp structures
- **Real estate handling**: Sell with practice, retain as rental, §1031 into other real estate
- **Pre-retirement income management**: Ramping up retirement contributions in final working years

## Deliverable tailoring

Medical practice memo should emphasize:
- QBI phase-in positioning (visually — a curve showing deduction restoration as TI drops)
- DB Plan recommendation with multi-year projection
- Retirement savings trajectory (starting point → retirement goal)
- RCReports benchmark with source and percentile
- Self-rental / real estate handling if applicable

## Red flags to watch

- Reasonable comp below 25th percentile RCReports benchmark (reclassification risk)
- No DB/CBP plan for 50+ physician with consistent $500K+ income (missed opportunity)
- QBI deduction claimed in Zone 3 (SSTB) — reporting error
- Self-rental treated as passive income
- Owned building at 27.5/39-year straight line with no cost seg
- No §83(b) election for startup-practice partner stock
