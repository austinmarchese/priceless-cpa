# E-Commerce Industry Playbook

Priceless CPA's e-commerce niche — product-based businesses selling via Amazon, Shopify, DTC, marketplaces, or wholesale. Typical client range: $1M-$5M revenue, growing.

## Industry characteristics

**Revenue model**: Physical or digital product sales. Multiple channels common (Amazon FBA + Shopify + eBay + wholesale). Subscription box or recurring revenue variants.

**Cost structure**: COGS dominated (40-70% of revenue); platform fees (10-15%); ads (10-25%); fulfillment (5-15%); often low operating overhead.

**Margin profile**: Gross margins 30-55%; net margins after ads and all operating costs 10-25% typical for healthy operators; can be razor-thin for Amazon-native.

**Growth pattern**: Often highly seasonal (Q4 holiday spike, then Q1-Q2 funding the next cycle). Inventory cycles drive cash flow stress.

## Common tax pain points

### 1. Inventory accounting

**The issue**: Small e-commerce operators routinely use cash-method accounting, but per §471(c), taxpayers with average gross receipts over the threshold must account for inventory as non-incidental materials and supplies OR use inventory methods.

**2025 threshold (indexed, post-OBBBA)**: Average gross receipts >$30M over prior 3 tax years triggers required inventory method.

**Below threshold**: §471(c) provides simplified methods; can elect to treat inventory as non-incidental materials and supplies — deducted when sold or used.

**Above threshold**: Full inventory accounting required. FIFO, LIFO, or specific identification. §263A (UNICAP) may apply for larger operators.

**Engagement action**: Verify method at Phase 0; flag if inconsistent treatment across years.

### 2. Sales tax nexus (Wayfair, 2018)

**The issue**: Post-Wayfair, states assert economic nexus based on sales volume or transaction count. Most states: $100K or 200 transactions annual threshold.

**Common inherited problems**:
- Client registered in 2-3 states but selling into 30+
- Never registered but should have years ago — retroactive exposure
- Marketplace facilitator rules (Amazon collects on behalf of seller in most states) — but not all; not for all product categories
- Shopify / DTC sales have NO marketplace facilitator protection

**Engagement action**: Sales tax nexus assessment usually a separate engagement ($2K-$5K per state for historical catch-up). Flag exposure in Phase 3; partner conversation about remediation path.

### 3. Platform fee classification

**Amazon fees**:
- Referral fees (% of sale): COGS-adjacent, netted against revenue OR line-item expense (platform fees)
- FBA fulfillment fees: operating expense (fulfillment)
- FBA storage fees: operating expense (storage)
- Ads (PPC): operating expense (advertising)
- Long-term storage: operating expense
- Returns processing: operating expense

**Common error**: Treating Amazon "net deposit" as revenue. Should be gross revenue minus separately identified categories of fees.

**Impact**: Misclassifies margin and obscures real operating picture. Also affects state gross receipts tax calculations.

### 4. COGS landed cost accuracy

**Landed cost** = product cost + freight in + duties + inspection + other direct costs to get inventory to sale-ready state.

**Common error**: Product cost only tracked; freight/duties expensed directly. Understates COGS, overstates margins, distorts inventory value.

**Engagement action**: For clients with meaningful imports, review landed cost methodology in Phase 1.

### 5. Amazon 1099-K reporting

Amazon issues 1099-K for gross unadjusted payment — includes sales tax, fees, refunds, everything. Reported amount ≠ revenue. Client's tax return should reconcile 1099-K to actual revenue (net of returns, fees properly categorized).

### 6. Foreign supplier payments

Chinese, Indian, Vietnamese manufacturers paid via wire or Alibaba Trade Assurance:
- Generally NOT subject to 1099 reporting (foreign payee, not performing services in US)
- Form W-8BEN-E should be on file
- Some form of due diligence documentation advisable
- §1446(f) withholding typically not triggered

### 7. Section 174 R&D coordination (post-OBBBA)

If client has custom software development (Shopify app, proprietary platform, ML/AI recommendation engine):
- Post-OBBBA: domestic R&D immediate expensing restored for tax years after 2024
- §41 R&D credit separately available
- Software wages are generally qualified research expenses
- See CREDITS-REFERENCE for §41 mechanics

## Strategies to run

### Near-automatic

- **QBI Optimization**: E-commerce is non-SSTB. Full 20% deduction available. Above threshold, W-2/UBIA limit applies. Coordinate with reasonable comp.
- **S Corp Reasonable Comp**: Run RCReports analysis annually. E-commerce founders often overpay reasonable comp vs. distribution optimization.
- **Solo 401(k) / SEP**: Owner savings vehicle. Solo 401(k) typically wins.
- **Hiring children**: Especially if kids help with photos, product packaging, social media. Must be legitimate work.

### Situational

- **R&D Credit**: If custom platform development, recommend R&D Credit Study as separate engagement. Often $25K-$150K annual credit for $500K+ dev spend.
- **Cost Segregation**: If client owns warehouse or fulfillment facility, typically 20-35% of building cost reclassifiable to 5/7/15-year property.
- **PTET Election**: For state income tax states (NY, CA, NJ, etc.), entity-level tax often valuable.
- **Inventory method change**: If growing past the §471(c) threshold, Form 3115 method change may be required.

### Exit planning triggers

- **QSBS §1202**: If client anticipates sale/acquisition in 5+ years, consider C Corp conversion timing. OBBBA's tiered 3/4/5-year holding improves partial exclusion options.
- **Earnout structuring**: Tax-efficient earnout terms (contingent capital gain treatment vs. compensation treatment) — partner conversation before LOI signed.

## Deliverable tailoring

E-commerce memo should emphasize:
- Quarterly cash flow impact of recommendations (inventory-heavy businesses stress over cash)
- Clear scenario comparison (current method vs. optimized)
- Multi-state sales tax exposure callout (even if not in scope, flag for separate engagement)
- Platform-specific considerations (Amazon fee P&L reconstruction if ongoing issue)

## Red flags to watch

- Prior return shows "Amazon income" as net amount (revenue reconstruction needed)
- Inventory value on balance sheet zero or static (methodology broken)
- No sales tax registration or filing history but high multi-state sales volume
- S Corp with no reasonable comp (reclassification risk)
- Custom software development but no §174 or §41 treatment
- Foreign supplier payments with no W-8 documentation

## Engagement workflow notes

**Q1 engagement**: Focus on inventory method, prior-year return issues, sales tax nexus mapping, R&D credit study evaluation.

**Q2 engagement**: Mid-year reasonable comp calibration, PTET election verification, strategy execution for year.

**Q3 engagement**: Year-end projection, Q4 inventory timing decisions, last-chance retirement plan establishment for prior year (if applicable).

**Q4 engagement**: Year-end tax moves, equipment/§179 decisions, charitable giving if applicable, bonus depreciation on facility upgrades.
