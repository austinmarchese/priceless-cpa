# Promoter Patterns Partner Defense Framework

## Purpose of this file

This is NOT a strategy execution playbook. This is a PARTNER-FACING REFERENCE for handling situations where:

1. A client arrives with a tax strategy pitched by someone else (another CPA, financial advisor, promoter, or friend's tax guy)
2. Priceless is asked to implement, support, or sign off on it
3. Priceless needs to evaluate whether to participate, decline, or warn the client

This file gives partners the facts to respond professionally and protect both the client and the firm from exposure to problematic strategies.

## Why this matters (the firm-protection angle)

Priceless faces real exposure when participating in problematic strategies:

- **§6700 promoter penalty**: $1,000 or 100% of gross income derived from promoting (whichever is less) — applies to tax shelter promoters, which can include CPAs who participate
- **§6701 aiding and abetting penalty**: $1,000 per document for aiding tax understatement
- **§6694 preparer penalty**: $1,000 or greater of 50% of preparer fee
- **Circular 230 discipline**: suspension from practice before IRS for knowingly participating in abusive transactions
- **Civil liability to clients**: when strategies go bad, clients sue advisors who signed off

The calculus: declining a problematic strategy protects client AND firm. Participating in a clean strategy helps client. Participating in a problematic strategy = unprotected downside.

Default posture when promoted strategies appear: slow down, evaluate, get partner involved. Don't respond at staff level.

## The current promoter landscape (2026)

Strategies Priceless is actively seeing pitched or promoted:

### 1. Foreign Currency Trading Partnerships ("FX partnerships")

**Typical pitch**: Client invests in partnership. Partnership engages in FX trading. Losses allocated to client at substantial multiple of investment. Federal tax savings claimed at 37% of the allocated loss.

**Historical context**: FX straddle shelters have been targeted by IRS for decades. Blak Investments, Sugarloaf Fund, Saba Partnership — various shutdowns via litigation and regulation. §988 and §165 provisions aggressively interpreted by promoters.

**Current reality (2026)**: Still being marketed. New promoter names; structural similarities to past shutdowns. Economic substance weak. Promoter-level opinion letters used to justify.

**IRS posture**:
- Multiple listed transactions related to FX straddles
- §7701(o) economic substance doctrine codified
- §6662 accuracy-related penalty + §6694 preparer penalty exposure
- Active litigation pipeline

**Partner response when client mentions**:
- "We don't participate in FX trading partnership structures. The space has a long history of IRS enforcement and promoter penalties."
- Offer to review their specific structure for educational purposes if client wants to understand concerns
- Document refusal in writing to client
- If client pursues anyway with another advisor, Priceless disengages from those returns

### 2. §181 Movie Investment Partnerships (promoted version)

**Typical pitch**: "Invest $X in movie production; get $X+ in federal deductions." Structure promises full current-year write-off.

**Legitimate §181 scope**: §181 permits election to expense up to $15M of production costs for qualified films with significant production in US. Applies to ACTIVE film producer. Limited scope.

**Abused version**: Passive investor in syndicated partnership claims §181 election at investor level. Often combined with multiple partnerships or complex structures. Historically: many such structures haven't survived IRS scrutiny or Tax Court.

**Red flags**:
- Investor is passive, not active producer
- Deductions promised exceed investment
- Promoter opinion letter is the only legal support
- "Guaranteed" federal deduction marketing

**Distinction from legitimate**:
- Legitimate §181: active film producer with real production activity
- Legitimate option: BUY state film tax credits (see `MOVIE-TAX-CREDITS.md`) — completely different strategy

**Partner response**:
- "We don't participate in syndicated §181 movie investment partnerships."
- Clarify: we DO help with state film credit purchases — different transaction entirely
- Document refusal

### 3. Syndicated Conservation Easements

**Typical pitch**: Investor contributes cash to partnership; partnership acquires land; partnership donates conservation easement on land; donation valued substantially above cash contribution; deductions allocated to investors.

**IRS posture**:
- Multiple IRS Listed Transactions for syndicated conservation easements
- Historical and current Dirty Dozen appearances
- Thousands of prior-year returns under examination
- Statutory response: SECURE 2.0 added §170(f)(19) restricting deduction when donor's allocable deduction exceeds 2.5× contribution amount
- Ongoing Tax Court litigation; many taxpayers losing

**Current reality (2026)**: Heavily targeted by IRS. §170(f)(19) limits many traditional syndicated structures. New structures attempting to work around — each requires scrutiny.

**Distinction**:
- **Legitimate**: Individual or family donates easement on owned land. No syndication. Appraisal reasonable. §170 deduction at FMV of easement.
- **Problematic**: Syndicated structure with promised multiples above investment. Valuations aggressive.

**Partner response**:
- "We don't participate in syndicated conservation easement structures."
- If client has personal land with genuine conservation value: legitimate §170 contribution possible; requires qualified appraiser and structure memo
- If syndicated: decline

### 4. "Cost Segregation" on Tax Shelter Scale

**Typical pitch**: Cost seg study claims 50-70% of property cost is 5-year MACRS (aggressive reclassification). Combined with bonus depreciation = very large first-year deduction.

**Reality check**:
- Legitimate cost seg: 15-30% of cost typically reclassified to 5/7/15-year
- Aggressive cost seg: 50%+ claimed — often without engineering support
- Quality cost seg engineering firms produce defensible studies
- Promoters of aggressive cost seg use unqualified "engineers" or no real study

**Partner response**:
- Priceless uses vetted cost seg providers only
- Refuse to accept aggressive reclassification percentages without engineering documentation
- If outside study provided: partner review; may request supplemental analysis or second-opinion study

### 5. "Augusta Rule on Steroids"

**Typical pitch**: $2,000-$10,000 daily rate for home rental to business; up to 14 days. Creates $28K-$140K annual deduction.

**Reality check**:
- Legitimate Augusta Rule (§280A(g)): real meetings; market-rate rent; up to 14 days; tax-free to homeowner
- Inflated version: rates multiple times market; rooms claimed as meeting space that aren't
- Sinopoli v. Commissioner (Tax Court Memo 2023) — partial disallowance; documentation requirements affirmed

**Current file**: `AUGUSTA-RULE-280A.md` in the strategies/ directory covers legitimate use. Abuse-level rates and documentation are the problem.

**Partner response**:
- Priceless uses market-rate analysis; not aggressive rates
- Document every flight/meeting contemporaneously
- For outside promoter recommending aggressive rates: decline to participate at that level; offer to do properly

### 6. "Captive Insurance" (§831(b))

**Typical pitch**: Client forms own insurance company. Pays premiums; insurance company invests premiums. Current deduction for premium payments; tax-deferred investment in captive.

**IRS posture**:
- Multiple abusive captive programs identified as Listed Transactions (§1.6011-4)
- IRS Notice 2016-66 (micro-captives in particular)
- Rest of abusive structures under targeted examination
- §831(b) election scrutinized when used primarily for tax benefit

**Legitimate captive use**: genuine business with real insurance needs (multi-family offices, large professional practices) form captives for actual risk management. Premium rates appropriate for actual risks.

**Red flags**:
- Premium rates substantially above commercial rates for similar coverage
- Coverage for risks that aren't real (e.g., administrative errors for small practice)
- Captive investments not diversified; concentrated in owner's business
- Promoter's "§831(b) election" marketing as tax strategy

**Partner response**:
- Priceless does NOT typically engage with promoted captive structures
- Client with genuine large-business insurance need: refer to captive specialist firm (not promoter)
- Document refusal of abusive structures

### 7. Various "IUL Tax Strategies" / Life Insurance Structures

**Typical pitch**: Indexed universal life insurance policies pitched as tax strategies — tax-deferred growth, tax-free loan access, death benefit to heirs.

**Legitimate use**: IUL is a legitimate insurance product. When aligned with estate planning or liquidity needs, it serves real purposes.

**Abuse indicators**:
- Marketed primarily as tax strategy (not insurance)
- Premium funding schedules designed around §7702 tax rules maximization
- "Infinite banking" or similar aggressive marketing
- Commissions driving recommendations

**§7702** sets limits on how much premium can be paid relative to death benefit for tax-favored treatment. Exceeding = MEC (Modified Endowment Contract); policy loans become taxable.

**Partner response**:
- Life insurance for genuine estate/liquidity/succession: legitimate discussion
- IUL marketed solely as tax strategy: red flag
- Coordinate with vetted insurance advisor if client interest; Priceless does not sell insurance

## The decision framework for evaluating any outside promoted strategy

When a client brings in a promoted strategy, the partner works through these questions:

### Question 1: Is this on any IRS list?

- IRS Listed Transactions (§1.6011-4): if yes, decline
- Dirty Dozen current / historical: caution
- Published IRS notices warning about structure: caution

Check: IRS list.json, Dirty Dozen recent publications, any specific IRS action.

### Question 2: Does it pass the economic substance test?

§7701(o) requires both:
- Meaningful change in economic position (beyond tax)
- Substantial non-tax purpose

If the economic substance is purely tax benefit → doesn't pass → likely abusive.

### Question 3: Is the tax benefit claimed proportional?

- Return on investment realistic?
- Deduction relative to investment reasonable?
- Claimed savings vs. legitimate risk-return?

Red flag: deduction multiple of investment amount without corresponding economic risk.

### Question 4: Who else is involved?

- Promoter's background and history
- Promoter's prior structures and outcomes
- Whether promoter is SEC-registered (for investment products)
- Legal opinion letters from known counsel

Red flag: unknown promoter; opinion letter from unfamiliar firm; "proprietary" structure claims.

### Question 5: Would Priceless recommend this without the promoter?

Test: Strip away the promoter, the pitch, the presentation. If a client came in and described the underlying economics — would Priceless think this is a good deal?

Usually if the answer is no, the strategy is essentially packaging and tax positioning without real underlying value.

### Question 6: What's the downside if IRS challenges?

- Deduction disallowed
- §6662 accuracy penalty (20-40%)
- Fraud penalty (§6663) if egregious
- Interest on deficiency
- Legal costs for defense
- Reputational damage

Is the upside worth the downside tail?

### Question 7: Is there a LEGITIMATE alternative accomplishing similar goals?

Often the promoter's goal is valid; the structure isn't.

- Wants large deduction? → legitimate charitable strategies
- Wants capital gain deferral? → QOZ, §1031
- Wants income shelter? → legitimate O&G working interest
- Wants estate reduction? → CLT, GRATs, foundation

Almost always a cleaner path to the same goal. Offer the client the alternative.

## Communication framework for declining

When Priceless declines an outside-promoted strategy, frame it professionally:

### With the client

"We've reviewed the [structure name] you mentioned. We're not going to participate for several reasons: [specific concerns — economic substance, IRS posture, audit risk, documentation concerns, etc.]. Here's what we'd recommend instead: [legitimate alternative]."

Focus on:
- Specific technical concerns (not personal attacks on promoter)
- Client's best interest (audit risk, penalty exposure)
- Alternative paths to same goal

### In writing

- Document the decline in client file
- Include the reasons
- Note the alternative offered
- If client pursues anyway: note this; engagement may need to be restructured or terminated for those returns

### With promoter (if contact)

If promoter contacts Priceless directly:
- Polite decline without engagement
- Don't debate structure specifics
- Don't share client information
- "Our firm doesn't participate in that type of structure"

### Disengagement scenarios

Client insists on pursuing promoted strategy against Priceless advice. Options:

1. **Exclude from engagement**: Priceless doesn't prepare returns reflecting the disputed strategy; client uses other preparer for that return
2. **Terminate engagement**: If strategy fundamentally inconsistent with Priceless's engagement letter scope or professional standards
3. **Written disclaimer**: Document that client obtained third-party opinion and chose to proceed; Priceless prepares return with full disclosure (rare; only when strategy is marginal rather than clearly abusive)

Partner owns these decisions. Senior staff doesn't unilaterally decide.

## Specific scripts for common situations

### "My friend's CPA is doing this for $10M clients"

"I understand. Different CPA firms have different risk tolerances. We've looked at that type of structure and the risk-adjusted math doesn't work for our clients. Here's what we can do that addresses your underlying goal..."

### "This promoter says it's IRS-approved"

"The IRS doesn't pre-approve tax strategies. Tax strategies get tested on audit and in Tax Court. Most aggressive structures ultimately lose. We want your tax position to withstand examination, not just be aggressive."

### "The deduction is huge — why wouldn't we do it?"

"Deduction size is only part of the analysis. The other parts are: (a) will it survive audit, (b) what's the downside if it doesn't, and (c) is there a cleaner way to achieve similar savings. On most of these, the cleaner way exists and doesn't expose you to penalty."

### "The promoter has a legal opinion letter"

"Opinion letters from promoter-related counsel are marketing documents. We look at the underlying structure and your specific facts. We'd want our own analysis on a structure of this magnitude, not someone else's."

### "I've already invested"

"Let's look at what you've committed to. We can help with proper reporting and documentation. If the strategy has issues, we'll be transparent about the risks in your engagement. But we may need to structure our work differently given the outside strategy — let me explain options."

## Priceless firm policy (locked)

The following rules are NON-NEGOTIABLE firm policy:

1. Priceless does NOT participate in IRS Listed Transactions
2. Priceless does NOT sign off on strategies without economic substance
3. Priceless does NOT rely on promoter-provided opinion letters as primary support
4. Partner reviews ANY outside-promoted strategy before Priceless participates
5. Priceless documents declines in client files
6. Priceless escalates to terminate engagements when clients insist on abusive structures
7. Priceless does NOT recommend strategies for which Priceless receives undisclosed compensation
