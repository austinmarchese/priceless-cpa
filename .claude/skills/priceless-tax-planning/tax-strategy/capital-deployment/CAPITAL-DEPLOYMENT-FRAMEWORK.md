# Capital Deployment Framework (Operator 8)

This file is the gateway to Priceless's Capital Deployment strategy cluster. Every strategy file in `capital-deployment/` assumes the client has already cleared the gates in this document. If a client hasn't cleared these gates, the Capital Deployment conversation shouldn't happen yet — stay with Operators 1-7 (entity, reasonable comp, retirement, QBI, SALT, state, charitable).

## What Operator 8 is

Capital Deployment is the eighth and final operator in Priceless's methodology. It applies when:

1. Operators 1-7 have been optimized (entity, reasonable comp, retirement, etc.)
2. Client still has significant tax exposure
3. Client has liquidity and horizon to deploy capital into tax-advantaged vehicles

The thesis: for high-AGI clients with excess capital, the marginal tax rate on further income is so high (37% federal + 3.8% NIIT + state) that deployment into a tax-favored vehicle produces better after-tax returns than just "paying the tax and investing normally."

## When Capital Deployment DOES NOT apply

Not every Full-Wealth client is a Capital Deployment candidate. Do not raise these strategies when:

- **AGI < $750K**: firm threshold. Below this, the marginal-rate math usually doesn't justify the illiquidity cost, audit risk, and complexity.
- **Operating capital at risk**: if the client's business needs working capital and the deployment would strain liquidity, it's wrong timing.
- **Short horizon**: most of these strategies assume 3-10 year holds. A client planning exit in 12 months shouldn't be deploying into a QOZ fund.
- **Audit-sensitive posture**: client has current IRS examination, prior controversy, or aggressive position history. Add Capital Deployment on top of existing exposure = compounds risk.
- **Client's wealth advisor handles this**: some Priceless clients use a multi-family office or wealth manager who drives capital deployment. Priceless coordinates, doesn't lead.

When in doubt: don't raise it. The worst Capital Deployment outcome is a reluctant client deployed into something they didn't really understand, then stuck in a 7-year lock-up wondering why.

## The AGI > $750K threshold

Why $750K and not higher or lower?

**Below $750K**: Client's marginal federal rate is typically 32-35%. Combined with state, call it 40%. Legitimate deployment strategies return 6-10% pre-tax. After-tax, conventional investment produces 3.6-6%. A tax-deferred deployment at 8% pre-tax returns 6.4% after 20% long-term cap gains (if structured that way) — not dramatically better than conventional investment with good planning.

**Above $750K**: Marginal rate hits 37% federal + 3.8% NIIT + state. Combined ~42-45%. Now the gap between "deploy into tax-favored vehicle" and "pay the tax, invest normally" widens materially.

**Sensitivity**: A consulting practice with $800K AGI is a candidate. An e-commerce business with $600K AGI isn't (yet). If the business is growing, have the conversation in Q3 of the year AGI crosses $750K — not before.

## The eight gates (before recommending ANY Capital Deployment strategy)

Before surfacing a specific Capital Deployment strategy with a client, verify all eight:

### Gate 1: AGI threshold

Client's CURRENT YEAR projected AGI > $750K. Not prior year; not projected for 2030; current or this year.

### Gate 2: Operators 1-7 optimized

Confirmed:
- Entity structure right (S Corp for service businesses; LLC for real estate; etc.)
- Reasonable comp benchmarked and current
- Solo 401(k) / DB plan maxed (or intentionally not maxed for stated reason)
- QBI deduction captured to the extent available
- State strategy current (PTET, residency)
- Charitable strategy current
- HSA / §162(l) / other retirement vehicles maxed

If Operators 1-7 have gaps, close those first. They're cheaper, lower-risk, and faster.

### Gate 3: Liquidity margin

Client has deployable capital beyond what's needed for operations, reserves, and near-term commitments. Rule of thumb: 12 months of business operating expenses in reserves + 12 months of personal expenses + all known commitments funded = remainder is "deployable."

If the deployable amount is less than $250K, most Capital Deployment strategies aren't practical (fund minimums, due diligence costs, compliance overhead all scale unfavorably at small amounts).

### Gate 4: Horizon tolerance

Client understands and accepts the illiquidity profile of the specific strategy being considered. Aircraft: 5-6 year hold minimum for bonus recapture profile. QOZ: 10 years for full basis step-up. CRT: irrevocable. Private foundation: perpetual.

If client has a specific event in the next 2-3 years (business sale, divorce, medical) that might require liquidity, flag explicitly.

### Gate 5: Audit tolerance

Client understands these strategies attract IRS attention when executed aggressively or with thin documentation. Client willing to:
- Maintain rigorous documentation
- Use qualified specialists (TPA, actuary, valuation, legal)
- Accept audit risk for the specific strategy
- Pay for audit defense if needed

If client is audit-averse by temperament, stick to Operators 1-7.

### Gate 6: Compliance commitment

These strategies have ongoing compliance burden: K-1s from partnerships, Form 5500 for plans, Form 990-PF for foundations, §6038 for foreign, Form 1099 for various, annual valuations, etc. Client accepts this or has staff/advisor to handle.

### Gate 7: Partner approval

Every Capital Deployment recommendation has a partner sign-off flag. Partner (Tony) personally reviews the specific client fit before the recommendation reaches the client memo.

No exceptions. Senior staff doesn't surface Capital Deployment to clients without partner pre-approval.

### Gate 8: Alignment with client stated goals

The strategy actually moves the client toward something they've said they want:
- Wealth accumulation → QOZ, aircraft with actual business use, oil & gas for income
- Legacy / philanthropy → private foundation, CRT, CLT, DAF advanced
- Tax deferral for immediate cash need → §1031, installment sale coordination
- Estate planning → CLT, private foundation, CRT with income retained

A strategy that's tax-efficient but isn't what the client actually wants fails the sniff test. Don't sell strategies; align them with stated intent.

## The role Priceless plays (coordination, not execution)

Every Capital Deployment strategy in this cluster is a **coordination playbook**. Priceless does NOT:

- Own or operate aircraft
- Drill or operate oil & gas wells
- Manage equipment leasing funds
- Act as trustee for CRT, CLT, or foundation
- Manage QOZ fund investments
- Act as §1031 qualified intermediary
- Broker movie tax credits
- Provide international tax structuring as primary legal opinion

Priceless's role:

1. **Identify the fit**: does this strategy match the client's situation?
2. **Quantify the benefit**: what's the tax impact across years?
3. **Vet the specialist**: who should the client work with? (aircraft broker, fund sponsor, trustee, etc.)
4. **Coordinate integration**: ensure the strategy integrates with client's overall tax picture
5. **Compliance**: handle tax return aspects (K-1 integration, Form 8938, Form 990-PF coordination, etc.)
6. **Audit defense**: if challenged, coordinate with specialist providers on defense

This means every Capital Deployment engagement has two (sometimes three) professional relationships:
- Priceless CPA (tax and coordination)
- Specialist provider (the strategy itself)
- Often: attorney (trust formation, foundation formation, international structuring)

Client must understand this upfront. Priceless doesn't replace specialists; Priceless orchestrates.

## Partner diligence on specialist providers

When recommending a specialist (aircraft broker, fund sponsor, trustee, etc.), partner owns the diligence:

- Track record (years in business, prior client outcomes)
- Regulatory status (SEC registration if applicable, fiduciary status)
- Fee structure transparency
- Referral relationships (Priceless does NOT accept referral fees from most Capital Deployment specialists — conflict concern)
- References from other CPA firm partners or clients

If a specialist is NEW to Priceless, partner interviews before first referral. After a specialist has proven track record with multiple Priceless clients, diligence is updated annually.

Maintain an internal specialist roster with notes on who's been vetted, when, for which strategies.

## Path B compensation framework (when applicable)

Some Capital Deployment strategies have Path B affiliate tie-ins. The firm-level rules:

- **Never hide Path B compensation**: always disclosed in engagement letter and memo if Priceless receives compensation from any party other than the client
- **Always offer Path A option**: client can engage specialist directly without Priceless Path B involvement
- **Path B is optional for client**: client chooses; firm accommodates
- **Path B compensation doesn't distort recommendations**: strategies recommended on tax merits alone; affiliate options mentioned after strategy decision made

Applicable to (among others):
- Insurance-structured strategies (whole life, IUL within CRT or other structures) — Priceless PR Advisors and future insurance affiliate
- Investment advisory (RIA-managed funds within QOZ or similar) — future RIA affiliate
- Some fund sponsors offer sub-advisor relationships

File-specific Path B notes in individual strategy files.

## Common errors in Capital Deployment practice

### Error 1: Raising Capital Deployment too early

Client at $500K AGI asking about aircraft deductions — the right response is "not yet; let's optimize Operators 1-7 first and revisit when your AGI crosses $750K." Jumping to Capital Deployment before the foundation is set creates complexity without proportional benefit.

### Error 2: Recommending strategies without specialist diligence

Partner recommends QOZ fund without vetting the sponsor. Fund underperforms or has compliance issues. Client blames Priceless. Always vet specialists before recommending; document the diligence.

### Error 3: Using Capital Deployment to compensate for sloppy Operators 1-7

Client has bad reasonable comp, no retirement plan, and is being pitched aircraft. Fixing the basics produces more value with less risk. Do the boring things first.

### Error 4: Underestimating ongoing compliance

K-1s late, Form 5500 missed, Form 990-PF error — these cost the firm time and the client money. Before recommending, ensure the compliance infrastructure exists (either Priceless handles or client's other advisors handle).

### Error 5: Over-concentration in one strategy

Client has $2M deployable; entire amount goes to oil & gas. If oil prices crater, concentration risk materializes. Diversification within Capital Deployment matters — $500K aircraft + $500K QOZ + $500K oil & gas + $500K DAF is often better than $2M in any one.

### Error 6: Ignoring client's actual goals

Client says "I want to leave a legacy to my kids" — you recommend aircraft. Wrong strategy. CRT or foundation. Listen before prescribing.

### Error 7: Forgetting the tax is the TAIL, not the DOG

Tail wagging dog error: client buys aircraft they don't need just for bonus depreciation. Six months later operations costs $200K/year, client regrets it. The economic substance has to work INDEPENDENT of the tax benefit. If tax benefit is the only reason, it's not a strategy — it's a tax shelter.

## Partner conversation framework

When partner (Tony) evaluates a potential Capital Deployment recommendation, these questions guide:

1. Does the client CLEARLY pass all eight gates?
2. Is the strategy aligned with the client's STATED goals (not inferred goals)?
3. What's the quantified tax benefit across the full holding period (not just year 1)?
4. Who's the specialist provider? Vetted? Track record? Fee structure?
5. What's the audit risk profile? What's our defense strategy?
6. What happens if the strategy underperforms or the client needs exit? Consequences?
7. Is there a simpler strategy (Operator 1-7 gap) that would be better for this client right now?

If answers are clear and positive on all seven, proceed to memo recommendation. If any question gets a weak answer, pause.
