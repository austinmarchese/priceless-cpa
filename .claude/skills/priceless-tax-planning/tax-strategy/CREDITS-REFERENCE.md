# Tax Credits Reference

Comprehensive reference for tax credits that Priceless CPA evaluates during engagements. Lives inside the tax-strategy library as a peer to individual strategy files.

**All credit amounts and provisions verified against current law including OBBBA 2025 (P.L. 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025) and IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (2026 inflation adjustments).**

## CRITICAL OBBBA 2025 CREDIT TERMINATIONS

**OBBBA accelerated the termination of MANY energy-related credits.** Practitioners must track these dates carefully:

| Credit (Code §) | Termination Date | Applies to |
|-----------------|------------------|------------|
| §30D New Clean Vehicle | **Sep 30, 2025** | Vehicles acquired after |
| §25E Previously-Owned Clean Vehicle | **Sep 30, 2025** | Vehicles acquired after |
| §45W Commercial Clean Vehicle | **Sep 30, 2025** | Vehicles acquired after |
| §25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement | **Dec 31, 2025** | Property placed in service after |

| §45L New Energy Efficient Home | **June 30, 2026** | Homes acquired after |
| §179D Energy Efficient Commercial Buildings | **June 30, 2026** | Construction begins after |

For vehicles: "acquired" per OBBBA means written binding contract + payment made by the termination date. Placed in service can be later.

## Why credits deserve their own reference

Credits differ from deductions:
- Credits reduce tax dollar-for-dollar (vs. deductions that reduce taxable income by dollar amount, then tax by rate)
- Credits can be refundable (below zero tax) or non-refundable (cap at current tax)
- Credits have strict qualifying criteria
- Credits carry forward (usually) but have expiration windows
- Credits can be missed entirely without taxpayer awareness

A $10,000 credit = $10,000 tax savings. A $10,000 deduction at 32% = $3,200 savings. Credits matter more dollar-for-dollar.

## INDIVIDUAL CREDITS

### Child Tax Credit (§24) — OBBBA MODIFIED

**What it is**: Credit for qualifying children under 17.

**OBBBA update**:
- Amount: $2,200 per qualifying child (up from $2,000 under TCJA)
- Up to $1,700 refundable as ACTC
- Indexed for inflation 2026+
- Permanent (OBBBA removed sunset)
- Plus $500 Credit for Other Dependents (ODC) for non-qualifying child dependents

**Phase-out**: Begins at $200K single / $400K MFJ AGI. $50 reduction per $1,000 over.

**Applies when**: Taxpayer has qualifying child under 17 with SSN (ITIN only = ODC).

**Common errors**: Dependent with ITIN not SSN; custody allocation unclear.

### Child & Dependent Care Credit (§21)

**What it is**: Credit for child care (under 13) or disabled dependent care enabling work.

**Amount (2025-2026 baseline; check indexed amounts)**: 20-35% of up to $3,000 expenses for one qualifying individual, $6,000 for two or more. Maximum $1,050-$2,100/year.

**Phase-out**: Drops to 20% floor at higher income.

**Interaction**: Dependent Care FSA up to $5,000; CDCTC applies to expenses beyond FSA.

### Saver's Credit (§25B)

**What it is**: Non-refundable credit for low-to-moderate income retirement contributions.

**Amount**: 10-50% of up to $2,000 ($4,000 MFJ). Maximum $1,000 ($2,000 MFJ).

**Phase-out (2025)**: Single AGI $38,250 / MFJ $76,500.

**Relevance for Priceless**: Most clients exceed threshold. May apply to adult children of clients or clients in low-income years.

### American Opportunity Credit (§25A(i))

**What it is**: Credit for higher education expenses, first 4 years.

**Amount**: 100% of first $2,000 + 25% of next $2,000 = up to $2,500/year per eligible student. 40% refundable.

**Phase-out**: Single AGI $80K-$90K / MFJ $160K-$180K.

**Interaction with 529**: Cannot use same dollars — 529 distributions reduce AOC-eligible expenses.

### Lifetime Learning Credit (§25A(c))

**What it is**: Credit for any post-secondary education, no 4-year limit.

**Amount**: 20% of up to $10,000 expenses = up to $2,000/year per return. Non-refundable.

**Phase-out**: Same as AOC — Single AGI $80K-$90K / MFJ $160K-$180K.

### Adoption Credit (§23)

**What it is**: Credit for qualified adoption expenses.

**Amount (2026)**: Up to ~$17,280 per child (indexed; verify Rev. Proc. 2025-32).

**Phase-out**: Begins at AGI around $260K range (indexed).

### ⚠ Residential Clean Energy Credit (§25D) — TERMINATING

**Status**: **Terminated for expenditures after December 31, 2025** per OBBBA §70201.

**Amount**: 30% of qualified expenditures through 2025 cutoff.

**Critical for 2025 planning**: If client is considering solar/battery/geothermal, installation must be complete (§25D(e)(8)(A): expenditure treated as made when original installation is completed) by December 31, 2025.

**For 2026+**: No credit available for residential clean energy.

### ⚠ Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (§25C) — TERMINATING

**Status**: **Terminated for property placed in service after December 31, 2025** per OBBBA.

**Amount (through 2025)**: 30% of costs, capped at $1,200/year for most improvements, $2,000 for heat pumps/biomass.

**For 2026+**: No credit available.

### ⚠ Clean Vehicle Credit (§30D) — TERMINATING

**Status**: **Terminated for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025** per OBBBA §70202.

**Amount**: Up to $7,500.

**Acquired** means written binding contract + payment made on or before Sept 30, 2025. Placed-in-service can be later.

**For clients considering EV purchase**: flag the 9/30/2025 deadline immediately.

### ⚠ Previously-Owned Clean Vehicle Credit (§25E) — TERMINATING

**Status**: **Terminated for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025** per OBBBA.

**Amount**: Up to $4,000 (or 30% of sale price if less).

### ⚠ Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit (§30C) — TERMINATING

**Status**: **Terminated for property placed in service after June 30, 2026** per OBBBA.

**Amount**: Residential: 30% up to $1,000. Commercial: 30% up to $100,000 per location.

**Must be in eligible census tract** (non-urban or low-income).

**For clients installing EV chargers**: deadline is June 30, 2026. Plan accordingly.

### Premium Tax Credit (§36B)

**What it is**: Refundable credit for ACA marketplace health coverage.

**Status**: Enhanced subsidies (removed 400% FPL cap) extended temporarily; check current law for post-2025 status.

**Phase-out/subsidies**: Based on income relative to federal poverty level and benchmark plan cost.

**Relevance for Priceless**: Self-employed clients in low-income years; clients in transition periods.

## BUSINESS CREDITS

### Research and Development Credit (§41)

**What it is**: Credit for qualified research expenses. **THE highest-value credit** for software/AI, tech, and product-development businesses.

**Status**: Unchanged by OBBBA — remains permanent. OBBBA §70202 separately restored §174 immediate expensing for domestic R&D (incurred post-2024), which increases after-tax value of R&D activities.

**Amount**: 20% of qualified research expenses (QREs) in excess of a base amount (Regular method) OR 14% Alternative Simplified Credit (ASC) method.

**Qualifying activities (4-part test)**:
1. Permitted purpose (develop new or improved business component)
2. Elimination of uncertainty
3. Process of experimentation
4. Technological in nature (hard sciences)

**Industries where highly applicable**:
- Software/AI companies — MASSIVE, routinely missed
- E-commerce with custom platform development
- Manufacturers developing new products
- Construction companies developing new techniques
- Some service businesses developing proprietary tools

**Typical value**: For software companies with $500K-$5M in engineering wages, credit often $50K-$500K per year.

**Payroll tax offset election**: Qualified small businesses (under $5M gross receipts current year; no gross receipts 5+ years prior) can offset up to $500K against payroll taxes — valuable for pre-revenue startups.

**§174 restored under OBBBA**: Domestic R&D immediate expensing restored for tax years after 2024. §41 credit separately available.

**Documentation required**:
- Employee time tracking (% of each on qualified activities)
- Project documentation (component, uncertainties, experimentation)
- Contractor agreements
- Supply receipts
- Technical documentation

**Priceless approach**: For potential R&D clients, recommend R&D Credit Study as separate engagement ($5K-$15K fee; often $50K-$500K credit). Alternatively, partner with specialty R&D credit firm.

**Audit profile**: Top IRS audit area. Robust documentation critical.

**Carryforward**: 20 years forward, 1 year back.

### Retirement Plan Startup Costs Credit (§45E)

**What it is**: Credit for small employer starting new retirement plan.

**Amount (post-SECURE 2.0)**:
- Employers with 50 or fewer employees: 100% of admin costs (up to $5,000/year for first 3 years)
- Employers 51-100 employees: 50% of costs (up to $5,000/year)
- **Auto-enrollment bonus**: $500/year for first 3 years if auto-enrollment
- **Employer contributions credit** (smallest employers): up to $1,000/employee for 5 years (phased)

**Applies when**: Employer with ≤100 employees receiving ≥$5K compensation adopts new retirement plan (first time).

**Relevance**: SECURE 2.0 massively expanded. For small business owner establishing Solo 401(k) or small plan — real savings on setup costs.

**Priceless approach**: Flag for any client starting new retirement plan.

### Small Employer Health Insurance Credit (§45R)

**What it is**: Credit for small employers offering SHOP (Small Business Health Options Program) coverage.

**Amount**: Up to 50% of premiums paid (35% for tax-exempt employers).

**Applies when**: <25 FTE employees; average annual wages under ~$62K (indexed); employer pays at least 50% of premiums; coverage through SHOP.

**Relevance**: SHOP less common than direct carrier. Check client's setup.

### Work Opportunity Tax Credit (§51)

**What it is**: Credit for hiring from target groups (veterans, long-term unemployed, SSI recipients, ex-felons, vocational rehab referrals, etc.).

**Amount**: Up to $2,400 per qualifying employee in first year (first $6,000 × 40%); up to $9,600 for certain veterans.

**Applies when**: WOTC certification obtained from state workforce agency within 28 days of start date.

**Common errors**: Pre-screening not done (Form 8850 must be submitted before/at hiring); certification not obtained.

**Relevance**: Restaurants, retail, home services most applicable.

### Employer-Provided Child Care Credit (§45F)

**What it is**: Credit for employer providing child care facility or services.

**Amount**: 25% of qualified child care expenses + 10% of resource/referral expenses ($150K combined max).

**Relevance**: Large employers mainly. Flag for larger clients.

### Paid Family and Medical Leave Credit (§45S)

**What it is**: Credit for employer paying FMLA-like leave.

**Status (post-OBBBA)**: Made permanent under OBBBA with modifications.

**Amount**: 12.5-25% of wages paid during leave.

**Applies when**: Written paid leave policy, at least 2 weeks annual leave, leave pay at least 50% of normal wages.

### Disabled Access Credit (§44)

**What it is**: Credit for small business accessibility improvements.

**Amount**: 50% of eligible expenses between $250-$10,250 = max $5,000 credit.

**Applies when**: Small business (≤$1M gross receipts OR ≤30 employees).

### Rehabilitation Credit (§47)

**What it is**: Credit for rehabilitating certified historic structures.

**Amount**: 20% of QREs, spread over 5 years (4% per year).

**Applies when**: Certified historic structure, NPS-certified rehabilitation, QREs > greater of $5K or adjusted basis.

**Relevance**: Real estate investors restoring historic commercial buildings.

### Low-Income Housing Credit (§42)

**What it is**: Credit for investing in affordable housing.

**Status**: Expanded under OBBBA (permanent extension with enhancements).

**Amount**: 10-year credit stream roughly 4% or 9% of qualified basis per year.

**Relevance**: Investor-level typically via K-1 from LIHTC partnership.

### New Markets Tax Credit (§45D) — OBBBA EXTENDED

**Status**: Permanent under OBBBA.

**What it is**: Credit for investment in designated low-income community businesses via Community Development Entities (CDEs).

### Business Energy Investment Credit (§48)

**What it is**: Credit for commercial solar, fuel cells, geothermal, battery storage, etc.

**Status under OBBBA**: Significantly curtailed.
- Wind and solar facilities beginning construction after July 4, 2026 are ineligible if placed in service after 2027
- Various restrictions on foreign entity involvement (FEOC rules)
- Other technologies phase out post-2033

**Amount**: Base 6%; bonus 30% if prevailing wage and apprenticeship; plus adders for domestic content, energy community, low-income community (can exceed 50%).

**Relevance for Priceless industries**: Commercial building owners, car washes, gas stations installing solar.

**Urgency**: For clients considering energy investment, plan installation / construction timeline around the 2026-2027 cutoffs.

### ⚠ §179D Energy Efficient Commercial Buildings Deduction — TERMINATING

**Status**: Terminated for property with construction beginning after **June 30, 2026** per OBBBA.

**Amount (through cutoff)**: Up to $5.81/sq ft (prevailing wage + apprenticeship required for max).

**Allocable to designer/architect/engineer** for government-owned buildings.

**Relevance for Priceless industries**:
- Construction clients designing/building efficient commercial buildings
- Real estate investor clients making energy upgrades

**Urgency**: All construction must begin by June 30, 2026 to qualify.

### ⚠ §45L New Energy Efficient Home Credit — TERMINATING

**Status**: Terminated for homes acquired after **June 30, 2026**.

**Amount**: $2,500 per Energy Star dwelling; $5,000 per Zero Energy Ready Home (ZERH).

**Relevance**: Real estate developer clients, home builder clients.

### Carbon Oxide Sequestration Credit (§45Q)

**What it is**: Credit for capturing and sequestering qualified carbon oxide.

**Status under OBBBA**: Enhanced parity provisions — base credit amount increased to $17/ton ($36/ton for direct air capture).

**Relevance**: Niche. Not typical Priceless client.

### Advanced Manufacturing Production Credit (§45X)

**What it is**: Credit for domestic manufacture of components (solar, wind, battery).

**Status under OBBBA**: Modified with supply chain restrictions (FEOC rules).

## STATE CREDITS

State credit landscapes vary. General categories:

**State R&D credits**: Many states offer higher rates than federal (CA: 15%; NY: up to 9%). Stack with federal §41.

**State film/production credits**: Tradable credits; specialty area.

**State historic rehab credits**: Many states mirror federal §47 at 20-25%. Stacks.

**State low-income housing credits**: Pair with federal LIHTC.

**State job creation credits**: Various programs for adding employees in specific regions/industries.

**State energy credits**: State-level additional incentives on federal energy credits.

**For specific clients**: When state credit applicability identified, engage specialty provider or verify current state program. Credits change frequently.

## ACA-related modifications (OBBBA)

- OBBBA introduced new ACA requirements for eligibility verification
- New restrictions on Employee Retention Credit (ERC) claims and modified statute of limitations
- Practitioners should verify ERC eligibility carefully before advising clients

## CREDITS EVALUATION WORKFLOW

### Phase 3 evaluation

For each client, run through the catalog:

**1. Individual credits**:
- Children/dependents → CTC ($2,200) / ODC / CDCTC
- Education expenses → AOC / LLC
- Home improvements → §25C / §25D (through 2025 only)
- Vehicle purchases → §30D / §25E (through 9/30/2025 only)
- EV charger installation → §30C (through 6/30/2026)

**2. Business credits**:
- R&D activity? → §41 (DEEP DIVE, often separate engagement) + §174 coordination
- New retirement plan? → §45E (SECURE 2.0 expanded)
- Health insurance via SHOP? → §45R
- Hiring target groups? → WOTC §51
- Energy property installation? → §48 (business, with urgency) / §25D (residential, through 2025 only)
- Historic rehab? → §47
- Paid family leave policy? → §45S (permanent post-OBBBA)

**3. State credits**:
- State R&D credit (if federal §41 applicable)
- State-specific programs

**4. Prior-year missed credits**:
- Was each applicable credit claimed?
- If not, amendment analysis

## Common errors we inherit re: credits

- **R&D credit never evaluated** for software/AI clients
- **§45E not claimed** for new Solo 401(k) (SECURE 2.0 expansion)
- **§25C / §25D stacked wrong** (same dollars under both)
- **EV credit missed** before 9/30/2025 deadline
- **EV credit point-of-sale election** missed (often better than claiming on return)
- **Historic rehab credit missed** when rehab started without certification
- **State R&D stacking missed** when federal claimed
- **Payroll tax offset election missed** for qualified small business R&D
- **§162 expense taken when §41 credit-eligible**
