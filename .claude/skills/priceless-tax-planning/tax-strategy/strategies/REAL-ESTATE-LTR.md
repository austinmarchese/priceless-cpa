# Long-Term Rental (LTR) Tax Planning

Long-term residential and commercial rentals — the traditional rental real estate playbook. Less aggressive than STR strategy, more broadly applicable, core to real estate investing tax planning.

## The basic mechanic

Owner acquires property, rents to tenant on long-term basis (typical: 12-month lease, sometimes multi-year commercial):

- **Revenue**: Rental income from tenant
- **Expenses**: Mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, repairs, maintenance, management fees, utilities (if landlord pays), supplies, depreciation
- **Depreciation**: 27.5 years residential; 39 years commercial (straight-line)
- **Net result**: Often net tax loss (depreciation dominates)

**§469 classification**: Rental real estate is per se passive unless:
1. REPS qualification (see separate file), OR
2. Property fits STR 7-day exception (see REAL-ESTATE-STR.md), OR
3. Material participation in a non-rental real estate trade/business

**Passive loss trap**: Without qualifying for an exception, net losses suspend. Can only offset passive income (other rental net income, passive partnership income).

**Release event**: Loss releases in year of disposition of the activity (fully taxable sale).

## When LTR is most valuable

### Wealth-building foundation

- Steady cash flow with leveraged amplification
- Long-term appreciation and inflation hedge
- Depreciation shields cash flow from current-year tax
- §1031 allows tax-deferred swaps into larger properties

### Portfolio builder

Client with W-2 or active business income who gradually accumulates rentals:
- Each property: manageable tax treatment
- Cumulative passive losses suspend (can be valuable later)
- At retirement: rental income becomes primary, prior losses absorb
- Disposition: released losses offset gain

### Real estate professional

Spouse of W-2 earner or full-time real estate investor qualifying as REPS:
- Non-passive treatment of losses
- Losses offset W-2 income (for MFJ, offsets both spouses)
- With cost seg + 100% bonus: very aggressive year-1 sheltering

## Key mechanics

### Depreciation

Residential (1-4 unit + more with rental intent): 27.5 years, straight-line. Commercial: 39 years. Begins in month placed in service (mid-month convention).

**Basis allocation**: Purchase price + acquisition costs allocated between land (not depreciable) and building. Land typically 15-30% of total; check property tax assessment for guidance.

**Improvements**: Replaced roof, HVAC, etc. — typically capitalized and depreciated as separate asset (shorter life if qualifies). Post-OBBBA §179 and bonus depreciation expanded the options.

**Repairs vs. improvements** (Treas. Reg. §1.263(a)-3):
- Routine maintenance: expensed
- Betterment, restoration, adaptation: capitalized
- Safe harbors: de minimis (<$2,500/item per election), routine maintenance

### Passive loss tracking

§469 losses suspended — report on Form 8582. Each activity tracked. Released upon:
- Disposition of that specific activity (releases against gain + absorbs from other passive income)
- Complete disposition of all passive activities
- Death (basis step-up, suspended losses lost generally — but check current law)

**Grouping election (§1.469-4)**: Can combine multiple rental activities to be treated as single activity for material participation. Beneficial sometimes; requires careful planning (election is generally irrevocable).

### §199A safe harbor for rentals

Rental real estate qualifies as trade/business for QBI deduction if Notice 2019-07 safe harbor met:
- Separate books and records per rental real estate enterprise
- 250+ hours of rental services per year (can be owner or contractors)
- Contemporaneous records of hours and services

Or if general §162 trade/business standard met (facts and circumstances).

**For qualifying rentals**: Net rental income contributes to QBI (20% deduction available).

### Financing considerations

**§163(j) interest limitation**: Post-OBBBA reverted to EBITDA basis. Most real estate investors preserve interest deductibility.

**Real property trade/business election**: §163(j)(7)(B) election. Electing out of §163(j). Requires ADS depreciation (slower, 30 years residential / 40 years commercial). Generally less needed post-OBBBA EBITDA reversion.

### Exit planning

**§1031 like-kind exchange**:
- 45-day identification, 180-day close
- Qualified intermediary required
- Real for real (any real property)
- Basis carries over (or stepped up to replacement cost minus deferred gain)

**Installment sale**: Report gain over years as collected.

**Charitable remainder trust**: Donate property; receive income stream; charitable deduction; avoid capital gains.

**Hold to step-up**: Inherit at stepped-up basis; eliminates embedded gain. Standard estate planning for real estate wealth.

## Cost segregation + 100% bonus depreciation post-OBBBA

For LTR with basis > $500K (residential) or >$250K (commercial), cost seg often valuable:

- Typical residential reclassification: 15-25% to 5/7/15-year
- Combined with 100% bonus (post-OBBBA permanent): massive year-1 deduction
- Interaction with passive loss rules: if no REPS, creates suspended loss (valuable at disposition)

## Common errors

- No §199A safe harbor documentation — QBI missed
- Cost seg not done on property > $500K basis
- Repairs capitalized that should be expensed
- Improvements expensed that should be capitalized
- §1031 attempted without QI — fails
- §163(j) not analyzed for highly leveraged property
- REPS claim without proper documentation

## Interaction with strategies

### Cost seg + passive

Without REPS, cost seg creates bigger suspended loss. Not immediately beneficial but:
- Releases at disposition (offsets gain)
- Can absorb future passive income
- Shelter is deferred not denied

### REPS qualification

See separate file. Transforms passive into non-passive; cost seg becomes current-year benefit.

### PTET

If LTR held via partnership or S Corp in PTET state, entity-level state tax deductible at federal level (beyond SALT cap).

### Charitable

Appreciated rental property donated to DAF or charity: avoid recapture + capital gains. Special considerations for §1245/§1250 property.
