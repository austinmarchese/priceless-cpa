# Real Estate Professional Status (REPS)

REPS qualification transforms rental real estate activities from passive (losses suspended) to non-passive (losses freely offset other income). For clients with substantial rental real estate AND time to devote to it, REPS is often the single most valuable tax status.

## The two-part test (§469(c)(7)(B))

To qualify as a real estate professional:

1. **More than 50% of personal services** performed during the tax year must be in real property trades or businesses in which taxpayer materially participates, AND

2. **More than 750 hours** of services performed in real property trades or businesses in which taxpayer materially participates

**For joint filers**: EITHER spouse can qualify. The qualifying spouse need not be the owner. MFJ treatment: non-passive losses flow to both spouses' tax return.

**Real property trade or business** (§469(c)(7)(C)):
- Development
- Construction
- Rental
- Operation
- Management
- Brokerage
- Reconstruction
- Acquisition
- Conversion
- Leasing

## Material participation within real estate activities

REPS alone isn't enough. The taxpayer must ALSO materially participate in EACH rental activity for its losses to be non-passive. By default, each property is a separate activity.

**Election to aggregate (§1.469-9(g))**: Treat all interests in rental real estate as a single activity for material participation purposes. Most REPS taxpayers make this election. Requirements:
- Election made on timely filed original return
- Written statement attached to return
- Generally binding for all future years until revoked
- Revocation requires material change in facts

With aggregation election: material participation tested across all rental real estate collectively. Usually easy to meet 500-hour test if pursuing REPS.

## The >50% of personal services test (critical)

This is the harder test for most people.

Total personal services during tax year × 50% must be less than hours in real property trades/businesses.

**Full-time W-2 earner**: Typically has 2,000+ hours in W-2 work. To pass >50% test, would need 2,001+ hours in real estate. Usually impossible.

**Best candidates** for passing >50%:
- **Non-working spouse**: 0 W-2 hours, so any real estate hours exceed 50%
- **Part-time W-2 spouse**: e.g., 800 W-2 hours + 900 real estate hours passes
- **Self-employed in real estate**: mostly or entirely in real property
- **Retired**: 0 W-2, real estate activities can easily dominate

**Common arrangement**: High-income W-2 spouse + homemaker spouse. Homemaker spouse qualifies REPS. Couple's rental losses non-passive. Offset both spouses' income.

## Material participation: the 7 tests (§1.469-5T)

Meet any ONE:
1. **500+ hours** in the activity
2. **Substantially all hours** by any individual
3. **100+ hours and more than anyone else**
4. **Significant participation in aggregate** (>500 hours across SPAs)
5. **Material participation in 5 of last 10 years**
6. **Personal service activity, material participation in any 3 prior years**
7. **Facts and circumstances** (regular, continuous, substantial)

For REPS with aggregation election, Test 1 (500+ hours in rental real estate overall) most common.

## Documentation standards (CRITICAL)

IRS aggressively challenges REPS. Tax Court cases frequent. Taxpayers LOSE when documentation inadequate.

**Contemporaneous time log**:
- Date
- Activity description
- Duration (specific hours, not "full day")
- Property/activity identification

**Best practice**: Daily log entered within days of activity. Electronic log (Google Calendar, Excel, app like REPStracker) with timestamp preservation.

**WORST practice**: Reconstructed after-the-fact from memory. Tax Court has repeatedly rejected reconstructed logs.

**Activities that count**:
- Tenant selection, application review
- Showings and property tours
- Lease negotiation and execution
- Rent collection, accounting
- Property maintenance and repairs (if self-performed)
- Contractor coordination and supervision
- Vendor selection and supervision
- Property inspections
- Bookkeeping and financial management
- Legal matters (evictions, disputes)
- Marketing and advertising
- Acquisition due diligence
- Capital improvement planning and oversight

**Activities that DON'T count**:
- "Investor" activities (studying markets, reading investment publications)
- Travel time to/from property (limited allowability)
- Commute-like passive time
- Personal use of property time

## Common scenarios

### Scenario 1: Homemaker spouse, 3 rental properties

Homemaker spouse manages all 3 rentals:
- Tenant communication
- Bookkeeping
- Contractor coordination
- Property inspections
- Marketing/listing

750-1000 hours easy across active management of 3 properties. Passes 750 hours. Passes >50% (no other work).

**Result**: REPS qualified. Rental losses non-passive, flow against high-income W-2 spouse's income.

### Scenario 2: Real estate agent + rental owner

Full-time real estate agent with rental portfolio:
- Brokerage activity: 1,500 hours/year
- Personal rental management: 400 hours/year
- Total real estate: 1,900 hours

Brokerage IS real property trade/business. Personal rental IS. Both count toward 750 and >50% tests.

Total real property: 1,900 hours. Other personal services: 0 (if real estate is only work).

**Result**: REPS qualified. Both brokerage and rental non-passive.

### Scenario 3: W-2 doctor with rental portfolio

Doctor works 2,200 hours/year. Owns 5 rentals, personally manages 300 hours/year.

- >50% test: Need 2,201+ real estate hours. Not possible.
- 750 hours test: Only 300. Fails.

Does NOT qualify as REPS. Rental losses suspend.

**Planning alternatives**: Spouse REPS, STR 7-day exception, hire real estate manager and document management, change work/life balance.

### Scenario 4: Part-time W-2 + rental management

Part-time consultant 800 hours/year. Manages 4 rentals 1,000 hours/year.

- >50% test: 1,000 real estate > 400 (half of 800 consulting). Passes.
- 750 hours test: 1,000 > 750. Passes.

**Result**: REPS qualified.

## Post-OBBBA interactions

### 100% bonus depreciation permanent

REPS + cost seg + 100% bonus = massive year-1 non-passive deductions.

Example: REPS-qualifying couple, $2M rental property acquired 2026, cost seg identifies $500K reclassifiable:
- Year 1 bonus depreciation: $500K
- Plus operating losses: additional
- Total year 1 tax loss: $550K-$650K
- Non-passive: offsets W-2 and other income
- At 37% bracket: $200K+ federal tax savings

### §163(j) EBITDA restoration

Highly leveraged real estate preserves interest deductibility.

## Audit risk mitigation

- **Contemporaneous log**: non-negotiable
- **Aggregation election**: filed properly on first-year return
- **Clear >50% test basis**: documentation of other work hours if applicable
- **Partner review**: every client claiming REPS should have documentation reviewed annually
- **Multi-year pattern**: sustainable REPS requires ongoing commitment

## Common errors

- Claiming REPS without meeting >50% test
- No contemporaneous time log
- Reconstructed log from memory
- Spouse claimed REPS without clear basis
- Aggregation election not filed
- Re-qualifying not done annually (REPS is year-by-year)
- Travel time inflated
- "Investor" activities counted (don't)
