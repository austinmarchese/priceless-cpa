# Q4 Workflow — October through December 31

Q4 is the execution quarter. Most strategies identified in Q3 must be implemented in Q4 to apply to the current year. The Q4 memo is short and action-oriented — less strategy analysis, more "here's what needs to happen before December 31."

## What Q4 covers (calendar overview)

**October**:
- Q3 memo has been delivered, strategies committed
- Extended 1040 returns due (Oct 15) — some bleed from Q3
- Begin Q4 execution tracking

**November**:
- Most strategies executed or on execution path
- Q4 memo prep begins
- Final year-end decisions finalize

**December**:
- Q4 memo delivered (early December)
- Year-end execution deadline (Dec 31)
- W-2 withholding final adjustments
- Year-end charitable giving
- Retirement plan contributions (final employee deferrals)

## The Q4 memo's role

Action-oriented execution checklist:
- Track strategies committed in Q3
- Identify any remaining Q4-actionable items
- Year-end withholding / estimated payment final adjustments
- Lead into Q1 of following year (year-end transitions)

Typical Q4 memo: shortest of the year. 50-70% of Q2 length. Clients expect crisp action items.

## Phase sequence for Q4 engagements

### Phase 1: Bookkeeping QA (year-end focus)

Focused on:
- Year-end adjusting entries planning (depreciation, accruals, deferred items)
- Fixed asset additions: classification confirmed for §179 / bonus
- Payroll through November reconciled; December final run considerations
- Any strategy transactions from Q3-Q4 properly recorded

Client will have year-end close in January/February, but Q4 memo can flag issues that should be addressed in December.

### Phase 2: Tax Projection (final update)

Most accurate projection of the year:
- 9-10 months of actuals
- Known remaining year events
- Strategy impacts from Q3 commitments reflected

Updates safe harbor status for final Q4 payment.

### Phase 2.5: Payroll Analysis (year-end)

S Corp clients specifically:
- Year-end W-2 positioning
- Any bonus / year-end comp adjustments
- Final §162(l) health insurance amount on W-2
- Final HSA contribution amount on W-2
- Final retirement plan employee deferral election

### Phase 3: Strategy (execution tracking)

Q3-committed strategies tracked for execution:
- Which are done
- Which are in progress
- Which need final push

Q4-only strategies:
- W-2 withholding adjustment (final paychecks can cover safe harbor gap)
- Year-end Roth conversions (must complete by Dec 31)
- Loss harvesting (must complete by Dec 31)
- Year-end charitable giving (DAF or direct, must complete by Dec 31)
- Final bonus depreciation / §179 decisions (assets placed in service by Dec 31)
- Final retirement plan contributions (employee deferrals by Dec 31; some employer by tax deadline)
- Final HSA contribution (employee side; can extend to April 15)
- Final entity elections for current year

### Phase 4: Synthesis

Q4 memo assembly. Execution-oriented:

- Status of Q3 commitments
- Year-end action items
- Final estimated payment (Q4 due Jan 15 following year)
- Withholding adjustments if needed
- Transition note to upcoming Q1

## Q4 estimated payment calculation

Standard: 25% of safe harbor target.

Q4 payment is special because:
- Due Jan 15 of following year
- Can use W-2 withholding adjustment mechanism (for W-2 recipients) as alternative
- Can be the catch-up payment if previous quarters came up short

W-2 withholding catch-up mechanism:
- Remaining pay periods × per-pay-period additional withholding = amount needed
- W-2 withholding treated as paid throughout year (per safe harbor rules)
- Can cure Q1-Q3 underpayment risk via Q4 withholding increase

For S Corp owners:
- December payroll can include increased FIT withholding
- Converts employer funds to personal FIT prepayment
- Specific calculation: (Safe Harbor Target - YTD FIT) / remaining December pay periods = additional per-pay-period withholding

## Year-end strategy execution priority list

**Must-execute by Dec 31**:
1. Loss harvesting (if applicable)
2. Roth conversions
3. DAF funding / charitable giving
4. §179 / bonus depreciation asset placements
5. Solo 401(k) employee deferrals
6. DB / Cash Balance plan establishment
7. Final reasonable comp via payroll
8. Year-end bonuses (if comp true-up needed)

**Can extend beyond Dec 31**:
1. Employer retirement contributions (tax filing deadline with extension)
2. HSA contributions (April 15 next year)
3. IRA contributions (April 15 next year)
4. SEP-IRA contributions (tax filing deadline with extension)

**Administrative but important**:
1. §125 cafeteria plan elections for next year
2. Next year's insurance enrollments
3. Next year's contribution elections
4. Next year's payroll setup (reasonable comp, §162(l), HSA for >2% shareholders)

## Q4 common findings

- Q3 strategies incompletely executed (need Q4 catch-up)
- Safe harbor gap requiring Q4 action
- Year-end charitable giving plan execution
- Asset purchases needing placed-in-service before Dec 31
- Missed HSA / retirement contributions — push to fund
- §162(l) or HSA coding errors on payroll discovered — correct before year-end W-2
- DAF contribution timing (need to initiate stock transfer by mid-December for year-end clearing)

## Q4 client communication

Framing:

"Your Q4 2026 memo — year-end execution focused.

Status of what we committed in Q3:
- Accountable plan: [done]
- Augusta Rule: [on schedule — 4 more meetings before year-end]
- [Cash Balance plan / DB plan]: [status]
- [Specific to client]

What still needs attention before December 31:
1. [Highest-priority action with specific deadline]
2. [Second action]
3. [Third action]

Your Q4 estimated payment of $X is due January 15, 2027. We recommend [specific payment approach — estimated payment vs. W-2 withholding adjustment].

Next memo: early March 2027 (Q1 2027 — annual anchor memo once 2026 is closed)."

Concise. Action-oriented. Keep client focused on execution.

## Q4 delivery timing

Target: early December delivery.

Why: clients need 3-4 weeks to execute year-end items. A late-December Q4 memo leaves too little time for:
- DAF funding (stock transfers take 1-2 weeks)
- Bonus payroll runs
- Major charitable gifts
- Year-end portfolio moves

## Q4-specific risks

- **Delivery too late**: If Q4 memo lands mid-December, clients can't execute several year-end items. Aim for early December.
- **Q3 recommendations not executed**: If client didn't act on Q3, be honest in Q4. Surface clearly. Don't pretend they were.
- **Over-promising DB/CBP**: If DB plan wasn't established by Q3, Q4 is too late for current year. Set expectations.
- **Missing year-end deadlines**: December gets hectic. Calendar critical dates in Karbon early.
- **Holiday timing**: Many custodians have cutoffs in mid-to-late December for year-end processing. Factor in.

## Year-end transition preparation

Q4 also sets up Q1 of next year:

- Prior-year return prep scheduled
- Bookkeeping closing process (December reconciliations)
- W-2 and 1099 production
- Carryforward amounts tracked for Q1 Tax Return Analysis
- Client profile updated for next year (new year's tier, new year's known events)
