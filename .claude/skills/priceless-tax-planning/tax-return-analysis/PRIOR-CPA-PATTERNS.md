# Prior CPA Patterns

This file documents common patterns observed in prior-CPA-prepared returns inherited by Priceless. Built up over time as we onboard new clients. Updated quarterly based on what we see.

This file is NOT a list of other firms by name. It's a catalog of common errors and missed strategies that help analysts recognize patterns quickly during tax-return analysis.

## Purpose

When Priceless takes on a new client, the prior year return tells us:
- What strategies the prior CPA used (or didn't)
- What positions they took (and whether they're supportable)
- What documentation patterns to expect
- What corrections or amendments may be warranted

Cataloging patterns helps new analysts spot issues faster. "This looks like a classic [pattern X] case" gets an experienced analyst to the right analysis quickly.

## Common patterns (v0.5 baseline — expand over time)

### Pattern A: The "zero reasonable comp" S Corp

**What it looks like**:
- S Corp with significant net income ($200K+)
- Owner on board as officer, actively working in business
- W-2 from S Corp reports $0 or implausibly low amount ($20K on a $500K-profit business)
- All owner income taken as distributions
- No reasonable comp methodology documented

**What's wrong**:
- Reclassification risk on audit: IRS argues all/most profit is wages
- §162(l) health insurance deduction lost (requires W-2 wages)
- Solo 401(k) employer contribution limited by the zero W-2
- Social security wage crediting lost (matters less now but could have mattered)

**Priceless approach**:
1. Flag in Return Analysis Report
2. Recommend reasonable comp analysis in current year
3. Evaluate amendment for prior years within statute (cost-benefit)
4. Document client conversation on approach going forward

### Pattern B: The "missed QBI" return

**What it looks like**:
- Owner of profitable pass-through entity
- AGI below or near §199A threshold
- Form 8995 or 8995-A absent from return
- Or form present but calculation obviously wrong

**What's wrong**:
- Missing 20% deduction worth up to 7.4% effective rate reduction
- Aggregation election not made where beneficial
- SSTB classification possibly wrong

**Priceless approach**:
1. Verify QBI missed (sometimes the calculation is correct but looks off)
2. Amendment opportunity if material and within statute
3. Aggregation election in current year if beneficial
4. SSTB reclassification if defensible

### Pattern C: The "never tracked basis" S Corp

**What it looks like**:
- Long-history S Corp (5+ years)
- No basis schedule in client records
- Prior CPA's workpapers don't include basis tracking
- Distributions regularly exceed current-year income
- Client asks about pulling more cash out

**What's wrong**:
- Basis may be exhausted; further distributions create capital gain
- Suspended losses from prior years not tracked
- No ability to plan distributions safely

**Priceless approach**:
1. Reconstruct basis from available records
2. If reconstruction uncertain, recommend separate basis study engagement
3. Partner manages client conversation (uncomfortable but necessary)
4. Going forward: annual basis maintenance becomes standard

### Pattern D: The "aggressive home office" return

**What it looks like**:
- S Corp owner's home office deduction
- Actual expense method claimed (not simplified)
- Square footage percentage unusually high (50%+)
- All home expenses claimed with no apparent allocation

**What's wrong**:
- Square footage percentage often not defensible on audit
- Full utility costs claimed without allocation
- No evidence of exclusive business use

**Priceless approach**:
1. Verify reasonable allocation
2. If clearly unsupportable, reduce to defensible position
3. Accountable plan reimbursement for the allowable portion going forward
4. Documentation protocol (photos, dedicated space description)

### Pattern E: The "phantom depreciation" property

**What it looks like**:
- Schedule E rental property
- Basis reconstruction shows mismatched depreciation
- Section 1250 depreciation not tracked (rental real estate)
- Section 179 claimed inappropriately (real property)
- Cost segregation study results unclear

**What's wrong**:
- Depreciation may be under- or over-claimed
- Section 1250 recapture exposure at sale not understood
- Basis step-up opportunities at death/estate transfers not captured

**Priceless approach**:
1. Reconstruct depreciation from scratch using property records
2. File Form 3115 (change in accounting method) if missed depreciation is material
3. Going forward: accurate depreciation tracking in client records

### Pattern F: The "never aggregated" multi-entity owner

**What it looks like**:
- Client owns 2-4 related pass-through entities
- All eligible for aggregation under §199A
- Aggregation election not made
- QBI deduction calculated separately for each

**What's wrong**:
- W-2/UBIA limitations applied per entity when combined would be better
- Total QBI deduction less than optimal

**Priceless approach**:
1. Evaluate aggregation eligibility (same owner 50%+, same fiscal year, non-SSTB, meet 2 of 3 relationship criteria)
2. Elect aggregation in current year
3. Amendment consideration for prior years if material and within statute

### Pattern G: The "missed §162(l)" S Corp owner

**What it looks like**:
- >2% S Corp shareholder with health insurance
- Premiums paid by the corp
- W-2 box 1 doesn't include premium amount
- No §162(l) deduction on personal return
- Or: premium in W-2 but also in boxes 3/5 (FICA overpayment)

**What's wrong**:
- Missing above-the-line deduction for self-employed health insurance
- Or paying unnecessary FICA/Medicare on premium amount

**Priceless approach**:
1. Flag specific coding errors on W-2
2. File Form 941-X to recover overpaid FICA/Medicare
3. Amend 1040 if deduction was missed
4. Going forward: payroll provider instruction to code correctly

### Pattern H: The "no PTET" high-income state client

**What it looks like**:
- Client in PTET-offering state (CA, NY, NJ, CT, IL, etc.)
- Pass-through entity with profitable operations
- AGI high enough that SALT cap materially limits deduction
- PTET election not made at entity level

**What's wrong**:
- Missing bypass of SALT cap
- Foregoing federal deduction value typically $5K-$50K

**Priceless approach**:
1. Verify PTET availability in relevant state
2. Current-year election if deadline available
3. Amendment opportunity if retroactive election available (rare; some states allow it)
4. Going forward: annual PTET election review

### Pattern I: The "crypto holder" who said no

**What it looks like**:
- Form 1040 crypto question answered "no"
- But bank records or client conversation reveal crypto holdings
- No Schedule D crypto activity
- No Form 8949 reporting

**What's wrong**:
- Answering "no" when client has crypto holdings is a false statement, even if no transactions occurred in year
- If transactions occurred, underreporting income

**Priceless approach**:
1. Partner-level conversation with client
2. Amendment consideration for unreported transactions
3. Voluntary disclosure if material
4. Going forward: accurate reporting, may involve specialty engagement

### Pattern J: The "never filed 8606" IRA holder

**What it looks like**:
- Client has IRA with non-deductible contributions over the years
- Form 8606 not filed in years where non-deductible contribution made
- Or filed inconsistently across years

**What's wrong**:
- Basis in IRA not tracked
- Future distributions will be fully taxed when partially tax-free
- Backdoor Roth strategy compromised without proper 8606

**Priceless approach**:
1. Reconstruct IRA basis from available records (contribution years, amounts, form 1099-R for distributions)
2. File Form 8606 retroactively where possible
3. Going forward: 8606 discipline

## Pattern templates for future additions

When a new pattern is observed, document:

1. **What it looks like** (how to spot it)
2. **What's wrong** (technical issue, missed value, or exposure)
3. **Priceless approach** (how we resolve it)
4. **Client communication considerations** (partner's guidance)
5. **Frequency** (how often we see it — helps prioritize detection)

Add to this file. Review quarterly to identify patterns worth incorporating into analyst training.

## Review cadence

- New patterns added monthly as engagements surface them
- Quarterly review by partner to ensure patterns are accurately described
- Annual review to retire patterns that no longer apply (e.g., post-TCJA changes that resolved common pre-TCJA errors)
- Training material for new analysts sourced from this file
