# Texas — State Tax Reference

## The essentials

Texas has no personal income tax and no state estate tax, making it a major destination for high-income individuals relocating from California, New York, and New Jersey. For Priceless clients based in Texas, state income tax is essentially not a planning variable at the personal level.

However, Texas has a franchise (margin) tax that applies to most businesses with revenue above a threshold. This is Texas's primary business tax and has undergone significant changes effective March 1, 2026 to align with current federal tax law. The franchise tax base is "margin" (revenue less certain deductions), making it different from both corporate income tax (profit-based) and sales-based gross receipts taxes.

Sales and use tax is a significant revenue source at 6.25% state + up to 2% local. Property taxes in Texas are among the highest in the country in percentage terms (compensating for lack of income tax).

For Priceless, Texas clients typically need: (1) franchise tax optimization, particularly margin calculation (COGS vs. compensation method), (2) sales/use tax compliance for businesses with tangible sales, (3) residency establishment for inbound clients, and (4) coordination for multi-state businesses where Texas is part of the portfolio.

## Personal income tax

**None.** Texas Constitution Art. VIII §24 prohibits a personal income tax (constitutional amendment adopted 1993; further strengthened by 2019 amendment requiring 2/3 legislative approval and voter ratification to impose).

There is no state personal income tax return.

### What this means for planning
- Wages, self-employment, interest, dividends, capital gains: all federal-only at state level
- QBI deduction: federal benefit only
- Capital gain recognition: federal only
- Roth conversions: no state tax impact
- Retirement plan contributions: federal benefit only

### Residency establishment (for inbound clients)

Texas has no formal residency declaration (no equivalent to FL's DR-501). Residency is established through factors:

**Primary indicators**:
- Physical presence (majority of time)
- Texas driver's license (must obtain within 90 days of establishing residence per Tex. Transp. Code §521.029)
- Texas voter registration
- Vehicle registration and titling in Texas
- Texas homestead designation for property tax exemption
- Primary bank accounts in Texas

**Supporting documentation**:
- Employment / business in Texas
- Professional and social ties in Texas
- Doctor, accountant, attorney in Texas

**No specific day count** — Texas doesn't have a 183-day rule (there's no state tax to avoid paying). Taxpayers move to TX to escape other states' tax burden; the Texas residency establishment serves as evidence for the departing state's audit.

### Homestead exemption (property tax)

**Homestead exemption on primary residence**:
- $100,000 exemption for school district M&O taxes (increased 2023 from $40K; per SB 2, 88th Legislature)
- $3,000 county exemption (if adopted)
- Additional exemptions for age 65+, disabled, veterans

**Homestead cap**: Appraised value growth capped at 10% annually on homestead properties (Texas Property Tax Code §23.23)

**Deadline**: April 30 of tax year to apply (Form 50-114)

**Impact for Priceless clients**: Homestead election reduces property tax significantly; particularly valuable in high-value property markets like Austin, Dallas suburbs. For clients moving to TX and buying expensive homes, homestead application on first year is critical.

## Business taxes

### Texas Franchise Tax (Margin Tax) — MAJOR 2026 UPDATE

**Authority**: Tex. Tax Code Chapter 171

**NOTABLE CHANGE**: Effective March 1, 2026, Texas updates franchise tax to align with current federal tax law. The Texas Comptroller has promulgated implementing rules in 2025-2026. **[VERIFY: Texas Comptroller rule 3.581 for specific 2026 changes; as of April 2026, significant updates affecting revenue calculation, COGS, and apportionment are in effect.]**

### Who pays

**Taxable entities** (Tex. Tax Code §171.0002):
- Corporations (including S corps at Texas level)
- LLCs
- Partnerships (general, limited, limited liability)
- Professional associations
- Business trusts

**NOT taxable**:
- Sole proprietorships
- General partnerships where all partners are natural persons ("passive entities" under specific conditions)
- Certain grantor trusts

**NO FEDERAL PASS-THROUGH PROTECTION**: An S corporation that is passed through for federal purposes IS taxable for Texas franchise tax. This is a major difference from most other state income tax regimes.

### Margin tax base

Texas franchise tax is on "margin" — computed as lesser of:
1. **70% of total revenue**
2. **Revenue minus COGS**
3. **Revenue minus compensation**
4. **Revenue minus $1,000,000** (post-2015 "EZ computation")

**Practical impact**: Taxable entity calculates four options and picks lowest. Most retail/wholesale businesses use COGS deduction; most service businesses use compensation deduction. Post-2026 changes affect specific items in each calculation.

### Rates (2026)

**General business rate**: 0.75% on taxable margin
**Retail/wholesale rate**: 0.375% on taxable margin
**EZ Computation rate**: 0.331% on taxable margin (if using $1M flat deduction)

### No-tax-due threshold

**$2.47M in annualized total revenue** (indexed biennially; current 2024-2025 value — **[VERIFY 2026 threshold]**):
- Entities with revenue at or below this threshold owe no franchise tax
- Must still file No Tax Due Information Report (Form 05-163)

### Compensation deduction (for margin method)

Compensation deduction (Tex. Tax Code §171.1013):
- Wages/salaries paid to officers, directors, owners, partners, employees
- Benefits cost (retirement, health insurance)
- $400,000 per person cap per employee [VERIFY 2026 cap]

This often favors service businesses with high compensation costs.

### COGS deduction (for margin method)

COGS deduction follows federal §471 principles with Texas-specific modifications (per Tex. Tax Code §171.1012):
- Direct costs of acquiring or producing goods
- Generally aligned with federal §471 post-March 1, 2026 rules (significant update)
- Capitalization rules differ from federal in some respects

**2026 Texas-federal alignment (effective March 1, 2026)**: Per recent Texas Comptroller rule updates, COGS definition more closely tracks federal post-OBBBA.

### Apportionment

**Single sales factor** since 2007 (Tex. Tax Code §171.103):
- Texas receipts ÷ total receipts
- Market-based sourcing for certain service receipts (post-2014 case law developments)
- Physical presence nexus for non-service businesses

### Economic Nexus for Franchise Tax

Post-*South Dakota v. Wayfair* (2018) and Texas Comptroller implementation:
- Economic nexus for franchise tax: $500,000 in Texas receipts (matches sales tax threshold)
- Out-of-state entities with $500K+ Texas receipts must register and file

### Filing

**Form**: 05-158 (Texas Franchise Tax Public Information Report) + Form 05-169 (Texas Franchise Tax Report)
**Due**: May 15 annually (unique date; not aligned with federal or fiscal year)
**Extensions**: Available via Form 05-164; generally 6 months to November 15

### Industry-specific rules

**Construction contractors**: Special COGS rules per Tex. Tax Code §171.1012(j)
**Oil and gas**: Special apportionment and deduction rules
**Professional services**: Often use compensation method; margin typically substantial
**Real estate**: Pass-through entities subject to franchise tax; margin calculation includes rental income

## Real estate considerations

### Property tax (Texas's primary revenue source)

Texas has NO state property tax; property taxes levied by local districts (counties, cities, schools, special districts).

**Average effective rate statewide**: ~1.8% of market value (among highest nationally)
**Austin, Dallas metro**: 2.0-2.4% effective
**Houston metro**: 2.1-2.4% effective

**Homestead cap**: 10% annual appraised value growth cap for homestead properties (TX Property Tax Code §23.23)

**Circuit-breaker protection**: Various exemptions for elderly (age 65+), disabled, disabled veterans

### Transfer taxes

**No state transfer tax on real estate.**

Texas is one of very few states without documentary stamp or realty transfer taxes. Major advantage for high-value real estate transactions.

**Recording fees**: County-level; typically $5-$30 per document recorded.

### §1031 Exchanges

Texas fully conforms to federal §1031 (no state tax = no state tax issue).

## Estate, gift, and inheritance taxes

**Texas estate tax**: None (repealed)
**Texas inheritance tax**: None
**Texas gift tax**: None

Federal estate tax applies at $15M exemption (post-OBBBA permanent). Texas residents face federal-only estate tax burden.

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 6.25%
**Local rates**: Up to 2% additional (cities, counties, MTAs, special purpose districts)
**Maximum combined rate**: 8.25% (statutory cap per Tex. Tax Code §321.101)

**Dallas/Austin/Houston combined**: 8.25% typical

**Economic nexus (Wayfair)**:
- $500,000 in total Texas revenue in preceding 12 months (Tex. Tax Code §151.107)
- Marketplace facilitator collection required

**Exemptions**:
- Most food for home consumption exempt
- Prescription medicines exempt
- Gas/electricity for residential use exempt

## Priceless client considerations

### S Corporation owners
- Texas treats S corps as taxable entities for franchise tax (distinct from federal pass-through)
- Compensation method often favorable for service businesses
- No personal income tax eliminates significant federal planning variable
- Annual franchise tax filing critical (Form 05-158)

### Real estate investors
- No personal income tax on rental income (federal only)
- Franchise tax if structured as entity — margin tax on rental income minus compensation/management costs
- High property tax is the main state-level cost
- No transfer tax on sales
- Homestead cap valuable for primary residence

### Professional practices
- No personal income tax on practice income (federal only)
- Professional associations and PLLCs subject to franchise tax on margin
- Compensation method favorable (high physician compensation deductible)
- Austin and Dallas are high-growth markets; many inbound from CA, NY

### Software / AI / tech
- Major inbound migration destination
- Austin ("Silicon Hills") tech concentration
- No state income tax for founders at exit (major advantage)
- Franchise tax on entity operations — margin method optimization important

### Construction
- Special COGS rules under §171.1012(j)
- Compensation method often unfavorable (many subcontractors)
- Sales tax on materials (typically passed through in fixed-fee contracts)

### Inbound from high-tax states
- No formal TX residency declaration; factors-based
- Get driver's license within 90 days
- Homestead exemption critical (April 30 deadline)
- Departing state residency audit preparation (especially NY, CA)

## Multi-state mechanics

### TX resident with out-of-state income
- No state tax to credit against (TX has no personal income tax)
- File non-resident returns in other states where income earned/business conducted
- Double taxation not an issue from TX side

### Non-resident with TX-source income
- No personal income tax = no individual state tax liability
- Franchise tax may apply if business activity
- Sales tax if tangible goods
- Property tax if owning TX real estate

### Multi-state pass-through ownership
- TX S corp owner: TX franchise tax on entity margin
- Out-of-state S corp with TX operations: franchise tax on TX apportioned margin
- Owner individual: no TX personal income tax

### Inbound relocation from CA/NY/NJ
- No formal TX residency declaration
- Establish physical presence, driver's license, voter registration
- Maintain documentation for departing state audit
- Dispose of or clearly convert prior-state property
