# North Carolina — State Tax Reference

## The essentials

North Carolina has continued its aggressive pro-growth tax reduction trajectory through 2026 and beyond. The state transitioned to a flat tax in 2014, has reduced the rate every year since 2022, and is scheduled to continue rate reductions through 2028 (potentially to 2.49%) subject to revenue triggers.

**2026 rate**: 3.99% flat (down from 4.25% in 2025; per statutory schedule under Session Law 2023-134)
**Corporate rate**: 2% in 2026 (down from 2.25% in 2025; scheduled to reach 0% by 2030)

North Carolina has a PTET (pass-through entity tax) program that uses the same flat rate as personal income. Charlotte and Research Triangle (Raleigh-Durham) are major growth metros attracting financial services, technology, and biotech — frequent destinations for Priceless clients relocating from NY, NJ, and CA.

For Priceless, NC clients typically need: (1) PTET election analysis (straightforward at flat rate), (2) verification of federal conformity posture (NC is fixed-date conformity; lags on some OBBBA provisions), and (3) residency establishment for inbound clients (Research Triangle Park, Charlotte).

### No retirement income specific exemption

Unlike Georgia or Illinois, NC does not have special retirement income exclusion. All retirement income taxed at 3.99% like other income.

**Exception**: Military retirement excluded from NC tax (per NC Code §105-153.5(b)(5a) and Bailey settlement for vested pre-8/12/1989 state/federal retirees).

### Residency rules

NC uses domicile + statutory residency per N.C. Gen. Stat. §105-153.4:

**Resident**: 
- Domiciled in NC, OR
- Present in NC for more than 183 days during tax year (presumption of residency)

**Domicile change**: Must show abandonment of prior domicile AND establishment of NC as new domicile. Factors include:
- Physical presence
- NC driver's license (required within 60 days of becoming resident)
- NC voter registration
- Vehicle registration
- Purchase/rental of NC residence

**Audit posture**: Moderate — less aggressive than NY or CA but NC DOR does audit residency changes when state tax liability reduction is substantial.

**Inbound to NC**: Charlotte and Raleigh-Durham receive significant inbound migration from NY, NJ, and other high-tax states. Standard residency establishment applies.

## Business taxes

### Corporate income tax — phasing to zero by 2030

**Current trajectory**:
- 2025: 2.25%
- 2026: 2.00%
- 2027: 1.75%
- 2028: 1.50%
- 2029: 1.00%
- 2030: 0% (complete phase-out)

Per 2021 budget (N.C. Gen. Stat. §105-130.3). Schedule is statutorily fixed (not trigger-based, unlike individual rate).

**Practical impact**: NC is becoming one of the most competitive corporate tax jurisdictions in the country. By 2030, corporations pay franchise tax only.

### Franchise tax (continues even as corporate income tax phases out)

**Effective 2025+** (per 2024 legislation):
- First $1,000,000 of tax base: $500 flat
- Tax base over $1,000,000: $1.50 per $1,000

**Tax base**: Greatest of three calculations (similar to TX margin):
- Net worth
- Investment in tangible property in NC
- Apportioned capital (for multi-state)

### PTE tax (pass-through entity tax)

**Authority**: N.C. Gen. Stat. §105-154.1, enacted 2021; eligibility expanded by Session Law 2023-134

**Rate**: 3.99% for 2026 (matches personal flat rate; automatically tracks rate reductions)

### Eligibility (post-SL 2023-134 expansion)

**Eligible**:
- Partnerships with partners that are individuals, estates, trusts, S corps, other partnerships, or certain non-profits
- S corporations
- LLCs taxed as partnerships or S corps
- Trusts with specified beneficiary types
- **Certain entities treated as corporations for federal purposes** (expanded per SL 2023-134)

**NOT eligible**:
- Sole proprietorships
- Single-member LLCs disregarded for federal

### Election mechanics

**Method**: Election made annually on timely-filed return
**Due date**: Original or extended due date of return (Form D-403 partnerships; Form CD-401S S corporations)
**Irrevocable**: Once made for a year, cannot be revoked after due date

### Estimated payments

Required if expected NC tax > $500 (C corp threshold):
- Quarterly estimated payments
- Form CD-429 or similar

### Credit to owners

NC uses a deduction mechanism similar to Georgia:
- PTE income subtracted from owner's NC taxable income
- Owner doesn't pay NC personal tax on PTE-taxed income
- No duplicate taxation

### Post-OBBBA SALT cap interaction

With flat 3.99% NC rate and OBBBA $40,400 cap:

**NC residents below $505K MAGI**: 
- Lower flat rate + moderate property tax (0.75% effective average) means $40,400 cap absorbs most state+local for middle-income
- PTET election still beneficial if combined state+local tax exceeds cap

**NC residents > $505K MAGI**:
- SALT phase-out brings cap to $10K at $565K+ MAGI
- PTET election meaningfully valuable
- At $1M AGI: $39,900 NC tax (3.99%); PTET saves $30K × 37% = ~$11K federal

### Major non-conformity: 85% bonus depreciation add-back

This is NC's most significant business tax divergence:
- Federal 100% bonus depreciation
- NC requires 85% of bonus depreciation added back
- Deducted over 5 years (20% per year for 5 years)

Creates federal-state timing mismatch. Example: $100K equipment with 100% federal bonus:
- Federal year 1: $100K deduction
- NC year 1: $15K deduction + $0 from add-back (add back $85K)
- NC years 2-5: $17K additional deduction per year (20% of $85K add-back)

## Real estate considerations

### Real Estate Transfer Tax
**State rate**: $1 per $500 of consideration (0.2%) — relatively low

### Property tax
**Average effective rate**: ~0.75% of market value (below national average)
**Homestead protections**: Circuit-breaker exemptions for elderly and disabled (limited)
**Low property tax + moderate income tax = attractive overall tax profile**

### §1031 Exchanges
NC conforms to federal §1031. Standard mechanics apply.

## Estate, gift, and inheritance taxes

**NC estate tax**: Repealed effective January 1, 2013 (S.L. 2013-10)
**NC inheritance tax**: None (repealed 1989)
**NC gift tax**: None

Federal estate tax applies at $15M exemption. NC clients face federal-only estate tax.

## Sales and use tax

**State rate**: 4.75%
**Local rates**: Counties add 2-2.75% typically
**Combined rate typical**: 6.75-7.5%
**Charlotte, Raleigh**: 7.25%

**Economic nexus (Wayfair)**:
- $100,000 in NC sales in preceding or current calendar year
- Marketplace facilitator collection required

## Multi-state mechanics

### NC resident with out-of-state income
- NC taxes worldwide income at 3.99%
- Tax credit for tax paid to other states (Schedule CR)

### Non-resident with NC-source income
- Form D-400NR non-resident return
- NC-source: services performed in NC, real estate in NC, NC business allocation

### Inbound from high-tax states
- Standard residency establishment
- Expect departing state's audit posture (especially from NY, NJ)
