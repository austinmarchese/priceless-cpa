# Long-Form Educational Video Format

Target platform: YouTube. Length: 10-20 minutes.

## Purpose

Long-form videos are the primary trust-building tool for Priceless CPA. They demonstrate depth of knowledge, build authority, and give potential clients enough time to evaluate whether Anthony is the right fit. A 15-minute video where Anthony breaks down a real tax strategy with specific numbers does more selling than any ad.

## Structure

### Intro (Under 60 Seconds)

The intro has one job: convince the viewer to keep watching.

- **Hook (0-5s).** Open with a specific, benefit-driven statement. Not "today we're going to talk about entity structuring." Instead: "The entity structure that saved you $30K at $300K in revenue is costing you $80K at a million. Here's how to fix it."
- **Stakes (5-15s).** Why this matters right now. What it costs them to ignore this. Use a specific dollar amount or scenario.
- **Roadmap (15-30s).** Brief preview of what the video covers. "I'm going to show you the three signals your entity structure is wrong, what to do about it, and the exact conversation to have with your CPA."
- **Credibility (optional, 30-45s).** Only if needed. "I see this in about 80% of the returns that come across my desk over $1M." Position through experience, not credentials.

No more than 60 seconds total. If the intro runs longer, cut it.

### Body (Ascending Value)

The body should build in value. Start with the most accessible point and work toward the most advanced. Each section should make the viewer think "I need to keep watching."

- **3-5 main sections.** Each one should be a self-contained insight that could stand alone as a short-form clip.
- **Ascending value.** The best insight comes last, not first. This keeps retention high through the back half of the video.
- **Concrete examples in every section.** "A client came to me with X. We did Y. The result was Z." Real scenarios with real numbers (anonymized).
- **Transitions between sections.** "Now that covers the entity side. But there's a second piece most people miss entirely." Each transition should create curiosity about what comes next.
- **Visual callouts.** Flag moments where a text overlay, diagram, or number on screen would help. "Show: $180K vs $95K" or "Diagram: before/after entity structure."

### Close (2-3 Minutes)

- **Recap the key takeaway.** One sentence that captures the entire video. "If your entity structure hasn't changed since you started, you're almost certainly overpaying."
- **Action step.** Tell them exactly what to do next. "Go back to your CPA and ask them this one question: has my entity structure been reviewed in the last 12 months?"
- **Soft CTA.** "If you want help figuring this out, link's in the description." No hard sell. The video itself is the pitch.
- **Tease the next video (optional).** If there is a natural follow-up topic, mention it. Builds series momentum.

## What Makes a Good Priceless CPA Long-Form Video

1. **One clear topic, deeply covered.** Not "10 tax tips." Instead, "How to restructure your entities when you cross $1M." Depth over breadth.

2. **Specific numbers throughout.** "$47K in savings," "$200K in accelerated deductions," "from $180K to $95K on the same income." Every claim has a dollar amount attached.

3. **Real scenarios (anonymized).** "A client came to me" stories are the backbone. They prove Anthony has done this, not just read about it.

4. **The "my CPA never told me this" reaction.** The best Priceless CPA videos make the viewer realize their current CPA is leaving money on the table. This is the emotional trigger that drives consultations.

5. **Peer-to-peer tone.** Anthony is not lecturing. He is explaining something to a smart person who just happens not to know this specific area. The viewer should feel respected, not talked down to.

6. **A natural short-form clip lives inside the video.** At least one 60-second segment should work as a standalone short. Plan for this in the outline.

## How It Differs from Generic Tax YouTube

| Generic Tax YouTube | Priceless CPA |
|---|---|
| Broad topics ("S-Corp explained") | Specific scenarios ("S-Corp salary when you cross $2M") |
| Textbook explanations | Client stories with real dollar amounts |
| Talks to beginners | Talks to experienced entrepreneurs with complexity |
| Lists of tips | Deep dive on one strategy |
| Credential-based authority ("as a CPA...") | Experience-based authority ("here's what I see all the time") |
| Generic advice anyone could give | Specific advice that requires seeing hundreds of situations |
| Hard CTA at the end | Soft redirect, let the content do the selling |

## Pre-Production Checklist

Before scripting, confirm:

- [ ] Topic maps to one of the five content pillars
- [ ] Title follows a proven outlier pattern (see [[raw/outliers/README.md]])
- [ ] There is a clear "my CPA never told me this" moment
- [ ] At least one anonymized client scenario with specific dollar amounts
- [ ] One segment that works as a standalone short-form clip
- [ ] Topic is not Google-able at this depth (goes beyond surface-level)
