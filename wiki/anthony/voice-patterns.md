# Anthony's Voice Patterns

Voice extracted from [[wiki/shorts/example-scripts.md]]

## Signature Phrases

These phrases appear repeatedly across Anthony's scripts. They are core to his voice.

- **"Here's what I see all the time:"** - His go-to transition into a common mistake or pattern. Positions him as someone who has seen hundreds of situations, not just one.
- **"That's not a strategy. That's compliance."** - His sharpest differentiator line. Draws a hard line between what most CPAs do and what Priceless CPA does.
- **"And that difference costs you thousands every year."** - The cost-of-inaction closer. Makes the stakes concrete.
- **"If your CPA hasn't [done X], ask them why."** - Challenges the listener to evaluate their current CPA without directly selling. Soft CTA disguised as advice.
- **"If your CPA only calls you during tax season, you don't have a tax strategist. You have a tax preparer."** - The core positioning statement. Preparer vs. strategist.
- **"This isn't some premium service reserved for billionaires. This is what competent tax strategy looks like."** - Democratizes proactive tax planning. Makes the listener feel like they deserve better.
- **"And if you're not getting it, you should probably ask why."** - Quiet closer. No hard sell. Plants the seed.

## Sentence Structure

- **Short and punchy.** Most sentences are under 15 words. He rarely uses compound sentences.
- **One idea per line.** Each sentence gets its own beat. Scripts read almost like poetry, one line per thought.
- **Parallel structure for contrast.** "A tax preparer looks at last year. A tax strategist looks at next year." He loves setting up A vs. B comparisons with identical sentence structure.
- **Fragments are fine.** "Makes sense at the time." "Same property. Same income. Completely different tax bill." He uses fragments for emphasis.
- **No filler.** No "basically," no "essentially," no "in other words." Every word earns its place.

## How He Introduces Topics

- **Direct address to a specific audience.** "S-corp owners," "Real estate investors," "If you're making $500K+ and own real estate." He qualifies the listener immediately so the right people lean in.
- **A bold, slightly provocative statement.** "Here's how to know if your CPA is actually doing their job." No warmup. No "today I want to talk about." He opens with the point.
- **A concrete scenario.** "The entity structure that worked when you made $300K might be bleeding you dry at a million." Starts with a situation the listener recognizes.

## How He Transitions Between Points

- **"Here's how it works:"** - Clean pivot from "why it matters" to "how it works."
- **"Here's why it matters:"** - Used when moving from a concept to the stakes.
- **Numbered lists with "One," "Two," "Three."** Spelled out, not numerals. Keeps the spoken rhythm natural.
- **"But" as a pivot.** "Makes sense at the time. But then they scale." He uses "but" to create a turn, showing where things go wrong.
- **Repeating the subject.** "A tax preparer tells you what you owe in April. A tax strategist tells you what you're going to owe in November." The repetition creates rhythm and makes the contrast stick.

## How He Closes

- **The challenge close.** "If your CPA can't answer these clearly, you don't have a tax strategist. You have a tax preparer." Puts the ball in the listener's court.
- **The quiet redirect.** "If your CPA hasn't mentioned cost seg, you need to ask why." No pitch. Just a question that will lead them to Priceless CPA if the answer is bad.
- **The stakes close.** "And that difference costs you thousands every year." Ends with what inaction costs.
- **The aspiration close.** "You stop waking up in March wondering how bad it's going to be. Because you already know. And there's a plan." Paints the picture of what life could look like.
- **Never a hard sell.** He does not say "book a call" or "visit my website" in the script body. The CTA lives in the caption or production notes, never in the words he speaks.

## Number Specificity

- Always use exact figures. "$47K" not "thousands." "$200K in deductions" not "a lot of deductions."
- Use dollar amounts with K/M shorthand in hooks. "$47K" is punchier than "$47,000."
- In body copy, spell it out when it sounds more natural. "$200K" in a hook, "two hundred thousand" in a slower section.
- Income thresholds are specific. "$300K," "$500K," "$1M," "$2M." Never "a lot of money" or "high income."
- Percentages when relevant. "25% vs. 35%" not "a higher rate."

## Words and Phrases to AVOID

- **Em dashes.** Never. Use commas, periods, or restructure.
- **"Basically" / "essentially" / "in other words."** Cut them. Say the thing directly.
- **"I think" / "I believe."** He states. He does not qualify with weak language.
- **"Quality service" / "we care about our clients."** Generic CPA language that says nothing.
- **"Tax issues" / "tax problems."** Too vague. Say "tax surprises," "overpaying," "wrong entity structure."
- **"Leverage" / "utilize" / "optimize" (as buzzwords).** Use plain language. "Use," "save," "restructure."
- **"Let me explain" / "So what I want to talk about today is."** No preamble. Start with the point.
- **Jargon without immediate context.** If he says "QBI deduction," the next sentence explains what it means in dollar terms.

## Tone Summary

Anthony speaks as a peer to high-net-worth entrepreneurs. He is not teaching down. He is not selling up. He is a smart, slightly frustrated expert who keeps seeing the same mistakes and wants to fix them. The tone is:

- **Confident, not cocky.** He knows his stuff but does not showboat.
- **Direct, not aggressive.** He calls out bad practices without attacking anyone personally.
- **Educational, not academic.** He explains complex tax concepts in plain language with real dollar amounts.
- **Peer-to-peer, not service-provider-to-client.** He talks to entrepreneurs the way entrepreneurs talk to each other.
- **Slightly frustrated with the CPA industry.** Not angry. Just tired of seeing the same mistakes over and over.
