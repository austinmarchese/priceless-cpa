# AI Writing Patterns to Avoid

Reference for identifying and eliminating AI-sounding patterns from tax content. Adapted from internal OS patterns. See `wiki/youtube-scripts/script-killers.md` for script-specific bans.

---

## The Core Problem

LLMs produce statistically average text. The result is simultaneously less specific and more exaggerated. Like shouting that a portrait shows a uniquely important person while the portrait itself fades into a generic sketch.

**The fix:** Be specific. Use exact numbers, real client scenarios, and precise tax code references. Anthony is the expert. Write like he talks.

---

## Vocabulary Red Flags

### High-frequency AI words (ban list)

Never use these in Priceless CPA content:

**Verbs:** delve, underscore, highlight, emphasize, showcase, garner, foster, leverage, utilize, streamline, navigate, unpack, unlock

**Adjectives:** robust, comprehensive, pivotal, crucial, vital, dynamic, vibrant, meticulous, intricate, groundbreaking, revolutionary, significant, profound

**Nouns:** landscape (figurative), tapestry (figurative), testament, interplay, intricacies, synergy, paradigm

**Connectors:** Additionally (to start sentences), Furthermore, Moreover, Consequently, As a result

**Puffery:** nestled, in the heart of, boasts (meaning "has"), a testament to, enduring legacy, rich heritage

### Better alternatives

| AI Pattern | What to write instead |
|------------|----------------------|
| "This underscores the importance of..." | Just state it |
| "It's crucial to note that..." | Cut it, say the thing |
| "This highlights..." | "Here's the thing:" |
| "Additionally," | "And" or start a new thought |
| "The landscape of X" | "X" |
| "Leverage your entity structure" | "Use your entity structure" |
| "Navigate complex tax situations" | "Handle your taxes" or be specific about what's complex |
| "Streamline your tax strategy" | Say what actually changes |
| "A robust approach to..." | Just describe the approach |

## Structural Tells

### Things AI over-does

1. **Significance statements.** Constantly explaining why something matters.
   - "This represents a significant shift in..."
   - "Contributing to the broader..."
   - "Setting the stage for..."

2. **Triple-adjective lists.** Excessive "X, Y, and Z" constructions.
   - "comprehensive, innovative, and transformative"
   - Often used to pad thin analysis

3. **"In conclusion" / "In summary."** Never summarize what you just said. Trust the reader.

4. **"It's worth noting that..."** If it's worth noting, just note it. Don't announce it.

5. **Vague attributions.** "Experts argue...", "Industry reports suggest..."
   - Anthony IS the expert. Say what he knows directly.

6. **Legacy/future sections.** "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook"
   - Formula: "Despite its [positive words], [subject] faces challenges..."

7. **Superficial -ing phrases.**
   - "...highlighting its importance"
   - "...reflecting broader trends"
   - "...contributing to the field"

### Things AI under-does

1. **Simple "is" and "are."** AI avoids these and substitutes "serves as", "stands as", "represents."
   - Just say "is."

2. **Specific details.** AI stays generic.
   - AI: "a significant tax savings"
   - Human: "saved $47K on their 2024 return"

3. **Personal voice.** AI sounds detached.
   - AI: "One might consider restructuring..."
   - Anthony: "Look, here's what I'd actually do..."

## Tax Content Specific Patterns

### Anthony IS the professional

Never write these phrases. They make no sense when the speaker is a CPA:

- "Consult with a tax professional"
- "Speak to your accountant about this"
- "Every situation is different, so seek professional advice"
- "This is not tax advice" (unless legally required for specific content)
- "Individual results may vary"
- "It depends on your specific circumstances" (be specific about WHAT it depends on)

### Instead, write with authority

- "Here's what I'd do in your situation..."
- "The way I handle this with clients..."
- "Nine times out of ten, the answer is..."
- "The IRS says X, but here's what that actually means for you..."

### Avoid generic tax platitudes

| Generic (ban) | Specific (use) |
|---------------|----------------|
| "Tax planning is important" | "Missing this election cost my client $30K" |
| "There are many strategies available" | Name the specific strategy |
| "Proper entity structuring can save taxes" | "Converting from Schedule C to S-Corp saved this client $22K in SE tax" |
| "Consult a professional" | "Here's exactly what to do" |
| "It depends" | Say what it depends ON, with numbers |

## Tone Patterns to Avoid

### Press release / travel guide tone
- "Priceless CPA offers a comprehensive suite of..."
- "Our team of dedicated professionals..."
- "We pride ourselves on delivering exceptional..."

### "Not just X, but Y" constructions
- "It's not just about saving taxes; it's about building wealth"
- These often correct a misconception nobody had. Just make the point.

### Hedging and disclaimers
- "While there are many factors to consider..."
- "It's important to remember that..."
- Cut the hedge. State the thing.

## Grammar Patterns

### Avoiding "is/are"
AI substitutes fancy constructions:
- "This strategy serves as a powerful tool" vs. "This strategy is powerful"
- Just use "is."

### Elegant variation
AI uses different synonyms each time to avoid repetition:
- First mention: "the entrepreneur"
- Second: "the business owner"
- Third: "the visionary founder"
- Fourth: "the seasoned professional"

**Fix:** Use their name, or "they." Consistency beats variety.

### Em-dash ban
Do not use em dashes anywhere. Use commas, periods, or restructure the sentence.

## Self-Check Before Finalizing

Scan every piece of content for:

- [ ] **Word check:** delve, underscore, highlight, crucial, pivotal, landscape, tapestry, testament, leverage, utilize, streamline, robust, navigate
- [ ] **Phrase check:** "serves as", "stands as", "not just X but Y", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion"
- [ ] **Structure check:** Triple-adjective lists, legacy sections, significance statements
- [ ] **Tone check:** Travel-guide puffery, vague attributions, hedging
- [ ] **Tax check:** "Consult a professional" or similar (Anthony IS the professional)
- [ ] **Specificity check:** Can any sentence apply to any topic? Replace with exact details.
- [ ] **Em-dash check:** Replace all em dashes with commas, periods, or restructured sentences
- [ ] **Authority check:** Does Anthony sound like the expert, or like someone deferring to one?
