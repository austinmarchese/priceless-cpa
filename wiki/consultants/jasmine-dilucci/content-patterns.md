# Jasmine DiLucci / Tax Leverage - Content Patterns

Recurring patterns in her content that make 14+ minute videos on seemingly simple topics feel essential.

## Pattern 1: Myth vs. Reality Framing

Nearly every video opens by naming a common belief, then systematically dismantling it with evidence. The myth is the hook. The reality is the payoff.

**How she uses it:**
- States the myth clearly and without judgment ("Most people believe...")
- Acknowledges why the myth exists (it's usually a half-truth)
- Introduces the actual legal standard
- Builds the evidence until the myth collapses under its own weight

**Why it works for long-form:** The myth creates tension. The viewer stays because they need to know if what they believed was wrong.

## Pattern 2: Legal Test Walkthrough

Multi-prong legal tests, broken down element by element. Each prong becomes its own mini-segment with its own evidence and examples.

**How she uses it:**
- Names the test and its source (IRC section, regulation)
- Numbers the prongs explicitly ("There are three requirements. Let's start with number one.")
- Spends 2-4 minutes on each prong
- Uses different examples for each prong to keep it fresh

**Why it works for long-form:** A three-prong test at 3 minutes per prong is 9 minutes of content that feels structured, not padded. The viewer knows where they are in the framework.

## Pattern 3: Court Case Stacking

Instead of citing one case and moving on, she stacks multiple cases showing the same pattern. This transforms a single data point into an undeniable trend.

**How she uses it:**
- Introduces the first case with context (who, what, outcome)
- Adds the second case to show it's not a one-off
- Stacks a third (or more) to establish the pattern
- Sometimes uses the volume itself as the argument: "80 cases say X, zero cases say Y"

**Why it works for long-form:** Each case adds 1-2 minutes of content that deepens the argument. The viewer doesn't feel like you're padding. They feel like you're building an airtight case.

## Pattern 4: Practical Application Bridge

After building the legal case, she always bridges to the viewer's specific situation. "So for YOUR situation, this means..."

**How she uses it:**
- Transitions explicitly from law to practice
- Uses "you" and "your" heavily in this section
- Gives specific scenarios the viewer might be in
- Tells them exactly what to document, file, or change

**Why it works for long-form:** This is where the viewer gets their personal payoff. Everything before this built the credibility. This section delivers the value.

## Pattern 5: Real-World Sourcing

Topics come from actual client situations and IRS notices, not from trending topics or competitor videos. This means her content has specificity that can't be replicated by someone who only does research.

**How she uses it:**
- References real situations without identifying clients
- Mentions actual IRS audit outcomes and notice types
- Discusses strategies she's personally implemented
- Shares what worked and what didn't in practice

**Why it works for long-form:** Specificity from practice creates content that feels different from research-based videos. The viewer senses the difference even if they can't name it.

## How She Makes 14+ Minute Videos on "Simple" Topics

The secret is depth, not breadth. A topic like "Can you deduct your home office?" seems like a 3-minute answer. Here's how she turns it into 14+ minutes:

1. **Name the common belief** (1 minute)
2. **Introduce the legal test** (1 minute)
3. **Walk through each element of the test** (2-4 minutes per element, with 2-3 elements = 4-12 minutes)
4. **Stack court cases for each element** (1-2 minutes per case, 2-3 cases = 2-6 minutes)
5. **Bridge to practical application** (2-3 minutes)
6. **Close with implementation steps** (1-2 minutes)

A three-element test with two court cases each gives you 15+ minutes of substantive content. Nothing feels padded because each layer adds real evidence.

The key insight: she doesn't make videos longer by adding more topics. She makes them longer by going deeper on fewer topics. One question, fully answered, with all the evidence.

## Application to Priceless CPA

These patterns work directly for Anthony's audience because:

- **Serial entrepreneurs with complex structures** need the legal test walkthrough. They're making decisions worth hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- **Court case stacking** builds the kind of trust that converts viewers into clients. If Anthony can cite three cases, the viewer trusts his advice over their current CPA who cited zero.
- **Real-world sourcing** is Anthony's natural advantage. He works with multi-entity entrepreneurs daily. That specificity can't be faked.
- **Myth vs. Reality** addresses their core frustration: their current CPA gave them surface-level advice. Anthony can show them what the law actually says.
