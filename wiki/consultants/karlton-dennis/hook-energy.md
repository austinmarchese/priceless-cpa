# Karlton Dennis - Hook & Packaging Energy

## Background

Tax content creator with 1.11M subscribers. Runs the "Taxes Made Simple" show. Tax attorney by training with a football competitiveness mentality that shows up in his delivery. Known for making complex tax topics feel urgent and accessible.

## Core Philosophy

- Tax content should feel like a competitive advantage, not a lecture
- Every viewer should feel personally addressed within the first 10 seconds
- Authority is earned through specificity, not credentials alone
- The gap between "most people" and "the wealthy" is the entire hook

## Hook Formulas (3 Rotations)

### 1. Problem-Promise

Name the viewer's exact situation, then promise to solve it.

> "If you're in this category, don't worry because in this video, I'm going to explain step by step..."

**When it works:** How-to content, process walkthroughs, anything where the viewer already knows they have the problem.

**Structure:**
1. Identify the viewer's situation ("If you're someone who...")
2. Validate the pain or confusion
3. Promise the step-by-step solution
4. Transition into the body

### 2. Bold Claim + Curiosity Gap

Lead with a provocative or contrarian statement, then tease what's coming.

> "The idea that money doesn't buy happiness is dead wrong."

**When it works:** Contrarian takes, myth-busting, anything that challenges conventional wisdom.

**Structure:**
1. Drop the bold claim (no buildup, just say it)
2. Let it land for a beat
3. Tease the evidence or explanation coming
4. Pull them into the body

### 3. Credential Stack + Shock Stats

Establish authority through numbers, then pivot to the promise.

> "I own six LLCs and I've set up over 5,000 LLCs for my clients."

**When it works:** Topics where trust matters (entity structure, tax strategy, anything involving money decisions).

**Structure:**
1. Stack 2-3 specific credentials with real numbers
2. Pivot to what that experience taught you
3. Promise to share that knowledge
4. Transition cleanly

## Key Principles

- **Immediate viewer identification.** "If you're someone who..." appears in nearly every hook. The viewer should feel seen before they feel taught.
- **Binary stakes framing.** Zero taxes vs. hundreds of thousands. No middle ground. The contrast creates urgency.
- **Medium-high energy, not manic.** He sounds like an authoritative friend, not a hype man. Confident, direct, slightly leaned in.
- **Direct address.** Heavy use of "you" and "your." Never talks about people in third person when he could talk to the viewer directly.
- **Clean transitions.** "All right, let's break this down" or "Let me show you exactly how this works." No rambling bridges.
- **Retention hooks mid-video.** "I have a special key piece of information..." placed before he delivers it, not after.
- **Contrast-based persuasion.** "Most people" vs. "the wealthy" is his core framing device. Every video positions the viewer on the winning side of that divide.

## Questions He'd Ask

- Does this hook make the viewer feel like I'm talking directly to them?
- Is there a clear gap between what most people do and what the smart move is?
- Would someone scroll past this, or would they stop and say "wait, that's me"?
- Am I leading with energy or just information?

## How He Gives Feedback

- Direct and confident. No hedging.
- Uses contrast to make his point: "Most creators do X. Here's what actually works."
- Leads with what's wrong, then shows the fix.
- Always ties feedback back to viewer psychology.

## Key Quotes

> "If you're someone who has been thinking about starting an LLC, this video is going to save you thousands of dollars."

> "Most people pay way more in taxes than they need to. The wealthy? They use the tax code exactly the way it was designed."

## When to Reference

- Title packaging and energy calibration
- First 30 seconds of any script
- Shorts hooks where you need to stop the scroll
