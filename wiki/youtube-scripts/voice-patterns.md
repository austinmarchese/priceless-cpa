# Anthony's Voice Patterns for Script Writing

Reference for writing YouTube scripts that sound authentically like Anthony from Priceless CPA.

See [[wiki/anthony/voice-patterns.md]] for Anthony's full brand voice across all content.

---

## Signature Phrases (USE THESE)

### Authority / Positioning
- "Here's what I see all the time"
- "If your CPA hasn't brought this up, ask them why"
- "That's not a strategy. That's compliance."
- "And that difference costs you thousands every year."
- "Here's what I'd tell you if you were sitting across from me right now."

### Explaining Tax Concepts
- "What this means in plain English is..."
- "So for you, this looks like..."
- "Let me break that down."
- "Here's exactly what this looks like in practice."
- "And here's the part nobody talks about."

### Building Stakes
- "That's $47K. Gone. Every year."
- "And your CPA probably never mentioned it."
- "This isn't aggressive. This isn't a gray area. This is the law."
- "The IRS is very clear on this."

### Credibility (Use Sparingly)
- "I've restructured over [X] entities this year alone."
- "Every single one of them was leaving money on the table."
- "I work with entrepreneurs making $500K to $3M, and the patterns are always the same."

## Transition Phrases

- "And so..." (natural flow)
- "Now here's the thing..." (important point coming)
- "Here's where it gets interesting..." (building anticipation)
- "But that's only half the picture." (before next section)
- "Most people..." > contrarian follow-up
- "Now I know what you're thinking..." (before objection handling)
- "But here's what most CPAs won't tell you..." (positioning)
- "Let me give you a real example." (before client story)

---

## Re-Hook Formulas (Every 60-90 Seconds)

Drop one of these before each new major point to prevent drop-off:

1. "That point was important, but if you don't couple it with this one, you're missing the full picture."
2. "Now before you start trying to [action], you need to consider this."
3. "But here's where it gets interesting..."
4. "This next part is probably the most important out of all of them."
5. "Now I know what you're thinking: [objection]. Let me address that."
6. "But wait, there's a catch that nobody talks about..."
7. "And this is the one that saves the most money, so pay attention."

## Number Specificity Rules

ALWAYS use exact numbers. Vague claims destroy authority.

| DO | DON'T |
|----|-------|
| "$47K" | "thousands" |
| "3 entities" | "multiple entities" |
| "$1.2M across two businesses" | "a high-income entrepreneur" |
| "Section 199A" | "the tax code" |
| "$200K in accelerated deductions" | "significant deductions" |
| "saved $147K in year one" | "saved a lot of money" |
| "IRC Section 1031" | "that exchange thing" |
| "37% marginal rate" | "a high tax bracket" |

When citing tax savings, always pair the dollar amount with the timeframe: "$47K in year one" not just "$47K."

## Framework Naming Patterns

Give concepts explicit names so viewers remember and reference them:

- "The quarterback model" (one CPA coordinating everything)
- "Tax preparer vs. tax strategist" (the industry gap)
- "Compliance vs. strategy" (reactive vs. proactive)
- "The proactive planning window" (timing for tax moves)
- "Entity stacking" (multi-entity structuring)

When introducing a framework: "I call this [Name]. Here's how it works..."

---

## Narrative Flow Rules

- Use "but" and "therefore" for momentum.
- Avoid "and then" (creates boring sequences).
- Vary sentence length for rhythm.
- Short sentences for emphasis. Longer ones for explanation.
- Let transitions flow naturally, not forced.
- Read every line aloud. If it sounds like writing, rewrite it.

---

## Tax Authority Voice

When citing legal basis, follow this pattern:

1. **Cite the source**: "Section 199A of the Internal Revenue Code..."
2. **Translate immediately**: "What this means in plain English is..."
3. **Apply to their situation**: "So for you, making $800K with two S-Corps, this looks like..."

Stack evidence. Don't rely on a single citation. Two or three references build a pattern that feels airtight.

If a tax topic has a multi-part test, walk through each element: "The first requirement is... The second part says... And here's the third one, and this is where most people get tripped up."
