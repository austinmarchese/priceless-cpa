# Script Killers

What to NEVER write in a Priceless CPA YouTube script. If any of these appear in a draft, fix them before presenting.

---

## Banned Phrases

Each phrase is banned for a specific reason. Knowing the reason helps you avoid similar mistakes.

| Phrase | Why It's Banned | Use Instead |
|--------|----------------|-------------|
| "In this video, we'll explore..." | Too formal. Sounds like a textbook, not a person. | Jump straight into the content. |
| "Without further ado..." | Cliche. Signals you've been wasting their time. | Cut the windup entirely. |
| "As you may know..." | Assumes knowledge. Either they know or they don't. | State the fact directly. |
| "It goes without saying..." | Then don't say it. Filler. | Just make the point. |
| "At the end of the day..." | Overused. Every YouTube creator says this. | "Here's what it comes down to:" |
| "Basically..." | Undermines expertise. Implies you're oversimplifying. | Explain it properly. |
| "I think" / "I believe" | Weakens authority. You're the expert, not guessing. | State it as fact. "This is..." / "The answer is..." |
| "Kind of" / "Sort of" | Vague. Destroys precision. | Be specific. Say what it IS. |
| "Amazing" / "Awesome" | Overused, generic. Says nothing. | Use specific outcomes: "$47K saved" not "amazing results." |
| "A lot of money" | Vague. Could mean $500 or $500K. | Use the exact number. Always. |
| "Significant savings" | Same problem. Means nothing without a number. | "$200K in accelerated deductions" |
| "Multiple entities" | Lazy. How many? | "3 entities" or "5 LLCs and an S-Corp" |
| "High-income earner" | Vague. Doesn't connect with anyone. | "$800K across two businesses" |
| "The tax code says..." | Which part? There are 75,000 pages. | "Section 199A of the Internal Revenue Code..." |

## Structural Killers

These are structural patterns that kill retention or weaken the script.

### Opening Killers
- **Starting with "Hey guys" or "What's up everyone"**: Generic. Doesn't filter for the right audience. Start with context that confirms the click.
- **Long windups before delivering value**: If the intro is over 60 seconds, cut it. Front-load the contrarian take.

### Mid-Script Killers
- **Apologizing for content length**: "I know this is a long one, but..." Never. If it's valuable, length is a feature.
- **Generic transitions**: "Moving on" / "Next up" / "Let's talk about." Use re-hook formulas instead.
- **Summarizing what you just said**: Trust the viewer. Move forward. Recapping mid-video signals you don't have enough content.
- **Rhetorical questions without immediate answers**: "Have you ever wondered...?" Either answer it immediately or cut the question.

### Closing Killers
- **Multiple endings**: Personal reflection + recap + CTA + tease = four endings. Pick ONE close structure.
- **Begging for likes/subscribes**: "Smash that like button" undermines authority. If the content is good, they'll subscribe.
- **Adding new information in the close**: The close is for action, not new content. If it's important, put it in the body.

## Content Killers

These weaken the substance and make the video feel generic.

- **Vague claims without specific numbers**: Every claim needs proof. "$47K saved" not "a lot of money saved."
- **Hypothetical examples when real client stories exist**: "Imagine a business owner who..." is always weaker than "I had a client making $1.2M across two businesses..."
- **Advice that can't be acted on**: "You should optimize your entity structure" is useless without steps. Always include the how.
- **"This works for everyone"**: It doesn't. Specify who it works for and who it doesn't. "If you're making under $100K, this strategy doesn't apply yet."
- **Theory without practical application**: Citing IRC Section 199A means nothing without showing what it looks like in practice for a $1M business owner.
- **Listing strategies without ranking them**: "Here are 5 strategies" is weaker than "Here are 5 strategies, and I'm saving the one that saves the most money for last."

## Em Dash Ban

Never use em dashes in any Priceless CPA script. They don't translate to spoken delivery and create awkward reading patterns.

| Bad | Good |
|-----|------|
| "Your CPA is filing returns -- not building strategy" | "Your CPA is filing returns. Not building strategy." |
| "Three entities -- two LLCs and an S-Corp -- all running through one return" | "Three entities. Two LLCs and an S-Corp, all running through one return." |
| "The answer is simple -- restructure" | "The answer is simple. Restructure." |

Use periods for emphasis. Use commas for natural pauses. Restructure if neither works.

## Quick Self-Check

Before submitting any draft, scan for these patterns:

1. Search for every banned phrase in the list above.
2. Check that every number is specific (no "a lot," "significant," "multiple").
3. Verify no em dashes appear anywhere.
4. Confirm the intro is under 60 seconds.
5. Check that every "imagine" or "let's say" could be replaced with a real client story.
6. Read the transitions. If any say "moving on" or "next up," replace with a re-hook formula.
