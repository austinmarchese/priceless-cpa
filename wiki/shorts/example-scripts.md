## Script 1: The 7 Questions

**Format:** Contrarian Take
**Topic:** CPA relationship quality
**Duration:** ~60 seconds

### Script

Here's how to know if your CPA is actually doing their job.

Ask them these 7 questions before you sign your return:

One — what strategies did you implement this year that reduced my tax liability?

Two — what's my projected tax liability for next year?

Three — is my current entity structure still optimal?

Four — am I paying myself the right S-corp salary?

Five — what retirement vehicles should I be using?

Six — am I maximizing my QBI deduction?

Seven — how are you coordinating with my other advisors?

If they can't answer these clearly, you don't have a tax strategist.

You have a tax preparer.

And that difference costs you thousands every year.

### Production Notes
- **Visual:** Text overlay for each question number
- **Tone:** Direct, confident, peer-to-peer
- **CTA:** "Drop a comment if you want the full checklist"

## Script 2: Tax Prep vs Tax Strategy

**Format:** Contrarian Take
**Topic:** Proactive vs reactive CPA
**Duration:** ~55 seconds

### Script

If your CPA only calls you during tax season, you don't have a tax strategist.

You have a tax preparer.

And here's the difference that's costing you money:

A tax preparer looks at last year. A tax strategist looks at next year.

A tax preparer tells you what you owe in April.

A tax strategist tells you what you're going to owe in November — and how to change it.

A tax preparer files your return.

A tax strategist questions your entity structure every time your income jumps.

Most entrepreneurs are paying $300K+ in taxes, running multiple entities, investing in real estate — and getting the same service as someone with a W-2.

That's not a strategy. That's compliance.

And compliance doesn't save you money. Strategy does.

## Script 3: The Entity Structure Trap

**Format:** Problem Warning
**Topic:** Entity structure
**Duration:** ~60 seconds

### Script

The entity structure that worked when you made $300K might be bleeding you dry at a million.

And most CPAs never bring it up.

Here's what I see all the time:

Entrepreneur starts a business, sets up an LLC, maybe elects S-corp status.

Makes sense at the time.

Then they scale. They start investing in real estate. They launch a second business. They hit seven figures.

But they're still running the same structure from when they were making 300K.

Now they're paying tens of thousands more than they need to.

Why? Because your entity structure should evolve as you grow.

What works at one income level actively hurts you at another.

If your CPA hasn't proactively brought up restructuring in the last year, ask them why.

Because the answer might save you a lot of money.

### Production Notes
- **Visual:** Could show simplified entity diagrams
- **Tone:** Educational but urgent
- **Target:** Anyone who's scaled significantly

## Script 4: Cost Segregation

**Format:** Before/After
**Topic:** Real estate tax strategy
**Duration:** ~55 seconds

### Script

Real estate investors — if you haven't heard of cost segregation, listen up.

A client came to me with a commercial property.

They were depreciating it over 39 years like everyone told them to.

We did a cost segregation study.

Now instead of writing off a few thousand a year, they accelerated over $200K in deductions in year one.

Same property. Same income. Completely different tax bill.

Here's how it works:

Cost segregation breaks down your property into components.

The land. The building. The carpets. The fixtures. The parking lot.

Each piece depreciates at different rates.

When you separate them properly, you front-load your deductions.

And if you qualify as a real estate professional or use the short-term rental loophole, those losses can offset your ordinary income.

If your CPA hasn't mentioned cost seg, you need to ask why.

### Production Notes
- **Visual:** Property photos, before/after numbers
- **Tone:** Expert sharing insider knowledge
- **Target:** RE investors who don't know about this

## Script 5: S-Corp Salary Mistake

**Format:** Problem Warning
**Topic:** S-corp optimization
**Duration:** ~50 seconds

### Script

S-corp owners — there's a sweet spot for your salary. And you're probably not hitting it.

Too high? You're paying unnecessary payroll taxes.

Too low? You're begging for an audit.

Here's what I see all the time:

The owner's paying themselves based on what feels right.

Or their CPA gave them a number once and they never revisited it.

But that number should change every single year based on your revenue, your profits, and what's happening in your business.

The IRS wants "reasonable compensation."

What's reasonable at $500K profit is different from $2M.

And the delta between getting it right and getting it wrong?

It can be tens of thousands of dollars a year.

If you haven't had a conversation about your S-corp salary this year, it's time to have one.

### Production Notes
- **Visual:** Calculator, payroll numbers
- **Tone:** Straightforward, practical
- **Target:** S-corp owners at any level

## Script 7: Real Estate Professional Status

**Format:** Contrarian Take
**Topic:** REP status / STR loophole
**Duration:** ~60 seconds

### Script

If you own real estate and have a business, you might be missing one of the best deductions in the tax code.

It's called Real Estate Professional status.

Or if you can't qualify for that, there's the short-term rental loophole.

Here's why it matters:

Normally, real estate losses are passive. They can only offset passive income.

But if you qualify as a Real Estate Professional — or you use the STR exception — those losses become active.

Now your depreciation, your cost seg deductions, your property losses can offset your business income.

We're talking potentially hundreds of thousands in deductions that normally would just sit there.

The catch? The rules are specific. You need to meet hour requirements. Document everything.

And most CPAs don't even bring it up because they don't want to deal with it.

If you have real estate and a high-income business, this is a conversation you need to have.

### Production Notes
- **Visual:** Real estate imagery, tax forms
- **Tone:** Expert revealing hidden knowledge
- **Target:** High-income entrepreneurs with RE

## Script 8: Questions to Fire Your CPA

**Format:** Challenge
**Topic:** CPA evaluation
**Duration:** ~50 seconds

### Script

I'm going to give you 3 questions.

If your CPA can't answer them, it might be time to move on.

Question one: "What proactive strategies did you implement this year — not just file, implement — that reduced my tax liability?"

If they can't point to specific moves, they're not doing strategy.

Question two: "What's my projected tax liability for this year, and when should I be making payments?"

If they can't give you a number, they're not doing planning.

Question three: "Is my current entity structure still optimal, or should we restructure?"

If they've never proactively brought this up, they're not thinking about your growth.

A CPA who can't answer these isn't a bad accountant.

They're just a compliance accountant.

And at your income level, compliance isn't enough.

### Production Notes
- **Visual:** Text overlay for each question
- **Tone:** Direct, challenging
- **Target:** Anyone questioning their CPA
