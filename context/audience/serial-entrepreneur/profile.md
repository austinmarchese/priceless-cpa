# Serial Entrepreneur - Primary Persona

The high-income, entrepreneurial business owner who needs a quarterback for their financial complexity.

## Who They Are

- **Age:** 35-45
- **Net worth:** $5-50M
- **What they call themselves:** Entrepreneur, serial entrepreneur
- **Location:** Spread across US, concentrated in coastal bigger cities
- **Family:** Mix — married with young kids, married no kids, or single (younger demographic)

## How They Built Wealth

Built wealth through **operating businesses** (ecom, agencies, service businesses), then reinvested profits into **real estate** (rental properties, syndications, investments).

They're entrepreneurial, not pure W-2. They understand business and investments deeply.

## Their Financial Structure

- **One main operating entity** — The core business (often S-corp)
- **Additional LLCs** — For real estate holdings, investments, side businesses
- **Almost always multiple entities** — But one primary operating vehicle
- **Complex and scattered** — Finances spread across entities, investments, and tax structures

## Current Setup (Before Priceless CPA)

- **Generic CPA** — Does taxes but doesn't understand the full picture
- **Older CPA** — Doesn't get modern business (ecom, crypto, digital)
- **Fragmented advisors** — Different CPAs, bookkeepers, attorneys who don't talk to each other
- **No deep relationship** — Haven't found someone they truly connect with
- **Reactive, not proactive** — Taxes get filed, but no strategic planning

## Core Pain Points

### The Surface Complaints
- "I feel like I'm overpaying on taxes"
- "No one's looking at the big picture"
- "I don't know if I'm set up right"
- "I need someone who actually gets what I'm doing"
- "My CPA never brings me ideas"

### The Real Pain
**Lack of a unified plan across investments, estate, and taxes** — which creates:
- Risk they can't see
- Money wasted on taxes they didn't need to pay
- Confusion about whether they're optimized
- No proactivity — they have to drive every conversation

### Emotional Undercurrent
- **Frustration** — Tired of explaining their situation to people who don't get it
- **FOMO** — Know other entrepreneurs are doing smarter things with tax strategy
- **Audit anxiety** — Want to know they're covered if something happens
- **Overwhelm** — Too many moving pieces, no one coordinating

## What They Want

### From Their CPA
- **Aggressive but legal** — Open to aggressive positions, but grounded in tax law
- **Proactive** — Brings ideas to them, doesn't wait to be asked
- **Someone who gets it** — Understands modern business models
- **A true relationship** — Concierge-level service, not transactional

### From Their Financial Setup
- **Simplicity** — Don't want to think about it constantly
- **Time savings** — Handle things without requiring their mental bandwidth
- **Asset protection & privacy** — They've built enough to need protection
- **Clear roadmap** — Know what's happening, when, and why

## Trigger Events

What makes them finally take action and reach out:

1. **Heard about a strategy** — Friend or podcast mentioned something their CPA never brought up
2. **Prior CPA made a mistake** — Signal that it's time to switch
3. **Moving jurisdictions** — Relocating to a new state
4. **Big event on the horizon** — Exit, sale, large deal coming
5. **Life event** — Estate planning need, partner change

## Objections & Hesitations

| Objection | What's Really Going On |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Trust** | "How do I know you're actually better than what I have?" |
| **Switching cost** | "It's going to take time and effort to move everything over" |
| **Risk** | "What if the transition doesn't go smoothly?" |
| **Timing** | "Is now the right moment to make this change?" |
| **Clarity** | "I need to see the plan before I commit" |

**Note:** Price is NOT the objection. High net worth clients pay for value. The barrier is trust and transition risk.
